Act I

At one side a craggy mountain, at the other a tower, the lower part of which serves as the prison of Sigismund. The door facing the spectators is half open. The action commences at nightfall.

SCENE I.

ROSAURA, CLARIN.

ROSAURA in man's attire appears on the rocky heights and descends to the plain. She is followed by CLARIN.

ROSAURA.

1Wild hippogriff swift speeding,
2Thou that dost run, the winged winds exceeding,
3Bolt which no flash illumes,
4Fish without scales, bird without shifting plumes,
5And brute awhile bereft
6Of natural instinct, why to this wild cleft,
7This labyrinth of naked rocks, dost sweep
8Unreined, uncurbed, to plunge thee down the steep?
9Stay in this mountain wold,
10And let the beasts their Phaeton behold.
11For I, without a guide,
12Save what the laws of destiny decide,
13Benighted, desperate, blind.
14Take any path whatever that doth wind
15Down this rough mountain to its base,
16Whose wrinkled brow in heaven frowns in the sun's bright face.
17Ah, Poland! in ill mood
18Hast thou received a stranger, since in blood
19The name thou writest on thy sands
20Of her who hardly here fares hardly at thy hands.
21My fate may well say so:—
22But where shall one poor wretch find pity in her woe?

CLARIN.

23Say two, if you please;
24Don't leave me out when making plaints like these.
25For if we are the two
26Who left our native country with the view
27Of seeking strange adventures, if we be
28The two who, madly and in misery,
29Have got so far as this, and if we still
30Are the same two who tumbled down this hill,
31Does it not plainly to a wrong amount,
32To put me in the pain and not in the account?

ROSAURA.

33I do not wish to impart,
34Clarin, to thee, the sorrows of my heart;
35Mourning for thee would spoil the consolation
36Of making for thyself thy lamentation;
37For there is such a pleasure in complaining,
38That a philosopher I've heard maintaining
39One ought to seek a sorrow and be vain of it,
40In order to be privileged to complain of it.

CLARIN.

41That same philosopher
42Was an old drunken fool, unless I err:
43Oh, that I could a thousand thumps present him,
44In order for complaining to content him!
45But what, my lady, say,
46Are we to do, on foot, alone, our way
47Lost in the shades of night?
48For see, the sun descends another sphere to light.

ROSAURA.

49So strange a misadventure who has seen?
50But if my sight deceives me not, between
51These rugged rocks, half-lit by the moon's ray
52And the declining day,
53It seems, or is it fancy? that I see
54A human dwelling?

CLARIN.

55So it seems to me,
56Unless my wish the longed-for lodging mocks.

ROSAURA.

57A rustic little palace 'mid the rocks
58Uplifts its lowly roof,
59Scarce seen by the far sun that shines aloof.
60Of such a rude device
61Is the whole structure of this edifice,
62That lying at the feet
63Of these gigantic crags that rise to greet
64The sun's first beams of gold,
65It seems a rock that down the mountain rolled.

CLARIN.

66Let us approach more near,
67For long enough we've looked at it from here;
68Then better we shall see
69If those who dwell therein will generously
70A welcome give us.

ROSAURA.

71See an open door
72(Funereal mouth 'twere best the name it bore),
73From which as from a womb
74The night is born, engendered in its gloom.

[The sound of chains is heard within.]

CLARIN.

75Heavens! what is this I hear?

ROSAURA.

76Half ice, half fire, I stand transfixed with fear.

CLARIN.

77A sound of chains, is it not?
78Some galley-slave his sentence here hath got;
79My fear may well suggest it so may be.

137Must I have less liberty?

139Dappled o'er with beauteous spots,

167Must I have less liberty?

174One lone man that liberty —

177Birds and fish that float or fly?

229Admiration to my ears.

231You produce a new amaze:

233I again would gazer be.

242If to see you gives me death,

244Something worse than death would grieve me,
245Anger, rage, corroding care,
246Death, but double death it were,
247Death with tenfold terrors rife,

249Gives the happy death, despair!
