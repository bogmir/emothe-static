Pedro Calderón de la Barca

Life is a dream

Notes

  1. Digital editor's note imparted I, 281

    "By these pains may be imparted,"

    The metre changes here to the vocal "asonante" in "a--e", and continues to the end of the Fourth Scene. [editor’s footnote]

  2. Digital editor's note children I, 622

    "children"

    The metre changes here to the "asonante" in "i—e", or their vocal equivalents, and is kept up for the remainder of the Act. (editor’s footnote)

  3. Digital editor's note happened II, 1020

    "happened"

    The metre of this and the following scene is the asonante in a—e. [editor’s footnote]

  4. Digital editor's note threatens II, 1793

    "When it some misfortune threatens!"

    The vocal asonante in e—e here commences, and continues to the end of the Sixteenth Scene. [editor’s footnote]

  5. Digital editor's note prisoned III, 2272

    "I, for what I know, am prisoned;"

    The asonante to the end of Scene IV. is in i—e, or their vocal equivalents. [editor’s footnote]

  6. Digital editor's note written III, 2302

    "By some half-starved poet written."

    These four lines are a paraphrase of the original. Clarin's jokes are different, and not much better. He says he spends his days studying philosophy in the works of 'Nicomedes' (or 'Not-eating'), and his nights perusing the decrees of the 'Nicene' Council (Concilio 'Niceno', the Council of 'No-Supper'). [Editor’s footnote]

  7. Digital editor's note mountain III, 3246

    "'Mid the thickets of the mountain,"

    Este verso y los dos siguientes están en boca de Segismundo en otras versiones/traducciones. En este caso es un soldaso («A SOLDIER») que hemos identificado con el soldado 1 («FIRST SOLDIER»). [Nota del editor digital]
