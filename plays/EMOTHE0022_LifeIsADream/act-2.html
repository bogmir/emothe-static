1104Cold as ice, the life-blood hardened

2182Since such thoughts my memory fill,
2183That it seems I'm dreaming still:
2184Nor is this a great mistake;

2189I can see when I am sleeping,
2190And when waking I can dream.

CLOTALDO.

2191What you may have dreamed of, say.

2192If I thought it only seemed,
2193I would tell not what I dreamed,
2194But what I beheld, I may.
2195I awoke, and lo! I lay
2196(Cruel and delusive thing!)

2198Bright with blooms from rosy bowers,

2210Doubtless you repaid me well?

2211No, not well: for, calling thee
2212Traitor vile, in furious strife

2215I was master, and would be

2217Love one woman could defend . . . . .
2218That, at least, for truth I deem,
2219All else ended like a dream,
2220That alone can never end.

CLOTALDO

2221From his place the King hath gone,aparte
2222Touched by his pathetic words:aparte
2223[Aloud] Speaking of the king of birds
2224Soaring to ascend his throne,
2225Thou didst fancy one thine own;
2226But in dreams, however bright,
2227Thou shouldst still have kept in sight
2228How for years I tended thee,
2229For 'twere well, whoe'er we be,
2230Even in dreams to do what's right.

SCENE XVIII.

SIGISMUND.

2231That is true: then let's restrain
2232This wild rage, this fierce condition
2233Of the mind, this proud ambition,
2234Should we ever dream again:
2235And we'll do so, since 'tis plain,
2236In this world's uncertain gleam,
2237That to live is but to dream:
2238Man dreams what he is, and wakes
2239Only when upon him breaks
2240Death's mysterious morning beam.
2241The king dreams he is a king,
2242And in this delusive way
2243Lives and rules with sovereign sway;
2244All the cheers that round him ring,
2245Born of air, on air take wing.
2246And in ashes (mournful fate!)
2247Death dissolves his pride and state:
2248Who would wish a crown to take,
2249Seeing that he must awake
2250In the dream beyond death's gate?
2251And the rich man dreams of gold,
2252Gilding cares it scarce conceals,
2253And the poor man dreams he feels

2255Dreams he too who rank would hold,
2256Dreams who bears toil's rough-ribbed hands,
2257Dreams who wrong for wrong demands,
2258And in fine, throughout the earth,
2259All men dream, whate'er their birth,
2260And yet no one understands.
2261'Tis a dream that I in sadness
2262Here am bound, the scorn of fate;
2263'Twas a dream that once a state

2265What is life? 'Tis but a madness.
2266What is life? A thing that seems,
