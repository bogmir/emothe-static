ACTO III

Aurora and the Marquis

AURORA

1447What I have told you is the honest truth.

MARQUIS

1448There is no way that you can persuade me.
1449Make sure we can’t be heard: watch what you say.

AURORA

1450I wished to give you this sad news, Marquis,
1451So as to ask you for your kind advice.

MARQUIS

1452In what predicament was it you saw
1453Casandra and Federico?

AURORA

Casandra and Federico? Hear me.
1454I do confess I loved the Count myself
1455And I can assure you that he loved me,
1456But he betrayed me like the Greek Ulysses.
1457In time our love increased; plans were being made
1458For our wedding when Casandra was brought here,
1459Trusting in given words, if men’s promises
1460Are to be trusted among their equals.
1461Federico went off to bring her here
1462And he came back so sad that when the Duke
1463Wanted to know his ills, and I told him
1464Our straits, he said he was jealous of you;
1465And as love permits one to cause mistrust
1466When things are going badly, I did try
1467To make him jealous of you, dear Carlos.
1468But my attempt had just the same effect
1469As on a diamond, because jealousy
1470Makes no mark when the subject’s not in love.
1471Well, seeing myself repudiated so
1472And Federico not in love with me,
1473I sought to find out what had been the cause
1474And, as jealousy has very keen eyes
1475That can even see through walls, I found it out.
1476Casandra’s dressing table is adorned
1477With portraits, ornaments and mirrors too
1478Along with two strongboxes in her chamber.
1479With my suspicions silently I went
1480And saw, oh, horror! there before my eyes
1481Reflected in a glass, how my false Count
1482Was kissing Casandra upon the lips.
1483This made me run to weep for my heartache
1484And also that of the two blind lovers,
1485Who seem to be competing in their love
1486And their disdain in absence of the Duke
1487And there they show for every eye to see
1488The greatest shamelessness upon this earth.
1489I thought the looking glass that showed their kiss
1490Darkened the image to conceal such lust;
1491But since love is a deal more curious,
1492Their infamous affair continues still
1493Much to the discredit of the poor Duke.
1494They say he returns victorious from the fray
1495With laurel branches adorning his brow
1496For the most glorious of feats of arms
1497With which the Pope puts down the enemy.
1498Do please tell me what course I am to take
1499In such a case. I suffer from suspicions
1500Of worse harm still, if what you say is true
1501And you sincerely love me, I’m afraid
1502You’ll imitate the Count and leave me too.

MARQUIS

1503Aurora, only death cannot be vanquished
1504And although many Phoenixes revive
1505Because they say they do not die at all
1506Those who live only for their own repute.
1507Go tell the Duke to marry us and if
1508He says yes we’ll be off to Mantua,
1509Where you will be completely out of danger.
1510If a tiger throws itself into the sea
1511Unable to withstand the dreadful pain
1512Of having hunters murder its offspring,
1513What will not do Achilles of Ferrara
1514To preserve both his honour and good name?
1515How do you think he’ll wipe that ugly stain
1516Forever more unless he seeks bloodshed,
1517If heaven does not first chastise his sins
1518By having forks of lightning strike him down?
1519So this is the advice I give to you.

AURORA

1520My troubled mind is glad to accept it.

MARQUIS

1521The glass mirror will be Medusa’s shield
1522In which this new Circe saw the vile kiss.

Federico and Batín

FEDERICO

1523He didn’t want to wait for a reception?

BATÍN

1524As soon as he saw the outskirts of Mantua,
1525He left his people and rode in alone,
1526Not even wanting you to be alerted;
1527Love finds it hard to check his need to see you
1528There is no greater love for the Duchess.
1529You are indeed the sunshine of his eye
1530And his patience has known four months’ eclipse.
1531Dear Count, prepare the triumphal parade
1532For everyone who enters, for the troops
1533Shall come adorned with myriad trophies.

FEDERICO

1534I always see Aurora with the Marquis.

AURORA

1535How dashing he is!!

FEDERICO

How dashing he is!! With such mild disdain
1536Do you attempt to calm my discontent?

AURORA

1537The Marquis taunted you successfully:
1538You seem to waken from four months of sleep!

MARQUIS

1539Good Count, I am afraid I did not know
1540You felt the way you tell me now you do.
1541I’ve courted Aurora in the belief
1542That there was no competitor but me,
1543But had I known that my rival was you
1544I’d have been loath to match myself with you.
1545You know I never saw you pursue her,
1546But if it is your wish I’ll leave her to you
1547Because your merits greatly outstrip mine.

Exit Marquis

AURORA

1548What is it that you are trying to do?
1549What frenzy has possessed you without love?
1550How often have you seen the Marquis with me
1551Since you became so sad and long after,
1552And yet you did not deign to look at me,
1553And much less did you deign to woo me?
1554And now you show your jealousy to me
1555When I’m about to wed the one I love?
1556Dear Count, that’s quite enough of this nonsense.
1557Let me be married and please realise
1558That I shall die before I shall pretend.
1559Go back to being sad again, dear Count,
1560Go back to your previous tranquillity,
1561For I have suffered very profoundly
1562For the cruel way in which you treated me.
1563I have such sentiments for you no longer.
1564God spare me from deceit and sophistry!
1565Upon your life, it’s too late now to use me!

Exit Aurora

BATÍN

1566What have you done?

FEDERICO

What have you done? Good Lord, I do not know!

BATÍN

1567You remind me of Emperor Tiberius
1568If you’ve no reason to split up this pair.
1569After he had his wife assassinated
1570He called her to sit down and eat with him.
1571And Messalla Corvinus was a Roman general
1572Who was unable to recall his name.

FEDERICO

1573And I cannot recall that I’m a man.

BATÍN

1574Or you are like that peasant who, after
1575Two years of wedlock with his country lass,
1576Came out and told her: “What black eyes you have!”

FEDERICO

1577Alas, Batín, it is my poor memory.

BATÍN

1578You’re like the Basque who left his mule harnessed
1579And wondered why it didn’t want to eat;
1580And while he stroked and patted the mule’s mane,
1581He had an animal doctor examine it.
1582And when this veterinarian saw the bit,
1583He sent the Basque man out and on his own
1584Took off the bit; and when the man came back
1585The mule had eaten every bite of fodder
1586And, after that, it ate the manger too.
1587“Good vet, I swear to God you are a doctor
1588And from now on I want you to cure me
1589As well as my poor mule and my family!”
1590What bit is it that you have in your mouth
1591That will not let you eat, if I’m your doctor?
1592Please let me know what you are waiting for.

FEDERICO

1593Batín, I really am in a sad state.

BATÍN

1594Well, don’t eat any fodder and say nothing.

Enter Casandra and Lucrecia

CASANDRA

1595Is he coming now?

LUCRECIA

Is he coming now? He is, milady.

CASANDRA

1596And how so fast?

LUCRECIA

And how so fast? He left them all to see you.

CASANDRA

1597Don’t believe it. I’d rather see my death.
1598Well then, kind sir, where is the Duke, my lord?

FEDERICO

1599They say he is now very close to here;
1600A sure sign of the love he feels for you.

CASANDRA

1601It kills me every time I realiseaparte
1602That I’ll no longer see you as I used to.aparte

FEDERICO

1603My love could hope for no more cruel deathaparte
1604Than the arrival of the Duke, my sire.aparte

CASANDRA

1605I am afraid, dear Count, I lose my senses.aparte

FEDERICO

1606Not I, because I have already lost them.aparte

CASANDRA

1607I am without a soul.aparte

FEDERICO

I am without a soul. I without life.aparte

CASANDRA

1608So what are we to do?aparte

FEDERICO

So what are we to do? We are to die.aparte

CASANDRA

1609Is there no other remedy than that?aparte

FEDERICO

1610There’s not, for if I lose you, what reasonaparte
1611Shall I have to go on living this life?aparte

CASANDRA

1612Is that why you are going to lose me then?aparte

FEDERICO

1613My plan is to pretend from this day forthaparte
1614That I’m in love with Aurora and serve her,aparte
1615I’ll even ask the Duke to give me her hand,aparte
1616In order to deceive him and the palace,aparte
1617Where I know they do not speak well of us.aparte

CASANDRA

1618Affronts? Will not mere jealousy suffice?aparte
1619And marry her? Are you in your right mind?aparte

FEDERICO

1620The danger we are in obliges me.aparte

CASANDRA

1621What? I swear to God that if you laugh at meaparte
1622When you have been the cause of such distress,aparte
1623I shall reveal –or else you don’t know me–aparte
1624Your perfidy and my unfaithfulness.aparte

FEDERICO

1625But milady…aparte

CASANDRA

But milady… There’s no more to be said.aparte

FEDERICO

1626But they will hear you.aparte

CASANDRA

But they will hear you. Don’t try to stop me!aparte
1627The Duke may have me killed ten times overaparte
1628But I shall not consent to your marriage.aparte

Enter Floro, Febo, Ricardo, Albano. Lucindo, the Duke behind, gallantly dressed in soldier’s uniform

RICARDO

1629They were all getting ready to receive you.

DUKE

1630It was wise to come early for love’s sake.

CASANDRA

1631How is it, Lord, that you could be persuaded
1632To come to Ferrara without warning?

FEDERICO

1633And if milady has reason to complain
1634It seems my love’s to blame for her anguish.

DUKE

1635My son, a father’s love, which knows no rest
1636In devotion for its own flesh and blood,
1637Was what brought forward the time of my trip;
1638For neither weariness nor exertion
1639Can make one with a wish to see his own
1640Put up with a long wait before returning.
1641And you, milady, do not take umbrage
1642If I should love the Count as much as you.

CASANDRA

1643Your blood and his virtue, my lord, set right
1644The favour that you show, and I thank you
1645Because your merit is no less than his.

DUKE

1646I know the two of you do love me well
1647And I am so obliged to both of you
1648That I shall gladly show it when the time comes.
1649I know that Federico ruled my state,
1650While I was absent, so well that no subject
1651Has had the slightest reason to complain.
1652While in the midst of battle I confess
1653That I imagined him running the land
1654As the most perfect senator of all.
1655Thank God that with dishonourable flight
1656The enemies of the good Pope of Rome
1657Respect him in my absence like myself!
1658With laurel crown upon my brow I kissed
1659His hand while Rome applauded my triumph
1660As though I were the Spanish consul Trajan.
1661And so I do intend from here on forth
1662To change vice into virtue for my name
1663To be not only praised for my conquests
1664But to be celebrated for my worth.
1665When a man rises to such heights as these
1666It is not right that he be more acclaimed
1667For his weaknesses than for his virtues.

RICARDO

1668Here come Carlos and Aurora, my lord.

Enter Carlos and Aurora

AURORA

1669Your lordship is most welcome here today
1670And she who adores you waits anxiously.

MARQUIS

1671Give good Carlos your hand; he wants his love
1672To be known.

DUKE

To be known. May the arms pay the soul’s debts
1673Of one who loves so well and passionately.
1674Although love suffers from long absences
1675It is repaid upon the happy day
1676That it enjoys the honey of its bliss.
1677Herewith, my dear loved ones, I wish to rest
1678After my long journey and, since it’s late,
1679You’ll celebrate my coming later on.

FEDERICO

1680May heaven guard you, sir, a hundred years.

Exeunt all with the Duke, and Batín and Ricardo remain

BATÍN

1681My friend Ricardo!

RICARDO

My friend Ricardo! My good friend Batín!

BATÍN

1682How did you fare in those so cruel wars?

RICARDO

1683All went according as justice would have it
1684With heaven on our side for our defence.
1685Lombardy has been freed and the enemy
1686Has fled most shamefully because the Duke,
1687The lion of the church, with one sole roar
1688Defeated them and made them down their arms.
1689The Duke has made his name in Italy;
1690For if Saul killed a thousand men one day
1691And maidens sang his glory, David killed
1692One hundred thousand men; and now the Duke
1693Has done a deed that’s tantamount to his.
1694There are no ladies, no more dinners now,
1695There are no shields and neither are there swords,
1696He thinks of nothing now but Casandra;
1697There is no other love but that professed
1698For the Count, his son, and the Duchess, his wife:
1699The Duke has now become quite sanctified.

BATÍN

1700What’s this I hear? What are you telling me?

RICARDO

1701While honours make some people proud and cruel,
1702And hold a poor opinion of everyone,
1703Thinking themselves immortal as the gods,
1704The Duke has turned quite humble and modest
1705And seems to disdain the laurels of success;
1706The flapping of flags has not turned his head.

BATÍN

1707Let’s pray to God after these humble ways
1708He’ll not become like in that tale by Aesop
1709Where, after plaguing Venus with petitions,
1710A Dominican cat, all black and white,
1711Was turned into a woman. Then one day,
1712All dressed up in her fine clothes and jewels,
1713She saw pass by one of those animals
1714That chew on paper like the best of poets
1715And made a leap to catch the little mouse
1716Showing that nature rules that once a cat
1717Always a cat, in secula seculorum.

RICARDO

1718Don’t be afraid the Duke, in consequence,
1719Will go back to his youth and his old ways
1720Especially if he begets offspring,
1721Whose little hands can soften an old heart.

BATÍN

1722I’d be so glad if that were to be true.

RICARDO

1723Goodbye, Batín.

BATÍN

Goodbye, Batín. But where are you off to?

RICARDO

1724I’m off to see my Fabia, who’s waiting.

Exit Ricardo

Enter Duke with some papers

DUKE

1725Is there some servant here?

BATÍN

Is there some servant here? Your humblest one.

DUKE

1726Batín!

BATÍN

Batín! God bless you. You are most welcome.
1727Give me your hand.

DUKE

Give me your hand. What were you doing here?

BATÍN

1728Ricardo was telling me about your deeds,
1729Comparing you to Hector of Italy.

DUKE

1730How did the Count rule while I was absent?

BATÍN

1731My lord, indeed I could say that, in peace,
1732He equalled your exploits on the battlefield.

DUKE

1733And did he get on well with Casandra?

BATÍN

1734I think there is no better stepmother
1735In all the world; she is extremely wise
1736The soul of virtue and of holiness.

DUKE

1737There is nothing I can acknowledge more
1738Than that she was kind to my son the Count:
1739And as the Count is the one I most love
1740Who was so sad when I went off to war
1741I am extremely pleased to hear you say
1742Casandra behaved with such kindness to him,
1743That they are friends and get on famously,
1744Which is just what my soul wants most badly
1745Of all the things I could ask heaven for.
1746Thus in my home there are two victories:
1747The one I bring from battle and the one
1748Of fair Casandra conquering Federico.
1749I think that that she will be my only love
1750Because of her behaviour towards my son
1751And I’ll abandon all my wanton ways
1752As I am tired of my lubricious youth.

BATÍN

1753The Pope has worked a miracle, my lord,
1754By sending the Duke of Ferrara to war
1755And making him come back a virtuous hermit.
1756By God, you might start up a monastery!

DUKE

1757I hope my subjects know I’m a changed man.

BATÍN

1758Why did Your Grace take such a short respite?

DUKE

1759Because when I was entering the palace
1760Some men were waiting for me on the steps
1761To give me these papers, and I preferred
1762To see what they were before going to rest
1763In case it happened that they were complaints.
1764Go now and I shall stay here on my own
1765For it’s a matter for rulers to see.

BATÍN

1766The heavens, which remunerate those men
1767Who give priority to public welfare,
1768Will shower you with laurels and with fame.

Exit

DUKE

1769This paper says:

Reads

This paper says: “My lord, I am Eustacio,
1770I work in palace gardens and, while teaching
1771Others to lay fine lawns and plant fair flowers,
1772I had six sons: I beg you to employ
1773The two eldest of these…” That is enough!
1774I’ll take more care of whom I recompense.

Reads

1775“Lucinda says she’s left without her spouse
1776Captain Arnaldo…” She requests redress.

Reads

1777“Albano, six years here…” A new request.

Reads

1778“Julio Camilo, imprisoned…” Another!

Reads

1779“Paula de San Germán, an honest maid…”
1780Well, if she’s honest, she needs nothing else
1781Unless she wants me to find her a spouse.
1782This one was sealed, and he who gave it to me
1783Looked so upset and so disconsolate
1784That I would like to have found out some more.

Reads

1785“My Lord, I wish to give you some sad news,
1786The Count and Duchess, while you were away…”
1787My thoughts did not deceive me: patience, then!

Reads

1788“Dared to offend your honour and your bed.”
1789How shall I overcome such shamelessness?

Reads

1790“If you watch out, you’ll see it for yourself.”
1791But what is this I read? Can it be true?
1792You know I am the father of this man
1793Who you declare to be cuckolding me!
1794It is a lie! This viciousness is false!
1795How could my sweet Casandra offend me?
1796Do you not know that the Count is my son?
1797But this paper says they’re woman and man.
1798What dreadful words and how they injure me!
1799But you will say there are no evil deeds
1800That are not to be found in humankind.
1801The anger of the Lord must fall on them!
1802This was the curse that Nathan placed on David;
1803I’m reviled too: my son is Absalom.
1804But it is sad if God chastises me
1805For concubines when Casandra’s my wife.
1806My libidinous ways as a young man
1807Have brought upon me now this punishment,
1808Although I did not lie with Bathsheba
1809Nor did I cause the death of Uriah.
1810Oh, my perfidious son, if it is true!
1811Because I don’t believe that any other
1812Would dare to commit such a heinous sin.
1813But if you have betrayed me, I do pray
1814That after I have brought about your death
1815I might bring back your life eternally
1816So as to have you killed over again
1817Every time I engender you afresh.
1818What great disloyalty! What violence!
1819Oh, absence, it is truly certified
1820That even a father cannot trust his son
1821If he’s away from home for a long time!
1822How can I know for sure the witnesses
1823I call to testify won’t slander me?
1824So what’s the point in checking out the facts?
1825No one would speak like this of someone’s son
1826If it were not true that he had betrayed him.
1827To punish him is not to wreak revenge,
1828He who punishes is not a revenger
1829And the news does not make me take revenge,
1830For damage to one’s honour does not need
1831Revenge: it is enough to make it known.

Enter Federico

FEDERICO

1832I know you are not resting, so I have come
1833To talk to you.

DUKE

To talk to you. May the heavens guard you.

FEDERICO

1834And I come to ask a favour of you too.

DUKE

1835My love will grant you anything you ask.

FEDERICO

1836When you bade me to wed my cousin Aurora,
1837My lord, I was inclined to do your will;
1838But Carlos was extremely jealous of me
1839Which made me decide not to obey you.
1840But then you went away and then I knew
1841That my great love had caused such illusions
1842That were indeed to blame for my confusion.
1843So we made peace and I promised, my lord,
1844That, to put right what I had said before,
1845I’d get married as soon as you allowed
1846After you’d given up your soldiering.
1847So that is what I come to beg you for.

DUKE

1848You couldn’t make me happier, dear Count.
1849Go now that I may tell it to your mother,
1850For it is only fair that she should know,
1851And it is not right that you are to wed
1852Without her knowledge; and she must give you
1853Her kind blessing, just as your father does.

FEDERICO

1854Aurora not being of her flesh and blood,
1855Why does Your Grace wish to inform Casandra?

DUKE

1856What does it matter that she’s not related
1857Seeing that your mother is Casandra?

FEDERICO

1858For many a years my mother Laurencia
1859Lies dead and buried in her hallowed grave.

DUKE

1860Do you not like it that I call her mother?
1861They say that all the time I was away
1862You got on very well, I’m glad to say.

FEDERICO

1863God knows that it’s not so, I promise you,
1864Though I’ve no reason to complain, I know
1865Your Grace loves her so much and rightly so,
1866That although they all thought her an angel
1867She did not act like an angel to me.

DUKE

1868I’m sorry they deceive me when they say
1869There is no one Casandra favours more.

FEDERICO

1870Sometimes she favours me and other times
1871She makes it clear that sons of other mothers
1872Can never be the same as one’s own sons.

DUKE

1873You speak the truth and I believe your words
1874And she could do me a great good by loving
1875You rather than loving me, for in that way
1876She would be sure of peace. May God guard you.

FEDERICO

1877And may God bless you too.

Exit

DUKE

And may God bless you too. I don’t know how
1878I could peruse the Count’s most guileful face.
1879What a hypocrite! What a shameless cad!
1880He uses the false story about Aurora
1881So that I won’t believe he cuckolds me!
1882What makes me confident of his betrayal
1883Is what he has to say about Casandra
1884Behaving badly towards him in my absence.
1885He thinks the best way to hush up his crime
1886Is to cover it up when it’s flagrant.
1887He does not like to have me call her mother
1888Because his father’s wife is his own lover
1889And it’s not right for him to call her mother.
1890But how can I believe this case so easily?
1891Couldn’t it be that one of the Count’s foes
1892Has made up this story with perfidy
1893In order that I will chastise the Count
1894And in this way will be himself avenged?
1895If I believe I’m not avenged but hurt.

Enter Casandra and Aurora

AURORA

1896My life depends on you, milady, now.

CASANDRA

1897You’ve made a wise choice indeed, Aurora.

AURORA

1898Here is the Duke.

CASANDRA

Here is the Duke. My Lord, you’re working still?

DUKE

1899I owe my state these signs of my esteem
1900To compensate for my prolonged absence.
1901Although I know the Count and you have ruled
1902Most skilfully and I am grateful to you.
1903The praise of both of you’s on every lip.

CASANDRA

1904It is the Count whom you must thank, not me,
1905I do not flatter him when I confirm
1906That he is chivalrous in all his acts,
1907As self-assured as he is talented:
1908He is the very image of yourself.

DUKE

1909I know that he has acted so like me
1910In all his acts that I might have been here.
1911I promise you, milady, recompense.

CASANDRA

1912I have a new request to make of you,
1913It is about Aurora: Carlos loves her
1914And wants to beg her hand; she loves him too
1915And I would be so pleased if you would grant it.

DUKE

1916I think that you have come too late to ask me:
1917The Count has just made me the same request.

CASANDRA

1918The Count has asked if he may marry her?

DUKE

1919That’s so, Casandra.

CASANDRA

That’s so, Casandra. The Count, you say?

DUKE

That’s so, Casandra. The Count, you say? Yes.

CASANDRA

1920I’d doubt the truth from any other source.

DUKE

1921I have already given my permission
1922The two of them are to be wed tomorrow.

CASANDRA

1923It shall be as Aurora disposes.

AURORA

1924Forgive me, sire, I’ll not marry the Count.

DUKE

1925(Oh, why should I wait any longer now?)aparte
1926Aurora, is the Count not superior
1927To the Marquis in gallantry, in reason
1928And in valour?

AURORA

And in valour? Well, that I cannot say.
1929When I wanted so badly to wed him
1930And I begged him, he only turned me down;
1931So now that he’s the one who desires me
1932It’s only fair that I should turn him down.

DUKE

1933But do it for my sake if not for his.

AURORA

1934Marriage must be with one that deserves love
1935And I now have no feelings for the Count.

Exit Aurora

DUKE

1936It is a strange decision that she’s made.

CASANDRA

1937Aurora’s right, although impertinent.

DUKE

1938She is not right, and marry him she shall!

CASANDRA

1939My Lord, don’t use your power in this case
