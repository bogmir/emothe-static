ACT II

Casandra and Lucrecia

LUCRECIA

691Your Highness has made me feel quite amazed.

CASANDRA

692No highnesses are sad, and less the lowly.
693But I would rather be a commoner
694Who wakes up next a farmer every day
695And not a lord in fine accoutrements.
696I wish to God I had been born a peasant
697So I might find someone who’d cherish me
698For my own merits and return my love.
699In that low background as in royalty
700One savours pleasures that are just as great
701As those enjoyed by those of lofty birth
702If love’s effects each night are tantamount.
703The sun does not shine on a married pair
704Through stained-glass windows at first light of dawn
705Embraced with greater love, nor more refreshed
706Because they lie at rest in marble halls.
707But the sun’s rays perhaps peeped in at dawn
708Through broken or cracked walls to shine upon
709Two bodies that together formed one soul.
710How lucky she who does not feel disdain
711But rises happily beside her man;
712And in the first fountain she comes upon
713Looks at her image and with both her hands
714Washes her face instead of drying tears
715Because her husband is a faithless man
716However much a duke he claims to be.
717Only one night I held him in my arms
718In a whole month; I saw him many more
719But he had no desire to come to me.
720Well how can I complain about my lot
721When I well knew before of his repute?
722And he would not consent to change his ways
723Although he went to live in other lands.
724A man who arrives home at break of day
725Having done anything that gives him pleasure
726Is taken for a man who lives quite free;
727Who is entitled then to clip his wings?
728But treating his good wife with such disdain,
729Forgetting all about his marriage vows,
730Either he makes a point of tempting fate
731Or he is nothing but an imbecile.
732The Duke must be of the opinion then
733That, after tying tight the wedding knot,
734The wife must be kept like a jewel at home
735To decorate and beautify the house
736As though she were a table or a chair.
737But this is something that I don’t condone
738For if the husband is exemplary
739The wife will always be his loyal slave.
740A woman who behaves with rectitude
741Wants to be deemed a housewife in her home
742And not just an old piece of furniture.
743Suffice it for him not to be beholden
744Without the need to show discourtesy,
745And he should give no reason for complaint
746Rather than later offer remedy.

LUCRECIA

747Your speech has made me pity you indeed
748For I can see you have just cause for woe.
749Who would have thought that after wedding you
750The Duke would be so vicious, or that he
751Would show you no respect or gallantry?
752If we were speaking of a paramour,
753You could perhaps provoke his jealousy
754To stimulate again his interest in you:
755You might show interest in another man
756To try to win him over once again.
757But who needs to win over her own spouse?
758Have you written to your father of your ills?

CASANDRA

759No, Lucrecia, no other knows my plight.

LUCRECIA

760According to nature and reason both,
761It would be better if you’d wooed the Count
762So, wed to you, the Duke might have no doubt
763That his grandson would be his rightful heir.
764The melancholy suffered by the Count
765Is more than justified, I fear, milady.

CASANDRA

766It is not his envy for me, Lucrecia,
767For I shall not be giving him siblings;
768So Federico can calmly rest assured
769That I am not the reason for his ills:
770It is indeed misfortune for us both.

The Duke, Federico and Batín

DUKE

771If I had known that it would make you sad, Count,
772I’d never have decided to be wed.

FEDERICO

773My lord, it was quite foolish on my part
774To feel upset because of your union.
775I have no doubt at all that you love me.
776You’re wise enough to know that if your marriage
777Upset me, I’d be sure to cover up.
778You can see I am suffering from ill health,
779But you can’t see what has brought it about.

DUKE

780Our physicians in Mantua and Ferrara
781Agree that marriage is the remedy
782For those affected by melancholy.

FEDERICO

783It was considered remedy for maidens
784But not for gentlemen of my esteem.

CASANDRA

785(The Duke has barely looked at me today.aparte
786What lack of courtesy and etiquette!aparte

LUCRECIA

787You cannot blame him if he hasn’t seen you.aparte

CASANDRA

788Pretending not to see me is despotic.aparte
789Come on, Lucrecia, one day he’ll be sorryaparte
790For having treated me with such disdain.)aparte

Exeunt Casandra and Lucrecia

DUKE

791Although I realise I am mistaken
792Regarding the reason for your poor health,
793I still believe that the ideal solution
794Is that you marry the one I propose.

FEDERICO

795Do you by any chance mean Aurora?

DUKE

796You’ve taken the words right out of my mouth,
797As though you had the very same intention.
798I’ve spoken to the assembly of sages,
799And they agree it would relieve your woe.

FEDERICO

800Indeed they know but little of my heart!
801They vainly do believe I feel dejected
802But think I am offended for no cause.
803They know I never disapproved your marriage,
804But wanted it for your deliverance.

DUKE

805So I believe and always have believed it,
806And pay for that obedience, Federico,
807By feeling self-reproach for having wed.

FEDERICO

808My lord, so that you will not think me angered
809For something that’s so easy to explain,
810To pay you for the love that you profess me
811I need to know my cousin’s sentiments;
812And gladly I shall then obey your order
813For otherwise it would be quite unfair.

DUKE

814I heard in her own words that she’d be willing.

FEDERICO

815I know for certain there’s a novelty:
816And it is that the Marquis of Ferrara
817Has stayed on in this place to pay her court.

DUKE

818But what is that to you, my Federico?

FEDERICO

819One who is due to wed his heart’s desire
820After another man has paid her court,
821Can be adjudged to write a palimpsest.

DUKE

822If men are willing to believe a rumour
823And lock up womenfolk in castles
824Only to shelter them from spying eyes;
825The purest looking glass will become tarnished
826With your breath if you wish to see yourself,
827What does it matter if you look with care?
828For then it can be cleaned and made like new
829As faultless as it was before you looked.

FEDERICO

830Your words and wit indeed encourage me.
831My Lord, when sparkling rays fly forth
832From someone’s forge and the blacksmith attempts
833To extinguish the flames that are so caused,
834He’ll sprinkle water on the raging fire;
835The flames will rise again rebelliously
836And the blaze will soon devour the damp surrounds.
837And thus a spouse who’s not at all aware
838Of the existence of a previous lover
839May fan the flames and stimulate desire
840But only manage to revive the fire.
841And so I must be wary of a lover
842For I have no desire to be the water
843That burns my honour and befouls my name.

DUKE

844I find you very dull and disrespectful,
845Dear Count. You speak about Aurora in a way
846That smacks of rudeness and indecency.

FEDERICO

847Pray, stay!

DUKE

Pray, stay! What for?

Exit

FEDERICO

Pray, stay! What for? Please wait, my lord, I pray.

BATÍN

848How cleverly you have gained the Duke’s favour!

FEDERICO

849I expect nothing but his enmity
850Because I want to be quite miserable;
851My desperation has reached such a peak,
852That nought can save me but my timely death.
853And should I die, I’d nothing loath come back
854To die a thousand times each new life gained.
855I feel so bad that I don’t dare to live
856Or die, because I’d see that coming back
857To life was only just to die once more.
858And if I’m not inclined to kill myself
859It is because I am so sick at heart
860That death means less than life can mean to me.

BATÍN

861According to your words, my dearest Count,
862You neither want to live nor yet to die;
863For you are rather a hermaphrodite
864Not knowing whether to choose life or death:
865Just as a hermaphrodite is a blend
866Of two sexes; you’re one of life and death.
867But, God Almighty knows that your despair
868Obliges me to ask you why you’re sad
869Or give me leave to go away and moan
870Because I am condemned for being loyal.
871Please give me your hand so I may kiss it.

FEDERICO

872Batín, if I could tell you what is wrong,
873I might be able to put an end to it;
874But my unhappiness is not cerebral,
875And only has relation with my sense.
876When I attempt to talk to comfort me
877I’m given pause each time I realise
878From tongue to soul there’s such a great distance.
879Be off, if you so wish, and leave me here
880For there is no sign of relief for me.

Enter Casandra and Aurora

CASANDRA

881What is the reason for your tears, my dear?

AURORA

882Your Grace, do you not think it cause enough
883That I am spurned and ignored by the Count?
884He says I am in love with the Marquis,
885That I’m in love with Carlos Gonzaga.
886Me? How? But no, I know what it’s about:
887He wants to go to Spain in his disgust
888At seeing his father’s marriage to Casandra.
889Before I was the apple of his eye
890But now his eye shows nothing but dislike.
891What new auroras brought with them the day
892When the Count stopped inquiring for Aurora?
893In what garden or at what fresh fountain
894Did he not speak to me of his desire?
895Were not my lips and brow bright flowers to him?
896When did he start to move away from me?
897What instant did he feel alive without me?
898How could he live should I not hearten him?
899Living day after day makes love stronger
900Between two souls that love has made as one.
901This has been going on since we were young,
902This love he now has killed with his betrayals.
903So strong was his desire for his lost legacy!

CASANDRA

904I’m sorry I’m to blame for this, Aurora,
905But try to harness your disquietude
906While I talk to him; although I must say
907It is not easy to assuage jealousy.

AURORA

908Jealousy?

CASANDRA

Jealousy? With the Marquis, so says the Duke.

AURORA

909Your Grace, you must believe that melancholy
910Is neither caused by love nor jealousy.

Exit Aurora

CASANDRA

911Federico!

FEDERICO

Federico! It is I, milady,
912Give me your hand so that your slave may kiss it.

CASANDRA

913Down on your knees? Dear Count, don’t bow so low
914For I indeed shall call you Excellency.

FEDERICO

915Thus you would mortify my love for you;
916I will not rise till you give me your hand.

CASANDRA

917Here are my arms. What’s wrong with you today?
918Whatever is it that you’ve seen in me?
919You’re trembling! Do you know how I love you?

FEDERICO

920My soul informed my heart, and then my heart
921Informed my face, which caused the blush you see.

CASANDRA

922Will you leave us alone a while, Batín?
923I’d like to have a few words with the Count.

BATÍN

924(The Count is addled and I wonder whyaparte
925Casandra wishes to hold forth with him alone!)aparte

Exit

FEDERICO

926(Oh, Heavens! As I die like a phoenix,aparte
927Put out the flames at once or I shall haveaparte
928To suffer another life just like this one.)aparte

CASANDRA

929Federico, although I know the words
930Aurora said about your fervent care,
931After the arrival in Ferrara of
932Marquis Carlos, the reason you’ll not wed,
933I can hardly believe you make so little
934Of your merits, which are, as wise men say,
935Mistrust and envy; he is more soldierly,
936Although the Marquis is gallant indeed,
937Than a chivalrous courtier. It so happens
938That I believe your sadness and reserve,
939The consequence of the Duke’s marrying me,
940Taking away from you your every right,
941As soon as I give birth, to his estate,
942Makes me the cause of all your misfortune.
943Right here and now I’ll set your mind at rest,
944For you can rest assured you’ll have no siblings
945Because the Duke has only married me
946To make his subjects happy; his libido
947—I cannot call it by another name—
948One single night did bring him to my bed
949—Which satisfied his needs for many years—
950And our bodies came together in love.
951And now he has returned to past delights
952With more frenzy than ever after me.
953As a runaway horse avoids drumbeat,
954Because I want to use a tasteful term,
955And leaves bits of his trappings all around:
956Thus pieces of his bit become scattered
957Spreading around him drops of foamy spittle,
958Here are the reins and there the snaffle strap,
959Here the crownpiece and there the snaffle bit;
960So goes the Duke, his marriage vows infringed,
961With common whores spreading his seed about.
962There go his fame, trophies, triumphal arches,
963His titles and ancestors’ reputation.
964There go valour, good health and time wasted
965Turning nights into days with foul lechery.
966So it is clear that you can rest assured
967That you will still inherit his estate,
968Or I shall write a letter to my father
969Saying that more than spouse he is a tyrant
970And he will come and free me from this cell
971That is his palace, if death does not come
972And put an end to all these outrages.

FEDERICO

973Your Grace did start off by berating me
974But then you finished off your speech in tears
975And causing even a heart of stone great grief.
976What’s this? No doubt you see me as the son
977Of him who has given you so much pain;
978But I will have you know I’m not his son
979As far as his behaviour is concerned.
980If you believe the words that I have spoken
981I am surprised you still think my anguish
982Is caused by such unworthy lucubrations.
983Does Federico need wealth to be himself?
984Will not my cousin, if I marry her,
985Also provide me with a rich estate?
986Or if I should decide to take my sword
987Against some prince and oust him from our land
988Would I not gain the spoils I take from him?
989My sadness is not caused by interest
990And though it takes me longer than it should
991I’d have you know, milady, that my life
992Is sadder far than any human being’s
993Since Cupid put his arrows in his bow.
994I’m dying now without a remedy;
995My life is ending slowly like a candle
996And I beg death in vain that it not wait
997Until the wax has totally been spent,
998But show some mercy to me and blow out
999The flame and extinguish quickly my life.

CASANDRA

1000Please dry your tears, gracious Federico,
1001God has not endowed men with tears but spirits.
1002Nature associates weeping with women
1003As their birthright, because they lack backbone.
1004Not so with men, who in one case may cry
1005And that is when they’ve relinquished their honour
1006While they are wreaking vengeance on their foe.
1007I curse Aurora and her jealousy,
1008For she has brought a valiant, prudent man,
1009So sweet and full of dignity, to such
1010A sorry state.

FEDERICO

A sorry state. It’s not true it’s Aurora.

CASANDRA

1011Well then, who is it?

FEDERICO

Well then, who is it? It’s the sun itself,
1012Auroras I see at every light of day.

CASANDRA

1013It’s not Aurora?

FEDERICO

It’s not Aurora? My sights are set higher.

CASANDRA

1014A woman who’s seen and spoken to you
1015And you’ve declared your love to her outright
1016Cannot be cruel enough to disdain you!
1017Don’t you see that’s the opposite effect
1018That seems impossible for such a cause?

FEDERICO

1019If you knew the impossible you’d say
1020I’m made of marble, because I survive
1021Though my survival is a miracle.
1022Did Phaethon not dare to confront the sun
1023With his chariot of gold? And what about
1024That other who stuck feathers on his back
1025Which were soon scattered by the wind and heat
1026And sent him tumbling to his doom below?
1027What Bellerophon saw when he was mounted
1028Upon his steed Pegasus, made the world
1029Look like a dot among the other stars.
1030The Greek Synon employed a pregnant horse
1031Filled with armed enemies to enter Troy,
1032Which was the cause of its downfall at last.
1033And Jason tried to be the first to cross
1034Reckless the sea aboard Argus’ vessel?
1035Can any of these equal my madness?

CASANDRA

1036Good Count, are you in love with a bronze statue
1037An alabaster nymph or goddess then?
1038The souls of women do not wear cold jasper;
1039A thin light curtain covers human thoughts.
1040Love never called to any soul with such
1041Great qualities that it did not respond:
1042“Here I am, but please enter gingerly.”
1043Declare your love, and be she who she may,
1044For the Greeks had good reason to depict
1045Venus enamoured of a satyr or faun.
1046The moon is higher, but for Endymion
1047She often came down from her silver seat
1048To Mount Latmus to visit him asleep.
1049Take my advice, dear Count, speak up at once
1050Don’t hold your tongue, for the purest fortress
1051Of all has entrances made out of wax.

FEDERICO

1052The hunter cleverly kindles a fire
1053Around the nest of a large pelican,
1054And he, beating his wings with wild abandon,
1055Climbs down the tree to rescue his offspring;
1056But by beating his wings he fans the flames
1057Instead of quenching it as he had wished;
1058And finally his wings are sorely burnt
1059So he is captured quickly on the ground
1060Because he cannot fly up in the air.
1061My thoughts, which are the offspring of my love,
1062Which I maintain within the nest of silence,
1063Are being burnt, milady, without fail;
1064My love tries beating wings to rescue them,
1065The fire grows and devours him entirely.
1066You deceive me and I am burnt alive;
1067You lead me on and I forget myself;
1068You hearten me and I rush off in fright;
1069You exhort me and I escape confused;
1070You free me and I manacle myself;
1071You take me off but I remain behind;
1072You teach me but I am afraid to act,
1073Because I feel I’m in such grave danger
1074That I consider that the lesser evil
1075Would be for me to die –for die I must–
1076Still suffering and saying not a word.

Exit Federico

CASANDRA

1077Neither heaven nor earth can ever see
1078A matter of greater complexity
1079Than worries caused by man’s imagination.
1080It turns fire into ice and shifts the image
1081Of desire, where war and peace and tempest
1082And tranquillity are waged, and is a thing
1083That, more than inform the soul, deceives it.
1084These obscure attempts, these clear confusions,
1085Rather than clarify my doubts with reasons
1086Have left me submerged in a quandary.
1087What tempests do winds move that are more changing
1088Than these forced truths in an imagination?
1089Because the worst tempests in all the world
1090Are those that take their course within the soul.
1091When I’m inclined to fancy I’m the object
1092Of what the Count desires with all his heart,
1093The same deceit tells me what I imagine
1094Is nothing but impossibility.
1095My cruel fate shows me my matrimony
1096And I accept these feelings that I have
1097For there is nothing so impossible
1098That the eyes of the conscience can’t peruse.
1099So many things are happening together,
1100This loves comes after an unfaithful spouse,
1101I think all this has come to drive me mad.
1102The impossible seems easy so I think
1103That I’m avenging infidelity;
1104But as this is such an unfair error
1105I am afraid my love for Federico
1106Will be avenged yet by my husband’s sword.
1107The Count has many natural virtues,
1108But I should be quite mad if I gave in
1109Without restraint to such a wild passion.
1110I want no more of this foolish confusion!
1111Oh holy heavens, come to my defence,
1112Although it would be justice to believe
1113That in the world there’d be no man of honour
1114If t’were offence to imagine offence.
1115Thus far neither mi honour nor my thoughts
1116Have betrayed me; all that I consented
1117Was but a figment of imagination.
1118It is a misdemeanour, not dishonour,
1119Before God to consent to imagine.
1120God sees and judges all our mental process
1121But our honour cannot be compromised
1122Unless it comes into the public eye.

Enter Aurora

AURORA

1123Your Grace and the Count had a lengthy talk,
1124What did he say?

CASANDRA

What did he say? He’s grateful for your love.
1125Rest assured, Aurora, and don’t be jealous.

Exit Casandra

AURORA

1126What scant comfort you give my ardent wishes!
1127How can one who adored my very thoughts
1128Now feel so very much disheartened
1129Because he’s lost the possibility
1130Of inheriting the name of Ferrara?
1131Love is powerful; the soul takes little notice
1132Of life and honour, but seeks you alone.
1133And Federico, who once loved me well,
1134Is maddened by the sadness he imagines
1135About Casandra’s future progeny.
1136But he faked jealousy to cover up
1137The truth, and it is wont to stimulate
1138Recondite love, so I shall try to make him
1139Jealous by faking love for the Marquis.

MARQUIS

1142Do hold your tongue, Rutilio, here’s Aurora.

RUTILIO

1143And you’re beside yourself despite such change.

Exit

MARQUIS

1144Aurora, since the bright day my eyes gave you,
1145With my soul as your spoils, my liberty;
1146Aurora, sent by the sun when night-time falls
1147Upon my misery; who paints with bright colours
1148All the pretty flowers that come into bloom,
1149Dawn comes with pearls and roses from your lips.
1150Since the day I came here from Mantua,
1151Unluckily I fell for your beauty,
1152Which no mortal man can ever resist.
1153How badly did I predispose my sham
1154Although my soul adores you, milady,
1155But it only makes you quickly tire of me
1156So that I may find sadness within you
1157When I so eager sigh for you, Aurora.
1158It was not sadness that I should see you,
1159For brightness never could be without cheer,
1160But that my love should cause you to forget.
1161I’ve organised my parting as the answer;
1162Escaping from your cruelty, I shall seek
1163Resistance in the miracles of absence
1164And in the vengeance of another love.
1165Give me your hand and let me go away.

AURORA

1166A lover or a courtesan who takes
1167So much to heart the first repudiation
1168Will not die of melancholy, Marquis.
1169Favours are not the stuff of early loves
1170But first the lovers must learn to love well.
1171You little love, you little suffer then;
1172But in this inequality, as freely
1173As you request permission to depart
1174I order you to stay and not to leave.

MARQUIS

1175Milady, you tell me with much bounty
1176To wait, although it seems like cruelty,
1177Not the ten years the Greek beleaguered Troy,
1178Nor yet the seven Laban promised Rachel,
1179But never-ending centuries I’ll wait
1180Like Tantalus, torn between doubt and faith.
1181I will request rewards for my high hopes.

AURORA

1182One must gain merits to achieve one’s goal.

FEDERICO

1192I think as well that he would like me to

FEDERICO

DUKE

FEDERICO

DUKE

FEDERICO

1211To the Marquis?

BATÍN

FEDERICO

AURORA

1213This sash is a sure token of first favour.

MARQUIS

1214’Twill be a chain around my neck, milady,
1215A handcuff on my arm never to leave it.

AURORA

1218(Although it is a paramour’s vengeance,aparte

BATÍN

1224–Just some of them, I say, and not them all–
1225The men who love them would idolise them.
1226Can you not see the sash?

FEDERICO

BATÍN

1227What sash? That is a funny thing indeed!
1228A sash that once was hers, the graceful one,
1229Planets revolving round the morning sun;
1230And now, as in an eclipse, the Marquis
1231Is wearing that token over his shoulder.
1232I recall it was the apple of discord,

FEDERICO

1234But those were different times from this, Batín.

AURORA

1235Come, I beg you enter the garden with me.

BATÍN

1236How freely does he take her hand in his!

FEDERICO

1237But what do you expect if they’re soul mates?

BATÍN

1238Is that your answer?

FEDERICO

Is that your answer? What would you have me say?

BATÍN

1239If one swan cannot contend with another
1240But flies off with its sash to distant waters;
1241Should a cock surprise a rival with its hens,
1242It’ll tear the crest of its crown with its beak,
1243And fluffing up its own crest and its wattle,
1244Will try to vanquish it in the night’s singing.
1245How then can you bear it that the Marquis
1246Is ready to rob you of your amour?

FEDERICO

1247Because the best vengeance against fair maids
1248Who provoke men is just to let them be,
1249For he who risks his honour may lose it.

BATÍN

1250Give me, I beg, a copy of this code
1251Of honour and behaviour for young men,
1252Because I’d like to learn it off by heart.
1253No, Count, your patience is a mystery;
1254Your thoughts of love are jugs in a chain well:
1255The first one leaves the water that the second
1256Takes up as soon as it is left inside.
1257You have a new love to replace Aurora;
1258If there’s too much water, how can it be
1259That one can still attend another jug?

FEDERICO

1260You’re being quite impertinent, Batín,
1261Because with cautious force you dare to know
1262What I myself don’t understand about me.
1263Go in and find out what the Duke is doing
1264And ask him all about his travel plans.

BATÍN

1265You call me foolish without cause enough
1266Because it is more foolish still by far
1267To give you cause to feel more anguish now.

Exit

FEDERICO

1268What are you after, impossible thought?
1269What do you want, what do you lead me to?
1270Why do you strip me of my senseless life
1271Up there where you are really out of place?
1272Hold still the wandering movement that pursues
1273The death of both; just let me rest awhile
1274And do not let this glorious hope end sadly.
1275No vanquished thought can grow without an aim,
1276But if you give it hope it will endure.
1277For him who loves and tries all is possible
1278And only you appeared before my eyes
1279As something forever impossible.

Enter Casandra

CASANDRA

1280Between affronts and vengeance love’s desirous
1281After such fickleness that has aroused
1282Ill-born expectations against my honour.
1283Love tries to bring close the inaccessible,
1284But joy cannot be based on the impossible.
1285In this my inclination towards betrayal
1286Caused by the Duke’s blatant debauchery,
1287I speculate with every kind of madness
1288To wreak a just revenge upon my spouse.
1289I’ll have the gallant Count, his son and heir,
1290To be the instrument of my revenge,
1291For so much indiscriminate wantonness
1292Can best be paid with total secrecy.
1293I saw he was embarrassed when he spoke
1294And even hesitant to speak his mind
1295Although, is it a greater insolence
1296For a man to speak without using words?
1297My soul rejoiced so much at his dilemma
1298Because I feel inside me if it’s love
1299It’s not betrayal after the Duke’s affairs,
1300And in my desperation I’ll not be
1301The last enamoured or the first unchaste.
1302Fathers have had relations with their daughters
1303And brothers have lain with their sisters too;
1304Therefore my wishes are not so abnormal
1305And my decision is not culpable.
1306But it is no excuse for such an offence
1307That I should mention others similar;
1308Because to sin it’s not legitimate
1309To follow examples that are also crimes.
1310Ah, here’s the Count, alas! But what’s my fear
1311Now that my mind has been made up at last?

FEDERICO

1312Here comes the naked blade that stole my life.
1313Oh, what divine beauty!

CASANDRA

Oh, what divine beauty! Are you still sad,
1314Federico?

FEDERICO

Federico? I must answer Your Grace
1315That haplessly my sadness is immortal.

CASANDRA

1316Your health will suffer from melancholy.

FEDERICO

1317I have some foolish ailments, milady,
1318That I can only say are mine alone.

CASANDRA

1319If I can be of help, please count on me:
1320My love for you is greater still than yours.

FEDERICO

1321I’d count on you but I’m afraid to do so.

CASANDRA

1322You told me that love was your malady.

FEDERICO

1323My joy and misery are the consequence.

CASANDRA

1324I’ll tell you an old story; it will prove
1325That love requires a lot of gallantry.
1326When with his step-mother he fell in love,
1327Antiochus fell gravely sad and ill.

FEDERICO

1328If he was wise he’d have laid down and died
1329For I am more disconsolate than he.

CASANDRA

1330The king, his father, quite preoccupied,
1331Sent for as many doctors as he could
1332But it was all in vain, because the cause
1333Was not a single woman from the court.
1334But Erasistratus, wiser than Galen
1335And Hippocrates, examined the prince,
1336And found out that his malady was lodged
1337In some place between his heart and his lips.
1338He took his pulse and made all the ladies
1339In palace walk before him one by one.

FEDERICO

1340Milady, I suppose some spirit spoke.

CASANDRA

1341When the stepmother came into the room
1342The doctor noticed that she was the cause
1343Because his pulse began to race wildly.

FEDERICO

1344What a strange way to act!

CASANDRA

What a strange way to act! That’s what they say!

FEDERICO

1345And did they find a remedy for him?

CASANDRA

1346Do not deny, Count, you show the same signs.

FEDERICO

1347And are you angry then?

CASANDRA

And are you angry then? I am not, sir.

FEDERICO

1348But do you feel pity for me?

CASANDRA

But do you feel pity for me? I do.

FEDERICO

1349Well, milady, I’ve got to such a stage
1350Where I no longer fear God or the Duke,
1351For my impossible infatuation
1352Has brought me to a state of desperation.
1353And finally, milady, I am left
1354Without me, without you and without God:
1355Without God because I so desire you;
1356Without me because I am without you,
1357Without you because I don’t possess you.
1358And just in case you do not understand,
1359I’ll make a speech explaining my reasons
1360So that you may discover you’re to blame
1361For making me a victim of such passion.
1362Although, Your Grace, to cease to be they say
1363Is a great ill, I’d rather cease to be
1364Than see myself in such a sorry state
1365As I see myself reduced because of you.
1366I find myself in this predicament:
1367With no desire to see if I still am
1368The same one as I was before, though I’ve
1369No mortal wish to see myself at all,
1370Thus finally, milady, I am left.
1371I dare not say I am the one I am,
1372And I am now enduring such a state
1373That still I don’t remember any more
1374That I owe God the life I give to you.
1375We’re both to blame for my state of non-being
1376Because forgotten by yourself I am
1377Without me, without you and without God.
1378Without me is not much, for there’s no life
1379If you don’t pray for it to God for me,
1380But without God, what life can my love have?
1381If I continue wanting you and He says
1382I should renounce the beauty I see in you,
1383It’s more than clear that I shall find myself
1384Without God because I so desire you.
1385What awful madness it is to conserve
1386One’s life at such a great profundity
1387When a man can no longer live his life
1388In you, in God Almighty or himself!
1389What are we two to do in consequence
1390Since I lost God for you, for you’re my god;
1391Without my God, because you are within me,
1392Without me because I am without you?
1393I’ll suffer anything to favour you.
1394I feel love, you feel only disdain;
1395So much so, I can say without restraint
1396Look who you’re with and look who you’re without!
1397Without you and without me I contend
1398With very little confidence, I fear,
1399Without me, ’cause in you I see no hope
1400Without you because I don’t possess you.

CASANDRA

1401When I imagine, Count, God and the Duke,
1402I must confess I tremble in some panic
1403For I see human power and divine
1404Joined together to form excessiveness;
1405But seeing that love is pardoned in the world,
1406I feel less guilty, for such just pardon
1407Makes culpability seem so much less.
1408I’ve been given a great deal of examples
1409That were considered serious misdeeds,
1410Because all those who wished to leave the path
1411Always take as their models those who sinned
1412And never those others who felt remorse.
1413If there can be a remedy for this
1414It is not to attempt to see and talk,
1415Or otherwise our lives will have to end
1416Or our love will have to be overpowered.
1417Run far away from me, for I don’t know
1418If I’ll succeed in running far from you
1419Or this will lead me to my suicide.

FEDERICO

1420Milady, I shall die: it is the best
1421That I can do for me. I want no life;
1422Already I’m a body without a soul
1423So I shall be off now to seek my death,
1424Although I do not think I’ll find it yet
1425Because of the pleasure I indulge in.
1426All I ask is that you give me your hand;
1427To give me the poison that has killed me.

CASANDRA

1428I condemn any principle if I ignite
1429The gunpowder, my good Federico.
1430Go with God.

FEDERICO

Go with God. What cruel betrayal this is!

CASANDRA

1431I was quite determined, but it is wise
1432To warn a hand takes venom to the heart.

FEDERICO

1433You were a mermaid to me, Casandra,
1434You sang your song so as to allure me
1435Into the sea where you put me to death.

CASANDRA

1436I must go fast away from here! Honour,
1437Take care! My good name, resist all temptations!

FEDERICO

1438I’m scarcely able to walk away from here.

CASANDRA

1439I’ve lost my soul and all my senses too.

FEDERICO

1440Oh, this is strange and dreadful derangement!

CASANDRA

1441I’m dying of love for you while I leave here.

FEDERICO

1442I’m not, because I am already dead.

CASANDRA

1443Beloved Count, you’ll be the death of me.

FEDERICO

1444And yet, although I’m dead, I’m glad to say,
1445That even though I’m losing you forever,
1446My soul’s immortal and will always love you.
