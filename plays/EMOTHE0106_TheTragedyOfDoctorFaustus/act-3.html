Act III

Enter the CHORUS.

Chorus

511Learnèd Faustus,
512To find the secrets of astronomy
513Graven in the book of Jove's high firmament,
514Did mount him up to scale Olympus' top,
515Where, sitting in a chariot burning bright
516Drawn by the strength of yokèd dragons' necks,
517He views the clouds, the planets, and the stars,
518The tropics, zones, and quarters of the sky,
519From the bright circle of the hornèd moon
520Even to the height of Primum Mobile ;
521And, whirling round with this circumference
522Within the concave compass of the pole,
523From east to west his dragons swiftly glide
524And in eight days did bring him home again.
525Not long he stayed within his quiet house
526To rest his bones after his weary toil,
527But new exploits do hale him out again,
528And, mounted then upon a dragon's back,
529That with his wings did part the subtle air,
530He now is gone to prove cosmography,
531That measures coasts and kingdoms of the earth,
532And, as I guess, will first arrive at Rome
533To see the Pope and manner of his court
534And take some part of holy Peter's feast,
535The which this day is highly solemnised.

Exit.

[III.i]

Enter FAUSTUS and MEPHISTOPHELES.

Faustus

536Having now, my good Mephistopheles,
537Passed with delight the stately town of Trier,
538Environed round with airy mountaintops,
539With walls of flint and deep intrenchèd lakes,
540Not to be won by any conquering prince;
541From Paris next, coasting the realm of France,
542We saw the river Maine fall into Rhine,
543Whose banks are set with groves of fruitful vines.
544Then up to Naples, rich Campania,
545Whose buildings, fair and gorgeous to the eye,
546The streets straight forth and paved with finest brick.
547There saw we learnèd Maro's golden tomb,
548The way he cut an English mile in length
549Through a rock of stone in one night's space.
550From thence to Venice, Padua, and the east,
551In one of which a sumptuous temple stands
552That threats the stars with her aspiring top,
553Whose frame is paved with sundry coloured stones,
554And roofed aloft with curious work in gold.
555Thus hitherto hath Faustus spent his time.
556But tell me now, what resting place is this?
557Hast thou, as erst I did command,
558Conducted me within the walls of Rome?

Mephistopheles

559I have, my Faustus, and for proof thereof
560This is the goodly palace of the Pope;
561And 'cause we are no common guests
562I choose his privy chamber for our use.

Faustus

563I hope his Holiness will bid us welcome.

Mephistopheles

564All's one, for we'll be bold with his venison.
565But now, my Faustus, that thou mayst perceive
566What Rome contains for to delight thine eyes,
567Know that this city stands upon seven hills
568That underprop the groundwork of the same.
569Just through the midst runs flowing Tiber's stream,
570With winding banks that cut it in two parts,
571Over the which two stately bridges lean,
572That make safe passage to each part of Rome.
573Upon the brid ge called Ponte Angelo
574Erected is a castle passing strong,
575Where thou shalt see such store of ordinance
576As that the double cannons, forged of brass,
577Do match the number of the days contained
578Within the compass of one complete year—
579Beside the gates and high pyramides
580That Julius Caesar brought from Africa.

Faustus

581Now, by the kingdoms of infernal rule,
582Of Styx, of Acheron, and the fiery lake
583Of ever-burning Phlegethon, I swear
584That I do long to see the monuments
585And situation of bright splendent Rome.
586Come, therefore, let's away!

Mephistopheles

587Nay stay, my Faustus. I know you'd see the Pope
588And take some part of holy Peter's feast,
589The which this day with high solemnity
590This day is held through Rome and Italy
591In honour of the Pope's triumphant victory.

Faustus

592Sweet Mephistopheles, thou pleasest me.
593Whilst I am here on earth, let me be cloyed
594With all things that delight the heart of man.
595My four-and-twenty years of liberty
596I'll spend in pleasure and in dalliance,
597That Faustus' name, whilst this bright frame doth stand,
598May be admirèd through the furthest land.

Mephistopheles

599'Tis well said, Faustus. Come, then, stand by me,
600And thou shalt see them come immediately.

Faustus

601Nay, stay, my gentle Mephistopheles,
602And grant me my request, and then I go.
603Thou know'st within the compass of eight days
604We viewed the face of heaven, of earth, and hell.
605So high our dragons soared into the air
606That, looking down, the earth appeared to me
607No bigger than my hand in quantity.
608There did we view the kingdoms of the world,
609And what might please mine eye I there beheld.
610Then in this show let me an actor be,
611That this proud pope may Faustus' cunning see.

Mephistopheles

612Let it be so, my Faustus. But first stay
613And view their triumphs as they pass this way,
614And then devise what best contents thy mind,
615By cunning in thine art, to cross the Pope
616Or dash the pride of this solemnity—
617To make his monks and abbots stand like apes
618And point like antics at his triple crown,
619To beat the beads about the friars' pates
620Or clap huge horns upon the cardinals' heads,
621Or any villainy thou canst devise,
622And I'll perform it, Faustus. Hark, they come.
623This day shall make thee be admired in Rome.

[They stand aside.]

Enter the CARDINALS [of France and Padua] and BISHOPS [of Lorraine and Rheims], some bearing crosiers, some the pillars; MONKS and FRIARS singing their procession. Then the POPE [ADRIAN] and RAYMOND, KING OF HUNGARY, with BRUNO [the rival Pope] led in chains. [Bruno's papal crown is borne in.]

Pope

624Cast down our footstool.

Raymond

Cast down our footstool. Saxon Bruno, stoop,
625Whilst on thy back his Holiness ascends
626Saint Peter's chair and state pontifical.

Bruno

627Proud Lucifer, that state belongs to me!
628But thus I fall to Peter, not to thee.

[He kneels in front of the throne.]

Pope

629To me and Peter shalt thou grovelling lie
630And crouch before the papal dignity.
631Sound trumpets, then, for thus Saint Peter's heir
632From Bruno's back ascends Saint Peter's chair.

A flourish while he ascends.

633Thus, as the gods creep on with feet of wool
634Long ere with iron hands they punish men,
635So shall our sleeping vengeance now arise
636And smite with death thy hated enterprise.
637Lord Cardinals of France and Padua,
638Go forthwith to our holy consistory
639And read amongst the statutes decretal
640What, by the holy council held at Trent,
641The sacred synod hath decreed for him
642That doth assume the papal government
643Without election and a true consent.
644Away, and bring us word with speed.

First Cardinal

645We go, my lord.

Exeunt CARDINALS.

Pope

646Lord Raymond—

[Pope Adrian and Raymond converse apart.]

Faustus

647Go haste thee, gentle Mephistopheles.aparte
648Follow the cardinals to the consistory,aparte
649And as they turn their superstitious booksaparte
650Strike them with sloth and drowsy idleness,aparte
651And make them sleep so sound that in their shapesaparte
652Thyself and I may parley with this Pope,aparte
653This proud confronter of the Emperor,aparte
654And in despite of all his holinessaparte
655Restore this Bruno to his libertyaparte
656And bear him to the states of Germany.aparte

Mephistopheles

657Faustus, I go.aparte

Faustus

658Dispatch it soon.aparte
659The Pope shall curse that Faustus came to Rome.aparte

Exeunt FAUSTUS and MEPHISTOPHELES.

Bruno

660Pope Adrian, let me have some right of law.
661I was elected by the Emperor.

Pope

662We will depose the Emperor for that deed
663And curse the people that submit to him.
664Both he and thou shalt stand excommunicate
665And interdict from Church's privilege
666And all society of holy men.
667He grows too proud in his authority,
668Lifting his lofty head above the clouds,
669And like a steeple overpeers the Church
670But we'll pull down his haughty insolence.
671And as Pope Alexander, our progenitor,
672Trod on the neck of German Frederick,
673Adding this golden sentence to our praise,
674'That Peter's heirs should tread on emperors
675And walk upon the dreadful adder's back,
676Treading the lion and the dragon down,
677And fearless spurn the killing basilisk',
678So will we quell that haughty schismatic
679And by authority apostolical
680Depose him from his regal government.

Bruno

681Pope Julius swore to princely Sigismund,
682For him and the succeeding popes of Rome,
683To hold the emperors their lawful lords.

Pope

684Pope Julius did abuse the Church's rights,
685And therefore none of his decrees can stand.
686Is not all power on earth bestowed on us?
687And therefore, though we would, we cannot err.
688Behold this silver belt, whereto is fixed
689Seven golden keys fast sealed with seven seals
690In token of our sevenfold power from heaven,
691To bind or loose, lock fast, condemn, or judge,
692Resign, or seal, or whatso pleaseth us.
693Then he and thou and all the world shall stoop,
694Or be assurèd of our dreadful curse
695To light as heavy as the pains of hell.

Enter FAUSTUS and MEPHISTOPHELES, [dressed] like the cardinals.

Mephistopheles

696Now tell me, Faustus, are we not fitted well?aparte

Faustus

697Yes, Mephistopheles, and two such cardinalsaparte
698Ne'er served a holy pope as we shall do.aparte
699But whilst they sleep within the consistory,aparte
700Let us salute his reverend Fatherhood.aparte

Raymond

701[To the Pope] Behold, my lord, the cardinals are returned.

Pope

702Welcome, grave fathers. Answer presently:
703What have our holy council there decreed
704Concerning Bruno and the Emperor,
705In quittance of their late conspiracy
706Against our state and papal dignity?

Faustus

707Most sacred patron of the Church of Rome,
708By full consent of all the synod
709Of priests and prelates, it is thus decreed:
710That Bruno and the German Emperor
711Be held as Lollards and bold schismatics
712And proud disturbers of the Church's peace.
713And if that Bruno by his own assent,
714Without enforcement of the German peers,
715Did seek to wear the triple diadem
716And by your death to climb Saint Peter's chair,
717The statutes decretal have thus decreed:
718he shall be straight condemned of heresy
719And on a pile of faggots burnt to death.

Pope

720It is enough. Here, take him to your charge,
721And bear him straight to Ponte Angelo,
722And in the strongest tower enclose him fast.
723Tomorrow, sitting in our consistory
724With all our college of grave cardinals,
725We will determine of his life or death.
726Here, take his triple crown along with you
727And leave it in the Church's treasury.

[Bruno's papal crown is given to Faustus and Mephistopheles.]

728Make haste again, my good lord cardinals,
729And take our blessing apostolical.

Mephistopheles

730So, so, was never devil thus blest before!aparte

Faustus

731Away, sweet Mephistopheles, begone.aparte
732The cardinals will be plagued for this anon.aparte

Exeunt FAUSTUS and MEPHISTOPHELES [with BRUNO].

Pope

733Go presently and bring a banquet forth,
734That we may solemnise Saint Peter's feast
735And with Lord Raymond, King of Hungary,
736Drink to our late and happy victory.

Exeunt.

[III.ii]

A sennet while the banquet is brought in. [Seats are provided at the banquet. Exeunt Attendants,] and then enter FAUSTUS and MEPHISTOPHELES in their own shapes.

Mephistopheles

737Now, Faustus, come, prepare thyself for mirth.
738The sleepy cardinals are hard at hand
739To censure Bruno, that is posted hence
740And on a proud-paced steed, as swift as thought,
741Flies o'er the Alps to fruitful Germany,
742There to salute the woeful Emperor.

Faustus

743The Pope will curse them for their sloth today,
744That slept both Bruno and his crown away.
745But now, that Faustus may delight his mind
746And by their folly make some merriment,
747Sweet Mephistopheles, so charm me here
748That I may walk invisible to all
749And do whate'er I please, unseen of any.

Mephistopheles

750Faustus, thou shalt. Then kneel down presently,

[Faustus kneels.]

751Whilst on thy head I lay my hand
752And charm thee with this magic wand.

[Presenting a magic girdle.]

753First wear this girdle; then appear
754Invisible to all are here.
755The planets seven, the gloomy air,
756Hell, and the Furies' forkèd hair,
757Pluto's blue fire, and Hecate's tree
758With magic spells so compass thee
759That no eye may thy body see.

[Faustus rises.]

760So, Faustus, now, for all their holiness,
761Do what thou wilt, thou shalt not be discerned.

Faustus

762Thanks, Mephistopheles. Now, friars, take heed
763Lest Faustus make your shaven crowns to bleed.

Mephistopheles

764Faustus, no more. See where the cardinals come.

Enter POPE and all the lords: [RAYMOND, KING OF HUNGARY, the ARCHBISHOP OF RHEIMS, etc., Friars and Attendants.] Enter the [two] CARDINALS [of France and Padua] with a book.

Pope

765Welcome, lord cardinals. Come, sit down.

[They sit.]

766Lord Raymond, take your seat. Friars, attend,
767And see that all things be in readiness,
768As best beseems this solemn festival.

First Cardinal

769First, may it please your sacred Holiness
770To view the sentence of the reverend synod
771Concerning Bruno and the Emperor?

Pope

772What needs this question? Did I not tell you
773Tomorrow we would sit i'th'consistory
774And there determine of his punishment?
775You brought us word even now, it was decreed
776That Bruno and the cursèd Emperor
777Were by the holy council both condemned
778For loathèd Lollards and base schismatics.
779Then wherefore would you have me view that book?

First Cardinal

780Your Grace mistakes. You gave us no such charge.

Raymond

781Deny it not. We are all witnesses
782That Bruno here was late delivered you,
783With his rich triple crown to be reserved
784And put into the Church's treasury.

Both Cardinals

785By holy Paul, we saw them not.

Pope

786By Peter, you shall die
787Unless you bring them forth immediately.—
788Hale them to prison. Lade their limbs with gyves!—
789False prelates, for this hateful treachery
790Curst be your souls to hellish misery.

[Exeunt Attendants with the two CARDINALS.]

Faustus

791So, they are safe. Now, Faustus, to the feast.aparte
792the Pope had never such a frolic guest.aparte

Pope

793Lord Archbishop of Rheims, sit down with us.

Archbishop

794[Sitting.] I thank your Holiness.

Faustus

795Fall to. The devil choke you an you spare.

Pope

796Who's that spoke? Friars, look about.
797Lord Raymond, pray fall to. I am beholding
798To the Bishop of Milan for this so rare a present.

Faustus

799[Snatching the meat.] I thank you, sir.

Pope

800How now? Who snatched the meat from me?
801Villains, why speak you not?—
802My good Lord Archbishop, here's a most dainty dish
803Was sent me from a Cardinal in France.

Faustus

804[Snatching the dish.] I'll have that, too.

Pope

805What Lollards do attend our Holiness,
806That we receive such great indignity?
807Fetch me some wine.

[Wine is brought.]

Faustus

808Ay, pray do, for Faustus is adry.aparte

Pope

809Lord Raymond, I drink unto your Grace.

Faustus

810[Snatching the cup.] I pledge your Grace.

Pope

811My wine gone, too? Ye lubbers, look about
812And find the man that doth this villany,
813Or by our sanctitude you all shall die!—

I pray, my lords, have patience at this troublesome

banquet.

Archbishop

Please it your Holiness, I think it be some

ghost crept out of purgatory and now is come unto

Pope

814It may be so.
815Go, then, command our priests to sing a dirge
816To lay the fury of this same troublesome ghost.

[Exit one.]

[The Pope crosses himself.]

Faustus

817How now? Must every bit be spicèd with a cross?

[The Pope crosses himself again.]

818Nay, then, take that!

[III.iii]

Enter CLOWN [ROBIN], and DICK with a cup.

Dick

Sirrah Robin, we were best look that your devil can

answer the stealing of this same cup, for the Vintner's

boy follows us at the hard heels.

Robin

'Tis no matter. Let him come. An he follow us, I'll

so conjure him as he was never conjured in his life,

I warrant him. Let me see the cup.

Enter VINTNER.

Dick

[Giving the cup to Robin.] Here 'tis. Yonder he comes. Now, Robin, now or never show thy cunning.

Vintner

O, are you here? I am glad I have found you. You

are a couple of fine companions! Pray, where's the

cup you stole from the tavern?

Robin

How, how? We steal a cup? Take heed what you say.

We look not like cup-stealers, I can tell you.

Vintner

Never deny't, for I know you have it, and I'll

search you.

Robin

Search me? Ay, and spare not.

Hold the cup, Dick.

[To the Vintner]

Come, come, search me, search me.

[The Vintner searches Robin.]

Vintner

[To Dick.] Come on, sirrah, let me search you now.

Dick

Ay, ay, do, do.

Hold the cup, Robin.

[The Vintner searches Dick.]

Vintner

Never outface me for the matter, for sure the cup

is between you two.

Vintner

A plague take you! I thought 'twas your knavery

to take it away. Come, give it me again.

Robin

Ay, much! When, can you tell? Dick, make me a

circle, and stand close at my back, and stir not for thy

[Exit the VINTNER, running.]
