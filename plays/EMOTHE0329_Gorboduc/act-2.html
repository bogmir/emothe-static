ACT II SCENE I

[Ferrex’s court]

Enter Ferrex, Hermon and Dordan.]

ferrex

909I marvel much what reason led the King
910My father, thus without all my desert,
911To reave me half the kingdom, which by course
912Of law and nature should remain to me.

hermon

913If you with stubborn and untamed pride
914Had stood against him in rebelling wise,
915Or if with grudging mind you had envied
916So slow a sliding of his aged years,
917Or sought before your time to haste the course
918Of fatal death upon his royal head,
919Or stain’d your stock with murder of your kin,
920Some face of reason might perhaps have seem’d
921To yield some likely cause to spoil ye thus.

ferrex

922The wreakful gods pour on my cursed head
923Eternal plagues and never-dying woes;
924The hellish prince adjudge my damned ghost
925To Tantale’s thirst, or proud Ixion’s wheel,
926Or cruel gripe to gnaw my growing heart,
927To during torments and unquenched flames,
928If ever I conceiv’d so foul a thought,
929To wish his end of life, or yet of reign!

dordan

930Ne yet your father, O most noble Prince,
931Did ever think so foul a thing of you:
932For he, with more than father’s tender love,
933While yet the fates do lend him life to rule
934(Who long might live to see your ruling well),
935To you, my lord, and to his other son,
936Lo, he resigns his realm and royalty,
937Which never would so wise a prince have done
938If he had once misdeem’d that in your heart
939There ever lodged so unkind a thought.
940But tender love, my lord, and settled trust
941Of your good nature and your noble mind
942Made him to place you thus in royal throne,
943And now to give you half his realm to guide –
944Yea, and that half which in abounding store
945Of things that serve to make a wealthy realm,
946In stately cities, and in fruitful soil,
947In temperate breathing of the milder Heaven,
948In things of needful use, which friendly sea
949Transports by traffic from the foreign parts,
950In flowing wealth, in honour and in force,
951Doth pass the double value of the part
952That Porrex hath allotted to his reign.
953Such is your case, such is your father’s love.

ferrex

954Ah love, my friends? Love wrongs not whom he loves.

dordan

955Ne yet he wrongeth you, that giveth you
956So large a reign, ere that the course of time
957Bring you to kingdom by descended right,
958Which time perhaps might end your time before.

ferrex

959Is this no wrong, say you, to reave from me
960My native right of half so great a realm,
961And thus to match his younger son with me
962In equal power, and in as great degree?
963Yea, and what son? The son whose swelling pride
964Would never yield one point of reverence,
965When I the elder and apparent heir
966Stood in the likelihood to possess the whole;
967Yea, and that son which from his childish age
968Envieth mine honour and doth hate my life.
969What will he now do, when his pride, his rage,
970The mindful malice of his grudging heart,
971Is arm’d with force, with wealth, and kingly state?

hermon

972Was this not wrong, yea, ill-advised wrong,
973To give so mad a man so sharp a sword,
974To so great peril of so great mishap,
975Wide open thus to set so large a way?

dordan

976Alas, my lord, what griefull thing is this,
977That of your brother you can think so ill?
978I never saw him utter likely sign
979Whereby a man might see or once misdeem
980Such hate of you, ne such unyielding pride.
981Ill is their counsel, shameful be their end,
982That, raising such mistrustful fear in you,
983Sowing the seed of such unkindly hate,
984Travail by treason to destroy you both.
985Wise is your brother, and of noble hope,
986Worthy to wield a large and mighty realm;
987So much a stronger friend have you thereby,
988Whose strength is your strength, if you gree in one.

hermon

989If nature and the gods had pinched so
990Their flowing bounty, and their noble gifts
991Of princely qualities, from you, my lord,
992And pour’d them all at once in wasteful wise
993Upon your father’s younger son alone,
994Perhaps there be that in your prejudice
995Would say that birth should yield to worthiness.
996But sith in each good gift and princely art
997Ye are his match, and in the chief of all –
998In mildness and in sober governance –
999Ye far surmount; and sith there is in you
1000Sufficing skill, and hopeful towardness
1001To wield the whole, and match your elder’s praise,
1002I see no cause why ye should lose the half.
1003Ne would I wish you yield to such a loss,
1004Lest your mild sufferance of so great a wrong
1005Be deemed cowardish and simple dread,
1006Which shall give courage to the fiery head
1007Of your young brother to invade the whole.
1008While yet, therefore, sticks in the people’s mind
1009The loathed wrong of your disheritance;
1010And ere your brother have by settled power,
1011By guileful cloak of an alluring show,
1012Got him some force and favour in the realm;
1013And while the noble Queen your mother lives,
1014To work and practise all for your avail;
1015Attempt redress by arms, and wreak yourself
1016Upon his life, that gaineth by your loss
1017Who now to shame of you, and grief of us,
1018Show now your courage meet for kingly state,
1019That they, which have avow’d to spend their goods,
1020Their lands, their lives and honours in your cause,
1021May be the bolder to maintain your part,
1022When they do see that coward fear in you
1023Shall not betray ne fail their faithful hearts.
1024If once the death of Porrex end the strife
1025And pay the price of his usurped reign,
1026Your mother shall persuade the angry King;
1027The lords your friends eke shall appease rage;
1028For they be wise, and well they can foresee
1029That ere long time your aged father’s death
1030Will bring a time when you shall well requite
1031Their friendly favour, or their hateful spite,
1032Yea, or their slackness to advance your cause.
1033‘Wise men do not so hang on passing state
1034Of present princes, chiefly in their age,
1035But they will further cast their reaching eye
1036To view and weigh the times and reigns to come’.
1037Ne is it likely, though the King be wroth,
1038That he yet will, or that the realm will, bear
1039Extreme revenge upon his only son;
1040Or if he would, what one is he that dare
1041Be minister to such an enterprise?
1042And here you be now placed in your own,
1043Amid your friends, your vassals, and your strength:
1044We shall defend and keep your person safe,
1045Till either counsel turn his tender mind,
1046Or age or sorrow end his weary days.
1047But if the fear of gods, and secret grudge
1048Of Nature’s law, repining at the fact,
1049Withhold your courage from so great attempt,
1050Know ye that lust of kingdoms hath no law:
1051The gods do bear and well allow in kings
1052The things that they abhor in rascal routs.
1053‘When king on slender quarrels run to wars,
1054And then in cruel and unkindly wise
1055Command thefts, rapes, murders of innocents,
1056The spoil of towns, ruins of mighty realms,
1057Think you such princes do suppose themselves
1058Subject to laws of kind and fear of gods?’
1059Murders and violent thefts in private men
1060Are heinous crimes and full of foul reproach,
1061Yet none offence, but deck’d with glorious name
1062Of noble conquests, in the hands of kings!
1063But if you like not yet so hot device,
1064Ne list to take such vantage of the time,
1065But, though with peril of your own estate,
1066You will not be the first that shall invade,
1067Assemble yet your force for your defence,
1068And for your safety stand upon your guard.

dordan

1069O Heaven, was there ever heard or known
1070So wicked counsel to a noble prince?
1071Let me, my lord, disclose unto your grace
1072This heinous tale, what mischief it contains:
1073Your father’s death, your brother’s and your own,
1074Your present murder and eternal shame.
1075Hear me, O King, and suffer not to sink
1076So high a treason in your princely breast!

ferrex

1077The mighty gods forbid that ever I
1078Should once conceive such mischief in my heart!
1079Although my brother hath bereft my realm,
1080And bear perhaps to me an hateful mind,
1081Shall I revenge it with his death therefore,
1082Or shall I so destroy my father’s life
1083That gave me live? The gods forbid, I say!
1084[To Hermon.] Cease you to speak so any more to me.
1085[To Dordan.] Ne you, my friend, with answer once repeat
1086So foul a tale. In silence let it die.
1087What lord or subject shall have hope at all
1088That under me they safely shall enjoy
1089Their goods, their honours, lands and liberties,
1090With whom neither one only brother dear,
1091Ne father dearer, could enjoy their lives?
1092But sith I fear my younger brother’s rage,
1093And sith perhaps some other man may give
1094Some like advice to move his grudging head
1095At mine estate, which counsel may perchance
1096Take greater force with him than this with me,
1097I will in secret so prepare myself,
1098As if his malice or his lust to reign
1099Break forth in arms or sudden violence,
1100I may withstand his rage and keep mine own.

[Exeunt Ferrex and Hermon.]

dordan

1101I fear the fatal time now draweth on
1102When civil hate shall end the noble line
1103Of famous Brute and of his royal seed.
1104Great Jove defend the mischief now at hand!
1105Oh that the Secretary’s wise advise
1106Had erst been heard when he besought the King
1107Not to divide his land, nor send his sons
1108To further parts from presence of his court,
1109Ne yet to yield to them his governance!
1110Lo, such are they now in the royal throne
1111As was rash Phaeton in Phoebus’ car;
1112Ne then the fiery steeds did draw the flame
1113With wilder random through the kindled skies,
1114Than traitorous counsel now will whirl about
1115The youthful heads of these unskilful kings.
1116But I hereof their father will inform:
1117The reverence of him perhaps shall stay
1118The growing mischiefs, while they yet are green.
1119If this help not, then woe unto themselves,
1120The Prince, the people, the divided land!

[Exit.]

ACT II SCENE 2

[Porrex’s court.]

[Enter Porrex, Philander, and Tyndar.]

porrex

1121And is it thus? And doth he so prepare
1122Against his brother as his mortal foe?
1123And now, while yet his aged father lives?
1124Neither regards he him, nor fears he me?
1125War would he have? And he shall have it so.

tyndar

1126I saw myself the great prepared store
1127Of horse, of armour, and of weapon there;
1128Ne bring I to my lord reported tales
1129Without the ground of seen and searched truth.
1130Lo, secret quarrels run about his court
1131To bring the name of you, my lord, in hate:
1132Each man almost can now debate the cause
1133And ask a reason of so great a wrong,
1134Why he, so noble and so wise a prince,
1135Is as unworthy reft his heritage,
1136And why the King, misled by crafty means,
1137Divided thus his land from course of right?
1138The wiser sort hold down their griefull heads;
1139Each man withdraws from talk and company
1140Of those that have been known to favour you.
1141To hide the mischief of their meaning there,
1142Rumours are spread of your preparing here;
1143The rascal numbers of unskilful sort
1144Are filled with monstrous tales of you and yours.
1145In secret I was counsell’d by my friends
1146To haste me thence, and brought you, as you know,
1147Letters from those that both can truly tell
1148And would not write unless they knew it well.

philander

1149My lord, yet ere you move unkindly war,
1150Send to your brother to demand the cause:
1151Perhaps some traitorous tales have fill’d his ears
1152With false reports against your noble grace,
1153Which, once disclos’d, shall end the growing strife,
1154That else not stay’d with wise foresight in time,
1155Shall hazard both your kingdoms and your lives.
1156Send to your father eke; he shall appease
1157Your kindled minds, and rid you of this fear.

porrex

1158Rid me of fear? I fear him not at all,
1159Ne will to him ne to my father send!
1160If danger were for one to tarry there,
1161Think ye it safety to return again?
1162In mischiefs, such as Ferrex now intends,
1163The wonted courteous laws to messengers
1164Are not observ’d, which in just war they use.
1165Shall I so hazard any one of mine?
1166Shall I betray my trusty friends to him,
1167That have disclos’d his treason unto me?
1168Let him entreat that fears; I fear him not.
1169Or shall I to the King my father send?
1170Yea, and send now, while such a mother lives
1171That loves my brother and that hateth me?
1172Shall I give leisure, by my fond delays,
1173To Ferrex to oppress me all unware?
1174I will not, but I will invade his realm
1175And seek the traitor prince within his court!
1176Mischief for mischief is a due reward:
1177His wretched head shall pay the worthy price
1178Of this his treason and his hate to me.
1179Shall I abide, and treat, and send, and pray,
1180And hold my yielden throat to traitor’s knife,
1181While I with valiant mind and conquering force
1182Might rid myself of foes and win a realm?
1183Yet rather, when I have the wretch’s head,
1184Then to the King my father will send.
1185The bootless case may yet appease his wrath;
1186If not, I will defend me as I may.

[Exeunt Porrex and Tyndar.]

philander

1187Lo, here the end of these two youthful kings,
1188The father’s death, the ruin of their realms.
1189‘O most unhappy state of counsellors
1190That light on so unhappy lord and times,
1191That neither can their good advice be heard
1192Yet must they bear the blames of ill success.’
1193But I will to the King, their father, haste,
1194Ere this mischief come to the likely end;
1195That if the mindful wrath of wreakful gods,
1196Since mighty Ilion’s fall nor yet appeased
1197With these poor remnants of the Trojan name,
1198Have not determin’d by unmoved fate
1199Out of this realm to rase the British line,
1200By good advice, by awe of father’s name,
1201By force of wiser lords, this kindled hate
1202May yet be quenched, ere it consume us all.

[Exit.]

chorus

1203When youth, not bridled with a guiding satay,
1204Is left to random of their own delight,
1205And wields whole realms by force of sovereign sway,
1206Great is the danger of unmaster’d might,
1207Lest skilless rage throw down with headlong fall
1208Their lands, their states, their lives, themselves and all.
1209When growing pride doth fill the swelling breast,
1210And greedy lust doth raise the climbing mind,
1211Oh, hardly may the peril be repress’d!
1212Ne fear of angry gods, ne laws [of] kind,
1213Ne country’s care can fired hearts restrain,
1214When force hath armed envy and disdain;
1215When kings of foreset will neglect the rede
1216Of best advice, and yield to pleasing tales
1217That do their fancies noisome humour feed,
1218Ne reason nor regard of right avails;
1219Succeeding heaps of plagues shall teach too late
1220To learn the mischiefs of misguided state.
1221Foul fall the traitor false that undermines
1222The love of brethren to destroy them both!
1223Woe to the prince that pliant ear inclines,
1224And yields his mind to poisonous tale that floweth
1225From flattering mouth! And woe to wretched land
1226That wasted itself with civil sword in hand!
1227Lo, thus it is, poison in gold to take,
1228And wholesome drink in homely cup forsake.
