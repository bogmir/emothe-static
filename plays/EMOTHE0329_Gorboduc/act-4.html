ACT IV SCENE I

[Enter Videna.]

videna

1420Why Should I live and linger forth my time
1421In longer life to double my distress?
1422O me, most woeful wight, whom no mishap
1423Long ere this day could have bereaved hence!
1424Mought not these hands by fortune, or by fate,
1425Have pierc’d this breast, and life with iron reft?
1426Or in this palace here, where I so long
1427Have spent my days, could not that happy hour
1428Once, once, have happ’d in which these hugy frames
1429With death by fall might have oppressed me?
1430Or should not this most hard and cruel soil,
1431So oft where I have press’d my wretched steps,
1432Sometime had ruth of mine accursed life,
1433To rend in twain and swallow me therein?
1434So had my bones possessed now in peace
1435Their happy grave within the closed ground,
1436And greedy worms had gnawen this pined heart
1437Without my feeling pain. So should not now
1438This living breast remain the ruthful tomb,
1439Wherein my heart yielden to death is grav’d,
1440Nor dreary thoughts with pangs of pining grief
1441My doleful mind had nor afflicted thus.
1442O my beloved son, O my sweet child,
1443My dear Ferrex, my joy, my life’s delight!
1444Is my beloved son, is my sweet child,
1445My dear Ferrex, my Joy, my life’s delight,
1446Murder’d with cruel death? O hateful wretch!
1447O heinous traitor both to heaven and earth!
1448Thou, Porrex, thou this damned deed hast wrought;
1449Thou, Porrex, thou shalt dearly bye the same;
1450Traitor to kin and kind, to sire and me,
1451To thine own flesh, and traitor to thyself!
1452The gods on thee in Hell shall wreak their wrath,
1453And here in earth this hand shall take revenge,
1454On thee, Porrex, thou false and caitiff wight!
1455If after blood so eager were thy thirst,
1456And murderous mind had so possessed thee;
1457If such hard heart of rock and stony flint
1458Liv’d in thy breast, that nothing else could like
1459Thy cruel tyrant’s thought but death and blood;
1460Wild savage beasts, mought not their slaughter serve
1461To feed thy greedy will, and in the midst
1462Of their entrails to stain thy deadly hands
1463With blood deserv’d, and drink thereof thy fill?
1464Or if nought else but death and blood of man
1465Might please thy lust, could none in Britain land,
1466Whose heart betorn out of his panting breast
1467With thine own hand or work what death thou would’st –
1468Suffice to make a sacrifice to pease
1469That deadly mind and murderous thought in thee
1470But he who in the self-same womb was wrapp’d,
1471Where thou in dismal hour receivedst life?
1472Or if needs, needs, thy hand must slaughter make,
1473Mightest thou not have reach’d a mortal wound,
1474And with thy sword have pierc’d this cursed womb
1475That the accursed Porrex brought to light,
1476And given me a just reward therefore?
1477So Ferrex yet sweet life mought have enjoy’d,
1478And to his aged father comfort brought,
1479With some young son un whom they both might live.
1480But whereunto waste I this ruthful speech
1481To thee that hast thy brother’s blood thus shed?
1482Shall I still think that from this womb thou sprung?
1483That I thee bare? Or take thee for my son?
1484No, traitor, no! I thee refuse for mine:
1485Murderer, I thee renounce, thou art not mine!
1486Never, O wretch, this womb conceived thee,
1487Nor never bode I painful throes for thee;
1488Changeling to me thou art, and not my child,
1489Nor to no wight that spark of pity knew.
1490Ruthless, unkind, monster of Nature’s work,
1491Thou never suck’d the milk of woman’s breast,
1492But from thy birth the cruel tiger’s teats
1493Have nursed thee. Not yet of flesh and blood
1494Form’d is thy heart, but of hard iron wrought,
1495And wild and desert woods bred thee to life.
1496But canst thou hope to scape my just revenge,
1497Or that these hands will not be wroke on thee?
1498Does thou not know that Ferrex’ mother lives
1499That loved him more dearly than herself?
1500And doth she live, and is not veng’d on thee?

[Exit.]

ACT IV SCENE 2

[Gorboduc’s court]

[Enter Gorboduc and Arostus.]

gorboduc

1501We marvel much whereto this lingering stay
1502Falls out so long: Porrex unto our court,
1503By order of our letters, is return’d,
1504And Eubulus receiv’d from us by hest
1505At his arrival here to give him charge
1506Before our presence straight to make repair,
1507And yet we have no word whereof he stays.

arostus

1508[looking offstage] Lo, where he comes and Eubulus with him.

[Enter Porrex and Eubulus.]

eubulus

1509According to your highness’ hest to me,
1510Here have I Porrex brought, even in such sort
1511As from his wearied horse he did alight,
1512For that your grace did will such haste therein.

gorboduc

1513We like and praise this speedy will in you
1514To work the thing that to your charge we gave.
1515Porrex, if we so far should swerve from kind
1516And from those bounds which law of Nature sets,
1517As thou hast done by vile and wretched deed,
1518In cruel murder of thy brother’s life,
1519Our present hand could stay no longer time,
1520But straight should bathe this blade in blood of thee
1521As just revenge of thy detested crime.
1522No, we should not offend the law of kind,
1523If now this sword of ours did slay thee here,
1524For thou hast murder’d him, whose heinous death
1525Even Nature’s force doth move us to revenge
1526By blood again, and justice forceth us
1527To measure death for death, thy due desert.
1528Yet sithens thou art our child, and sith as yet
1529In this hard case what word thou canst allege
1530For thy defence by us hath not been heard,
1531We are content to stay our will for that
1532Which justice bids us presently to work,
1533And give thee leave to use thy speech at full
1534If aught thou have to lay for thine excuse.

porrex

1535Neither, O King, I can or will deny
1536But that this hand from Ferrex life hath reft,
1537Which fact how much my doleful heart doth wail;
1538Oh would it might as full appear to sight
1539As inward grief doth pour it forth to me!
1540So yet perhaps if ever ruthful heart
1541Melting in tears within a manly breast
1542Through deep repentance of his bloody fact;
1543If ever grief, if ever woeful man,
1544Might move regrate with sorrow of his fault;
1545I think the torment of my mournful case,
1546Known to your grace, as I do feel the same,
1547Would force even Wrath herself to pity me.
1548But as the water troubled with the mud
1549Shows not the face which else the eye should see,
1550Even so your ireful mind with stirred thought
1551Cannot so perfectly discern my cause.
1552But this unhap, amongst so many heaps,
1553I must content me with, most wretched man,
1554That to myself I must reserve my woe
1555In pining thoughts of mine accursed fact,
1556Since I may not show here my smallest grief
1557Such as it is, and as my breast endures,
1558Which I esteem the greatest misery
1559Of all mishaps that fortune now can send.
1560Not that I rest in hope with plaint and tears
1561To purchase life, for to the gods I clepe
1562For true record of this my faithful speech;
1563Never this heart shall have the thoughtfull dread
1564To die the death that by your grace’s doom
1565By just desert shall be pronounc’d to me:
1566Nor never shall this tongue once spend the speech
1567Pardon to crave, or seek by suit to live.
1568I mean not this, as though I were not touch’d
1569With care of dreadful death, or that I held
1570Life in contempt, but that I know the mind
1571Stoops to no dread, although the flesh be frail;
1572And for my guilt, I yield the same so great
1573As in myself I find a fear to sue
1574For grant of life.

gorboduc

For grant of life. In vain, O wretch, thou showest
1575A woeful heart: Ferrex now lies in grave,
1576Slain by thy hand.

porrex

Slain by thy hand. Yet this, O father, hear:
1577And then I end. Your Majesty well knows
1578That when my brother Ferrex and myself
1579By your own hest were join’d in governance
1580Of this your grace’s realm of Britain land,
1581I never sought nor travail’d for the same,
1582Nor by myself, nor by no friend I wrought,
1583But from your Highness’ will alone it sprung,
1584Of your most gracious goodness bent to me.
1585But how my brother’s heart even then repin’d
1586With swollen disdain against mine equal rule,
1587Seeing that realm, which by descent should grow
1588Wholly to him, allotted half to me!
1589Even in your Highness’ court he now remains,
1590And with my brother then in nearest place,
1591Who can record what proof thereof was show’d,
1592And how my brother’s envious heart appear’d?
1593Yet I that judged it my part to seek
1594His favour and good will, and loath to make
1595Your Highness know the thing which should have brought
1596Grief to your grace, and your offence to him,
1597Hoping my earnest suit should soon have won
1598A loving heart within a brother’s breast,
1599Wrought in that sort that for a pledge of love
1600And faithful heart, he gave to me his land.
1601This made me think that he had banish’d quite
1602All rancour from his thought and bare to me
1603Such hearty love as I did owe to him;
1604But after once we left your grace’s court,
1605And from your Highness’ presence liv’d apart,
1606This equal rule still, still, did grudge him so
1607That now those envious sparks, which erst lay rak’d
1608In living cinders of dissembling breast,
1609Kindled so far within his heart disdain,
1610That longer could he not refrain from proof
1611Of secret practice to deprive me life
1612By poison’s force, and had bereft me so,
1613If mine own servant hired to this fact
1614And mov’d by truth with hate to work the same,
1615In time had not bewray’d it unto me.
1616When thus I saw the knot of love unknit,
1617All honest league and faithful promise broke,
1618The law of kind and truth thus rent in twain,
1619His heart on mischief set, and in his breast
1620Black treason hid, then, then, did I despair
1621That ever time could win him friend to me!
1622Then saw I how he smil’d with slaying knife
1623Wrapp’d under cloak; then saw I deep deceit
1624Lurk in his face and death prepar’d for me.
1625Even Nature mov’d me then to hold my life
1626More dear to me than his, and bade this hand
1627(Since by his life my death must needs ensue,
1628And by his death my life to be preserv’d)
1629To shed his blood, and seek my safety so:
1630And wisdom willed me without protract
1631In speedy wise to put the same in ure.
1632Thus have I told the cause that moved me
1633To work my brother’s death, and so I yield
1634My life, my death, to judgement of your grace.

gorboduc

1635O cruel wight, should any cause prevail
1636To make thee stain thy hands with brother’s blood?
1637But what of thee we will resolve to do
1638Shall yet remain unknown. Thou in the mean
1639Shalt from our royal presence banish’d be,
1640Until our princely pleasure further shall
1641To thee be show’d. Depart therefore our sight,
1642Accursed child!

[Exit Porrex.]

Accursed child! What cruel destiny,
1643What froward fate, hath sorted us this chance,
1644That even in those where we should comfort find,
1645Where our delight now in our aged days
1646Should rest and be, even there our only grief
1647And deepest sorrows to abridge our life,
1648Most pining cares and deadly thoughts do grow?

arostus

1649Your grace should now in these grave years of yours
1650Have found ere this the price of mortal joys,
1651How short they be, how fading here in earth,
1652How full of change, how brittle our estate,
1653Of nothing sure, save only of the death,
1654To whom both man and all the world doth owe
1655Their end at last. Neither should Nature’s power
1656In other sort against your heart prevail
1657Than as the naked hand whose stroke assays
1658The armed breast where force doth light in vain.

gorboduc

1659Many can yield right sage and grave advice
1660Of patient sprite to others wrapp’d in woe,
1661And can in speech both rule and conquer kind,
1662Who if by proof they might feel Nature’s force,
1663Would show themselves men as they are in deed,
1664Which now will needs be gods.
1665[Looking offstage] But what doth mean
1666The sorry cheer of her that here doth come?

[Enter Marcella.]

marcella

1667Oh, where is ruth? Or where is pity now?
1668Whither is gentle heart and mercy fled?
1669Are they exil’d out of our stony breasts,
1670Never to make return? Is all the world
1671Drowned in blood, and sunk in cruelty?
1672If not in women mercy may be found –
1673If not, alas, within the mother’s breast
1674To her own child, to her own flesh and blood –
1675If ruth be banish’d thence, if pity there
1676May have no place, if there no gentle heart
1677Do live and dwell, where should we seek it then?

gorboduc

1678Madam, alas! what means your woeful tale?

marcella

1679O silly woman I, why to this hour
1680Have kind and fortune thus deferr’d my breath,
1681That I should live to see this doleful day?
1682Will ever wight believe that such hard heart
1683Could rest within the cruel mother’s breast,
1684With her own hand to slay her only son?
1685But out, alas! these eyes beheld the same;
1686They saw the dreary sight, and are becomen
1687Most ruthful records of the bloody fact.
1688Porrex, alas, is by his mother slain,
1689And with her hand, a woeful thing to tell,
1690While slumb’ring on his carefull bed he rests,
1691His heart, stabb’d in with knife, is reft of life!

gorboduc

1692O Eubulus, oh draw this sword of ours,
1693And pierce this heart with speed! O hateful light!
1694O loathsome life! O sweet and welcome death!
1695Dear Eubulus, work this we thee beseech!

eubulus

1696Patient your grace, perhaps he liveth yet,
1697With wound received, but not of certain death.

gorboduc

1698Oh, let us then repair unto the place,
1699And see if Porrex live, or thus be slain.

[Exeunt Gorboduc and Eubulus.]

marcella

1700Alas, he liveth not; it is too true,
1701That with these eyes of him, a peerless prince,
1702Son to a king, and in the flower of youth,
1703Even with a twink a senseless stock I saw.

arostus

1704O damned deed!

marcella

O damned deed! But hear his ruthful end.
1705The noble Prince, pierc’d with the sudden wound,
1706Out of his wretched slumber, hastily start,
1707Whose strength now failing, straight he overthrew,
1708When in the fall his eyes, even new unclos’d,
1709Beheld the Queen, and cried to her for help.
1710We then, alas, the ladies which that time
1711Did there attend, seeing that heinous deed,
1712And hearing him oft call the wretched name
1713Of mother, and to cry to her for aid,
1714Whose direful hand gave him mortal wound,
1715Pitying, alas – for nought else could we do –
1716His ruthful end, ran to the woeful bed,
1717Dispoiled straight his breast, and all we might,
1718Wiped in vain with napkins next at hand,
1719The sudden stream of blood that flushed fast
1720Out of the gaping wound. Oh, what a look,
1721Oh, what a ruthful, steadfast eye methought
1722He fix’d upon my face, which to my death
1723Will never part from me, when with a braid
1724A deep-fet sigh he gave, and therewithal
1725Clasping his hands, to Heaven he cast his sight;
1726And straight pale Death pressing within his face,
1727The flying ghost his mortal corpse forsook.

arostus

1728Never did age bring forth so vile a fact.

marcella

1729O hard and cruel hap, that thus assign’d
1730Unto so worthy a wight so wretched end!
1731But most hard, cruel heart, that could consent
1732To lend the hateful destinies that hand
1733By which, alas, so heinous crime was wrought!
1734O queen of adamant! O marble breast!
1735If not the favour of his comely face,
1736If not his princely cheer and countenance,
1737His valiant, active arms, his manly breast,
1738If not his fair and seemly personage,
1739His noble limbs in such proportion cast
1740As would have rapt a silly woman’s thought –
1741If this mought not have mov’d thy bloody heart
1742And that most cruel hand the wretched weapon
1743Even to let fall, and kiss’d him in the face,
1744With tears for ruth to reave such one by death,
1745Should Nature yet consent to slay her son?
1746O mother, thou to murder thus thy child!
1747Even Jove with justice must with lightning flames
1748From Heaven send down some strange revenge on thee!
1749Ah, noble Prince, how oft have I beheld
1750Thee mounted on thy fierce and trampling steed,
1751Shining in armour bright before the tilt
1752And charge thy staff to please thy lady’s eye
1753That bow’d the headpiece of thy friendly foe?
1754How oft in arms on horse to bend the mace?
1755How oft in arms on foot to break the sword,
1756Which never now these eyes may see again?

arostus

1757Madam, alas, in vain these plaints are shed.
1758Rather with me depart, and help to suage
1759The thoughtfull griefs that in the aged King
1760Must needs by nature grow, by death of this
1761His only son, whom he did hold so dear.

marcella

1762What wight is that which saw that I did see,
1763And could refrain to wail with plaint and tears?
1764Not I, alas; that hearts is not in me.
1765But let us go, for I am griev’d anew,
1766To call to mind the wretched father’s woe.

[Exeunt.]

chorus

1767When greedy lust in royal seat to reign
1768Hath reft all care of gods and eke of men,
1769And cruel heart, wrath, treason, and disdain
1770Within ambitious breast are lodged, then
1771Behold how mischief wide herself displays,
1772And with the brother’s hand the brother slays.
1773When blood thus shed doth stain the heaven’s face,
1774Crying to Jove for vengeance of the deed,
1775The mighty god even moveth from his place,
1776With wrath to wreak. Then sends he forth with speed
1777The dreadful Furies, daughters of the night,
1778With serpents girt, carrying the whip of ire,
1779With hair of stinging snakes, and shining bright
1780With flames and blood, and with a brand of fire:
1781These for revenge of wretched murder done
1782Do make the mother kill her only son.
1783Blood asked blood, and death must death requite;
1784Jove by his just and everlasting doom
1785Justly hath ever so requited it.
1786The times before record, and times to come,
1787Shall find it true, and so doth present proof
1788Present before our eyes for our behoof.
1789O happy wight that suffers not the snare
1790Of murderous mind to tangle him in blood!
1791And happy he that can in time beware
1792By other’s harms and turn it to his good:
1793But woe to him that, fearing not to offend,
1794Doth serve his lust, and will not see the end.

[The Chorus exits.]
