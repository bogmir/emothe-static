ACT III SCENE I

[Gorboduc’s court]

[Enter Gorboduc with a letter, Eubulus, Arostus and Attendants etc.]

gorboduc

1229O cruel fates! O mindful wrath of gods,
1230Whose vengeance neither Simois’ stained streams
1231Flowing with blood of Trojan princes slain,
1232Nor Phrygian fields made rank with corpses dead
1233Of Asian kings and lords, can yet appease,
1234Ne slaughter of unhappy Priam’s race,
1235Nor Ilion’s fall made level with the soil,
1236Can yet suffice, but still continu’d rage
1237Pursues our line, and from the farthest seas
1238Doth chase the issues of destroyed Troy.
1239‘Oh no man happy, till his end be seen!’
1240If any flowing wealth and seeming joy
1241In present years might make a happy wright,
1242Happy was Hecuba, the woefullest wretch
1243That ever liv’d to make a mirror of,
1244And happy Priam with his noble sons,
1245And happy I, till now! Alas, I see
1246And feel my most unhappy wretchedness:
1247Behold, my lords, read ye this letter here.
1248Lo, it contains the ruin of our realm,
1249If timely speed provide not hasty help.
1250Yet, O ye gods, if ever woeful king
1251Might move ye, kings of kings, wreak it on me
1252And on my sons, not on this guiltless realm!
1253Send down your wasting flames from wrathful skies,
1254To reave me and my sons the hatefull breath!
1255Read, read, my lords. This is the matter why
1256I call’d ye now to have your good advice.

The letter from Dordan, the counsellor of the elder prince. Eubulus readeth the letter.

eubulus

1257‘My sovereing lord, what I am loath to write
1258But loathest am to see, that I am forc’d
1259By letters now to make you understand.
1260My Lord Ferrex, your eldest son, misled
1261By traitorous fraud of young untemper’d wits,
1262Assembleth force against your younger son:
1263Ne can my counsel yet withdraw the heat
1264And furious pangs of his inflamed head.
1265Disdain, saith he, of his disheritance
1266Arms him to wreak the great pretended wrong
1267With civil sword upon his brother’s life.
1268If present help do nor restrain this rage,
1269This flame will waste your sons, your land, and you.
1270Your majesty’s faithful and most
1271humble subject, Dordan,’

arostus

1272O King, appease your grief and stay your plaint;
1273Great is the matter, and a woeful case,
1274But timely knowledge may bring timely help.
1275Send for them both unto your presence here;
1276The reverence of your honour, age, and state,
1277Your grave advice, the awe of father’s name,
1278Shall quickly knit again this broken peace.
1279And if in either of my lords your sons
1280Be such untamed and unyielding pride
1281As will not bend unto your noble hests;
1282If Ferrex, the elder son, can bear no peer,
1283Or Porrex, not content, aspires to more
1284Than you him gave above his native right,
1285Join with the juster side. So shall you force
1286Them to agree, and hold the land in stay.

eubulus

1287[looking offstage] What meaneth this? Lo, yonder
1288comes in haste
1289Philander from my lord, your younger son.

[Enter Philander.]

gorboduc

1290The gods send joyful news!

philander

The gods send joyful news! The mighty Jove
1291Preserve your Majesty, O noble King.

gorboduc

1292Philander, welcome. But how doth my son?

philander

1293Your son, sir, lives, and healthy I him left.
1294But yet, O King, the want of lustful health
1295Could not be half so griefull to your grace
1296As these most wretched tidings that I bring.

gorboduc

1297O Heavens, yet more? No end of woes to me?

philander

1298Tyndar, O King, came lately from the court
1299Of Ferrex to my lord your younger son,
1300And made report of great prepared store
1301For war, and saith that it is wholly meant
1302Against Porrex, for high disdain that he
1303Lives now a king and equal in degree
1304With him that claimeth to succeed the whole,
1305As by due title of descending right.
1306Porrex is now so set on flaming fire,
1307Partly with kindled rage of cruel wrath,
1308Partly with hope to gain a realm thereby,
1309That he in haste prepareth to invade
1310His brother’s land, and with unkindly war
1311Threatens the murder of your elder son;
1312Ne could I him persuade that first he should
1313Send to his brother to demand the cause,
1314Nor yet to you to stay this hatefull strife.
1315Wherefore sith there no more I can be heard,
1316I come myself now to inform your grace,
1317And to beseech you, as you love the life
1318And safety of your children and your realm,
1319Now to employ your wisdom and your force
1320To stay this mischief ere it be too late.

gorboduc

1321Are they in arms? Would he not send to me?
1322Is this the honour of a father’s name?
1323In vain we travail to assuage their minds,
1324As if their hearts, whom neither brother’s love,
1325Nor father’s awe, nor kingdom’s care can move,
1326Our counsels could withdraw from raging heat:
1327Jove slay them both, and end the cursed line!
1328For though perhaps fear of such mighty force
1329As I, my lords, joined with your noble aids,
1330May yet raise, shall repress their present heat,
1331The secret grudge and malice will remain;
1332The fire not quench’d, but kept in close restraint,
1333Fed still within, breaks forth with double flame:
1334Their death and mine must pease the angry gods.

philander

1335Yield not, O king, so much to weak despair:
1336Your sons yet live, and long, I trust, they shall.
1337If fates had taken you from earthly life
1338Before beginning of this civil strife,
1339Perhaps your sons in their unmaster’d youth,
1340Loose from regard of any living wight,
1341Would run on headlong, with unbridled race,
1342To their own death and ruin of this realm.
1343But sith the gods, that have the care for kings,
1344Of things and times dispose the order so
1345That in your life this kindled flame breaks forth,
1346While yet your life, your wisdom, and your power
1347May stay the growing mischief, and repress
1348The fiery blaze of their enkindled heat.
1349It seems, and so ye ought to deem thereof,
1350That this debate to happen in your days,
1351That you yet living may the same appease,
1352And add it to the glory of your latter age,
1353And they your sons may learn to live in peace.
1354Beware, O King, the greatest harm of all,
1355Lest by your wailful plaints your hasten’d death
1356Yield larger room unto their growing rage:
1357Preserve your life, the only hope of stay.
1358And if your highness herein list to use
1359Wisdom or force, counsel or knightly aid,
1360Lo, we, our persons, powers and lives are yours;
1361Use us till death, O King; we are your own.

eubulus

1362Lo, here the peril that was erst foreseen
1363When you, O King, did first divide your land,
1364And yield your present reign unto your sons:
1365But now, O noble prince, now is no time
1366To wail and plain, and waste your woeful life.
1367Now is the time for present good advice:
1368Sorrow doth dark the judgement of the wit.
1369‘The heart unbroken and the courage free
1370From feeble faintness of bootless despair
1371Doth either rise to safety or renown
1372By noble valure of unvanquish’d mind,
1373Or yet doth perish in more happy sort.’
1374Your grace may send to either of your sons
1375Some one both wise and noble personage,
1376Which with good counsel and with weighty name
1377Of father shall present before their eyes
1378Your hest, your life, your safety, and their own,
1379The present mischief of their deadly strife.
1380And in the while, assemble you the force
1381Which your commandment and the speedy haste
1382Of all my lords here present can prepare:
1383The terror of your mighty power shall stay
1384The rage of both, or yet of one at least.

[Enter Nuntiun.]

nuntius

1385O King, the greatest grief that ever prince did hear.
1386That ever woeful messenger did tell,
1387That ever wretched land hath seen before,
1388I bring to you: Porrex, your younger son,
1389With sudden force invaded hath the land
1390That you to Ferrex did allot to rule,
1391And with his own most bloody hand he hath
1392His brother slain, and doth possess his realm.

gorboduc

1393O Heavens, send down the flames of your revenge!
1394Destroy, I say, with flash of wreakful fire
1395The traitor son, and then the wretched sire!
1396But let us go, that yet perhaps I may
1397Die with revenge, and pease the hatefull gods.

[Exeunt all but the Chorus.]

chorus

1398The lust of kingdom knows no sacred faith,
1399No rule of reason, no regard of right,
1400No kindly love, no fear of Heaven’s wrath,
1401But with contempt of gods, and man’s despite,
1402Through bloody slaughter doth prepare the ways
1403To fatal sceptre and accursed reign:
1404The son so loathes the father’s lingering days,
1405Ne dreads his hand in brother’s blood to stain.
1406O wretched prince, ne dost thou yet record
1407The yet fresh murders done within the land
1408Of thy forefathers, when the cruel sword
1409Bereft Morgan his life with cousin’s hand?
1410Thus fatal plagues pursue the guilty race,
1411Whose murderous hand imbru’d with guiltless blood
1412Asks vengeance still before the Heaven’s face,
1413With endless mischiefs on the cursed brood.
1414The wicked child thus brings to woeful sire
1415The mournful plaints, to waste his very life;
1416Thus do the cruel flames of civil fire
1417Destroy the parted reign with hateful strife,
1418And hence doth spring the well from which doth flow
1419The dead black streams of mourning, plaints and woe.
