Act IV

Scene 1

Enter Caesar, Agrippa, and Maecenas, with his army, Caesar reading a letter.

CAESAR

1848He calls me “boy,” and chides as he had power
1849To beat me out of Egypt. My messenger
1850He hath whipped with rods, dares me to personal combat,
1851Caesar to Antony. Let the old ruffian know
1852I have many other ways to die; meantime
1853Laugh at his challenge.

MAECENAS

Laugh at his challenge. Caesar must think,
1854When one so great begins to rage, he’s hunted
1855Even to falling. Give him no breath, but now
1856Make boot of his distraction. Never anger
1857Made good guard for itself.

CAESAR

Made good guard for itself. Let our best heads
1858Know that tomorrow the last of many battles
1859We mean to fight. Within our files there are,
1860Of those that served Mark Antony but late,
1861Enough to fetch him in. See it done,
1862And feast the army; we have store to do ’t,
1863And they have earned the waste. Poor Antony.

They exit.

2240Itself with strength. Seal, then, and all is done.—
2241Eros!—I come, my queen.—Eros!—Stay for me.
2242Where souls do couch on flowers, we’ll hand in hand,
2243And with our sprightly port make the ghosts gaze.
2244Dido and her Aeneas shall want troops,
2245And all the haunt be ours.—Come, Eros, Eros!

Enter Eros.

EROS

2246What would my lord?

ANTONY

What would my lord? Since Cleopatra died
2247I have lived in such dishonor that the gods
2248Detest my baseness. I, that with my sword
2249Quartered the world and o’er green Neptune’s back
2250With ships made cities, condemn myself to lack
2251The courage of a woman—less noble mind
2252Than she which, by her death, our Caesar tells
2253“I am conqueror of myself.” Thou art sworn, Eros,
2254That when the exigent should come, which now
2255Is come indeed, when I should see behind me
2256Th’ inevitable prosecution of
2257Disgrace and horror, that on my command
2258Thou then wouldst kill me. Do ’t. The time is come.
2259Thou strik’st not me; ’tis Caesar thou defeat’st.
2260Put color in thy cheek.

EROS

Put color in thy cheek. The gods withhold me!
2261Shall I do that which all the Parthian darts,
2262Though enemy, lost aim and could not?

ANTONY

Though enemy, lost aim and could not? Eros,
2263Wouldst thou be windowed in great Rome and see
2264Thy master thus with pleached arms, bending down
2265His corrigible neck, his face subdued
2266To penetrative shame, whilst the wheeled seat
2267Of fortunate Caesar, drawn before him, branded
2268His baseness that ensued?

EROS

His baseness that ensued? I would not see ’t.

ANTONY

2269Come, then, for with a wound I must be cured.
2270Draw that thy honest sword, which thou hast worn
2271Most useful for thy country.

EROS

Most useful for thy country. O, sir, pardon me!

ANTONY

2272When I did make thee free, swor’st thou not then
2273To do this when I bade thee? Do it at once,
2274Or thy precedent services are all
2275But accidents unpurposed. Draw, and come.

EROS

2276Turn from me then that noble countenance
2277Wherein the worship of the whole world lies.

ANTONY

2278Lo thee!

He turns away.

EROS

2279My sword is drawn.

ANTONY

My sword is drawn. Then let it do at once
2280The thing why thou hast drawn it.

EROS

The thing why thou hast drawn it. My dear master,
2281My captain, and my emperor, let me say,
2282Before I strike this bloody stroke, farewell.

ANTONY

2283’Tis said, man, and farewell.

EROS

2284Farewell, great chief. Shall I strike now?

ANTONY

Farewell, great chief. Shall I strike now? Now, Eros.

EROS

2285Why, there, then.

Stabs himself.

Why, there, then. Thus I do escape the sorrow
2286Of Antony’s death.

Dies.

ANTONY

Of Antony’s death. Thrice nobler than myself,
2287Thou teachest me, O valiant Eros, what
2288I should and thou couldst not. My queen and Eros
2289Have by their brave instruction got upon me
2290A nobleness in record. But I will be
2291A bridegroom in my death and run into ’t
2292As to a lover’s bed. Come then, and, Eros,
2293Thy master dies thy scholar. To do thus
2294I learned of thee.
2295He stabs himself. How, not dead? Not dead?
2296The guard, ho! O, dispatch me!

Enter a company of the Guard, one of them named Dercetus.

FIRST GUARD

The guard, ho! O, dispatch me! What’s the noise?

ANTONY

2297I have done my work ill, friends. O, make an end
2298Of what I have begun!

SECOND GUARD

Of what I have begun! The star is fall’n.

FIRST GUARD

2299And time is at his period.

ALL

And time is at his period. Alas, and woe!

ANTONY

2300Let him that loves me strike me dead.

FIRST GUARD

2301Not I.

SECOND GUARD

2302Nor I.

THIRD GUARD

2303Nor anyone.

All but Antony and Dercetus exit.

DERCETUS

2304Thy death and fortunes bid thy followers fly.

He takes Antony’s sword.

2305This sword but shown to Caesar with this tidings
2306Shall enter me with him.

Enter Diomedes.

DIOMEDES

2307Where’s Antony?

DERCETUS

2308There, Diomed, there.

DIOMEDES

2309Lives he? Wilt thou not answer, man?

Dercetus exits.

ANTONY

2310Art thou there, Diomed? Draw thy sword, and give me
2311Sufficing strokes for death.

DIOMEDES

Sufficing strokes for death. Most absolute lord,
2312My mistress Cleopatra sent me to thee.

ANTONY

2313When did she send thee?

DIOMEDES

When did she send thee? Now, my lord.

ANTONY

When did she send thee? Now, my lord. Where is she?

DIOMEDES

2314Locked in her monument. She had a prophesying fear
2315Of what hath come to pass. For when she saw—
2316Which never shall be found—you did suspect
2317She had disposed with Caesar, and that your rage
2318Would not be purged, she sent you word she was dead;
2319But fearing since how it might work, hath sent
2320Me to proclaim the truth, and I am come,
2321I dread, too late.

ANTONY

2322Too late, good Diomed. Call my guard, I prithee.

DIOMEDES

2323What ho! The Emperor’s guard! The guard, what ho!
2324Come, your lord calls.

Enter four or five of the Guard of Antony.

ANTONY

2325Bear me, good friends, where Cleopatra bides.
2326’Tis the last service that I shall command you.

FIRST GUARD

2327Woe, woe are we, sir, you may not live to wear
2328All your true followers out.

ALL

All your true followers out. Most heavy day!

ANTONY

2329Nay, good my fellows, do not please sharp fate
2330To grace it with your sorrows. Bid that welcome
2331Which comes to punish us, and we punish it,
2332Seeming to bear it lightly. Take me up.
2333I have led you oft; carry me now, good friends,
2334And have my thanks for all.

They exit bearing Antony and the body of Eros.

Scene 15

Enter Cleopatra and her maids aloft, with Charmian and Iras.

CLEOPATRA

2335O Charmian, I will never go from hence.

CHARMIAN

2336Be comforted, dear madam.

CLEOPATRA

Be comforted, dear madam. No, I will not.
2337All strange and terrible events are welcome,
2338But comforts we despise. Our size of sorrow,
2339Proportioned to our cause, must be as great
2340As that which makes it.

Enter Diomedes below.

As that which makes it. How now? Is he dead?

DIOMEDES

2341His death’s upon him, but not dead.
2342Look out o’ th’ other side your monument.
2343His guard have brought him thither.

Enter Antony below, and the Guard bearing him.

CLEOPATRA

His guard have brought him thither. O sun,
2344Burn the great sphere thou mov’st in. Darkling stand
2345The varying shore o’ th’ world! O Antony, Antony,
2346Antony! Help, Charmian! Help, Iras, help!
2347Help, friends below! Let’s draw him hither.

ANTONY

Help, friends below! Let’s draw him hither. Peace!
2348Not Caesar’s valor hath o’erthrown Antony,
2349But Antony’s hath triumphed on itself.

CLEOPATRA

2350So it should be that none but Antony
2351Should conquer Antony, but woe ’tis so!

ANTONY

2352I am dying, Egypt, dying. Only
2353I here importune death awhile until
2354Of many thousand kisses the poor last
2355I lay upon thy lips.

CLEOPATRA

I lay upon thy lips. I dare not, dear,
2356Dear my lord, pardon, I dare not,
2357Lest I be taken. Not th’ imperious show
2358Of the full-fortuned Caesar ever shall
2359Be brooched with me; if knife, drugs, serpents have
2360Edge, sting, or operation, I am safe.
2361Your wife Octavia, with her modest eyes
2362And still conclusion, shall acquire no honor
2363Demuring upon me. But come, come, Antony.—
2364Help me, my women!—We must draw thee up.—
2365Assist, good friends.

They begin lifting him.

ANTONY

Assist, good friends. O, quick, or I am gone.

CLEOPATRA

2366Here’s sport indeed. How heavy weighs my lord!
2367Our strength is all gone into heaviness;
2368That makes the weight. Had I great Juno’s power,
2369The strong-winged Mercury should fetch thee up
2370And set thee by Jove’s side. Yet come a little.
2371Wishers were ever fools. O, come, come, come!

They heave Antony aloft to Cleopatra.

2372And welcome, welcome! Die when thou hast lived;
2373Quicken with kissing. Had my lips that power,
2374Thus would I wear them out.

She kisses him.

ALL

2375A heavy sight!

ANTONY

2376I am dying, Egypt, dying.
2377Give me some wine, and let me speak a little.

CLEOPATRA

2378No, let me speak, and let me rail so high
2379That the false huswife Fortune break her wheel,
2380Provoked by my offense.

ANTONY

Provoked by my offense. One word, sweet queen:
2381Of Caesar seek your honor with your safety—O!

CLEOPATRA

2382They do not go together.

ANTONY

They do not go together. Gentle, hear me.
2383None about Caesar trust but Proculeius.

CLEOPATRA

2384My resolution and my hands I’ll trust,
2385None about Caesar.

ANTONY

2386The miserable change now at my end
2387Lament nor sorrow at, but please your thoughts
2388In feeding them with those my former fortunes
2389Wherein I lived the greatest prince o’ th’ world,
2390The noblest, and do now not basely die,
2391Not cowardly put off my helmet to
2392My countryman—a Roman by a Roman
2393Valiantly vanquished. Now my spirit is going;
2394I can no more.

CLEOPATRA

I can no more. Noblest of men, woo’t die?
2395Hast thou no care of me? Shall I abide
2396In this dull world, which in thy absence is
2397No better than a sty? O see, my women,
2398The crown o’ th’ Earth doth melt.—My lord!

Antony dies.

2399O, withered is the garland of the war;
2400The soldier’s pole is fall’n; young boys and girls
2401Are level now with men. The odds is gone,
2402And there is nothing left remarkable
2403Beneath the visiting moon.

CHARMIAN

Beneath the visiting moon. O, quietness, lady!

Cleopatra swoons.

IRAS

2404She’s dead, too, our sovereign.

CHARMIAN

2405Lady!

IRAS

2406Madam!

CHARMIAN

2407O madam, madam, madam!

IRAS

2408Royal Egypt! Empress!

Cleopatra stirs.

CHARMIAN

2409Peace, peace, Iras!

CLEOPATRA

2410No more but e’en a woman, and commanded
2411By such poor passion as the maid that milks
2412And does the meanest chares. It were for me
2413To throw my scepter at the injurious gods,
2414To tell them that this world did equal theirs
2415Till they had stolen our jewel. All’s but naught.
2416Patience is sottish, and impatience does
2417Become a dog that’s mad. Then is it sin
2418To rush into the secret house of death
2419Ere death dare come to us? How do you, women?
2420What, what, good cheer! Why, how now, Charmian?
2421My noble girls! Ah, women, women! Look,
2422Our lamp is spent; it’s out. Good sirs, take heart.
2423We’ll bury him; and then, what’s brave, what’s noble,
2424Let’s do ’t after the high Roman fashion
2425And make death proud to take us. Come, away.
2426This case of that huge spirit now is cold.
2427Ah women, women! Come, we have no friend
2428But resolution and the briefest end.
