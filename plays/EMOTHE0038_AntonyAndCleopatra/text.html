William Shakespeare

Antony and Cleopatra

Act I

Scene 1

Enter Demetrius and Philo.

PHILO

1Nay, but this dotage of our general’s
2O’erflows the measure. Those his goodly eyes,
3That o’er the files and musters of the war
4Have glowed like plated Mars, now bend, now turn
5The office and devotion of their view
6Upon a tawny front. His captain’s heart,
7Which in the scuffles of great fights hath burst
8The buckles on his breast, reneges all temper
9And is become the bellows and the fan
10To cool a gypsy’s lust.

Flourish. Enter Antony, Cleopatra, her Ladies, the Train, with Eunuchs fanning her.

To cool a gypsy’s lust. Look where they come.
11Take but good note, and you shall see in him
12The triple pillar of the world transformed
13Into a strumpet’s fool. Behold and see.

CLEOPATRA

14If it be love indeed, tell me how much.

ANTONY

15There’s beggary in the love that can be reckoned.

CLEOPATRA

16I’ll set a bourn how far to be beloved.

ANTONY

17Then must thou needs find out new heaven, new Earth.

Enter a Messenger.

MESSENGER

18News, my good lord, from Rome.

ANTONY

19Grates me, the sum.

CLEOPATRA

20Nay, hear them, Antony.
21Fulvia perchance is angry. Or who knows
22If the scarce-bearded Caesar have not sent
23His powerful mandate to you: “Do this, or this;
24Take in that kingdom, and enfranchise that.
25Perform ’t, or else we damn thee.”

ANTONY

Perform ’t, or else we damn thee.” How, my love?

CLEOPATRA

26Perchance? Nay, and most like.
27You must not stay here longer; your dismission
28Is come from Caesar. Therefore hear it, Antony.
29Where’s Fulvia’s process? Caesar’s, I would say—both?
30Call in the messengers. As I am Egypt’s queen,
31Thou blushest, Antony, and that blood of thine
32Is Caesar’s homager; else so thy cheek pays shame
33When shrill-tongued Fulvia scolds. The messengers!

ANTONY

34Let Rome in Tiber melt and the wide arch
35Of the ranged empire fall. Here is my space.
36Kingdoms are clay. Our dungy earth alike
37Feeds beast as man. The nobleness of life
38Is to do thus; when such a mutual pair
39And such a twain can do ’t, in which I bind,
40On pain of punishment, the world to weet
41We stand up peerless.

CLEOPATRA

We stand up peerless. Excellent falsehood!
42Why did he marry Fulvia, and not love her?
43I’ll seem the fool I am not. Antony
44Will be himself.

ANTONY

Will be himself. But stirred by Cleopatra.
45Now for the love of Love and her soft hours,
46Let’s not confound the time with conference harsh.
47There’s not a minute of our lives should stretch
48Without some pleasure now. What sport tonight?

CLEOPATRA

49Hear the ambassadors.

ANTONY

Hear the ambassadors. Fie, wrangling queen,
50Whom everything becomes—to chide, to laugh,
51To weep; whose every passion fully strives
52To make itself, in thee, fair and admired!
53No messenger but thine, and all alone
54Tonight we’ll wander through the streets and note
55The qualities of people. Come, my queen,
56Last night you did desire it.
57To the Messenger. Speak not to us.

Antony and Cleopatra exit with the Train.

DEMETRIUS

58Is Caesar with Antonius prized so slight?

PHILO

59Sir, sometimes when he is not Antony
60He comes too short of that great property
61Which still should go with Antony.

DEMETRIUS

Which still should go with Antony. I am full sorry
62That he approves the common liar who
63Thus speaks of him at Rome; but I will hope
64Of better deeds tomorrow. Rest you happy!

They exit.

Scene 2

Enter Enobarbus, Lamprius, a Soothsayer, Rannius, Lucillius, Charmian, Iras, Mardian the Eunuch, Alexas, and Servants.

CHARMIAN

Lord Alexas, sweet Alexas, most anything Alexas, almost most absolute Alexas, where’s the soothsayer that you praised so to th’ Queen? O, that I knew this husband which you say must charge his horns with garlands!

ALEXAS

65Soothsayer!

SOOTHSAYER

66Your will?

CHARMIAN

67Is this the man?—Is ’t you, sir, that know things?

SOOTHSAYER

68In nature’s infinite book of secrecy
69A little I can read.

ALEXAS

70To Charmian. Show him your hand.

ENOBARBUS

71To Servants. Bring in the banquet quickly, wine enough
72Cleopatra’s health to drink.

CHARMIAN

Giving her hand to the Soothsayer. Good sir, give me good fortune.

SOOTHSAYER

73I make not, but foresee.

CHARMIAN

74Pray then, foresee me one.

SOOTHSAYER

75You shall be yet far fairer than you are.

CHARMIAN

76He means in flesh.

IRAS

77No, you shall paint when you are old.

CHARMIAN

78Wrinkles forbid!

ALEXAS

79Vex not his prescience. Be attentive.

CHARMIAN

80Hush.

SOOTHSAYER

81You shall be more beloving than beloved.

CHARMIAN

82I had rather heat my liver with drinking.

ALEXAS

83Nay, hear him.

CHARMIAN

Good now, some excellent fortune! Let me be married to three kings in a forenoon and widow them all. Let me have a child at fifty to whom Herod of Jewry may do homage. Find me to marry me with Octavius Caesar, and companion me with my mistress.

SOOTHSAYER

84You shall outlive the lady whom you serve.

CHARMIAN

85O, excellent! I love long life better than figs.

SOOTHSAYER

86You have seen and proved a fairer former fortune
87Than that which is to approach.

CHARMIAN

Then belike my children shall have no names. Prithee, how many boys and wenches must I have?

SOOTHSAYER

88If every of your wishes had a womb,
89And fertile every wish, a million.

CHARMIAN

90Out, fool! I forgive thee for a witch.

ALEXAS

You think none but your sheets are privy to your wishes.

CHARMIAN

91To Soothsayer. Nay, come. Tell Iras hers.

ALEXAS

92We’ll know all our fortunes.

ENOBARBUS

Mine, and most of our fortunes tonight, shall be—drunk to bed.

IRAS

Giving her hand to the Soothsayer. There’s a palm presages chastity, if nothing else.

CHARMIAN

E’en as the o’erflowing Nilus presageth famine.

IRAS

93Go, you wild bedfellow, you cannot soothsay.

CHARMIAN

Nay, if an oily palm be not a fruitful prognostication, I cannot scratch mine ear.—Prithee tell her but a workaday fortune.

SOOTHSAYER

94Your fortunes are alike.

IRAS

95But how, but how? Give me particulars.

SOOTHSAYER

96I have said.

IRAS

97Am I not an inch of fortune better than she?

CHARMIAN

Well, if you were but an inch of fortune better than I, where would you choose it?

IRAS

98Not in my husband’s nose.

CHARMIAN

Our worser thoughts heavens mend. Alexas—come, his fortune, his fortune! O, let him marry a woman that cannot go, sweet Isis, I beseech thee, and let her die, too, and give him a worse, and let worse follow worse, till the worst of all follow him laughing to his grave, fiftyfold a cuckold. Good Isis, hear me this prayer, though thou deny me a matter of more weight, good Isis, I beseech thee!

IRAS

Amen, dear goddess, hear that prayer of the people. For, as it is a heartbreaking to see a handsome man loose-wived, so it is a deadly sorrow to behold a foul knave uncuckolded. Therefore, dear Isis, keep decorum and fortune him accordingly.

CHARMIAN

Amen.

ALEXAS

Lo now, if it lay in their hands to make me a cuckold, they would make themselves whores but they’d do ’t.

ENOBARBUS

Hush, here comes Antony.

CHARMIAN

Not he. The Queen.

Enter Cleopatra.

CLEOPATRA

99Saw you my lord?

ENOBARBUS

100No, lady.

CLEOPATRA

101Was he not here?

CHARMIAN

102No, madam.

CLEOPATRA

103He was disposed to mirth, but on the sudden
104A Roman thought hath struck him.—Enobarbus!

ENOBARBUS

105Madam?

CLEOPATRA

106Seek him and bring him hither.—Where’s Alexas?

ALEXAS

107Here at your service. My lord approaches.

Enter Antony with a Messenger.

CLEOPATRA

108We will not look upon him. Go with us.

All but Antony and the Messenger exit.

MESSENGER

109Fulvia thy wife first came into the field.

ANTONY

110Against my brother Lucius?

MESSENGER

111Ay.
112But soon that war had end, and the time’s state
113Made friends of them, jointing their force ’gainst Caesar,
114Whose better issue in the war from Italy
115Upon the first encounter drave them.

ANTONY

116Well, what worst?

MESSENGER

117The nature of bad news infects the teller.

ANTONY

118When it concerns the fool or coward. On.
119Things that are past are done, with me. ’Tis thus:
120Who tells me true, though in his tale lie death,
121I hear him as he flattered.

MESSENGER

I hear him as he flattered. Labienus—
122This is stiff news—hath with his Parthian force
123Extended Asia: from Euphrates
124His conquering banner shook, from Syria
125To Lydia and to Ionia,
126Whilst—

ANTONY

Whilst— “Antony,” thou wouldst say?

MESSENGER

Whilst— “Antony,” thou wouldst say? O, my lord!

ANTONY

127Speak to me home; mince not the general tongue.
128Name Cleopatra as she is called in Rome;
129Rail thou in Fulvia’s phrase, and taunt my faults
130With such full license as both truth and malice
131Have power to utter. O, then we bring forth weeds
132When our quick winds lie still, and our ills told us
133Is as our earing. Fare thee well awhile.

MESSENGER

134At your noble pleasure.

Messenger exits.

Enter another Messenger.

ANTONY

135From Sicyon how the news? Speak there.

SECOND MESSENGER

136The man from Sicyon—

ANTONY

The man from Sicyon— Is there such an one?

SECOND MESSENGER

137He stays upon your will.

ANTONY

He stays upon your will. Let him appear.

Second Messenger exits.

138These strong Egyptian fetters I must break,
139Or lose myself in dotage.

Enter another Messenger with a letter.

Or lose myself in dotage. What are you?

THIRD MESSENGER

140Fulvia thy wife is dead.

ANTONY

Fulvia thy wife is dead. Where died she?

THIRD MESSENGER

141In Sicyon.
142Her length of sickness, with what else more serious
143Importeth thee to know, this bears.

He hands Antony the letter.

ANTONY

Importeth thee to know, this bears. Forbear me.

Third Messenger exits.

144There’s a great spirit gone! Thus did I desire it.
145What our contempts doth often hurl from us,
146We wish it ours again. The present pleasure,
147By revolution lowering, does become
148The opposite of itself. She’s good, being gone.
149The hand could pluck her back that shoved her on.
150I must from this enchanting queen break off.
151Ten thousand harms more than the ills I know
152My idleness doth hatch.—How now, Enobarbus!

Enter Enobarbus.

ENOBARBUS

153What’s your pleasure, sir?

ANTONY

154I must with haste from hence.

ENOBARBUS

Why then we kill all our women. We see how mortal an unkindness is to them. If they suffer our departure, death’s the word.

ANTONY

155I must be gone.

ENOBARBUS

Under a compelling occasion, let women die. It were pity to cast them away for nothing, though between them and a great cause, they should be esteemed nothing. Cleopatra, catching but the least noise of this, dies instantly. I have seen her die twenty times upon far poorer moment. I do think there is mettle in death which commits some loving act upon her, she hath such a celerity in dying.

ANTONY

156She is cunning past man’s thought.

ENOBARBUS

Alack, sir, no, her passions are made of nothing but the finest part of pure love. We cannot call her winds and waters sighs and tears; they are greater storms and tempests than almanacs can report. This cannot be cunning in her; if it be, she makes a shower of rain as well as Jove.

ANTONY

157Would I had never seen her!

ENOBARBUS

O, sir, you had then left unseen a wonderful piece of work, which not to have been blest withal would have discredited your travel.

ANTONY

158Fulvia is dead.

ENOBARBUS

159Sir?

ANTONY

160Fulvia is dead.

ENOBARBUS

161Fulvia?

ANTONY

162Dead.

ENOBARBUS

Why, sir, give the gods a thankful sacrifice. When it pleaseth their deities to take the wife of a man from him, it shows to man the tailors of the Earth; comforting therein, that when old robes are worn out, there are members to make new. If there were no more women but Fulvia, then had you indeed a cut, and the case to be lamented. This grief is crowned with consolation; your old smock brings forth a new petticoat, and indeed the tears live in an onion that should water this sorrow.

ANTONY

163The business she hath broachèd in the state
164Cannot endure my absence.

ENOBARBUS

And the business you have broached here cannot be without you, especially that of Cleopatra’s, which wholly depends on your abode.

ANTONY

165No more light answers. Let our officers
166Have notice what we purpose. I shall break
167The cause of our expedience to the Queen
168And get her leave to part. For not alone
169The death of Fulvia, with more urgent touches,
170Do strongly speak to us, but the letters too
171Of many our contriving friends in Rome
172Petition us at home. Sextus Pompeius
173Hath given the dare to Caesar and commands
174The empire of the sea. Our slippery people,
175Whose love is never linked to the deserver
176Till his deserts are past, begin to throw
177Pompey the Great and all his dignities
178Upon his son, who—high in name and power,
179Higher than both in blood and life—stands up
180For the main soldier; whose quality, going on,
181The sides o’ th’ world may danger. Much is breeding
182Which, like the courser’s hair, hath yet but life
183And not a serpent’s poison. Say our pleasure,
184To such whose place is under us, requires
185Our quick remove from hence.

ENOBARBUS

Our quick remove from hence. I shall do ’t.

They exit.

Scene 3

Enter Cleopatra, Charmian, Alexas, and Iras.

CLEOPATRA

186Where is he?

CHARMIAN

Where is he? I did not see him since.

CLEOPATRA

187To Alexas. See where he is, who’s with him, what he does.
188I did not send you. If you find him sad,
189Say I am dancing; if in mirth, report
190That I am sudden sick. Quick, and return.

Alexas exits.

CHARMIAN

191Madam, methinks, if you did love him dearly,
192You do not hold the method to enforce
193The like from him.

CLEOPATRA

The like from him. What should I do I do not?

CHARMIAN

194In each thing give him way; cross him in nothing.

CLEOPATRA

195Thou teachest like a fool: the way to lose him.

CHARMIAN

196Tempt him not so too far. I wish, forbear.
197In time we hate that which we often fear.

Enter Antony.

198But here comes Antony.

CLEOPATRA

But here comes Antony. I am sick and sullen.

ANTONY

199I am sorry to give breathing to my purpose—

CLEOPATRA

200Help me away, dear Charmian! I shall fall.
201It cannot be thus long; the sides of nature
202Will not sustain it.

ANTONY

Will not sustain it. Now, my dearest queen—

CLEOPATRA

203Pray you stand farther from me.

ANTONY

Pray you stand farther from me. What’s the matter?

CLEOPATRA

204I know by that same eye there’s some good news.
205What, says the married woman you may go?
206Would she had never given you leave to come.
207Let her not say ’tis I that keep you here.
208I have no power upon you. Hers you are.

ANTONY

209The gods best know—

CLEOPATRA

The gods best know— O, never was there queen
210So mightily betrayed! Yet at the first
211I saw the treasons planted.

ANTONY

I saw the treasons planted. Cleopatra—

CLEOPATRA

212Why should I think you can be mine, and true—
213Though you in swearing shake the thronèd gods—
214Who have been false to Fulvia? Riotous madness,
215To be entangled with those mouth-made vows
216Which break themselves in swearing!

ANTONY

Which break themselves in swearing! Most sweet queen—

CLEOPATRA

217Nay, pray you seek no color for your going,
218But bid farewell and go. When you sued staying,
219Then was the time for words. No going then!
220Eternity was in our lips and eyes,
221Bliss in our brows’ bent; none our parts so poor
222But was a race of heaven. They are so still,
223Or thou, the greatest soldier of the world,
224Art turned the greatest liar.

ANTONY

Art turned the greatest liar. How now, lady?

CLEOPATRA

225I would I had thy inches. Thou shouldst know
226There were a heart in Egypt.

ANTONY

There were a heart in Egypt. Hear me, queen:
227The strong necessity of time commands
228Our services awhile, but my full heart
229Remains in use with you. Our Italy
230Shines o’er with civil swords; Sextus Pompeius
231Makes his approaches to the port of Rome;
232Equality of two domestic powers
233Breed scrupulous faction; the hated grown to strength
234Are newly grown to love; the condemned Pompey,
235Rich in his father’s honor, creeps apace
236Into the hearts of such as have not thrived
237Upon the present state, whose numbers threaten;
238And quietness, grown sick of rest, would purge
239By any desperate change. My more particular,
240And that which most with you should safe my going,
241Is Fulvia’s death.

CLEOPATRA

242Though age from folly could not give me freedom,
243It does from childishness. Can Fulvia die?

ANTONY

244She’s dead, my queen.

He shows her papers.

245Look here, and at thy sovereign leisure read
246The garboils she awaked; at the last, best,
247See when and where she died.

CLEOPATRA

See when and where she died. O, most false love!
248Where be the sacred vials thou shouldst fill
249With sorrowful water? Now I see, I see,
250In Fulvia’s death, how mine received shall be.

ANTONY

251Quarrel no more, but be prepared to know
252The purposes I bear, which are or cease
253As you shall give th’ advice. By the fire
254That quickens Nilus’ slime, I go from hence
255Thy soldier, servant, making peace or war
256As thou affects.

CLEOPATRA

As thou affects. Cut my lace, Charmian, come!
257But let it be; I am quickly ill and well;
258So Antony loves.

ANTONY

So Antony loves. My precious queen, forbear,
259And give true evidence to his love, which stands
260An honorable trial.

CLEOPATRA

An honorable trial. So Fulvia told me.
261I prithee turn aside and weep for her,
262Then bid adieu to me, and say the tears
263Belong to Egypt. Good now, play one scene
264Of excellent dissembling, and let it look
265Like perfect honor.

ANTONY

Like perfect honor. You’ll heat my blood. No more!

CLEOPATRA

266You can do better yet, but this is meetly.

ANTONY

267Now by my sword—

CLEOPATRA

Now by my sword— And target. Still he mends.
268But this is not the best. Look, prithee, Charmian,
269How this Herculean Roman does become
270The carriage of his chafe.

ANTONY

271I’ll leave you, lady.

CLEOPATRA

272Courteous lord, one word.
273Sir, you and I must part, but that’s not it;
274Sir, you and I have loved, but there’s not it;
275That you know well. Something it is I would—
276O, my oblivion is a very Antony,
277And I am all forgotten.

ANTONY

And I am all forgotten. But that your Royalty
278Holds idleness your subject, I should take you
279For idleness itself.

CLEOPATRA

For idleness itself. ’Tis sweating labor
280To bear such idleness so near the heart
281As Cleopatra this. But, sir, forgive me,
282Since my becomings kill me when they do not
283Eye well to you. Your honor calls you hence;
284Therefore be deaf to my unpitied folly,
285And all the gods go with you. Upon your sword
286Sit laurel victory, and smooth success
287Be strewed before your feet.

ANTONY

Be strewed before your feet. Let us go. Come.
288Our separation so abides and flies
289That thou, residing here, goes yet with me,
290And I, hence fleeting, here remain with thee.
291Away!

They exit.

Scene 4

Enter Octavius Caesar, reading a letter, Lepidus, and their Train.

CAESAR

292You may see, Lepidus, and henceforth know,
293It is not Caesar’s natural vice to hate
294Our great competitor. From Alexandria
295This is the news: he fishes, drinks, and wastes
296The lamps of night in revel, is not more manlike
297Than Cleopatra, nor the queen of Ptolemy
298More womanly than he; hardly gave audience, or
299Vouchsafed to think he had partners. You shall find there
300A man who is th’ abstract of all faults
301That all men follow.

LEPIDUS

That all men follow. I must not think there are
302Evils enough to darken all his goodness.
303His faults in him seem as the spots of heaven,
304More fiery by night’s blackness, hereditary
305Rather than purchased, what he cannot change
306Than what he chooses.

CAESAR

307You are too indulgent. Let’s grant it is not
308Amiss to tumble on the bed of Ptolemy,
309To give a kingdom for a mirth, to sit
310And keep the turn of tippling with a slave,
311To reel the streets at noon and stand the buffet
312With knaves that smells of sweat. Say this becomes him—
313As his composure must be rare indeed
314Whom these things cannot blemish—yet must Antony
315No way excuse his foils when we do bear
316So great weight in his lightness. If he filled
317His vacancy with his voluptuousness,
318Full surfeits and the dryness of his bones
319Call on him for ’t. But to confound such time
320That drums him from his sport and speaks as loud
321As his own state and ours, ’tis to be chid
322As we rate boys who, being mature in knowledge,
323Pawn their experience to their present pleasure
324And so rebel to judgment.

Enter a Messenger.

LEPIDUS

And so rebel to judgment. Here’s more news.

MESSENGER

325Thy biddings have been done, and every hour,
326Most noble Caesar, shalt thou have report
327How ’tis abroad. Pompey is strong at sea,
328And it appears he is beloved of those
329That only have feared Caesar. To the ports
330The discontents repair, and men’s reports
331Give him much wronged.

CAESAR

Give him much wronged. I should have known no less.
332It hath been taught us from the primal state
333That he which is was wished until he were,
334And the ebbed man, ne’er loved till ne’er worth love,
335Comes feared by being lacked. This common body,
336Like to a vagabond flag upon the stream,
337Goes to and back, lackeying the varying tide
338To rot itself with motion.

Enter a Second Messenger.

SECOND MESSENGER

To rot itself with motion. Caesar, I bring thee word
339Menecrates and Menas, famous pirates,
340Makes the sea serve them, which they ear and wound
341With keels of every kind. Many hot inroads
342They make in Italy—the borders maritime
343Lack blood to think on ’t—and flush youth revolt.
344No vessel can peep forth but ’tis as soon
345Taken as seen, for Pompey’s name strikes more
346Than could his war resisted.

CAESAR

Than could his war resisted. Antony,
347Leave thy lascivious wassails. When thou once
348Was beaten from Modena, where thou slew’st
349Hirsius and Pansa, consuls, at thy heel
350Did famine follow, whom thou fought’st against,
351Though daintily brought up, with patience more
352Than savages could suffer. Thou didst drink
353The stale of horses and the gilded puddle
354Which beasts would cough at. Thy palate then did deign
355The roughest berry on the rudest hedge.
356Yea, like the stag when snow the pasture sheets,
357The barks of trees thou browsèd. On the Alps
358It is reported thou didst eat strange flesh
359Which some did die to look on. And all this—
360It wounds thine honor that I speak it now—
361Was borne so like a soldier that thy cheek
362So much as lanked not.

LEPIDUS

363’Tis pity of him.

CAESAR

364Let his shames quickly
365Drive him to Rome. ’Tis time we twain
366Did show ourselves i’ th’ field, and to that end
367Assemble we immediate council. Pompey
368Thrives in our idleness.

LEPIDUS

369Tomorrow, Caesar,
370I shall be furnished to inform you rightly
371Both what by sea and land I can be able
372To front this present time.

CAESAR

To front this present time. Till which encounter,
373It is my business too. Farewell.

LEPIDUS

374Farewell, my lord. What you shall know meantime
375Of stirs abroad, I shall beseech you, sir,
376To let me be partaker.

CAESAR

377Doubt not, sir. I knew it for my bond.

They exit.

Scene 5

Enter Cleopatra, Charmian, Iras, and Mardian.

CLEOPATRA

378Charmian!

CHARMIAN

379Madam?

CLEOPATRA

380Ha, ha! Give me to drink mandragora.

CHARMIAN

381Why, madam?

CLEOPATRA

382That I might sleep out this great gap of time
383My Antony is away.

CHARMIAN

My Antony is away. You think of him too much.

CLEOPATRA

384O, ’tis treason!

CHARMIAN

O, ’tis treason! Madam, I trust not so.

CLEOPATRA

385Thou, eunuch Mardian!

MARDIAN

Thou, eunuch Mardian! What’s your Highness’ pleasure?

CLEOPATRA

386Not now to hear thee sing. I take no pleasure
387In aught an eunuch has. ’Tis well for thee
388That, being unseminared, thy freer thoughts
389May not fly forth of Egypt. Hast thou affections?

MARDIAN

390Yes, gracious madam.

CLEOPATRA

391Indeed?

MARDIAN

392Not in deed, madam, for I can do nothing
393But what indeed is honest to be done.
394Yet have I fierce affections, and think
395What Venus did with Mars.

CLEOPATRA

What Venus did with Mars. O, Charmian,
396Where think’st thou he is now? Stands he, or sits he?
397Or does he walk? Or is he on his horse?
398O happy horse, to bear the weight of Antony!
399Do bravely, horse, for wot’st thou whom thou mov’st?
400The demi-Atlas of this Earth, the arm
401And burgonet of men. He’s speaking now,
402Or murmuring “Where’s my serpent of old Nile?”
403For so he calls me. Now I feed myself
404With most delicious poison. Think on me
405That am with Phoebus’ amorous pinches black,
406And wrinkled deep in time? Broad-fronted Caesar,
407When thou wast here above the ground, I was
408A morsel for a monarch. And great Pompey
409Would stand and make his eyes grow in my brow;
410There would he anchor his aspect, and die
411With looking on his life.

Enter Alexas from Antony.

ALEXAS

412Sovereign of Egypt, hail!

CLEOPATRA

413How much unlike art thou Mark Antony!
414Yet coming from him, that great med’cine hath
415With his tinct gilded thee.
416How goes it with my brave Mark Antony?

ALEXAS

417Last thing he did, dear queen,
418He kissed—the last of many doubled kisses—
419This orient pearl. His speech sticks in my heart.

CLEOPATRA

420Mine ear must pluck it thence.

ALEXAS

Mine ear must pluck it thence. “Good friend,” quoth he,
421“Say the firm Roman to great Egypt sends
422This treasure of an oyster; at whose foot,
423To mend the petty present, I will piece
424Her opulent throne with kingdoms. All the East,
425Say thou, shall call her mistress.” So he nodded
426And soberly did mount an arm-gaunt steed,
427Who neighed so high that what I would have spoke
428Was beastly dumbed by him.

CLEOPATRA

Was beastly dumbed by him. What, was he sad, or merry?

ALEXAS

429Like to the time o’ th’ year between th’ extremes
430Of hot and cold, he was nor sad nor merry.

CLEOPATRA

431O, well-divided disposition!—Note him,
432Note him, good Charmian, ’tis the man! But note him:
433He was not sad, for he would shine on those
434That make their looks by his; he was not merry,
435Which seemed to tell them his remembrance lay
436In Egypt with his joy; but between both.
437O, heavenly mingle!—Be’st thou sad or merry,
438The violence of either thee becomes,
439So does it no man’s else.—Met’st thou my posts?

ALEXAS

440Ay, madam, twenty several messengers.
441Why do you send so thick?

CLEOPATRA

Why do you send so thick? Who’s born that day
442When I forget to send to Antony
443Shall die a beggar.—Ink and paper, Charmian.—
444Welcome, my good Alexas.—Did I, Charmian,
445Ever love Caesar so?

CHARMIAN

Ever love Caesar so? O, that brave Caesar!

CLEOPATRA

446Be choked with such another emphasis!
447Say “the brave Antony.”

CHARMIAN

Say “the brave Antony.” The valiant Caesar!

CLEOPATRA

448By Isis, I will give thee bloody teeth
449If thou with Caesar paragon again
450My man of men.

CHARMIAN

My man of men. By your most gracious pardon,
451I sing but after you.

CLEOPATRA

I sing but after you. My salad days,
452When I was green in judgment, cold in blood,
453To say as I said then. But come, away,
454Get me ink and paper.
455He shall have every day a several greeting,
456Or I’ll unpeople Egypt.
457They exit.

Act II

Scene 1

Enter Pompey, Menecrates, and Menas, in warlike manner.

POMPEY

458If the great gods be just, they shall assist
459The deeds of justest men.

MENAS

The deeds of justest men. Know, worthy Pompey,
460That what they do delay they not deny.

POMPEY

461Whiles we are suitors to their throne, decays
462The thing we sue for.

MENAS

The thing we sue for. We, ignorant of ourselves,
463Beg often our own harms, which the wise powers
464Deny us for our good; so find we profit
465By losing of our prayers.

POMPEY

By losing of our prayers. I shall do well.
466The people love me, and the sea is mine;
467My powers are crescent, and my auguring hope
468Says it will come to th’ full. Mark Antony
469In Egypt sits at dinner, and will make
470No wars without doors. Caesar gets money where
471He loses hearts. Lepidus flatters both,
472Of both is flattered; but he neither loves,
473Nor either cares for him.

MENAS

Nor either cares for him. Caesar and Lepidus
474Are in the field. A mighty strength they carry.

POMPEY

475Where have you this? ’Tis false.

MENAS

Where have you this? ’Tis false. From Silvius, sir.

POMPEY

476He dreams. I know they are in Rome together,
477Looking for Antony. But all the charms of love,
478Salt Cleopatra, soften thy wanned lip!
479Let witchcraft join with beauty, lust with both;
480Tie up the libertine in a field of feasts;
481Keep his brain fuming. Epicurean cooks
482Sharpen with cloyless sauce his appetite,
483That sleep and feeding may prorogue his honor
484Even till a Lethe’d dullness—

Enter Varrius.

Even till a Lethe’d dullness— How now, Varrius?

VARRIUS

485This is most certain that I shall deliver:
486Mark Antony is every hour in Rome
487Expected. Since he went from Egypt ’tis
488A space for farther travel.

POMPEY

489I could have given less matter
490A better ear.—Menas, I did not think
491This amorous surfeiter would have donned his helm
492For such a petty war. His soldiership
493Is twice the other twain. But let us rear
494The higher our opinion, that our stirring
495Can from the lap of Egypt’s widow pluck
496The ne’er lust-wearied Antony.

MENAS

The ne’er lust-wearied Antony. I cannot hope
497Caesar and Antony shall well greet together.
498His wife that’s dead did trespasses to Caesar;
499His brother warred upon him, although I think
500Not moved by Antony.

POMPEY

Not moved by Antony. I know not, Menas,
501How lesser enmities may give way to greater.
502Were ’t not that we stand up against them all,
503’Twere pregnant they should square between themselves,
504For they have entertainèd cause enough
505To draw their swords. But how the fear of us
506May cement their divisions and bind up
507The petty difference, we yet not know.
508Be ’t as our gods will have ’t. It only stands
509Our lives upon to use our strongest hands.
510Come, Menas.

They exit.

Scene 2

Enter Enobarbus and Lepidus.

LEPIDUS

511Good Enobarbus, ’tis a worthy deed,
512And shall become you well, to entreat your captain
513To soft and gentle speech.

ENOBARBUS

To soft and gentle speech. I shall entreat him
514To answer like himself. If Caesar move him,
515Let Antony look over Caesar’s head
516And speak as loud as Mars. By Jupiter,
517Were I the wearer of Antonio’s beard,
518I would not shave ’t today.

LEPIDUS

519’Tis not a time for private stomaching.

ENOBARBUS

Every time serves for the matter that is then born in ’t.

LEPIDUS

520But small to greater matters must give way.

ENOBARBUS

521Not if the small come first.

LEPIDUS

522Your speech is passion; but pray you stir
523No embers up. Here comes the noble Antony.

Enter, at one door, Antony and Ventidius.

ENOBARBUS

524And yonder Caesar.

Enter, at another door, Caesar, Maecenas, and Agrippa.

ANTONY

525To Ventidius. If we compose well here, to Parthia.
526Hark, Ventidius.

They talk aside.

CAESAR

527To Maecenas. I do not know, Maecenas. Ask Agrippa.

LEPIDUS

528To Caesar and Antony. Noble friends,
529That which combined us was most great, and let not
530A leaner action rend us. What’s amiss,
531May it be gently heard. When we debate
532Our trivial difference loud, we do commit
533Murder in healing wounds. Then, noble partners,
534The rather for I earnestly beseech,
535Touch you the sourest points with sweetest terms,
536Nor curstness grow to th’ matter.

ANTONY

Nor curstness grow to th’ matter. ’Tis spoken well.
537Were we before our armies, and to fight,
538I should do thus.

Flourish.

CAESAR

539Welcome to Rome.

ANTONY

540Thank you.

CAESAR

541Sit.

ANTONY

542Sit, sir.

CAESAR

543Nay, then.

They sit.

ANTONY

544I learn you take things ill which are not so,
545Or, being, concern you not.

CAESAR

Or, being, concern you not. I must be laughed at
546If or for nothing or a little, I
547Should say myself offended, and with you
548Chiefly i’ th’ world; more laughed at, that I should
549Once name you derogately when to sound your name
550It not concerned me.

ANTONY

551My being in Egypt, Caesar, what was ’t to you?

CAESAR

552No more than my residing here at Rome
553Might be to you in Egypt. Yet if you there
554Did practice on my state, your being in Egypt
555Might be my question.

ANTONY

Might be my question. How intend you, practiced?

CAESAR

556You may be pleased to catch at mine intent
557By what did here befall me. Your wife and brother
558Made wars upon me, and their contestation
559Was theme for you; you were the word of war.

ANTONY

560You do mistake your business. My brother never
561Did urge me in his act. I did inquire it,
562And have my learning from some true reports
563That drew their swords with you. Did he not rather
564Discredit my authority with yours,
565And make the wars alike against my stomach,
566Having alike your cause? Of this my letters
567Before did satisfy you. If you’ll patch a quarrel,
568As matter whole you have to make it with,
569It must not be with this.

CAESAR

It must not be with this. You praise yourself
570By laying defects of judgment to me; but
571You patched up your excuses.

ANTONY

You patched up your excuses. Not so, not so.
572I know you could not lack—I am certain on ’t—
573Very necessity of this thought, that I,
574Your partner in the cause ’gainst which he fought,
575Could not with graceful eyes attend those wars
576Which fronted mine own peace. As for my wife,
577I would you had her spirit in such another.
578The third o’ th’ world is yours, which with a snaffle
579You may pace easy, but not such a wife.

ENOBARBUS

Would we had all such wives, that the men might go to wars with the women!

ANTONY

580So much uncurbable, her garboils, Caesar,
581Made out of her impatience—which not wanted
582Shrewdness of policy too—I grieving grant
583Did you too much disquiet. For that you must
584But say I could not help it.

CAESAR

But say I could not help it. I wrote to you
585When rioting in Alexandria; you
586Did pocket up my letters, and with taunts
587Did gibe my missive out of audience.

ANTONY

Did gibe my missive out of audience. Sir,
588He fell upon me ere admitted, then;
589Three kings I had newly feasted, and did want
590Of what I was i’ th’ morning. But next day
591I told him of myself, which was as much
592As to have asked him pardon. Let this fellow
593Be nothing of our strife; if we contend,
594Out of our question wipe him.

CAESAR

Out of our question wipe him. You have broken
595The article of your oath, which you shall never
596Have tongue to charge me with.

LEPIDUS

597Soft, Caesar!

ANTONY

598No, Lepidus, let him speak.
599The honor is sacred which he talks on now,
600Supposing that I lacked it.—But on, Caesar:
601The article of my oath?

CAESAR

602To lend me arms and aid when I required them,
603The which you both denied.

ANTONY

The which you both denied. Neglected, rather;
604And then when poisoned hours had bound me up
605From mine own knowledge. As nearly as I may
606I’ll play the penitent to you. But mine honesty
607Shall not make poor my greatness, nor my power
608Work without it. Truth is that Fulvia,
609To have me out of Egypt, made wars here,
610For which myself, the ignorant motive, do
611So far ask pardon as befits mine honor
612To stoop in such a case.

LEPIDUS

To stoop in such a case. ’Tis noble spoken.

MAECENAS

613If it might please you to enforce no further
614The griefs between you, to forget them quite
615Were to remember that the present need
616Speaks to atone you.

LEPIDUS

Speaks to atone you. Worthily spoken, Maecenas.

ENOBARBUS

Or, if you borrow one another’s love for the instant, you may, when you hear no more words of Pompey, return it again. You shall have time to wrangle in when you have nothing else to do.

ANTONY

617Thou art a soldier only. Speak no more.

ENOBARBUS

That truth should be silent I had almost forgot.

ANTONY

618You wrong this presence; therefore speak no more.

ENOBARBUS

619Go to, then. Your considerate stone.

CAESAR

620I do not much dislike the matter, but
621The manner of his speech; for ’t cannot be
622We shall remain in friendship, our conditions
623So diff’ring in their acts. Yet if I knew
624What hoop should hold us staunch, from edge to edge
625O’ th’ world I would pursue it.

AGRIPPA

626Give me leave, Caesar.

CAESAR

627Speak, Agrippa.

AGRIPPA

628Thou hast a sister by the mother’s side,
629Admired Octavia. Great Mark Antony
630Is now a widower.

CAESAR

Is now a widower. Say not so, Agrippa.
631If Cleopatra heard you, your reproof
632Were well deserved of rashness.

ANTONY

633I am not married, Caesar. Let me hear

Agrippa further speak.

AGRIPPA

634To hold you in perpetual amity,
635To make you brothers, and to knit your hearts
636With an unslipping knot, take Antony
637Octavia to his wife, whose beauty claims
638No worse a husband than the best of men;
639Whose virtue and whose general graces speak
640That which none else can utter. By this marriage
641All little jealousies, which now seem great,
642And all great fears, which now import their dangers,
643Would then be nothing. Truths would be tales,
644Where now half-tales be truths. Her love to both
645Would each to other and all loves to both
646Draw after her. Pardon what I have spoke,
647For ’tis a studied, not a present thought,
648By duty ruminated.

ANTONY

By duty ruminated. Will Caesar speak?

CAESAR

649Not till he hears how Antony is touched
650With what is spoke already.

ANTONY

651What power is in Agrippa,
652If I would say “Agrippa, be it so,”
653To make this good?

CAESAR

To make this good? The power of Caesar, and
654His power unto Octavia.

ANTONY

His power unto Octavia. May I never
655To this good purpose, that so fairly shows,
656Dream of impediment. Let me have thy hand.
657Further this act of grace; and from this hour
658The heart of brothers govern in our loves
659And sway our great designs.

CAESAR

And sway our great designs. There’s my hand.

They clasp hands.

660A sister I bequeath you whom no brother
661Did ever love so dearly. Let her live
662To join our kingdoms and our hearts; and never
663Fly off our loves again.

LEPIDUS

Fly off our loves again. Happily, amen!

ANTONY

664I did not think to draw my sword ’gainst Pompey,
665For he hath laid strange courtesies and great
666Of late upon me. I must thank him only,
667Lest my remembrance suffer ill report;
668At heel of that, defy him.

LEPIDUS

At heel of that, defy him. Time calls upon ’s.
669Of us must Pompey presently be sought,
670Or else he seeks out us.

ANTONY

671Where lies he?

CAESAR

672About the Mount Misena.

ANTONY

673What is his strength by land?

CAESAR

674Great and increasing;
675But by sea he is an absolute master.

ANTONY

676So is the fame.
677Would we had spoke together. Haste we for it.
678Yet, ere we put ourselves in arms, dispatch we
679The business we have talked of.

CAESAR

The business we have talked of. With most gladness,
680And do invite you to my sister’s view,
681Whither straight I’ll lead you.

ANTONY

682Let us, Lepidus, not lack your company.

LEPIDUS

683Noble Antony, not sickness should detain me.

Flourish. All but Enobarbus, Agrippa, and Maecenas exit.

MAECENAS

684To Enobarbus. Welcome from Egypt, sir.

ENOBARBUS

Half the heart of Caesar, worthy Maecenas!—My honorable friend Agrippa!

AGRIPPA

Good Enobarbus!

MAECENAS

We have cause to be glad that matters are so well digested. You stayed well by ’t in Egypt.

ENOBARBUS

Ay, sir, we did sleep day out of countenance and made the night light with drinking.

MAECENAS

Eight wild boars roasted whole at a breakfast, and but twelve persons there. Is this true?

ENOBARBUS

This was but as a fly by an eagle. We had much more monstrous matter of feast, which worthily deserved noting.

MAECENAS

She’s a most triumphant lady, if report be square to her.

ENOBARBUS

When she first met Mark Antony, she pursed up his heart upon the river of Cydnus.

AGRIPPA

There she appeared indeed, or my reporter devised well for her.

ENOBARBUS

685I will tell you.
686The barge she sat in like a burnished throne
687Burned on the water. The poop was beaten gold,
688Purple the sails, and so perfumed that
689The winds were lovesick with them. The oars were silver,
690Which to the tune of flutes kept stroke, and made
691The water which they beat to follow faster,
692As amorous of their strokes. For her own person,
693It beggared all description: she did lie
694In her pavilion—cloth-of-gold, of tissue—
695O’erpicturing that Venus where we see
696The fancy outwork nature. On each side her
697Stood pretty dimpled boys, like smiling Cupids,
698With divers-colored fans, whose wind did seem
699To glow the delicate cheeks which they did cool,
700And what they undid did.

AGRIPPA

And what they undid did. O, rare for Antony!

ENOBARBUS

701Her gentlewomen, like the Nereides,
702So many mermaids, tended her i’ th’ eyes,
703And made their bends adornings. At the helm
704A seeming mermaid steers. The silken tackle
705Swell with the touches of those flower-soft hands
706That yarely frame the office. From the barge
707A strange invisible perfume hits the sense
708Of the adjacent wharfs. The city cast
709Her people out upon her; and Antony,
710Enthroned i’ th’ market-place, did sit alone,
711Whistling to th’ air, which but for vacancy
712Had gone to gaze on Cleopatra too
713And made a gap in nature.

AGRIPPA

And made a gap in nature. Rare Egyptian!

ENOBARBUS

714Upon her landing, Antony sent to her,
715Invited her to supper. She replied
716It should be better he became her guest,
717Which she entreated. Our courteous Antony,
718Whom ne’er the word of “No” woman heard speak,
719Being barbered ten times o’er, goes to the feast,
720And for his ordinary pays his heart
721For what his eyes eat only.

AGRIPPA

For what his eyes eat only. Royal wench!
722She made great Caesar lay his sword to bed;
723He ploughed her, and she cropped.

ENOBARBUS

He ploughed her, and she cropped. I saw her once
724Hop forty paces through the public street,
725And having lost her breath, she spoke and panted,
726That she did make defect perfection,
727And breathless pour breath forth.

MAECENAS

728Now Antony must leave her utterly.

ENOBARBUS

729Never. He will not.
730Age cannot wither her, nor custom stale
731Her infinite variety. Other women cloy
732The appetites they feed, but she makes hungry
733Where most she satisfies. For vilest things
734Become themselves in her, that the holy priests
735Bless her when she is riggish.

MAECENAS

736If beauty, wisdom, modesty can settle
737The heart of Antony, Octavia is
738A blessèd lottery to him.

AGRIPPA

A blessèd lottery to him. Let us go.
739Good Enobarbus, make yourself my guest
740Whilst you abide here.

ENOBARBUS

Whilst you abide here. Humbly, sir, I thank you.

They exit.

751Would I had never come from thence, nor you thither.

ANTONY

752If you can, your reason?

753Say to me, whose fortunes shall rise higher,

755Caesar’s.

758Noble, courageous, high, unmatchable,

760Becomes afeard, as being o’erpowered. Therefore
761Make space enough between you.

762To none but thee; no more but when to thee.

764Thou art sure to lose; and of that natural luck

766When he shines by. I say again, thy spirit
767Is all afraid to govern thee near him;
768But he away, ’tis noble.

769Say to Ventidius I would speak with him.

771He hath spoken true. The very dice obey him,
772And in our sports my better cunning faints
773Under his chance. If we draw lots, he speeds;
774His cocks do win the battle still of mine

CHARMIAN

CLEOPATRA

796As well a woman with an eunuch played

798As well as I can, madam.

806And say “Aha! You’re caught.”

808Did hang a salt fish on his hook, which he
809With fervency drew up.

812Ere the ninth hour, I drunk him to his bed,
813Then put my tires and mantles on him, whilst

816That long time have been barren.

MESSENGER

CLEOPATRA

817Antonio’s dead! If thou say so, villain,
818Thou kill’st thy mistress. But well and free,
819If thou so yield him, there is gold, and here
820My bluest veins to kiss, a hand that kings
821Have lipped and trembled kissing.

MESSENGER

822First, madam, he is well.

CLEOPATRA

823Why, there’s more gold. But sirrah, mark, we use
824To say the dead are well. Bring it to that,
825The gold I give thee will I melt and pour
826Down thy ill-uttering throat.

MESSENGER

827Good madam, hear me.

CLEOPATRA

828Well, go to, I will.
829But there’s no goodness in thy face—if Antony
830Be free and healthful, so tart a favor
831To trumpet such good tidings! If not well,
832Thou shouldst come like a Fury crowned with snakes,
833Not like a formal man.

MESSENGER

Not like a formal man. Will ’t please you hear me?

CLEOPATRA

834I have a mind to strike thee ere thou speak’st
835Yet if thou say Antony lives, is well,
836Or friends with Caesar or not captive to him,
837I’ll set thee in a shower of gold and hail
838Rich pearls upon thee.

MESSENGER

Rich pearls upon thee. Madam, he’s well.

CLEOPATRA

Rich pearls upon thee. Madam, he’s well. Well said.

MESSENGER

839And friends with Caesar.

CLEOPATRA

And friends with Caesar. Th’ art an honest man.

MESSENGER

840Caesar and he are greater friends than ever.

CLEOPATRA

841Make thee a fortune from me.

MESSENGER

Make thee a fortune from me. But yet, madam—

CLEOPATRA

842I do not like “But yet.” It does allay
843The good precedence. Fie upon “But yet.”
844“But yet” is as a jailer to bring forth
845Some monstrous malefactor. Prithee, friend,
846Pour out the pack of matter to mine ear,
847The good and bad together: he’s friends with Caesar,
848In state of health, thou say’st, and, thou say’st, free.

MESSENGER

849Free, madam, no. I made no such report.
850He’s bound unto Octavia.

CLEOPATRA

2811O sir, you are too sure an augurer:
2812That you did fear is done.

CAESAR

That you did fear is done. Bravest at the last,
2813She leveled at our purposes and, being royal,
2814Took her own way. The manner of their deaths?
2815I do not see them bleed.

DOLABELLA

I do not see them bleed. Who was last with them?

FIRST GUARD

2816A simple countryman that brought her figs.
2817This was his basket.

CAESAR

This was his basket. Poisoned, then.

FIRST GUARD

This was his basket. Poisoned, then. O Caesar,
2818This Charmian lived but now; she stood and spake.
2819I found her trimming up the diadem
2820On her dead mistress; tremblingly she stood,
2821And on the sudden dropped.

CAESAR

And on the sudden dropped. O, noble weakness!
2822If they had swallowed poison, ’twould appear
2823By external swelling; but she looks like sleep,
2824As she would catch another Antony
2825In her strong toil of grace.

DOLABELLA

In her strong toil of grace. Here on her breast
2826There is a vent of blood, and something blown.
2827The like is on her arm.

FIRST GUARD

2828This is an aspic’s trail, and these fig leaves
2829Have slime upon them, such as th’ aspic leaves
2830Upon the caves of Nile.

CAESAR

Upon the caves of Nile. Most probable
2831That so she died, for her physician tells me
2832She hath pursued conclusions infinite
2833Of easy ways to die. Take up her bed,
2834And bear her women from the monument.
2835She shall be buried by her Antony.
2836No grave upon the earth shall clip in it
2837A pair so famous. High events as these
2838Strike those that make them; and their story is
2839No less in pity than his glory which
2840Brought them to be lamented. Our army shall
2841In solemn show attend this funeral,
2842And then to Rome. Come, Dolabella, see
2843High order in this great solemnity.

They all exit, the Guards bearing the dead bodies.
