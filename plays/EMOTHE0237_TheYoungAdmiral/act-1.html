The Young Admiral

Enter Prince and Alberto.

Alberto

1My lord, you're sad.

Prince

My lord, you're sad. I am thinking, Alberto,
2Of many things, have I not cause?

Alberto

Of many things, have I not cause? You may
3Think on 'em with less trouble.

Prince

Think on 'em with less trouble. But of all,
4What dost imagine most afflicts me? I'll
5Prevent thy answer: I am not troubled
6With the present threat'nings of the enemy,
7With all his preparations to invade us.

Alberto

8You have more confidence in Vittori, sent
9To meet the insulting King; he has been fortunate
10In many wars.

Prince

In many wars. The wars consume Vittori!
11He has been too fortunate.

Alberto

He has been too fortunate. Your wishes are
12Against the common peace: if he prove not
13A happy admiral, we are lost.

Prince

A happy admiral, we are lost. Be thou
14And all thy name lost, and may no age
15Find it again! How dare you interrupt us?
16When we do want your counsel, we’ll call for you.

Alberto

17I am gone, sir.

[Exit.]

Enter Julio.

Prince

I am gone, sir. My Julio, welcome.
18What speaks Cassandra yet?

Julio

19Nothing to encourage you – the same obstinate thing:
20Vittori has her heart. She much condemns
21The roughness which you mixed with your last courtship;
22She says your father may command her life,
23But you must be a stranger to her bosom.

Prince

24I was too rude at my last visit.

Julio

25Rather, sir, too tame.

Prince

26Have I for this drawn war upon my country,
27Neglected Sicily’s daughter, left a stain
28Upon his court, and paid his entertainment
29With wounding one he loved?

Julio

With wounding one he loved? His favorite!
30You had been less, sir, than yourself to have suffered
31His insolence; nor was't an act becoming
32His master to send hither to negotiate
33A marriage for his daughter and, when you
34So far engaged yourself upon a visit,
35To permit any of his gaudy upstarts
36Affront your person.

Prince

Affront your person. I acquit the King:
37'Twas no state quarrel. High with wine, he did
38Throw some disgrace on our Italian ladies,
39Whilst he would magnify some beauties there.
40This he did second with a pride and rudeness
41My patience was not tame enough to suffer,
42And, careless of all danger, I did punish him.

Julio

43'Twas home and handsome.

Prince

'Twas home and handsome. I must owe to Fortune –

Julio

44For your return, she did but do her duty
45To make it swift and happy.

Prince

To make it swift and happy. I confess
46The Princess used me nobly, though my fancy
47Was not surprised; for here I kept the image
48Of fair Cassandra, whose divine beauty
49Doth scorn all competition.

Julio

Doth scorn all competition. Did you love
50Cassandra before you went to Sicily?

Prince

51Yes, but with too much silence, and that love
52Did make me apprehend more fiercely the
53Occasion to break off all foreign treaty.
54Horatio's fall, and my quitting the country
55Upon't, the King interprets a disgrace
56To his daughter and himself and, in revenge,
57Hath added this new tempest to the sea,
58Meant to our ruin, Julio.

Julio

Meant to our ruin, Julio. All their fury
59May soon be interrupted, if Vittori
60Manage his business well.

Prince

Manage his business well. That's all my trouble.

Julio

61What?

Prince

What? Vittori: there's the devil on't, he may
62Be fortunate and overcome.

Julio

Be fortunate and overcome. Can there
63Be ill in that?

Prince

Be ill in that? Ill? Thou art shallow – I
64Made him not admiral but to engage
65His youth and spirit, apt to fly on dangers,
66To perish in his hot pursuit of honor.
67If he come home with victory, my father
68And his wise state must give him thanks, the people
69Giddily run to meet the conqueror
70And owe their lives, and safety, to his triumph.
71But where am I? What peace brings it to me?
72What blessing is't to hear the general voice
73Shoot their wild joys to heaven and I, in torment,
74Certain to lose my hopes in fair Cassandra?

Julio

75There may be ways at home to remove him
76And plant you in your wishes.

Prince

And plant you in your wishes. It would be
77Most happiness to hear his death.

Julio

Most happiness to hear his death. That may
78Ruin a kingdom.

Prince

Ruin a kingdom. Ruin twenty more,
79So I enjoy her first! Nothing can be
80Too precious to forfeit. I am mad,
81And my desires, by opposition, grow
82More violent.

Julio

More violent. I thought your masculine soul
83Less capable of vexation. Shall a subject,
84Whom with your breath you may blow out o'th'world,
85Raise such a storm within you?

Prince

Raise such a storm within you? No, he shan't!
86I've found myself again – come, I’ll be merry.
87But I will have Cassandra spite of fate!

Julio

88Resolve, and have her.

Prince

Resolve, and have her. Stay, it were convenient
89We did know how to do this, Julio.

Julio

90You're in the right, sir; it were first, indeed,
91Convenient to know how.

Prince

Convenient to know how. Thou knowst his father –

Julio

92Signor Alphonso?

Prince

93A bold and daring gentleman, all flame
94When he is moved and careless of a danger
95To vindicate his honor.

Julio

To vindicate his honor. What of this?

Prince

96He shall bear the foundation of a plot
97To make me lord of my desires.

Julio

To make me lord of my desires. He’ll rather
98Meet tortures than consent, his arm is not
99Yet withered, and while he can lift a sword,
100He will employ it to revenge Vittori.

Prince

101Thou art no politician, Julio. –

Enter Fabio.

102How now, what news with you?

Fabio

How now, what news with you? An't please your grace,
103An humble creature of yours, proud of the least
104Occasion to express how faithfully
105My heart is fixed to serve you.

Prince

My heart is fixed to serve you. What’s your business?

Fabio

106I have business of some consequence.
107I had not been so bold else to disturb
108Your princely conference, for I durst never
109Assume that impudent garb that other courtiers
110Are known by; my devotion has been still
111To appear in modest services.

Prince

To appear in modest services. To'th'point.

Fabio

112It were a point of deep neglect to keep
113Your grace in expectation, yet delays
114Make joys the sweeter: arrows that fly compass
115Arrive with as much happiness to the mark
116As those are shot point-blank!

Prince

As those are shot point-blank! This courtier lovesaparte
117To hear himself talk.aparte
118We know your care.

Fabio

We know your care. And cost, my lord, sometimes;
119For they that hold intelligence abroad
120To benefit their country must not make
121Idols of their estates, and 'tis a happiness
122To sell their fortunes for their prince's smile,
123Which I am confident you will vouchsafe
124When you have heard my news.

Prince

When you have heard my news. Would you would vouchsafe
125To let us hear!

Fabio

To let us hear! Vouchsafe, my lord! Alas,
126You may command my tongue, my hands, my feet,
127My head; I should account that limb superfluous
128That would not be cut off to do you service.

Prince

129I do command thee silence – dost hear? Silence.

Fabio

130It is a virtue, my good lord, I know,
131But where the tongue has something to deliver
132That may delight a prince’s ear, and so forth –

Julio

133Now there’s some hope! He's come to his "and so forth".aparte

Fabio

134The news concerns the Admiral Vittori.

Prince

135What of him? Is he slain?

Fabio

136The stars forbid! He is returned, my lord,
137Triumphant, brave and glorious –

Prince

Triumphant, brave and glorious – Be dumb!
138Another syllable, I’ll ha' thy tongue out
139And leave no root, lest there grow out another.
140Was all your circumstance for this?

Julio

Was all your circumstance for this? My lord,aparte
141You are too open-breasted – let this fellowaparte
142See into your heart! Wise men disguise their counselsaparte
143Till things are ripe.aparte

Prince

Till things are ripe. [To Fabio] Begone! Pox o'your legs
144An the curse ha' not been before! Yet stay,
145Give order that no man go forth to meet him:
146Until our pleasure’s further known, command
147The Governor o'th'city place a guard
148About the gates; let no man's face appear
149Without the walls. The King, our father, means
150To salute him first in person. D'ye stand?

Fabio

151"Give order that no man go forth to meet him!" –
152I shall, my lord.

[Exit.]

Prince

I shall, my lord. He shall be entertained;aparte
153I feel new armies in my breast.aparte

Enter Alphonso.

I feel new armies in my breast. His father!aparte
154[Whispers to Julio] Thine ear, Julio.

Julio

[Whispers to Julio] Thine ear, Julio. I shall attend you straight. –
155[To Alphonso] My honorable lord.

Alphonso

[To Alphonso] My honorable lord. Your servant, Julio.
156Where is the Prince? I beg your grace's pardon.

[Exit Julio.]

Prince

157O my good lord! Your son, I hear,'s returned
158With honor, has defeated the Sicilian
159Bravely.

Alphonso

Bravely. He has, an't please your highness; heaven
160Has smiled upon his undertaking. It
161Renews my youth to hear it.

Prince

Renews my youth to hear it. He had good soldiers,
162But all their valor still conspires to make
163The general a garland. He must wear
164The conquering bays, whose blood soever pays for't.

Alphonso

165My lord!

Prince

My lord! Nay, nay, I envy not his victory.

Alphonso

166You envy him! It was your cause he fought,
167And for his country.

Prince

And for his country. Right, and 'tis the cause
168That often prospers: that without his valor
169Would ha' defensed itself.

Alphonso

Would ha' defensed itself. If all virtue
170Were left to her own protection, my lord,
171Unarmed with strength and policy, best states
172Would find shrewd innovations.

Prince

Would find shrewd innovations. You had best
173Tell me I lie.

Alphonso

Tell me I lie. I dare not think so folly.

Prince

174You're a traitor! –

[Re-]enter Julio with a guard.

You're a traitor! – Lay hands on him!

Alphonso

175He that shall dare to say Alphonso is
176A traitor, let his veins partake no blood
177Of yours, and he shall curse he had a tongue.

Prince

178Disarm the rebel, and to prison with him!

Alphonso

179Ingrateful Prince!

Prince

Ingrateful Prince! I’ll tame your ruffian spirit. –

Exeunt [Alphonso and guard].

180So, so, I’ll now acquaint my father, Julio,
181Who must allow my act: diseases that
182Are desperate require a rugged handling. –
183This is for thee, Cassandra!aparte

Exeunt.

Enter Vittori, Mauritio, Captain and Soldiers[,with ensigns displayed].

Vittori

184Stand!

1 Soldier

Stand! Stand!

2 Soldier

Stand! Stand! Stand!

3 Soldier

Stand! Stand! Stand! Stand!

Vittori

185The King received intelligence?

Mauritio

The King received intelligence? Our ships
186Must needs report that loud enough.

Vittori

Must needs report that loud enough. 'Tis strange.
187Is it not possible we have mistook
188The shore, transported with our naval victory?
189Speak, gentlemen! Or do we dream?

Mauritio

Speak, gentlemen! Or do we dream? Those walls
190Are certainly the same, and that the city
191Peopled when we launched forth and full of prayers
192For our success.

Captain

For our success. It may be they reserve
193Their welcome till we march into the city.

Mauritio

194They may have some conceit.

Vittori

They may have some conceit. A general silence
195Like night dwells round about us, and no sign
196That men inhabit; have we won at sea
197To lose ourselves upon the land? Or in
198Our absence hath some monster landed here
199And made it desolate, devoured the natives,
200And made 'em creep into the earth again?

Mauritio

201They might salute us with one piece of ordnance.

Vittori

202They cannot take us for their enemies.
203Captain, inquire the cause; let none else move.
204Yet stay, unless it be some strange mortality –
205And yet that cannot be –, have we brought home
206Their safety purchased through so many horrors
207And is this all the payment for our conquest,
208To shut the gates upon us?

Captain

To shut the gates upon us? Force them open
209With the cannon, shake their walls about their ears –
210They are asleep.

Vittori

They are asleep. For such another rashness,
211Thy head shall be the bullet of that cannon
212And shot into the town; go to! Be temperate.
213As I grudge none the merit of their valor,
214I must hear none so bold.

Captain

I must hear none so bold. I ha' done, sir.

Vittori

215Subjects are bound to fight for princes, they
216Not bound to the reward of every service.
217I look upon thee, now fighting at sea,
218And have forgot this error; give no breath
219To such a thought hereafter. Honor pays
220Double where kings neglect, and he is valiant
221Truly that dares forget to be rewarded.

1 Soldier

222This is but cold comfort for a knapsack-man.

Vittori

223And yet 'tis strange the King should thus neglect us,
224This is cheap entertainment for a conqueror.
225Is't not, Mauritio? Misery of soldiers!
226When they have sweat blood for their country's honor,
227They stand at others' mercy.

Mauritio

They stand at others' mercy. They have slept since,
228And dreamt not of our sufferings.

Vittori

And dreamt not of our sufferings. Is the Princeaparte
229Alive, to whom we owe our country's quarrel,aparte
230The difference of both kingdoms?aparte
231Our war and fortunes justify his act,aparte
232Can he be guilty of this shame? No more!aparte
233There's something would fain mutiny within me;aparte
234Strangle the snakes betime, Vittori.aparte
235This was a way to forfeit all our fames.
236Fold up your ensigns, throw off all the pride
237That may express a triumph – we'll march on
238As we had overbought our victory.

Mauritio

239The gates are opened now, and we discover
240A woman by her veil, in mourning habit,
241Coming this way.

Vittori

Coming this way. Alone? More strange and fatal:
242It may be 'tis my genius come to give
243A melancholy warning of my death,
244As Brutus had from his. I'll stand my destiny,
245Yet bearing the resemblance of a woman
246It will less terrify. Who should this be? –

Enter Cassandra veiled, in mourning.

247Lady, your garment speaks you a sad woman.
248Griefs should salute no nearer, if it were
249In poor Vittory's power to dispossess you
250Of any sorrow.

Cassandra

Of any sorrow. O my dear Vittori!
251My wishes aim at none beside.

[Lifts her veil.]

Vittori

My wishes aim at none beside. Cassandra!
252We are rewarded. [Kisses her.] Had Vittori taken
253Into his body a thousand wounds, this kiss
254Had made me well again, or but one drop
255Of this rich balsam, for I know thy tears
256Are joy to see Vittori safe; the King,
257With all the glories of his province, cannot
258Do half this honor to his Admiral.
259I have a place above all happiness,
260And meet a greater empire in thy love
261Than fame or victory hath ever boasted.
262My own, my best Cassandra!

[Embraces her.]

Cassandra

My own, my best Cassandra! Call again
263That temper, which hath made Vittori honored,
264And if my tears – which carry something more
265Than joy to welcome home my best-loved lord –
266Affect you with no sadness, which I wish not,
267Yet look upon this mourning, not put on
268To counterfeit a grief, and that will tell you
269There is necessity for you to know
270Somewhat to check the current of your triumph.

Mauritio

271What prodigies are these?

Vittori

What prodigies are these? I was too careless
272Of this sad habit – joy to see thy face
273Made me distinguish nothing else. Proceed
274And punish my too prodigal embraces:
275It is not fit I be in one thought blest
276And thou in such a livery.

Cassandra

And thou in such a livery. When you say
277You have strength enough to entertain the knowledge
278Of such an injury.

Vittori

Of such an injury. If it only point
279At me, speak it at once, I am collected,
280Shalt see I will be conqueror at home;
281If it concern thyself, let it not flow
282Too fast, but rather let my ear receive it
283By such degrees as may not kill too soon
284But leave me some life only to revenge it.

Cassandra

285The Prince, whose cause engaged your war abroad,
286Hath ill rewarded you at home.

Vittori

Hath ill rewarded you at home. He cannot!

Cassandra

287Sir, in your absence I have suffered for you,
288Hourly solicited to my dishonor.

Vittori

Hourly solicited to my dishonor. Ha!

Cassandra

289For though he called it love, I might suspect it:
290His personal visits, messengers, rich presents
291Left me not quiet to enjoy myself.
292I told him I had given my faith already,
293Contracted yours; impatient of my answers,
294He urged his greatness, sware he would enjoy me
295Or be no prince in Naples; I am yet
296Preserved and welcome home my dearest safety.

Vittori

297The Prince do this!

Cassandra

The Prince do this! This is but half the story:
298By his command none dare salute your victory,
299Or pour their glad hearts forth at your return.
300To these he hath newly added the dishonor of
301Your father, whom he hath commanded close
302Prisoner i'th'castle upon some pretense
303Of treason. In my eyes you may behold
304How people shed their sorrow, as the guard
305Led him to prison, none so bold to ask
306The cause that made him suffer in his misery.

Vittori

307Will the King suffer this?

Cassandra

Will the King suffer this? Alas, his age
308Hath made him tame, a too indulgent father
309To such a son, whose will is all the law,
310Controlling what he pleases in this fall
311Of justice. Which way will Vittori take?

Vittori

312Mauritio, didst hear this? We must ask
313Forgiveness that we have been valiant,
314Repent our duties and that victory
315We bought so dear. We should have died at sea
316And then, perhaps, been talked on in the crowd
317Of honest men for giving up our lives,
318Which for our service they may now take from us.
319We are not yet i'th'snare, and we have power
320To stifle their designs and prevent our
321Dishonorable fall.

Mauritio

Dishonorable fall. The soldiers' hearts
322Are yours.

Vittori

Are yours. No, Mauritio, let 'em be the King's:
323If such as they forget their office, we
324Must keep our thoughts unstained, I'll to the King
325But without any train.

Mauritio

But without any train. In this, you do not
326Consult your safety.

Vittori

Consult your safety. Safety is a lecture
327To be read to children – I do always carry
328My own security within, Mauritio.
329Yet do not think I am desperate: I'll take
330No knowledge of the Prince's action
331But give account of my engagement, that's
332Not much amiss. The King, I know, is gracious,
333And the Prince too, however passion play
334This rebel in our soul.

Mauritio

This rebel in our soul. You shan't need, sir:
335The King is coming hither.

Vittori

The King is coming hither. And the Prince!
336Let's all look smooth; the King is come himself
337To gratulate our success. –

338The poor Vittori, who at your feet lays
339His heart and victory, and that which gave
340Him power to do you service.

King of Naples

Him power to do you service. We receive it
341And here discharge your soldiers, who shall tast
342Of our particular bounty.

Soldiers

Of our particular bounty. Heaven preserve the King!

Prince

343[To Fabio] Sirrah, did not I give strict charge
344That none should pass the gates? How came she hither?

Fabio

345"No man," an't like your grace, I did remember
346And durst not prevaricate in one syllable
347Of my commission: she is a lady, sir.

Prince

348You would be an officious hangman, I perceive;
349I'll find you understanding.

Vittori

I'll find you understanding. Let me prostrate
350My duty to your highness and be honored
351To kiss your hand.

Prince

To kiss your hand. Vittori, I'll not flatter,
352I have no grace for him, whose father durst
353Attempt an insolence upon my person
354Which the son may be guilty of in his blood.

Vittori

355My father insolent and I guilty, sir,
356Because I share his blood? Oh, that I knew
357In what part of my veins to find those drops,
358That I might sacrifice 'em to your anger
359And expiate my father's sin!

Prince

360I came not to expostulate.

Vittori

361Is this all my reward?

Prince

Is this all my reward? Your valor has
362Been paid in the success, what you have done
363Was duty, if you have not mixed our cause
364With private and particular revenge.

Vittori

365You speak not this to me, sir.

Prince

You speak not this to me, sir. Yes, to you!
366We do not fear the bugbears in your forehead.
367You will hear more.

Vittori

You will hear more. Sir, you have mercy in you.

King of Naples

368You have displeased our son, Vittori.

Vittori

369I? Witness the angels –

King of Naples

I? Witness the angels – I must tell you too
370Your father has transgressed beyond example.

Vittori

371Good heaven forgive him! Is this all,
372All my reward?

King of Naples

All my reward? What would you ask?

Vittori

373Ask? – Why, I ask my father.

King of Naples

Ask? – Why, I ask my father. Your father?

Vittori

374Goodness, leave me not the wonder
375Of all mankind. – Gentlemen – all gone!

Cassandra

376Alas, Vittori!

Vittori

Alas, Vittori! I, that commanded thousands
377This morning, am not owner of one servant.
378Dost thou stay with me?

Cassandra

Dost thou stay with me? My prophetic soul
379Knew this before.

[Re-]enter King [of Naples], Alberto [and] Fabio.

Vittori

Knew this before. The King returns, Cassandra.

King of Naples

380We've thought upon't, Vittori, and without
381The counsel of our son will condescend
382To your father's liberty. He is yours upon
383Condition: you and he, and this your mistress,
384Go into present banishment.

Vittori

385How! Banishment?

King of Naples

How! Banishment? I run my son's distaste –
386There is no time for study. He affects
387That lady: if you stay, something may follow
388To'th'general repentance. Troth, I pity thee.
389Here take our signet, time and absence may
390Correct all.

Exeunt King [of Naples, Alberto and Fabio].

Cassandra

Correct all. Oh, embrace it, dear Vittori!
391We shall meet safely everywhere but here.
392Enlarge your father, and we cannot miss
393A happier fate.

Vittori

A happier fate. Can my Cassandra think so?

395But these are strange turns, madam; Naples hath
