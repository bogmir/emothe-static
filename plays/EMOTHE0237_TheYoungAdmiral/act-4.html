Enter Rosinda [and] Cassandra.

Rosinda

1001But did the Prince affect thee so, Cassandra?

Cassandra

1002I have told you, madam, every circumstance;
1003I should but flatter my own misery
1004To speak it less. Misfortune had not made me
1005Your prisoner now if he had been more temperate.

Rosinda

1006But did thy heart allow him no affection?
1007Thou wert much unkind.

Cassandra

Thou wert much unkind. He had my duty, madam,
1008Which still I owe him as my prince, but I
1009Had but one faith, and that was given Vittori.
1010I fear I have displeased you.

Rosinda

I fear I have displeased you. No, thou hast not.
1011Dost think he loves thee still?

Cassandra

Dost think he loves thee still? I know not, madam,
1012But I hope not.

Rosinda

But I hope not. Would I could hope so too!
1013Thou hast deserved my confidence, and although
1014Thou canst not help me, I must tell thee all.
1015I love that Prince, loved when I first saw him,
1016And when he courted me, I thought 'twas necessary
1017To show I had a soft heart, but he flattered
1018And took too soon occasion of his absence.
1019The wounds he left upon Horatio
1020Were not so deep as mine, which howsoe'er
1021I have disguised yet from my father's eye,
1022Can find no cure without his surgery
1023That left them in my bosom; to this end
1024I urged my father to this war and begged
1025With many prayers to witness his revenge.

Cassandra

1026That was a desperate remedy. How if
1027Your father be o'ercome and you made prisoners?

Rosinda

1028We shall find death or ransom: the first would
1029Conclude my sufferings; th'other not much harm us,
1030Perhaps advance my ends. But if the victory
1031Should crown our army, I should interpose
1032To make conditions for the Prince; fate must
1033Decide one of these ways.

Cassandra

Decide one of these ways. Madam, I pity you.
1034Sure if the Prince knew with what constancy
1035Your love breathes after him, he would find a passion
1036To meet your noble flame.

Rosinda

To meet your noble flame. I know not whether
1037To pray for victory or to be conquered,
1038For till the wars conclude I must despair
1039To see whom my desires pursue.

Cassandra

To see whom my desires pursue. 'Tis possible
1040That you may see him, madam.

Rosinda

That you may see him, madam. When?

Cassandra

That you may see him, madam. When? This night,
1041And speak with him, without exposing your
1042Person to any danger.

Rosinda

Person to any danger. Prithee do not
1043Mock me, sweet friend.

Cassandra

Mock me, sweet friend. You were compassionate
1044Of me, and 'tis but duty I should answer it
1045With my desires to serve you. Not to hold
1046Your thoughts in expectation, is there any
1047Gentleman near whom you dare trust?

Rosinda

Gentleman near whom you dare trust? With what?

Cassandra

1048With carriage of a paper. I shall run
1049Some hazard, but there's nothing can weigh down
1050That goodness you have showed me, being a stranger.
1051I'll frame a letter, madam, in my name,
1052And by some charm of love invite him to
1053Your tent; if he retain part of that flame
1054Which did so command in him, be assured
1055The Prince will come.

Rosinda

The Prince will come. Thou wert create to make
1056Me blest, but with what safety can he reach
1057Thus far and not be known?

Cassandra

Thus far and not be known? He to whose trust
1058You give this secret shall remove that fear.

Rosinda

1059There is a captain –

Cassandra

There is a captain – Best of all.

Rosinda

There is a captain – Best of all. Fabrichio.

Cassandra

1060Send for him straight: if you allow this device,
1061I'll presently dispatch the amorous summons.

Rosinda

1062I'll call thee sister!

Cassandra

I'll call thee sister! Call me servant, madam;
1063In that I am honored.

Exit.

Enter Flavia disguised [as a hag].

Flavia

In that I am honored. Are you ready, madam?

Rosinda

For what?

Flavia

To laugh; I am turned enchantress, and now 'tis upon the minute, Pazzorello, by the boy's directions, comes for his magical armor.

Rosinda

I have something of more consequence to finish, but I may be at the end of your mirth.

Exit.

Flavia

Prosper in all your wishes.

Enter Didimo.

Didimo

Flavia! That's excellent, Hecate never looked so dreadfully. Where's the Princess?

Flavia

She commanded not to expect her, but she'll not be long absent. Where's the gamester?

Didimo

Almost within reach of your voice. You'll remember the circumstance, that he may be capable of the charm; he's mad to be enchanted.

Flavia

I warrant you; I have some furies to assist me too. Conduct him hither. [Exit Didimo.] If the fool after this conceiving himself bewitched should grow valiant and do wonders, who can help it? If he have but the wit to keep his own counsel, let him take his course. But he approaches.

[Re-]enter Pazzorello and Didimo.

Didimo

That is she.

Pazzorello

That old hag?

Didimo

Good words! She has come two hundred mile to day upon a distaff: salute her, she expects it.

Pazzorello

Would you have me kiss the devil?

Didimo

Do, I say. –

1064This is the gentleman, my loving aunt,
1065For whom I do beseech your powerful spells.

Flavia

1066To make him slick and shot-free?

Didimo

To make him slick and shot-free? Right, dear aunt.
1067He is a precious friend of mine, and one
1068That will be ready servant to your pleasures
1069At midnight or what hour you please to call him.

Pazzorello

Thou wouldst not ha' me lie with the old witch? What a generation of hobgoblins should we have together!

Didimo

1070Nor, for this benefit, shall you find him only
1071Obedient to yourself, but very dutiful
1072To any devil you have.

Flavia

To any devil you have. He is welcome, child.

Pazzorello

What a saltpeter breath she has!

Flavia

Where is Mephistopheles?

Pazzorello

No more devils, if you love me.

Flavia

I must have some to search him.

Pazzorello

Search me? Where? For what?

Didimo

How much was I overseen not to give you warning! Be not afraid; what have you about you?

Pazzorello

About me, where? In my breeches? What do you mean? I shall be cut for the stone.

Didimo

Have you any money about you?

Pazzorello

Yes, I have money of all complexions in my pocket.

Didimo

Away with it, as you love yourself! Not for your right hand have one piece of gold or silver about you, no charms can fasten on you then; her spells can have no power if you do not throw it away instantly – give me't, I'll keep it from her knowledge, this were a trick indeed –. Have you no goldfinches in your fob?

Pazzorello

I defy him that has anything in the likeness of coin.

Didimo

This is all money in your pocket, and come to be made shot-free!

Pazzorello

What must I do now?

Didimo

Kneel down and expect with obedience and admiration what will become on you. – [Pazzorello kneels and Didimo blindfolds him.] Great aunt, the gentleman is clear and ready. – You are sure you have no more impediment of this nature? If you dissemble and be killed afterward, thank yourself.

Flavia

Where be my spirits?

Didimo

He humbly desires you would finish him as privately as might be: he does not know the constitution of every devil, and to make too many acquainted, if he could be finished otherwise, your art may dispense.

Flavia

He must cut off his little finger, then.

Pazzorello

How! Cut off my finger?

Didimo

What did you mean? Here's a ring, a diamond.

Pazzorello

I had forgot it.

Didimo

No more, off with't if you love your hand! Here's a jest to fool away your life quickly; not for the world present it to her. – Great lady of the Laplanders, this gentleman implores your mercy to his joints, and offering this trifle, humbly prays you would honor him to wear it for his sake.

Flavia

Comes it freely off?

Pazzorello

It came off very hard, but I beseech your learned beldamship to accept it as a token of my duty.

Flavia

1073I do, and thus prepared, delay
1074My charms no longer. – "Come away,
1075You spirits that attend upon
1076This powerful incantation.
1077Have you brought that sacred juice
1078Which at such a time we use?
1079Distil it gently I command,
1080Holding his ears with either hand."

[Didimo pulls his ears and throughout the scene continues to play the part of the spirits in beating and kicking Pazzorello as Flavia directs.]

Pazzorello

Oh, my ears!

Didimo

The more pain she puts you to now, the less you'll feel hereafter, sir.

Flavia

1081"Now rub his temples, forehead eke,
1082Give his nose a gentle tweak.
1083Strike off paleness and bestow
1084On either cheek a lusty blow,
1085Take him by the hair and pull it;
1086Now his head's free from sword and bullet."

Pazzorello

What will they do with the rest of my body?

Flavia

1087"Grasp his neck till he groan twice."

Pazzorello

Oh, oh!

Flavia

1088"Enough, now let the young man rise,
1089Thus on his shoulders I dispense
1090My wand to keep all bullets thence
1091And other weapons that would harm.
1092Pinch him now on either arm,
1093fairy-like."

Pazzorello

Oh, pox o' the devil – oh!

Flavia

1094"On his breast give him a thump
1095And two kicks upon the rump.
1096No circumstance must be forgot
1097To make him free from stick and shot,
1098And now my potent charms are done.
1099This man is free from sword and gun."

Didimo

Bounce! You're made forever.

Flavia

1100"Farewell to both, for now must I
1101On my wingèd jennet fly. –
1102Suckle and Hoppo, fetch long strides
1103By your mistress as she rides.

Exit Flavia, [Pazzorello rises, and then Didimo unbinds his eyes].

Pazzorello

Whither is she gone now?

Didimo

Home to a witches' upsitting; she's there by this time.

Pazzorello

Where?

Didimo

In Lapland; she will cross the sea in an egg-shell, and upon land hath a thousand ways to convey herself in a minute: I did but whistle and she came to me.

Pazzorello

She knows your whistle, belike! Well, art thou sure I am enchanted now?

Didimo

It concerns you to be sure on't, and I must tell you one thing: if you make the least doubt on't, you'll endanger all; charms in this kind are nothing without the imagination, believe it, and if any sword or bullet have power to hurt you, ne'er trust your granam again.

Pazzorello

Nay, nay, I do believe it and will be valiant accordingly; they pinched and kicked me devilishly for all that.

Didimo

You're the better proof for't; you cannot be pinched or kicked too much in such a cause. What! To be made slick and shot-free? Now do I foresee you'll be captain within these three days; you cannot avoid it, sir, who will not honor that man whom the bullets are afraid of? [Re-]enter Rosinda, Cassandra [and] Fabrichio. The Princess.

Fabrichio

1104Repent your grace thought me a gentleman
1105If I fail in this duty.

Rosinda

If I fail in this duty. Not a syllable
1106Of me.

Fabrichio

Of me. I am charmed.

Cassandra

Of me. I am charmed. Happy success attend you!

Fabrichio

1107Your highness has much honored me, and, lady,
1108I kiss your fair hand.

Pazzorello

I kiss your fair hand. Captain, Captain, a word!

Fabrichio

I am in haste now.

Exit.

Pazzorello

Sure the Captain's afraid of me: he knows by instinct what I am.

Didimo

Your grace missed excellent mirth.

Rosinda

'Tis done then? Bid him follow us.

Exeunt [Rosinda and Cassandra].

Didimo

The Princess desires to speak with you.

Pazzorello

Desires to speak with me! – You have not told her?

Didimo

D'ye think I would betray you?

Pazzorello

Would somebody would challenge me to fight before her! If the ladies knew I were stick-free, they would tear me in pieces for my company.

Didimo

You do not know what you may get by your body that way; I attend you.

Pazzorello

1109Knives, daggers, swords, pikes, guns, both great and small,

Alphonso

1113Lay such a stain upon our family?
1114Speak it, my lord, no more: no private injury

1116He dares not fight their cause!

Alberto

1117The Prince hath cooled his resolution
1118By this time.

Alphonso

By this time. Ha? You are mystical.

1119Him word, the first attempt he makes against
1120The town, your head must answer it. I cannot
1121Believe howe'er particular wrongs inflame him
1122To a revenge, but he retains that piety
1123Which nature printed in him toward a father.

1126My life were profitably spent to save
1127Thy honor, which is great in the world's eye!
1128Time shall be grieved to have preserved thy name
1129So long, and when this blot shall be observed
1130Upon the last leaf of thy chronicle,
1131It shall unsettle quite the reader's faith
1132To all the former story.

Alberto

Julio

1133It was the King's command I should deliver –

Alphonso

1134What?

Julio

What? What must displease you:
1135You must prepare for death.

Alphonso

You must prepare for death. Has my son put
1136Rebellion into act already? That
1137Will save my executioner a labor;
1138He has, I read it. Look into the tombs
1139Of all our ancestors and see their ashes
1140Look paler than before: the marble sweats,
1141The ebony pillars that so many years

1143The genius of our house groans at this treason.
