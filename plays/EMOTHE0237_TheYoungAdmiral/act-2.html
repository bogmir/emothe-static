The Young Admiral

2[.1]

Enter King [of Naples], Prince, Alberto [and] Fabio.

King of Naples

398What's to be done?

Prince

What's to be done? Done! You've undone all,
399Betrayed the crown you wear! I see it tremble
400Upon your head – give such a license to
401A rebel! Trust him abroad to gather
402Strength to the kingdom's ruin!

King of Naples

Strength to the kingdom's ruin! What can such
403A naked man attempt to make us fear?

Prince

404He carries with him a whole army, sir,
405The people's love, who want no giddiness
406Had they but opportunity, and such
407A master rebel as Vittori to
408Make spoil of all. – Who counseled him to this?

Alberto

409Not I, an't please your grace: I wish it heartily
410Undone.

Prince

Undone. You wish it, sir? Are wishes now
411The remedy for such a mischief? You,
412When the state bleeds, will wish it well again;
413You're fine court surgeons! – [To the King] Had you stayed his father,
414It might have checked his treason, or Cassandra –

Alberto

415That's his torment.aparte

Prince

That's his torment. We had been secure.
416Exasperated now with his affront,
417As never traitor wanted impudence
418To blanch o'er his rebellion, he may inflame
419The neighbor princes to conspire some war
420For his revenge.

Fabio

For his revenge. His grace says right: there may
421Be a consequence of much danger, and Vittori
422Has fame abroad.

King of Naples

Has fame abroad. I did it for the best,
423By his absence thinking to remove his anger.
424I could have been content to have honored him
425For, to say truth, his services did challenge
426More friendly payment.

Fabio

More friendly payment. To say truth, he was
427A noble, valiant gentleman and deserved –

Prince

428What deserved he?

Fabio

What deserved he? A halter, an't shall please
429Your highness; I did wonder at your patience
430He was not put to death.

Prince

He was not put to death. I must acknowledge
431Vittori has deserved for many services
432The love and honor of his country, fought
433Their battles and brought conquest home, made tame
434The seas that threatened us, secured the land;
435And Rome allowed some consuls for less victories,
436Triumphs and statues.

Fabio

Triumphs and statues. Most excellent Prince!
437How just he is.

Prince

How just he is. But, when opinion
438Of their own merit swells 'em into pride,
439Which sets a price on that, which modesty
440Should count an act of their obedience,
441They forfeit the reward of thanks and honor
442And betray poor and most vainglorious souls.
443Scipio and Antony, and other Romans,
444Deserved well of the Senate and were honored;
445But, when they ran to faction and pursued
446Ambitious ends to undo their country's peace,
447They were no longer patriots but declared
448Rome's poison, and like gangrenes on the state
449To be cut off, lest they corrupt the body.

Fabio

450Was ever prince so wise!

King of Naples

Was ever prince so wise! But son, son, how
451Can these stains reach Vittori? He hath given
452No argument to suspect his fall from loyalty.

Prince

453I do not, sir, accuse him, nor did I,
454More than became the spirit of a prince,
455Show I was sensible of his father's impudence.
456If you remember, when I urged what trespass
457His father had committed he urged aloud,
458"Was this all my reward?", as if his service
459Were obligation to make us suffer
460And justify their affronts. But I waste breath,
461Since you are so well pleased; my duty, sir,
462Shall speak me still your son, but let me take
463Boldness to prophesy their insolence
464Struck at my person first, but you will find
465Their pride reach higher: I am but a branch
466Superfluous and may be pruned away.
467You have, you say, no argument to suspect
468His fall from loyalty; if what's done to me
469Be dead within you, yet remember now
470You have disengaged, by exile, his relation
471And tie of subject: he owes now no faith to you.
472What that, and his disgrace and opportunity
473Abroad may frame him to, I leave to imagine.

[Going.]

King of Naples

474Nay, prithee, come back, thou hast awaked me;
475I find my rashness; I did never think
476There had been so much danger. We will study
477Timely prevention, let 'em be called back,
478Fly after 'em, and in our name command –

Prince

479You sha' not need.

King of Naples

You sha' not need. How "sha' not need"?

Prince

You sha' not need. How "sha' not need"? Your pardon.
480In hope your wisdom would allow it after,
481I have made that my act: Julio is gone
482With strict commission for that purpose.

King of Naples

With strict commission for that purpose. Julio?
483I thank thy care.

Fabio

484'Twas most divinely thought on, most maturely.
485Now all your jealousies are laid.

Prince

Now all your jealousies are laid. [To the King] I shall
486Compose myself at his return to wear
487What countenance you will direct.

King of Naples

What countenance you will direct. Cassandra
488You've sent for too?

Prince

You've sent for too? By any means, she is
489So precious to Vittori. Had she sinned
490Alone to merit banishment, he would follow her
491Through all the world.

King of Naples

Through all the world. Women are strangely attractive;
492Fame speaks her virtuous too.

Prince

Fame speaks her virtuous too. Some virtue she has –

Enter Julio[, with] Alphonso guarded.

493Julio has prospered. – [To Julio] Thou'st done good service. –
494Alphonso, though your late affront to us
495Be foul in its own nature and may encourage
496Others by your impunity, yet we have
497With the remembrance of your former actions
498Lost your offense. Vittori too shall find
499The honor he deserves.

Alphonso

The honor he deserves. How's this?

Prince

The honor he deserves. How's this? Where is he?
500He does not scorn our mercy! – Julio,
501Where is Cassandra?

Julio

502Shipped with Vittori, thank Alphonso for't,
503Whom you have pardoned; they are both at sea.

Prince

504Whirlwinds pursue 'em!

King of Naples

Whirlwinds pursue 'em! Where's your son, Alphonso?

Alphonso

505Embarked with his fair mistress. – [To the Prince] I observe,
506My lord, which way your anger moves; in vain
507You vex your soul for them. The sea's no part
508Of your command, the winds are masters there,
509Which cannot raise a storm so black and ominous
510As their own country.

Prince

As their own country. By what means escaped they?

Alphonso

511Take it from me, and after cut my head off.
512I charged him, as his heart wished to enjoy
513A father's blessing as he loved the honor
514Of his Cassandra, fearing some new plot
515To hire a bark and quickly put to sea,
516Whilst I made some stay to dispose affairs
517That might befriend us in another country.
518He did obey and had my prayers, the winds
519Conveyed him swiftly from the shore, and had
520Your creature, Julio, not made such haste,
521I had dispatched and in another vessel
522Followed his ship, but heaven determined I
523Should be again your prisoner. Use your power,
524But look to give account for every hair
525Of this old head, now withered in your service.

Prince

526To the castle with him!

Alphonso

To the castle with him! Ay, there's the King. –
527[To the King] Let me use one word more, royal sir, to you.

Prince

528You'll hear him?

Alphonso

You'll hear him? Fear not, Prince, my soul's not fall’n
529So low to beg compassion.

King of Naples

So low to beg compassion. Speak, Alphonso!

Alphonso

530My duty still preserved, I would advise
531Your age to quit the trouble of your kingdom
532And ask the Prince's leave to turn a Capuchin.
533Why should you stoop with burden o'such a state
534And have a son so active? Turn friar, my lord,
535And make the youngman king.

Prince

And make the youngman king. I must endure.

King of Naples

536Away with him!

Fabio

Away with him! I'll see him safe, my lord.

[Exit with Alphonso and guard.]

Enter a Messenger.

Prince

537What hasty news with you?

Messenger

538To arms, great sir, for your defense! There are
539New dangers from the sea.

King of Naples

New dangers from the sea. Another fleet?

Messenger

540And sailing this way, we suspect they are
541Sicilians.

King of Naples

542Vittori gave a blow to their design.

Prince

543D'ye but suspect it, villain?

Julio

D'ye but suspect it, villain? It may be
544Some scattered ships.

Prince

Some scattered ships. Has not Vittori mocked us
545And played the villain with your trust?

King of Naples

546They could not be reinforced so soon. – [To the Messenger] What number?

Messenger

547They cover, sir, the seas.

Prince

They cover, sir, the seas. Gather up forces to
548Prevent the landing.

Messenger

Prevent the landing. 'Tis imposible!
549They touch our shore by this time.

King of Naples

They touch our shore by this time. Then make safe
550The city.

Alberto

551It may be another fleet, meant to relieve
552The first and came not forth so soon.

King of Naples

553Now we want Vittori.

Exit.

Prince

554All the diseases Naples ever groaned with
555O'ertake Vittori! But Alphonso shall
556Pay dearly for this mischief.

Julio

Pay dearly for this mischief. Be not, sir,
557Dejected; 'tis more easy to defend
558At home than thrive in foreign war. These men
559Will find as proud resistance.

Prince

Will find as proud resistance. Canst thou think
560I do look pale for this? No, Julio,
561Although the sudden news might move me somewhat,
562I have a heart above all fear and can
563Know no distraction but Cassandra's absence;
564That makes me look so wild and tears my brain
565With the imagination.

Julio

With the imagination. But the state
566We are in requires you should be active, sir.

Prince

567Ah, Julio, the armies which I fear
568Are not abroad, they have made entrenchment here.

Exeunt.

[2.2]

A shout within[, then] enter the King of Sicily, Horatio, Trivulsi [and] Fabrichio.

Horatio

569Though Naples do not bid you welcome, sir,
570Ashore, the joys and duties of your subjects
571Cannot be silent.

King of Sicily

Cannot be silent. We do thank you all.
572The seas were kind, and the winds kissed our sails.
573All things conspired to our revenge.

Trivulsi

All things conspired to our revenge. Your justice
574Our very enemies acknowledge it
575And, conscious of their injury, are afraid
576To look upon us.

King of Sicily

To look upon us. Marshall of the field,
577Give present order for entrenchments,
578We'll quarter here; you shall make good that part
579With your horse troops and plant cannons on that hill
580To play upon the town. Naples shall find
581We did not venture all upon one stake;
582That petty loss at sea which made them triumph
583And, perhaps, careless of more opposition
584Shall dearly be accounted for, beside
585Dishonoring our daughter and our court
586By such a rude departure.

Horatio

By such a rude departure. As they had
587Scorned your alliance.

King of Sicily

Scorned your alliance. Thy particular
588Suff'rings, Horatio, and wounds are put
589Into the scale.

Horatio

Into the scale. They are not worthy, sir.
590Had his sword reached my heart, my death had been
591No sin compared to that affront he threw
592Upon yourself and daughter. I was bound
593To engage that blood was given me to serve you,
594And I do love those drops that, in a cause
595So just, made haste to show their duty to you
596Better than those that dwell within my heart.

King of Sicily

597We are confident of thy loyalty.

Enter Rosinda and Flavia.

Trivulsi

We are confident of thy loyalty. The Princess.

King of Sicily

598Alas, Rosinda! Thou were not bred to these
599Tumults and noise of war. Has not the sea
600Impaired thy health? I was too rash to allow
601Thy travail and expose thy tenderness
602To this rude voyage.

Rosinda

To this rude voyage. It appears to me
603A pleasant change of air. I have heard men talk
604Of many horrors that attend the seas,
605Of tempests and of dangers; I have seen
606Nothing to fright me. If the waves put on
607No other shape, I could exchange methinks
608My dwelling on the land.

Horatio

My dwelling on the land. We owe this happiness
609To you, fair Princess, for whose safer passage
610The breath of heaven did gently swell our sails.
611The waves were proud to bear so rich a lading
612And danced to th'music of the winds.

Rosinda

And danced to th'music of the winds. You show
613Your complement, my lord. Call you this Naples?

King of Sicily

614The kingdom of our enemy, which shall
615Groan for the inhabitants. Are all our forces
616Landed?

Trivulsi

Landed? Safe to your wishes and expect
617What they shall be commanded.

King of Sicily

What they shall be commanded. We must first
618Secure the ground we have: being defensed
619With works, we may prevent their sallies and
620Assault to our best advantage. – Still preserve
621Thy courage, my Rosinda; 'tis for thee
622We have adventured hither.

Rosinda

We have adventured hither. And you have
623Been kind to the petition of your daughter,
624Who can in duty wait upon your fortune.
625At home, I should have withered in your absence;
626I shall grow valiant here.

King of Sicily

I shall grow valiant here. My dearest child,
627Whose very eyes do kindle flames of courage
628In every soldier, be still safe and promise
629Thyself a brave revenge.

[Exeunt all but Rosinda and Flavia.]

Flavia

630What will become of us, madam?

Rosinda

631We must take our fortunes; I am sorry
632For thee.

Flavia

For thee. You have some reason for yourself.
633If any danger follow, I know where
634To place the cause, but I dare suffer with
635Your grace.

Enter Pazzorello and Didimo.

Your grace. Pazzorello, madam, and the page.

Rosinda

636He's come in good time to relieve our thoughts.

Pazzorello

Madam.

Didimo

Come, pluck up a good heart!

Pazzorello

'Tis coming out as fast as it can. Sweet Didimo, hold my head.

Didimo

Come, 'tis but a little seasickness.

Pazzorello

"Seasickness," quotha? – A vengeance of all drunken voyages, I can do nothing but –

Rosinda

How now, Pazzorello?

Pazzorello

O madam, never did man cast up so much and had so little skill in arithmetic! Nothing grieves me, but that I have not drunk for't. I have a perpetual motion in my belly, the four winds are together by the ears in my small guts; would I had never known the sea! Little did I think – Oh –

Flavia

Thou art a freshwater soldier.

Pazzorello

Freshwater? I know not. I'll be judged by the whole ship if I was not in a sweet pickle.

Rosinda

The worst is past, this is but physic.

Pazzorello

If I had thought the sea would have given me so many vomits, I would have seen it burned ere I would ha' ventured so far! I ha' purged both ways; an the enemy had met us before we landed, I should have scoured some on 'em.

Didimo

How do you now?

Pazzorello

The fit is not so violent altogether, o'shipboard I ran a tilt; howsoever, I beseech your grace that I may go home again.

Rosinda

There is no way by land.

Didimo

And a little more jogging at sea –

Pazzorello

The very word "sea" boils in my stomach and will make my mouth run over presently – Ho! It comes, it comes!

Exit.

Didimo

Madam, I have a great desire to attend him. I have cast a plot to make your highness merry.

Rosinda

You'll play the wag with him; we'll trust you to pursue it.

Didimo

I humbly thank your grace.

Exit.

Rosinda

637Flavia, does not the day look black o'th'sudden?

Flavia

638It has not the same complexion. I hear
639A noise too.

[Storm, with thunder and lightning.]

Rosinda

A noise too. From the sea, it grows loud.

Flavia

640'Tis well we are ashore. O me! I tremble
641To think what would be come on's an we had
642Not been afore this tempest; I thank providence.
643I was upon the sea once in a storm,
644But they use to clap the women under hatches;
645I never prayed so in my life.

[Re-]enter King [of Sicily,] Horatio, Trivulsi [and] Fabrichio.

I never prayed so in my life. The King!

King of Sicily

646I know not what to think: no sooner landed
647But such a storm pursue us! Does not this
648Affright Rosinda into paleness? – Dost
649Not feel an ague?

Rosinda

Not feel an ague? I have rather cause,
650Sir, to rejoice: it overtook us not
651Upon the sea, the fury of it there
652Might have been fatal.

Horatio

Might have been fatal. Be not troubled, sir,
653My soul doth from this omen prophesy
654The victory you wish upon this kingdom,
655Nor is it superstition to believe
656That heaven doth point us out the scourge to Naples
657By seconding our coming with a tempest.
658The waves were proud to entertain our navy,
659The fish in amorous courtship danced about
660Our ship, and no rude gale from any coast
661Was sent to hang upon our linen wings
662To interrupt our wishes. Not a star
663Muffled his brightness in a sullen cloud
664Till we arrived, and then observe how heaven
665Threatens the fall of this proud enemy
666By this prodigious tempest, which but gives
667Them warning of a greater.

King of Sicily

Them warning of a greater. We are confident
668Thou hast happily expounded. What lightning
669Darts from those angry exhalations!

Horatio

670It speaks the flame of our revenge.

King of Sicily

It speaks the flame of our revenge. What thunder!

Horatio

671The loudness of our cannon; let their fears
672Apply it and run mad with apprehension.

Trivulsi

673Our ships must needs fall foul on one another,
674Riding i'th'haven.

Horatio

Riding i'th'haven. Let 'em crack their ribs,
675We have the more necessity to tug for't.

King of Sicily

676Yet would thou wert at home!

Rosinda

Yet would thou wert at home! Fear not for me, sir,
677Your absence would present my imagination
678With more affliction. I suffer less
679In knowledge and shall rise by brave examples,
680Valiant above my sex; these horrors fright
681Not me.

King of Sicily

Not me. This fire will quicken the whole army –

[Enter] soldiers, pursued by Vittori [with his sword drawn, bearing] Cassandra half dead under his arm.

682What mutiny is here?

Vittori

What mutiny is here? Base villains, to take part
683With all the malice of the world against me!

King of Sicily

684What are you?

Vittori

What are you? I am a gentleman, and dare
685Rather than suffer a rude hand divorce
686This burden from my arms: defy you all.
687Alas, she will be gone! O my Cassandra!
688Thy soul sha'not forsake thee thus, I'll take it
689In with a kiss.

Trivulsi

In with a kiss. Some whom the wreck has cast
690Upon the shore.

Rosinda

Upon the shore. Pity the gentlewoman.

Vittori

691Come not too near: the man that first attempts
692This lady had better rip his mother's womb.

King of Sicily

693Whence are you?

Vittori

Whence are you? You are strangers I perceive;
694Then I presume to tell you I have more justice
695To tread upon this earth than you or any
696The proudest. It once gave us birth, and fate,
697Ungentle fate, hath sent us back to die here,
698But I will not outlive my dear Cassandra.

King of Sicily

699Do you delight in wounds? Resign that lady.

Vittori

700Not while my hand can manage this; the blood
701You take will make us walk on even pace
702To death, and when my soul can stay no longer,
703I'll leave a curse to blast you. But if you
704Bear hearts of flesh about you and will promise
705A pity to this poor departing spirit,
706I will not use a sword but give my life
707To be commanded from me at your pleasure:
708Your care will come too late.

King of Sicily

Your care will come too late. I promise by
709The word and honor of a King she shall
710Be carefully attended.

Vittori

Be carefully attended. Though that name
711Breed wonder in me, it secures all thoughts
712That may concern her safety.

[Resigns Cassandra to the attendants.]

King of Sicily

That may concern her safety. See, Rosinda,
713With as much diligence to this lady's health
714As you'd preserve your own.

Horatio

As you'd preserve your own. An excellent creature!

King of Sicily

715My faith is past.
716Now if you please, you may acquaint us with
717Your name and quality.

Vittori

Your name and quality. Something on the suddenaparte
718Weighs my heart lower: I ha' not power to thank him.aparte

King of Sicily

719Already you have expressed yourself this countryman.
720Be more particular.

Vittori

Be more particular. My name's Vittori.

King of Sicily, Horatio

721The Admiral of Naples?

Vittori

The Admiral of Naples? It was a title
722I had too late and lost it for my service.
723I cannot conjure up the dead to witness;
724There be some living that remember me.
725It was my chance to have the best at sea
726Against the bold Sicilian.

King of Sicily

Against the bold Sicilian. A chance say'st?

Vittori

727Few victories can boast more: all is but
728The die of war, which valor must obey.
729My lot was to bring peace and triumph home,
730And my reward was banishment; the sea
731Held me a sinful burden to the waves,
732Or else the blood I shed to mix with 'em
733In anger and revenge conspired to throw
734Our bark, with the distressed lading back
735Upon this flinty bosom of our country.
736You have at full my misery; be just
737To that poor lady, whatsoe'er I suffer.

King of Sicily

738Your fame was with us earlier. – Entertain him.

[Soldiers] disarm Vittori.

739You are welcome, man; there's cause we should
740Be kind to you.

Vittori

Be kind to you. Will a King stain his honor?

King of Sicily

741Know, miserable man, thy destinies
742Have made thee his, that will exact severe
743Most happy storm!aparte
744Thy master too shall find a punishment
745How fortunate we are!aparte

Vittori

746I ask no mercy for myself: be kind
747To that poor lady, as you're a prince, and I
748Will kiss my fate.

King of Sicily

749We violate no promise made to her,
750Blest heavens!aparte
751You shall pay dear for all.aparte

Vittori

You shall pay dear for all. O my Cassandra,
752When at the expense of all my blood I have bought
753Thy precious life from these hard-hearted men!
754Shed one tear on me, and I am paid again.

Exeunt.
