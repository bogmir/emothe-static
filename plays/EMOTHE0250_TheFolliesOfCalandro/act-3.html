Bernardo Dovizi da Bibbiena

The Follies of Calandro

← Act II Act IV →

Act III

Scene I

Fessenio, alone

Fessenio

455Ladies and gentlemen, we have here the spoils of love: whoever wants to acquire politeness, insight, and knowledge need only buy these clothes and wear them awhile. They belong to Calandro, who took them off when he got into the trunk –a man so clever that he has fallen in love with another man, believing him a woman, and so powerful that he can die and come back to life as he chooses. Let whoever wants to buy them show the color of his money: I can sell them with a clear conscience as they belong to a dead man. Ha, ha! And now Lidio, dressed as a woman, is waiting merrily for this handsome lover, who, to tell truth, is uglier than Bramante himself. I ran ahead to meet the Prostitute that I made an appointment with; she is going to cooperate. Here she is now, and there is the porter with the trunk, which he believes is full of precious merchandise: he doesn’t know that it contains the vilest stuff on earth. Doesn’t anybody want these clothes? No? Well, good-bye then. Be of good cheer: I am going to lead the gelding to the mare.

Scene II

A Prostitute, Fessenio, a porter, two or three customs inspectors, Calandro

Prostitute

456Here I am, Fessenio. Let’s go.

Fessenio

457Let the trunk go first. No, porter, not that way. Over here.

Prostitute

458What’s inside?

Fessenio

459Something for you, pettikins.

Prostitute

460What is it?

Fessenio

461Silks and fine clothes.

Prostitute

462Whose are they?

Fessenio

463They belong to the man you are going to entertain, my dear.

Prostitute

464Oh, will he give some of them to me?

Fessenio

465Yes, if you do what I said.

Prostitute

466I will. Don’t you worry.

Fessenio

467Remember that your name is Santilla, and everything else I told you.

Prostitute

468Leave everything to me.

Fessenio

469Otherwise you won’t get a penny.

Prostitute

470I say don’t worry. What do these customs inspectors want with the porter?

Fessenio

471Quiet! And listen.

Prostitute

472Tell me, what’s inside that trunk?

Porter

473Why, what’s the matter?

Inspector

474Have you passed the customs?

Porter

475No, sir.

Inspector

476What’s inside?

Porter

477I don’t know.

Inspector

478Better tell the truth.

Porter

479They told me it was silk. Dresses and such as that.

Inspector

480Silk?

Porter

481Yes, sir.

Inspector

482Is it locked?

Porter

483I don’t think so.

Inspector

484Set in down.

Porter

485Ah no, sir.

Inspector

486Set in down, stupid. Do you want me to hit you?

Porter

487Oh, oh! Our plan is spoiled. He is going to find out everything, and we shall all be ruined.

Prostitute

488What’s the matter?

Fessenio

489Our plain is spoiled. (To Prostitute) Sofilla, you must help me.

Prostitute

490What can I do?

Fessenio

491Cry, shout, tear your hair, as if you were mourning for someone.

Prostitute

492But why?

Fessenio

493You will soon find out.

Prostitute

494(weeping loudly) Boohoo! Boohoo!

Inspector

495What’s this? Somebody dead?

Fessenio

496(approaching, to Inspector) What are you looking for?

Inspector

497The porter told us this trunk contained dutiable merchandise, instead of which it seems to contain a corpse.

Fessenio

498That’s right, It does.

Inspector

499Who is it?

Fessenio

500It’s this poor woman’s husband. Don’t you see how hard she is taking it?

Inspector

501Why are you carrying him in a trunk?

Fessenio

502To fool the police, if you must know.

Inspector

503To fool the police? Why?

Fessenio

504Because no one would have anything to do with us if they found out the truth.

Inspector

505What is the truth?

Fessenio

506This man in dead of the plague.

Inspector

507The plague! Oh, and I touched it!

Fessenio

508To your harm, I fear.

Inspector

509Where are you taking him?

Fessenio

510Someplace we can bury him, or drop him in the river.

Calandro

511(sitting up in trunk) You want to drown me, do you? But I’m not dead, you rascals. I’m not dead, I say!

Fessenio

512Oh, everyone is running away, and no wonder: this is the work of the devil. Run, Sofilla! Run, porter! Run, everybody!

Scene III

Calandro, Fessenio

Calandro

513(emerging from trunk, striking Fessenio) So you wanted to drown me, did you, you wretch?

Fessenio

514Why, master, what’s the matter? Why do you want to hit me?

Calandro

515You want to know why, do you?

Fessenio

516Yes, sir.

Calandro

517Because you deserve it, you scoundrel. That’s why.

Fessenio

518This is what comes of trying to do a good deed. Are you angry with me because I have saved your life?

Calandro

519And how did you save mi life, may I ask?

Fessenio

520Why, I kept you from going to the customhouse, didn’t I?

Calandro

521And suppose I had gone there, what would have happened?

Fessenio

522What would have happened? I should have let them carry you there –then you’d have seen.

Calandro

523What the devil do you mean?

Fessenio

524You act as if you were born yesterday. You were being smuggled: they would have sold you just as they do everything that’s smuggled.

Calandro

525Well, then, you did right after all. Forgive me, Fessenio.

Fessenio

526(arranging his clothes, which Calandro has rumpled) Next time I hope you think twice before you get angry. (Aside) I’ll get even with him for this.

Calandro

527I will. But tell me, who was that ugly-looking woman who ran away just now?

Fessenio

528Ah, don’t you know her?

Calandro

529No.

Fessenio

530That was death, who was with you in the trunk.

Calandro

531With me? What do you mean?

Fessenio

532Just that. With you.

Calandro

533I didn’t see her there.

Fessenio

534You don’t see sleep either, do you, when you are sleeping? Or thirst when you drink, or hunger when you eat? You don’t see life either, for that matter, although you are alive. But it is with you just the same.

Calandro

535It’s true I have never seen these things.

Fessenio

536Exactly. And when you die, you don’t see death.

Calandro

537H’m. Why did the porter run away?

Fessenio

538He was afraid of death. So I’m afraid you won’t be able to see Santilla today.

Calandro

539Then I might as well be dead.

Fessenio

540I don’t know how I can arrange it now –unless you are willing to do a little work.

Calandro

541Fessenio, I’ll do anything to see her. I’ll even go to bed barefoot.

Fessenio

542Oh, no, nothing like that.

Calandro

543What, then?

Fessenio

544You’ll have to be the porter yourself. Nobody will be able to tell the difference: you’ve already taken for a short time, your face has changed somewhat, too. I’ll pretend I’m the carpenter who made the trunk. Don’t worry about Santilla. She’ll put two and two together immediately: she’s a regular sibyl.

Calandro

545A good idea. I would do anything to gain Santilla’s love.

Fessenio

546(aside) Did you ever see such an anxious lover? (Calandro lifts the trunk with difficulty.) Look out, you’re going to drop it! There, that’s it. Steady now. Are you sure you’re all right?

Calandro

547(groaning) Fine.

Fessenio

548Well then, carry it to her house. I’ll follow and meet you at the door. (Exit Calandro.) That jackass’s load is becoming to him. Now I have to find the Prostitute and lead her into the house by the back door. I’m afraid Lidio may have to let Calandro kiss him, but no matter –Fulvia’s kisses will then seem all the sweeter. Here comes Samia. She didn’t see Calandro talking with me –fortunatley.

Scene IV

Fessenio, Samia

Fessenio

549Where have you been?

Samia

550To Ruffo the magician. My mistress sent me.

Fessenio

551What did he say?

Samia

552That he was coming to see her.

Fessenio

553My, my, women are so silly. Well, I have to go find Lidio and tell him what your mistress asked me to.

Samia

554Is he at home?

Fessenio

555Yes.

Samia

556What do you think he will do?

Fessenio

557Frankly, I’m not too hopeful. But I can’t say for sure.

Samia

558Things certainly don’t seem to be turning out very well for my mistress and me. Well, good-bye.

Fessenio

559Good-bye.

Scene V

Samia, Fulvia

Samia

560My mistress is not going to like this. Neither Lidio nor Ruffo’s familiar spirit has given her much encouragement.

Fulvia

561Why were you gone so long?

Samia

562I wasn’t able to find Ruffo until just now.

Fulvia

563What does he say?

Samia

564That the spirit answered him... how was it? I don’t remember.

Fulvia

565You goose-brain, you’d better remember!

Samia

566Oh, yes, now it is coming back to me. He said he made an ambiguous reply.

Fulvia

567You mean ambiguous?

Samia

568Yes, that’s it. Ambiguous.

Fulvia

569He said nothing else?

Samia

570That he was going to try again.

Fulvia

571Anything else?

Samia

572That he was at your service and that as soon as he had anything good to report, he would come here right away.

Fulvia

573Ah, me! That’s not so good. But what about Lidio?

Samia

574He thinks about as much of you as he would a pair of old shoes.

Fulvia

575Did you find him?

Samia

576Yes, and talked with him, too.

Fulvia

577Well, what did he say?

Samia

578You won’t like it.

Fulvia

579Ah, me! Tell me anyway.

Samia

580He pretends he never met you.

Fulvia

581What are you saying?

Samia

582That’s the way it is.

Fulvia

583How could you have understood him to say such a thing?

Samia

584He spoke to me in a way that frightened me.

Fulvia

585Maybe he was joking with you.

Samia

586No, my lady. He was insulting.

Fulvia

587Maybe you didn’t talk to him right.

Samia

588Bo, my lady. You should never have sent me.

Fulvia

589Maybe someone was with him, eh?

Samia

590I took him aside.

Fulvia

591Maybe you talked too loud.

Samia

592I whispered into his ear.

Fulvia

593But what did he say?

Samia

594He drove me away.

Fulvia

595He doesn’t love me anymore, then. Is that it?

Samia

596No, nor respect you either.

Fulvia

597You really think so?

Samia

598I know it.

Fulvia

599Alas, can I believe my own ears?

Samia

600You can, my lady.

Fulvia

601He didn’t ask you how I was.

Samia

602He said he never heard of you.

Fulvia

603So he’s forgotten me, has he?

Samia

604You’re lucky that he doesn’t hate you.

Fulvia

605How unhappy I am! I know now for certain that Lidio is unfaithful. How sad is the destiny of women! And how few women find lovers who are completely satisfactory! Poor me, I loved too deeply and gave so much of myself that I am no longer my own mistress! Cruel heavens, why don’t you make Lidio love me as I love him, or let me run away from him as he runs away from me? But what am I thinking of? –Not to love Lidio, and to run away from him? Ah, no, I can’t do that, nor do I want to. On the contrary, I am going to seek him out. Why, since he has come to me so often in women’s clothes, shouldn’t I, just this once, try going to him dressed like a man? It’s reasonable enough, and he might even appreciate my going to such trouble on his account. Why don’t I do it then? I there any reason why I should pine away like this? There is no pain like that of a woman who has wasted her youth, and whoever thinks that once it’s gone you can get it back is mistaken. When will I ever find another Lidio? A man like that is worth any amount of trouble. And now is the right time, because he’s at home and my husband is away at the farm. Who dares to stop me then? My mind is made up; I shall do it. I didn’t think that magician was going to do much good anyway. The best way to get something done is to do it yourself. Others do it at their own convenience and never take the same interest. If I go to him myself, he will see my tears, hear my sobs, and listen to my prayers: I shall throw myself at his feet, hug him around the neck, and pretend to die. No one could be so cruel as not to take pity on me then. No one hearing words of love spoken straight from the heart could possibly be indifferent to them, and in love all things are possible. Therefore I am going to dress myself up like a man and go to him. Samia, you stay here at the door and don’t let anyone linger in front of the house: if somebody sees me coming out, they might recognize me.

Scene VI

Samia, Fulvia

Samia

606How unhappy we poor women are! When we fall in love, we are done for. Fulvia, who was always so prudent, doesn’t know what she’s doing now that she’s in love with Lidio. Since hw won’t come to her, she has decided to go to him dressed like a man, never thinking of what might happen if she were discovered. Well, she ought to be satisfied now: she’s given him everything, including her honour and her body, and he thinks no more of her than of the dirt under his feet. Our sex is truly to be pitied. Here she is already, rigged out in men’s clothes. It didn’t take her long.

Fulvia

607Well, Samia, I am of to find Lidio. You stay here and keep the door locked until I come back. Do you understand?

Samia

608Yes, my lady. But do be careful.

Scene VII

Fulvia, alone

Fulvia

609Love can make a person do anything. I used to be so timid I was afraid to leave my room alone, and now, moved by love, I don’t even hesitate to leave the house alone in men’s clothing. I am going straight to Lidio’s house, though it’s a good distance, and I’ll make him listen to me. No one will be there except the old woman who waits on him and maybe Fessenio, who knows everything anyway. No one will recognize me; I don’t have anything to worry about. And even be sorry than not to act and be sorry; nothing ventured, nothing gained.

Scene VIII

Samia, alone

Samia

610She is gone to seek her pleasure. I was wrong to blame her, for he who is indifferent to love is no better than a beast of the field. I am never so happy myself as when I am with my lover Lusco, who is waiting for me now in the courtyard. We’ll have the whole house to ourselves. I’ll follow my mistress’ example: a person’s a fool not to take his pleasure when the opportunity comes along. (Calling.) Lusco! Oh, Lusco!

Scene IX

Fessenio

Fessenio

611Calandro is with the Prostitute now; they’ll have some pretty secrets between them. I must tell Fulvia what’s going on –she will die laughing. And no wonder! It’s enough to make a dead man laugh. (Approaches Calandro’s house, knocks.) H’m. The door seems to be locked. Hello there! Anybody home?

Scene X

Fessenio outside Calandro’s house, Samia inside

Fessenio

612(knocking) Anybody there? Can’t you hear me? Open up!

Samia

613Who’s knocking?

Fessenio

614It’s me, Fessenio. Let me come in, Samia.

Samia

615Wait a minute.

Fessenio

616What’s the matter? Why don’t you open the door?

Samia

617I’m trying to put the key in the lock.

Fessenio

618Haven’t you got in yet?

Samia

619I’m trying. Oh, it’s no use. I can’t do it.

Fessenio

620Why not?

Samia

621The hole is stopped up.

Fessenio

622Blow on the key.

Samia

623I’m doing the best I can.

Fessenio

624Why are you taking so long?

Samia

625I have it nice and wer now; it ought to do the trick.

Fessenio

626Well, hurry up.

Samia

627There, you hear? It’s unlocked now; you can come in.

Fessenio

628Why do you have everything locked up like this?

Samia

629My mistress’ orders. She wanted the door locked.

Fessenio

630Why?

Samia

631With you I can talk freely. She has gone to Lidio dressed like a man.

Fessenio

632Samia! You don’t mean it?

Samia

633I do indeed. I’m supposed to stay here and keep everything locked up until she comes back.

Scene XI

Fessenio, alone

Fessenio

634Truly there is nothing too dangerous or too foolish for a lover to attempt: here Fulvia has gone to Lidio’s house, not knowing that her husband is also there. He is bound to believe the worst, seeing her there alone and dressed like a man. He’ll be so furious he may notify her relatives. I’d better hurry to see if there is something I can do. Oh, heavens, here comes Fulvia now, and she has Calandro with her, leading him by the collar. I’ll step aside and listen; that away I’ll find out what has happened.

Scene XII

Fulvia, Calandro

Fulvia

635So you were going to the farm, were you? You miserable creature, don’t you have enough to do at home that you go prowling around like this? And to think that this is the man I have given my love to and remained faithful to all these years! Now I know why you have been keeping away from me the last few night: you’ve been saving your energy. I don’t know how I keep myself from tearing your eyes out. Did you think you could pull the wool over my eyes so easily? I heard about it before this, and that’s why I dressed myself up like this –to find out for myself if it was true. And now I’m leading you by the collar, like the dirty dog you are, so that all the world can see and take pity on me who have had to put up with your outrages. Do you suppose that if I were like you, and didn’t care what I did, I couldn’t have a good time, too? I’m not as old or as ugly as all that. And it’s only because I have some self-respect that I didn’t revenge myself on that woman I found you with. But I’m not finished yet: I’m going to get even with you both.

Calandro

636Have you finished?

Fulvia

637Yes.

Calandro

638Than, damn you, I’m the one who ought to be angry. I was enjoying a paradise on earth when you came along and spoiled everything. You are not worth one of her old shoes, for at least she knows how to make love. Her lips are sweeter than wine, she shines brighter than Diana, and she has the wisdom of a Fata Morgana. And if you do her harm, you had better watch out.

Fulvia

639That’s enough out of you. Inside the house! Inside, I say!

Scene XIII

Fessenio, alone

Fessenio

640Well, well, live and learn. O love, how great is your power! What poet, what doctor, what philosopher can hope to rival you as teacher? All knowledge, all doctrine is secondary to yours. What else but love would have enabled Fulvia, in so tight a spot, to act so intelligently? I never saw anything like it. There she is over there, standing in her doorway. I’ll say something encouraging to her about Lidio –that’ll cheer her up. Poor thing! I feel sorry for her.

Scene XIV

Fulvia, Fessenio, Samia

Fulvia

641Ah, Fessenio, see how unlucky I’ve been –to find instead of Lidio, that wretched husband of mine. But I got out of it all right.

Fessenio

642I know; I heard everything. Don’t show yourself too plainly –somebody might see you in those clothes. There, that’s better.

Fulvia

643You are right. I don’t seem to be able to think about anything but Lidio. Tell me, dear Fessenio, have you seen him?

Fessenio

644Yes, my lady.

Fulvia

645Recently?

Fessenio

646I have, my lady.

Fulvia

647What does he say?

Fessenio

648He’s not going to leave Rome right away.

Fulvia

649But when will I be able to speak with him?

Fessenio

650Maybe today. I was on my way to talk with him when I saw you and Calandro.

Fulvia

651Try once more, dear Fessenio. For us both.

Fessenio

652I’ll see what I can do. Meanwhile try not to worry.

Fulvia

653Try not to worry, eh? That’s a good one. I won’t have a moment’s peace till you get back.

Fessenio

654Good-bye, my lady.

Fulvia

655Good-bye, Fessenio, and don’t be long. (Exit Fessenio.) This suspense is killing me. Samia, tell me what I ought to do.

Samia

656Maybe Ruffo’s spirit will be able to think of something.

Fulvia

657He’s certainly takin his time about it. Samia, go and see the magician again.

Samia

658Very well, my lady.

Fulvia

659Remind him that we are waiting, and come back at once.

Samia

660Just as soon as I find him, my lady.

Scene XV

Samia, Ruffo

Samia

661Oh, what luck! There is Ruffo. God bless you, sir.

Ruffo

662Hello, Samia, What is it?

Samia

663My mistress is dying to know if you have any news for her.

Ruffo

664I believe everything is going to be all right.

Samia

665But when?

Ruffo

666I am going to make a complete report to Fulvia.

Samia

667All this is taking so long.

Ruffo

668Samia, there are some things that can’t be rushed. The stars have to be just right, and I have to find the right magical words; then I have to gather herbs, rocks, and water from various wells. Finally I have to summon my familiar spirit. All these things naturally take time.

Samia

669Weel, as long as it works in the long run.

Ruffo

670There is every reason for hope.

Samia

671(looking offstage) Oh, oh! Do you know who her lover is?

Ruffo

672Not for sure.

Samia

673(pointing to Santilla as Lidio, who has just appeared) It’s that man over there.

Ruffo

674Do you know him well?

Samia

675I should think so. Less than two hours ago I was talking with him.

Ruffo

676What did he have to say?

Samia

677He acted as if I were some sort of criminal.

Ruffo

678Go try again: see if the spirit hasn’t softened him up a bit.

Samia

679Shall I?

Ruffo

680By all means.

Samia

681Well, I’ll try.

Ruffo

682After you talk with him, go back to Fulvia and tell her I’m coming to see her right away.

Samia

683Yes, sir.

Ruffo

684(aside) I’ll step aside and listen to this conversation.

Scene XVI

Fannio, Santilla as Lidio, Samia

Fannio

685Oh, Lidio, here is Fulvia’s maid coming toward us. Remember that her name is Samia, and be nice to her.

Santilla

686I intend to.

Samia

687Well, sir, are you still upset?

Santilla

688No, no, not at all. Samia, I hope you’ll forgive me. The last time I saw you I had something on my mind; I wasn’t my usual self. How is my beloved Fulvia?

Samia

689Do you really want to know?

Santilla

690Indeed I do.

Samia

691She wants you to give her your heart.

Santilla

692I can’t do that.

Samia

693Why not?

Santilla

694Don’t you know that it already belongs to her?

Samia

695God only knows if you are telling the truth. First you couldn’t even remember her, and now you want me to believe that she is your only love –as if I didn’t know that you don’t love her and don’t want to go near her.

Santilla

696On the contrary, until I see her again I am as good as dead.

Samia

697The spirit certainly seems to have done a good job. So you will go to see her as usual?

Santilla

698As usual? How do you mean?

Samia

699Why, dressed as a woman.

Santilla

700Ah, yes, of course. Dressed like a woman. Yes, of course. Tell her that.

Samia

701Oh, sir, that will make her so happy! I’d better not linger here any longer, and I’ll take the back street home. It wouldn’t do for anybody to see me talking to you and then going back to lady Fulvia. Good-bye, sir.

Santilla

702Good-bye.

Scene XVII

Santilla as Lidio, Fannio, Ruffo

Santilla

703Did you hear that?

Fannio

704Yes, and I heard that “as usual”, too. It’s obvious she’s mistaking you for someone else.

Santilla

705It seems so.

Fannio

706We’d better tell Ruffo about this. Here he is now.

Ruffo

707Well, gentlemen, have you made up your minds?

Santilla

708You don’t think we’d let something like that slip through our fingers, do you?

Ruffo

709Aha, our friend has reconsidered. Well, you are right, Ruffo, because she’s a great beauty.

Santilla

710I know all about her.

Fannio

711(to Santilla) You are sure to again some pleasure from it.

Ruffo

712And profit as welll.

Fannio

713Ruffo, if I understand you correctly, you said that Fulvia came to you, other means having failed, from which I gather that she has tried other means. From something that happened today, we have reason to believe that Lidio has been mistaken by her –and by her maid as well –for someone else. Therefore it will be necessary, as a precaution, for you to tell her to make no reference to what has happened in the past. Otherwise she would find out everything, and there would be a terrible scandal. Be sure to warn her of this.

Ruffo

714You were wise to think of this precaution, and I’ll not forget to do as you say. Well, we’re wasting time here; let’s get this thing started. I’d better go to her first.

Santilla

715We’ll meet you here when you come back.

Fannio

716Lidio, you go ahead. I’ll be along in a minute.
(Exit Santilla.) Ruffo, let me have a word with you.

Fannio

717I’am going to tell you a secret. You’d never guess it, no matter how hard you tried. But you must promise not to tell it to a single soul.

Ruffo

718May God deny my every wish, if I ever breathe a word of it.

Fannio

719If you do, I will be ruined, and you would not gain the reward you expect.

Ruffo

720Don’t worry. What is it?

Fannio

721My master, Lidio, is a hermaphrodite.

Ruffo

722A hermaphrodite. Well, what of it?

Fannio

723Not hermaphrodite. Hermaphrodite. Heavens, how dense you are!

Ruffo

724What does that mean?

Fannio

725Don’t you know?

Ruffo

726No; that’s why I ask.

Fannio

727A hermaphrodite is someone who is half man and half woman.

Ruffo

728Is Lidio like that?

Fannio

729He is.

Ruffo

730You mean he has the sex organs of a woman and the sex organs of a man, too?

Fannio

731Yes, sir.

Ruffo

732Come to think of it, I always thought there was something a little effeminate in his voice and manner.

Fannio

733This first time, therefore, he will use only the woman’s organs with Fulvia. She will think that this is because she asked him to come in the form of a woman; she’ll think the spirit did this to please her, and she’ll have so much faith in his power that she will worship you forever, for being his master.

Ruffo

734This is one of the best schemes I have ever heard: we stand to make a fortune.

Fannio

735Is the woman generous?

Ruffo

736I should think so! True lovers, you know, share everything they have –money, clothes, livestock, everything. A woman who loves as deeply as the lady Fulvia would even give her life.

Fannio

737What you say please me.

Ruffo

738As what you have told me about that hermaphrodite please me.

Fannio

739I’m glad you can’t pronounce that word, because even if you wanted to repeat it you couldn’t.

Ruffo

740Better go to Lidio’s now and get yourselves dressed up. I’ll tell Fulvia she can expect him.

Fannio

741I’m to go as my master’s maid. Right?

Ruffo

742Right. And be sure to be ready when I come back.

Fannio

743I will. It’s a good thing I have the necessary clothes.

Scene XVIII

Ruffo, Samia

Ruffo

744So far things couldn’t have worked out better. By now Samia has probably spoken with Fulvia, and she’ll be waiting for me. I’ll tell her the spirit has arranged everything; then I’ll give her this image and tell her to say certain words and make certain gestures before it, that this will bring about what she wants, and I shall warn her not to say a word about it to anyone except her maid. Ah, I see Samia now, standing in the doorway.

Samia

745Come in, Ruffo. My mistress is waiting for you downstairs, because her sheep of a husband is upstairs.

Scene XIX

Samia, Fessenio

Samia

746What is it, Fessenio?

Fessenio

747I have to see the lady Fulvia.

Samia

748She can’t see you now.

Fessenio

749Why not?

Samia

750She’s with the magician.

Fessenio

751Let me in anyway.

Samia

752No, I can’t.

Fessenio

753Women are such fools.

Samia

754Maybe yours are.

Fessenio

755It’s a good thing I know how to hold my temper. I’ll take a walk and come back later.

Samia

756That’s a good idea.

Exit

Fessenio

757If Fulvia knew what I knew, she wouldn’t worry about spirits. Lidio yearns for her even more than she does for him; he wants to see her this very day. But I want to be the one to tell her this, because I know she’ll give me something. I’m not going to let Samia do it; that’s why I didn’t tell her anything. But I must go now: if Fulvia were to see me, she might think I came to see her magician, who must be that man I see coming out of the house.

Scene XX

Ruffo, alone

Ruffo

758Everything is going beautifully. Fulvia has given me a nice sum of money; at this rate my fortune will soon be made, and then good-bye to these rags. What a stroke of luck! This is a rich woman and, unless I am badly mistaken, more in love than she is wise. She’ll have need of me again, unless I miss my guess. Isn’t it strange how people’s dreams sometimes come true? Last night I dreamed I caught a pheasant. I pulled the pretty feathers from his tail and stuck them in my cap. If the lady Fulvia lets herself be caught like this, as I think she will, I’ll pluck her likewise. Then I’ll be well off for a while, at least, and I’ll know how to make the most of that. But who is that woman I see waving to me? I don’t know her.

Scene XXI

Ruffo, Fannio in women’s dress

Ruffo

759Fannio! Your disguise is so good I didn’t recognize you.

Fannio

760Am I pretty?

Ruffo

761Yes, indeed. And now go and console the lady Fulvia.

Fannio

762I’m afraid she won’t get much consolation –not this time, anyway.

Ruffo

763Why, yes she will. Lidio is going to use the female organ with her.

Fannio

764That’s right. Well, shall we go?

Ruffo

765Yes. Is Lidio ready?

Fannio

766He’s waiting for me nearby, and he’s done such a good job that no one would ever guess he wasn’t a woman.

Ruffo

767Wonderful. Well, Fulvia is expecting you, so get Lidio and go to her. I’ll wait here and see what happens. I see she is standing on her doorstep now, waiting. It didn’t take her long to get ready.

Scene XXII

Fessenio, Fulvia

Fessenio

768No need to worry anymore, my lady.

Fulvia

769What do you mean?

Fessenio

770I mean that Lidio is more anxious to see you than you are to see him. No sooner did I tell him what you asked me to than he got himself ready; he’s on his way here now.

Fulvia

771Dear Fessenio, this is the kind of news I like to hear, and you shall be rewarded for it. Listen to Calandro upstairs, asking for his clothes so he can go out again. Better not stay, or he’ll see us together. Oh, how glad you have made me! Things are really looking up now. As soon as I have let that buzzard out of his cage, I shall feel free.

Exit

Fessenio

772These lovers are going to make up for lost time, and if Lidio is wise, he’ll forget about his sister. Calandro won’t be at home; they will have lots of time to enjoy themselves. I can afford to take a walk now. Ah, Calandro is coming out. I’d better go, because if he stop to talk with me, he might see Lidio, who ought to be here any minute.

Scene XXIII

Calandro, Lidio as Santilla, Santilla in woman’s dress accompanied by Fannio in woman’s dress

Calandro

773I see I am in luck: no sooner do I step outside the house than I see my beloved coming my way. Heavens! What shall I say to her? Good morning? No, it’s not that early. Good evening? No, it’s not that late. Good bless you? No, too casual. Mistress of my soul? But that’s not a greeting. Heart of my body? No, too commonplace. Angel face? Too vulgar. Divine spirit? No, that’s not right either. Oh, my, here she is now. Spirit, heart, angel: which shall I say? Oh, damn, what a fool I am! I was mistaken; this isn’t Santilla –there she is over there. (Addresses Santilla.) Good morning... that is, good evening. Oh, no, she’s not the one. It must be the other one after all. (Approaches Lidio.) No, I think it was the other. No, it must be this one. No, it’s the other. Damn!

Lidio

774This madman thinks I am a woman: he has fallen in love with me and will follow me all the way to his house. I’ll go home, change, and go to Fulvia later.

Calandro

775It’s the one across the street. I’ll go follow her.

Santilla

776(Follows Lidio)(to Fannio) He’s gone the other way. Quick, now –let’s slip inside before he comes back. There is Fulvia, waiting to let us in.
