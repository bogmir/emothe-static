Bernardo Dovizi da Bibbiena

The Follies of Calandro

← Act IV

Act V

Scene I

Samia, Santilla as Lidio, Lidio

Samia

907How true it is that women do to money what the sun does to ice: they consume it completely. My mistress no sooner read Ruffo's note than she gave me this bag of money to take to Lidio. There he is now. Now, Lidio, you will see how the lady Fulvia keeps her word. (To Santilla.) Come, here it is. Take it.

Santilla

908Here I am.

Lidio

909No, give it here.

Samia

910Oh, I made a mistake. (To Santilla.) Excuse me, sir. I wanted this gentleman, not you.

Santilla

911Now you are mistaken. Come, do your business with me, and let that gentleman go.

Samia

912You are right; I’m sorry. (To Lidio.) Good-bye, sir. (To Santilla.) You come here.

Lidio

913What do you mean good-bye? Here, look at me.

Samia

914(To Lidio) Oh, yes... it is you after all. (To Santilla.) It’s this gentleman I want. You may leave us.

Santilla

915Leave you? What do you mean? Am I not Lidio?

Samia

916Why, yes, I think you are. (To Lidio.) You go away, sir.

Lidio

917What’s the matter with you? Look at me carefully. Am I not the one?

Samia

918Why, of course. I’m sorry. You are Lidio. (To Santilla.) You leave us, please.

Santilla

919You fool, I am Lidio; he’s not.

Samia

920Well, well, you are right.
(To Lidio.) Do please leave us alone.

Lidio

921What are you trying to do, you bitch? You know the money belongs to me, yet you want to give it to this fellow.

Santilla

922What do you mean, yours? Give it to me.

Lidio

923No, to me.

Santilla

924Why to you? You are not Lidio; I am.

Lidio

925Give it here.

Santilla

926Here!

Samia

927Don’t touch it, either of you, or I’ll scream. Let me have a good look at you both. Good, God, what a miracle! These two resemble each other more than snow resembles snow, or one egg another. I don’t know which of you is Lidio: first it seems the one, then the other. But I know how I’ll find out. Tell me, is either of you gentlemen in love?

Lidio

928I am.

Santilla

929And so am I.

Samia

930Which one?

Lidio

931I.

Santilla

932I.

Samia

933Where does this money come from?

Lidio

934From a lady.

Santilla

935From my sweetheart.

Samia

936Oh, heavens! Still not clear. Tell me, who is this sweetheart?

Lidio

937Fulvia.

Santilla

938Fulvia.

Samia

939Which of you is her lover?

Lidio

940I am.

Santilla

941I am.

Lidio

942What do you mean, you are?

Santilla

943I mean I am.

Lidio

944You’re not. I am.

Samia

945Ah, me, what is all this? Which Fulvia are you talking about?

Lidio

946Calandro’s wife.

Santilla

947(To Samia) And your mistress.

Samia

948Still no light. Either I have lost my mind, or these men are bewitched. Tell me, how were you dressed when you went to her?

Lidio

949As a woman.

Santilla

950As a young lady.

Samia

951This is too much. But oh, now I will find out. When did my mistress want her lover to visit her?

Lidio

952By day.

Santilla

953At midday.

Samia

954What the devil! This is certainly a fiendish plot of some kind, thought up by that damned spirit. I’d better go back to my mistress with this money and let her give it to whomever she wishes. Gentlemen, I’m sure you understand –I don’t know which of you to give it to. My mistress will surely know who her lover is, so whichever of you is Lidio go to see her and you may be sure that she will give it to you.

Exit

Lidio

955(aside) My own reflection in the mirror looks less like me than does this fellow; I shall soon know who he is. Such adventures don’t happen every day, and Fulvia may change her mind. This is quite a lot of money. I’d better go pay her a visit, the way I used to.

Exit

Santilla

956That must be the man that I was mistaken for. I wonder what is keeping Fannio? If he were here, as he said he would be, we could go to Fulvia’s and maybe collect a lot of money. But he’s not, and I have to do something about this wedding I’m supposed to be in.

Scene II

Fessenio, Santilla, Fannio

Fessenio

957(apart) I couldn’t find Lidio anywhere.

Santilla

958(apart) What am I going to do now?

Fessenio

959(apart) If I don’t get to the bottom of this, and find out if it’s true that Lidio has been changed into a woman, it won’t be my fault. Oh! Is that him over there? No, I don’t think so. Yes, it is. He does seem changed.

Santilla

960(apart) Ah, me!

Fessenio

961(apart) He is talking to himself.

Santilla

962(apart) What a mess all this is!

Fessenio

963(apart) I wonder what’s the matter?

Santilla

964(apart) Must I be ruined so soon?

Fessenio

965(apart) What ruin is he talking about?

Santilla

966(apart) Because of too much love?

Fessenio

967(apart) I wonder what he means by that.

Santilla

968(apart) Will I have to stop wearing these clothes?

Fessenio

969(apart) My, my, his voice certainly has become feminine. What a pity!

Santilla

970(apart) And lose all the freedom I have enjoyed?

Fessenio

971(apart) Well, it’s true, all right. There’s no longer any doubt about it.

Santilla

972(apart) Now everyone will know I’m a woman. I’ll never be able to pass for a man again.

Fessenio

973(apart) True enough. The harm’s been done now.

Santilla

974(apart) From now on I must call myself Santilla, and not Lidio.

Fessenio

975(apart) Sad but true.

Santilla

976(apart) I’d be better off if they had let me die the day Modone was captured.

Fessenio

977(apart) Oh, cruel fate! He’s taking this very hard. If I hadn’t heard it from his own lips, I’d never believe it. I’ll speak to him. (To Lidio.) Oh, Lidio.

Santilla

978Who is this wretch?

Fessenio

979Why do you call me a wretch, as if you didn’t know who I am?

Santilla

980I never knew you and I don’t care to.

Fessenio

981So you don’t know your own servant anymore?

Santilla

982You my servant?

Fessenio

983If you don’t want me, I’ll be someone else’s.

Santilla

984Please leave me alone. I have no time for drunkards.

Fessenio

985Either you are drunk or you have lost your memory. But don’t think you can hide from me, because I know all about your little accident.

Santilla

986What accident?

Fessenio

987When the magician changed you into a woman.

Santilla

988I a woman?

Fessenio

989Yes, a woman.

Santilla

990You are mistaken. (Fessenio approaches, attempts to feel her.) You wretch! What are you trying to do?

Fessenio

991I want to make sure.

Santilla

992How dare you touch me, you brute?

Fessenio

993(persisting) I’ll find out or die in the attempt.

Santilla

994Miserable creature, don’t you dare! Help! Fannio! Help!

Fannio

995What’s going on here?

Santilla

996This impudent fellow claims that I am a woman, and is trying to feel me against my will.

Fannio

997How dare you do such a thing?

Fessenio

998What right have you to come between my master and me?

Fannio

999Your master?

Fessenio

1000Of course. Why?

Fannio

1001My good man, you are mistaken. I know for a fact that you are not his servant, nor he your master, because I am his servant, and he is my master.

Fessenio

1002That cannot be. I am his servant, and he is my master. You are both lying.

Santilla

1003I’m not surprised that you speak with such arrogance, since you acted with such presumption.

Fessenio

1004Nor am I surprised that you have forgotten who I am, since you have forgotten your own identity.

Fannio

1005(to Fessenio) Mind what you say to him.

Santilla

1006I don’t know my own identity?

Fessenio

1007My lord, or rather my lady, if you knew who you were, you would know who I am.

Santilla

1008I know well enough who I am, but as for you, I have no idea.

Fessenio

1009Say rather that in losing yourself you have found someone else.

Santilla

1010And who is it that I have found?

Fessenio

1011Your sister Santilla, for now, since you have changed into a woman, she is in you; and since you are no longer a man, you have lost yourself. You are no longer Lidio.

Santilla

1012Lidio? Who is Lidio?

Fessenio

1013Poor fellow! He doesn’t remember a thing. Come, master, don’t you remember being Lidio of Medone, son of Demetrius, brother of Santilla, pupil of Polinico, master of Fessenio, lover of the lady Fulvia?

Santilla

1014Fannio, did you hear that? Oh, yes, I know the lady Fulvia.

Fessenio

1015He remembers no one but Fulvia; it’s obvious he’s been bewitched.

Scene III

Lidio in women’s dress, Fessenio, Santilla as Lidio, Fannio

Lidio

1016Fessenio, oh, Fessenio!

Fessenio

1017Who is that woman waving to me? (To Fannio.) You wait –I haven’t finished with you yet.

Santilla

1018Fannio, if I dared to think that my brother was still alive, I would be full of hope. That person (indicating Lidio) must be the one they have mistaken me for.

Fannio

1019You are not sure of your brother’s death?

Santilla

1020By no means.

Fannio

1021For now I am quite certain that that is indeed our lost Lidio, and now it seems to me that I also recognize this man as – Fessenio!

Santilla

1022Oh, God, I begin to feel my heart grow light with joy.

Fessenio

1023(to Lidio) I’m still not sure if you are Lidio or if the other one is. Let me have a better look at you.

Lidio

1024What’s the matter with you? Have you been drinking?

Fessenio

1025Yes, you are Lidio all right, and you are a man, too.

Lidio

1026I am on my way to see the lady Fulvia.

Fessenio

1027That’s right; she’s waiting to give you the money.

Santilla

1028What’s that you said?

Fessenio

1029If I’ve said or done anything to offend you, I beg your pardon, for now I realize that you have been mistaken for my master.

Santilla

1030Who is your master?

Fessenio

1031One Lidio of Modone, and he looks so much like you I thought you were he.

Santilla

1032Dear Fannio, everything is clear now. (To Fessenio) What is your name?

Fessenio

1033Fessenio, sir, at your service.

Santilla

1034Now there is no longer any doubt of it. Oh, how happy we are! (Embraces Fessenio.) Dear Fessenio, you are mine!

Fessenio

1035What are all these caresses for? And why do you say I am yours? When I said I was your servant a moment ago, I was lying: I am not, not are you my master. I have another master, so you must seek another servant.

Santilla

1036You are mine, and I am yours!

Fannio

1037(embraces Fessenio) Oh, my dear Fessenio!

Fessenio

1038What does all this mean? Something’s going on here that I don’t know about.

Fannio

1039This is Santilla, your master’s sister.

Fessenio

1040Our Santilla?

Fannio

1041Shh, no so loud. Yes, it is. And I am Fannio.

Fessenio

1042(embraces Fannio.) Fannio! My dear fellow!

Fannio

1043Not here; someone’s coming. Don’t say anything about it.

Scene IV

Samia, Fessenio, Santilla as Lidio, Fannio

Samia

1044Boohoo! Boohoo! My mistress and I are done for. Ruined! And out of a clear sky. Boohoo!

Fessenio

1045What’s the matter with you, Samia?

Samia

1046Oh, my poor mistress!

Fessenio

1047Why, what’s the matter?

Samia

1048Oh, my dear Fessenio, we are done for.

Fessenio

1049But what is it? Come, speak up.

Samia

1050Bad news.

Fessenio

1051How so?

Samia

1052Calandro’s brothers founds your master Lidio with my mistress. They have sent for Calandro and for her brothers, too. They are going to denounce her to them and maybe kill Lidio.

Fessenio

1053What’s this? Oh, my poor master! Are they holding him prisoner?

Samia

1054No.

Fessenio

1055Why didn’t he run away then?

Samia

1056Because of my mistress. She is in hopes that before Calandro and her brothers get to the house, the magician will change Lidio back into a woman. That way he’ll keep his life and my mistress her reputation: if he saved himself now, by running away, she would surely be ruined. That’s where I’m going now –to see Ruffo.

Fessenio

1057Wait a minute. What part of the house is Lidio in?

Samia

1058Both he and my mistress are in the bedroom on the first floor.

Fessenio

1059Isn’t there a window in the rear of that room?

Samia

1060Yes, he could escape easily enough if he wanted to.

Fessenio

1061That’s not why I asked. If someone were to slip in through that window, would he be notice?

Samia

1062Probably not.

Fessenio

1063Samia, this magician business is a lot of nonsense. If you want to save you mistress, go back to the house, and if there’s anybody in the hall, try to get rid of them.

Samia

1064Well, all right. I’ll do as you say, but I hope you know what you’re doing.

Fessenio

1065Don’t worry. Run along now.

Exit Samia

Santilla

1066Ah, me, Fessenio, it look as if fate has arranged that I should no sooner find my brother than I should lose him again; no sooner does he come to life than he must lose it.

Fessenio

1067Now is no time for tears: this situation calls for quick action and clear thinking. No one is looking now, so swap clothes with Fannio. Quickly –that’s it. Don’t worry; I’m coming with you. Fannio, you wait here. Now, Santilla, I’ll tell you what you must do.

Exeuent Fessenio and Santilla

Fannio

1068What a strange predicament this brother and sister find themselves in! This will either be the happiest or the worst day of their lives, depending on how it ends. It seems that heaven made them similar not only in their appearance but also in their fortunes: each of them is now in a position where what profits one profits both and where what harms one harms the other. Until I can see further ahead I am unable either to rejoice or despair, fear or hope. I pray heaven that Lidio and Santilla will somehow find a way out of all this. In the meantime, I’ll step aside and wait for the outcome.

Scene V

Lidio alone

Lidio

1069I have been delivered from great danger, and I scarcely know how it happened. While I was in that room, bewailing the misfortune that overtook my mistress and me, someone sent by Fessenio jumps into the room through the rear window, dressed himself in my clothes, and gives me his. Then Fessenio told me to slip out the window, saying not to worry, that everything would turn out all right. Just a few moments ago things couldn’t have looked worse; now I am pleased with the way they seem to be working out. Fessenio stayed behind to tell Fulvia something. I’d better wait here to see what happens. Oh, there is my mistress now, standing on the threshold. She looks happy, too.

Scene VI

Fulvia alone

Fulvia

1070This has been quite a day for me, but everything has turned out for the best, thank heaven. I couldn’t be more pleased: my reputation has been saved and so has Lidio’s life. And now it will be easier than ever for me to see him as often as I like. Whoever wouldn’t be satisfied with that wouldn’t be human.

Scene VII

Calandro, accompanied by two of Fulvia’s brothers, Fulvia

Calandro

1071Wretched woman, what are you doing at that door? How can you stand there and wait for me like that, knowing you have hung horns on me? I don’t know how I can keep myself from beating the breath out of your body, but first I want you to see me kill the man you have in that room, you slut, and then I’m going to tear the eyes out of your head.

Fulvia

1072Alas, my husband, what is the meaning of all this? Why do you act as if I were guilty of something, when I am not? What is the reason for such cruelty?

Calandro

1073Shameless woman, how can you keep pretending, as if we didn’t know you have a man in there, dressed in woman’s clothes?

Fulvia

1074My brothers, this wretched man wants to expose what I have always kept hidden, and that is my patience and the many outrages I have suffered at his hands. There is no wife in all the world more faithful than I, nor treated worse. And he dares to say that I have made him wear horns!

Calandro

1075It’s true, you miserable creature, and now I’m going to prove it to your brothers.

Fulvia

1076Go on in, all of you, and see who it is I have in my room that this miserable little worm says he is going to kill.

Scene VIII

Lidio alone

Lidio

1077Fessenio said everything would be all right, but it doesn’t look that way, and I’m beginning to be suspicious. I didn’t know who that was he made me swap clothes with. I don’t see Fessenio, and Calandro has gone in the house, threatening Fulvia. He is beside himself with rage and may hurt her. If I hear any trouble, I’ll go inside, no matter what, and either protect her or die for her. No true lover was ever lacking in courage.

Scene IX

Fannio, Lidio

Fannio

1078There is Lidio over there, or rather Santilla. Let’s change again. Give me back my clothes, and I’ll give you yours.

Lidio

1079Why, what do you mean?

Fannio

1080It’s been such a short time since Fessenio made you change clothes that you can’t have forgotten. Come, give me those and take these.

Lidio

1081Certainly I remember changing clothes, but those aren’t the ones I gave you.

Fannio

1082What’s wrong with you? Do you think I’d have gone and sold them?

Lidio

1083Don’t make me angry. Oh, there is Fessenio.

Scene X

Fessenio alone

Fessenio

1084Ha, ha, ha! They thought that under that dress they’d find the young man they suspected Fulvia of amusing herself with: they were going to kill him and denounce her, but when they found out it was a girl, they quieted down and apologized to Fulvia, saying she was the most virtuous woman in all the world. Now her reputation is secure, and so is my own happiness. They have let Santilla go; I see her coming out now. And there is Lidio, too.

Scene XI

Santilla, Fessenio, Lidio, Fannio

Santilla

1085Where is my brother, Fessenio?

Fessenio

1086There he is over there, still wearing the clothes you gave him. Let’s go to him. Lidio, do you know who this is?

Lidio

1087Not for sure. Tell me.

Fessenio

1088The one who saved you by taking your place at the lady Fulvia’s, and for whom you have been looking so long.

Lidio

1089Who?

Fessenio

1090Your Santilla.

Lidio

1091My sister?

Santilla

1092I am you sister, and you are my brother.

Lidio

1093Are you really Santilla? But I see now that you are. Oh, my dear sister, for whom I have wished and searched so long! Now at last I am satisfied; now I have gained my wish; now I am the happiest man alive.

Santilla

1094Beloved brother of mine, though I see you and touch you, I can scarcely believe you are really he whom I thought dead and for whom I mourned so long. Now that I find you are alive, my joy is as great as my grief was then.

Lidio

1095And I, my dear sister, love you all the more because of what you did for me today: if it hadn’t been for you, I wouldn’t be alive to say this.

Santilla

1096Now that I have recovered somewhat from this shock, allow me to present to you our servant Fannio, who has always served me faithfully.

Lidio

1097Dear Fannio, I remember you quite well, and in having served one of us you have served us both. You shall be rewarded for it.

Fannio

1098My greatest reward, sir, is to see you alive and rejoined with your sister.

Santilla

1099Dear Fessenio, why are you looking at us so hard?

Fessenio

1100Never in all my life have I seen a man resemble another man so much as you and your brother resemble each other. I understand now the reason for all these strange mistakes.

Santilla

1101They have been strange indeed.

Lidio

1102And more numerous than either of you realizes.

Fessenio

1103We’ll have time to talk about that later. Right now the most important thing is this: when I told the lady Fulvia that this was your sister Santilla, she was greatly pleased and said that, if everyone is willing, Santilla would become the wife of her son Flaminio.

Santilla

1104Now I understand why, when we were in the room together, she kissed me tenderly and said: I don’t know which of us three is the happiest; Lidio has found a sister, I a daughter, and you a husband.

Lidio

1105It could all be true, too.

Fannio

1106I know of a better plan still.

Lidio

1107What is that?

Fannio

1108As Fessenio says, you two are so much alike that you would fool anybody.

Santilla

1109I know what you mean –that Lidio should take my place in the marriage with Perillo’s daughter.

Lidio

1110That’s settled then?

Santilla

1111As settled as the weather.

Lidio

1112How fortunate we are! After so fierce a storm, how beautiful a calm! We shall be even better off than we were in Modone.

Fessenio

1113As much better off than Italy is worthier than Greece; than Rome is statelier tha Modone; and than two fortunes are better than one. We shall all be gainers.

Lidio

1114Come, let’s get started.

Fessenio

1115(to audience) Good people, the weddings will take place tomorrow. Whoever wishes to see them should stay, but whoever to avoid the inconvenience of waiting till then, let him go about his business, as we are finished with ours. VALETE ET PLAUDITE.
