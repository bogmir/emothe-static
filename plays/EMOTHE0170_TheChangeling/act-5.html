Enter BEATRICE. A clock strikes one

BEATRICE

1One struck, and yet she lies by’t! – O my fears!
2This strumpet serves her own ends, ’tis apparent now,
3Devours the pleasure with a greedy appetite,
4And never minds my honour or my peace,
5Makes havoc of my right. But she pays dearly for’t:
6No trusting of her life with such a secret,

171For any man to pledge me.

VERMANDERO

For any man to pledge me. Horrid villain!
172Keep life in him for further tortures.

DE FLORES

Keep life in him for further tortures. No: −
173I can prevent you, here’s my penknife still.
174It is but one thread more, [Stabs himself] − and now ’tis cut.
175Make haste, Joanna, by that token to thee
176Canst not forget, so lately put in mind;
177I would not go to leave thee far behind.

Dies

BEATRICE

178Forgive me, Alsemero, all forgive!
179’Tis time to die, when ’tis a shame to live.

Dies

VERMANDERO

180O, my name is ent’rèd now in that record record Pronounced with stress on the second syllable.
181Where till this fatal hour ’twas never read.

ALSEMERO

182Let it be blotted out; let your heart lose it,
183And it can never look you in the face,
184Nor tell a tale behind the back of life
185To your dishonour. Justice hath so right
186The guilty hit, that innocence is quit
187By proclamation, and may joy again.
188Sir, you are sensible of what truth hath done;
189’Tis the best comfort that your grief can find.

TOMAZO

190[To VERMANDERO] Sir, I am satisfied; my injuries
191Lie dead before me. I can exact no more,
192Unless my soul were loose, and could o’ertake
193Those black fugitives, that are fled from thence,

[Pointing to the bodies]

194To take a second vengeance. But there are wraths
195Deeper than mine, ’tis to be feared, about ’em.

ALSEMERO

196What an opacous body had that moon
197That last changed on us! Here’s beauty changed
198To ugly whoredom; here, servant-obedience
199To a master-sin, imperious murder;
200I, a supposed husband, changed embraces
201With wantonness, but that was paid before;
202[To TOMAZO] Your change is come too, from an ignorant wrath
203To knowing friendship. Are there any more on’s?

ANTONIO

Yes, sir, I was changed too, from a little ass as I was to a great fool as I am; and had like to ha’ been changed to the gallows but that you know my innocence always excuses me.

FRANCISCUS

208I was changed from a little wit to be stark mad,
209Almost for the same purpose.

ISABELLA

Almost for the same purpose. [To ALIBIUS] Your change is still behind,
210But deserve best your transformation transformation Pronounced as five syllables: “trans-for-ma-ti-on”. :
211You are a jealous coxcomb, keep schools of folly,
212And teach your scholars how to break your own head.

ALIBIUS

213I see all apparent, wife, and will change now
214Into a better husband, and never keep
215Scholars that shall be wiser then myself.

ALSEMERO

216[To VERMANDERO] Sir, you have yet a son’s duty living;
217Please you, accept it. Let that your sorrow,
218As it goes from your eye, go from your heart.
219Man and his sorrow at the grave must part.

EPILOGUE

ALSEMERO

1All we can do, to comfort one another,
2To stay a brother’s sorrow for a brother,
3To dry a child from the kind father’s eyes,
4Is to no purpose; it rather multiplies.
5Your only smiles have power to cause re-live
6The dead again, or in their rooms to give
7Brother a new brother, father a child;
8If these appear, all griefs are reconciled.

Exeunt OMNES
