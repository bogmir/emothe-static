Act IV [, Scene i]

[Dumb show]

Enter GENTLEMEN, VERMANDERO meeting them with action of wonderment at the flight of PIRACQUO. Enter ALSEMERO, with JASPERINO and GALLANTS; VERMANDERO points to him, the GENTLEMEN seeming to applaud the choice. [Exeunt in procession VERMANDERO], ALSEMERO, JASPERINO and GENTLEMEN. [Enter] BEATRICE the bride, following in great state, accompanied with DIAPHANTA, ISABELLA, and other GENTLEWOMEN. [Enter] DE FLORES after all, smiling at the accident. ALONZO’s ghost appears to DE FLORES in the midst of his smile; startles him, showing him the hand whose finger he had cut off. They pass over in great solemnity.

Enter BEATRICE

BEATRICE

1This fellow has undone me endlessly:
2Never was bride so fearfully distressed.
3The more I think upon th’ ensuing night,
4And whom I am to cope with in embraces −
5One who’s ennobled both in blood and mind,
6So clear in understanding (that’s my plague now),
7Before whose judgement will my fault appear
8Like malefactors’ crimes before tribunals
9(There is no hiding on’t) − the more I dive
10Into my own distress. How a wise man
11Stands for a great calamity! There’s no venturing
12Into his bed, what course soe’er I light upon,
13Without my shame, which may grow up to danger.
14He cannot but in justice strangle me
15As I lie by him, as a cheater use me;
16’Tis a precious craft to play with a false die
17Before a cunning gamester. Here’s his closet,
18The key left in’t, and he abroad i’ th’ park;
19Sure ’twas forgot, I’ll be so bold as look in’t.

[Opens closet]

20Bless me! A right physician’s closet ’tis,
21Set round with vials, every one her mark too.
22Sure he does practice physic for his own use,
23Which may be safely called your great man’s wisdom.
24What manuscript lies here? ‘The Book of Experiment,
25Called Secrets in Nature’; so ’tis, ’tis so
26‘How to know whether a woman be with child or no.’
27I hope I am not yet; if he should try though!
28Let me see: ‘folio forty-five.’ Here ’tis;
29The leaf tucked down upon’t, the place suspicious.

30‘If you would know whether a woman be with child or 31not, give her two spoonfuls of the white water in glass C −’

32Where’s that glass C? O, yonder, I see’t now −

33‘and if she be with child, she sleeps full twelve hours after; 34if not, not.’

35None of that water comes into my belly:
36I’ll know you from a hundred. I could break you now,
37Or turn you into milk, and so beguile
38The master of the mystery, but I’ll look to you.
39Ha! That which is next is ten times worse:
40‘How to know whether a woman be a maid or not.’
41If that should be applied, what would become of me?
42Belike he has a strong faith of my purity,
43That never yet made proof; but this he calls

44‘A merry sleight, but true experiment, the author 45Antonius Mizaldus. Give the party you suspect the quantity 46of a spoonful of the water in the glass M, which upon 47her that is a maid makes three several effects: ’twill make 48her incontinently gape, then fall into a sudden sneezing, 49last into a violent laughing; else dull, heavy, and lumpish.’

50Where had I been?
51I fear it, yet ’tis seven hours to bedtime.

DIAPHANTA

DIAPHANTA

BEATRICE

DIAPHANTA

We shall have time to sit by’t. Now I’m sad again.

BEATRICE

116It lays itself so gently too!aparte

DIAPHANTA

119Pray tell me, madam, what trick call you this?

BEATRICE

DIAPHANTA

The carriage of this business. I shall carry’t well,

BEATRICE

Because I love the burden. About midnight
123You must not fail to steal forth gently,

DIAPHANTA

That I may use the place. O fear not, madam;

[Act IV, Scene ii]

Enter VERMANDERO and SERVANT

VERMANDERO

1I tell thee, knave, mine honour is in question,
2A thing till now free from suspicion,
3Nor ever was there cause. Who of my gentlemen
4Are absent? Tell me, and truly, how many and who.

SERVANT

5Antonio, sir, and Franciscus.

VERMANDERO

6When did they leave the castle?

SERVANT

7Some ten days since, sir, the one intending to Briamata, 8th’other for Valencia.

VERMANDERO

9The time accuses ’em. A charge of murder
10Is brought within my castle gate, Piracquo’s murder;
11I dare not answer faithfully their absence.
12A strict command of apprehension
13Shall pursue ’em suddenly, and either wipe
14The stain off clear, or openly discover it.
15Provide me wingèd warrants for the purpose.

Exit SERVANT

16See, I am set on again.

Enter TOMAZO

TOMAZO

17I claim a brother of you.

VERMANDERO

I claim a brother of you. Y’are too hot;
18Seek him not here.

TOMAZO

Seek him not here. Yes, ’mongst your dearest bloods,
19If my peace find no fairer satisfaction.
20This is the place must yield account for him,
21For here I left him; and the hasty tie
22Of this snatched marriage gives strong testimony
23Of his most certain ruin.

VERMANDERO

Of his most certain ruin. Certain falsehood!
24This is the place indeed: his breach of faith
25Has too much marred both my abusèd love −
26The honourable love I reserved for him −
27And mocked my daughter’s joy. The prepared morning
28Blushed at his infidelity; he left
29Contempt and scorn to throw upon those friends
30Whose belief hurt ’em. O, ’twas most ignoble
31To take his flight so unexpectedly,
32And throw such public wrongs on those that loved him!

TOMAZO

33Then this is all your answer?

VERMANDERO

Then this is all your answer? ’Tis too fair
34For one of his alliance, and I warn you
35That this place no more see you.

Exit

Enter DE FLORES

TOMAZO

That this place no more see you. The best is,
36There is more ground to meet a man’s revenge on. −
37Honest De Flores?

DE FLORES

Honest De Flores? That’s my name indeed.
38Saw you the bride? Good sweet sir, which way took she?

TOMAZO

39I have blest mine eyes from seeing such a false one.

DE FLORES

40I’d fain get off, this man’s not for my company:aparte
41I smell his brother’s blood when I come near him.aparte

TOMAZO

42Come hither, kind and true one; I remember
43My brother loved thee well.

DE FLORES

My brother loved thee well. O purely, dear sir!
44− Methinks I am now again a-killing on him,aparte
45He brings it so fresh to me.aparte

TOMAZO

He brings it so fresh to me. Thou canst guess, sirrah −
46One honest friend has an instinct of jealousy −
47At some foul guilty person?

DE FLORES

48’Las, sir, I am so charitable, I think none
49Worse than myself. − You did not see the bride then?

TOMAZO

50I prithee, name her not. Is she not wicked?

DE FLORES

51No, no: a pretty, easy, round-packed sinner,
52As your most ladies are (else you might think
53I flattered her), but, sir, at no hand wicked
54Till th’are so old their chins and noses meet,
55And they salute witches. − I am called, I think, sir.
56− His company ev’n o’erlays my conscience.aparte

Exit

TOMAZO

57That De Flores has a wondrous honest heart;
58He’ll bring it out in time, I’m assured on’t.
59O, here’s the glorious master of the day’s joy.
60’Twill not be long till he and I do reckon.

Enter ALSEMERO

61Sir!

ALSEMERO

Sir! You are most welcome.

TOMAZO

Sir! You are most welcome. You may call that word back:
62I do not think I am, nor wish to be.

ALSEMERO

63’Tis strange you found the way to this house then.

TOMAZO

64Would I’d ne’er known the cause! I’m none of those, sir,
65That come to give you joy, and swill your wine;
66’Tis a more precious liquor that must lay
67The fiery thirst I bring.

ALSEMERO

The fiery thirst I bring. Your words and you
68Appear to me great strangers.

TOMAZO

Appear to me great strangers. Time and our swords
69May make us more acquainted. This the business: −
70I should have [had] a brother in your place;
71How treachery and malice have disposed of him
72I’m bound to enquire of him which holds his right,
73Which never could come fairly.

ALSEMERO

Which never could come fairly. You must look
74To answer for that word, sir.

TOMAZO

To answer for that word, sir. Fear you not;
75I’ll have it ready drawn at our next meeting.
76Keep your day solemn. Farewell, I disturb it not;
77I’ll bear the smart with patience for a time.

Exit

ALSEMERO

78’Tis somewhat ominous, this: a quarrel ent’rèd
79Upon this day. My innocence relieves me;

Enter JASPERINO

80I should be wondrous sad else. − Jasperino,
81I have news to tell thee, strange news.

JASPERINO

I have news to tell thee, strange news. I ha’ some too,
82I think as strange as yours. Would I might keep
83Mine, so my faith and friendship might be kept in’t.
84Faith, sir, dispense a little with my zeal,
85And let it cool in this.

ALSEMERO

And let it cool in this. This puts me on,
86And blames thee for thy slowness.

JASPERINO

And blames thee for thy slowness. All may prove nothing,
87Only a friendly fear that leapt from me, sir.

ALSEMERO

88No question it may prove nothing; let’s partake it, though.

JASPERINO

89’Twas Diaphanta’s chance − for to that wench
90I pretend honest love, and she deserves it −
91To leave me in a back part of the house,
92A place we chose for private conference;
93She was no sooner gone but instantly
94I heard your bride’s voice in the next room to me
95And, lending more attention, found De Flores
96Louder than she.

ALSEMERO

Louder than she. De Flores? Thou art out now.

JASPERINO

97You’ll tell me more anon.

ALSEMERO

You’ll tell me more anon. Still I’ll prevent thee;
98The very sight of him is poison to her.

JASPERINO

99That made me stagger too, but Diaphanta
100At her return confirmed it.

ALSEMERO

At her return confirmed it. Diaphanta!

JASPERINO

101Then fell we both to listen, and words passed
102Like those that challenge interest in a woman −

ALSEMERO

103Peace, quench thy zeal; ’tis dangerous to thy bosom.

JASPERINO

104Then truth is full of peril.

ALSEMERO

Then truth is full of peril. Such truths are. −
105O, were she the sole glory of the earth,
106Had eyes that could shoot fire into kings’ breasts,
107And touched, she sleeps not here! Yet I have time,
108Though night be near, to be resolved hereof;
109And prithee do not weigh me by my passions.

JASPERINO

110I never weighed friend so.

ALSEMERO

I never weighed friend so. Done charitably.
111That key will lead thee to a pretty secret,

[Gives key]

112By a Chaldean taught me, and I’ve [made]
113My study upon some. Bring from my closet
114A glass inscribed there with the letter M,
115And question not my purpose.

JASPERINO

And question not my purpose. It shall be done, sir.

Exit

[Act IV, Scene iii]

Enter ISABELLA and LOLLIO

ISABELLA

1O heaven! Is this the waxing moon?
2Does love turn fool, run mad, and all [at] once?
3Sirrah, here’s a madman, akin to the fool too,
4A lunatic lover.

LOLLIO

5No, no! − Not he I brought the letter from?

ISABELLA

6Compare his inside with his out, and tell me.

[Gives him the letter]

LOLLIO

7The out’s mad, I’m sure of that; I had a taste on’t. 8[Reads] ‘To the bright Andromeda, chief chambermaid to 9the Knight of the Sun, at the sight of Scorpio, in the middle 10region, sent by the bellows-mender of Aeolus. Pay the 11post.’ This is stark madness.

ISABELLA

12Now mark the inside. 13[Takes the letter and she reads] ‘Sweet 14lady, having now cast off this counterfeit cover of a 15madman, I appear to your best judgement a true and 16faithful lover of your beauty.’

LOLLIO

17He is mad still.

ISABELLA

18‘If any fault you find, chide those perfections in you which 19have made me imperfect: ’tis the same sun that causeth to 20grow and enforceth to wither, −’

LOLLIO

21O rogue!

ISABELLA

22‘− Shapes and transhapes, destroys and builds again. I 23come in winter to you, dismantled of my proper ornaments: 24by the sweet splendour of your cheerful smiles I 25spring and live a lover.’

LOLLIO

26Mad rascal still!

ISABELLA

LOLLIO

ISABELLA

34Very likely.

ISABELLA

38The first place is thine, believe it, Lollio,

LOLLIO

ISABELLA

41So.

LOLLIO

ISABELLA

LOLLIO

ISABELLA

LOLLIO

49The key of thy wardrobe.

LOLLIO

[Gives her the key]

Exit

ALIBIUS

ALIBIUS

ISABELLA

ANTONIO

[Kneels and listens]

118Hark how they roar and rumble in the straits!

ANTONIO

120Pox upon you, let me alone!

ISABELLA

121Why shouldst thou mount so high as Mercury,
122Unless thou hadst reversion of his place?
123Stay in the moon with me, Endymion,
124And we will rule these wild rebellious waves
125That would have drowned my love.

ANTONIO

126I’ll kick thee if again thou touch me,
127Thou wild unshapen antic; I am no fool,
128You bedlam!

ISABELLA

You bedlam! But you are, as sure as I am, mad.
129Have I put on this habit of a frantic,
130With love as full of fury to beguile
131The nimble eye of watchful jealousy,
132And am I thus rewarded?

[Reveals herself]

ANTONIO

133Ha, dearest beauty!

ISABELLA

Ha, dearest beauty! No, I have no beauty now,
134Nor never had, but what was in my garments.
135You a quick-sighted lover? Come not near me!
136Keep your caparisons, y’are aptly clad;
137I came a feigner, to return stark mad.

Exit

Enter LOLLIO

ANTONIO

138Stay, or I shall change condition
139And become as you are.

LOLLIO

140Why, Tony, whither now? Why, fool?

ANTONIO

141Whose fool, usher of idiots? You coxcomb!
142I have fooled too much.

LOLLIO

143You were best be mad another while then.

ANTONIO

144So I am, stark mad: I have cause enough,
145And I could throw the full effects on thee,
146And beat thee like a fury!

LOLLIO

147Do not, do not! I shall not forbear the gentleman under 148the fool, if you do − alas, I saw through your fox-skin 149before now! Come, I can give you comfort. My mistress 150loves you, and there is as arrant a madman i’ th’ house as 151you are a fool, your rival, whom she loves not. If after the 152masque we can rid her of him, you earn her love, she says, 153and the fool shall ride her.

ANTONIO

154May I believe thee?

LOLLIO

155Yes, or you may choose whether you will or no.

ANTONIO

156She’s eased of him; I have a good quarrel on’t.

LOLLIO

157Well, keep your old station yet, and be quiet.

ANTONIO

158Tell her I will deserve her love.

[Exit]

LOLLIO

159And you are like to have your desire.

Enter FRANCISCUS

FRANCISCUS

160[Sings] ‘Down, down, down a-down a-down’; and then with a horse-thrick
161To kick Latona’s forehead, and break her bowstring.

LOLLIO

162[Aside] This is t’other counterfeit; I’ll put him out of his humour. 163[Takes out letter and reads] ‘Sweet lady, having now cast 164[off] this counterfeit cover of a madman, I appear to your 165best judgement a true and faithful lover of your beauty.’ 166This is pretty well for a madman.

FRANCISCUS

167Ha! What’s that?

LOLLIO

168‘Chide those perfections in you which [have]made me 169imperfect.’

FRANCISCUS

170I am discovered to the fool.

LOLLIO

171I hope to discover the fool in you, ere I have done with 172you. ‘Yours all, or one beside himself, Franciscus.’ This 173madman will end sure.

FRANCISCUS

174What do you read, sirrah?

LOLLIO

175Your destiny, sir. You’ll be hanged for this trick, and 176another that I know.

FRANCISCUS

177Art thou of counsel with thy mistress?

LOLLIO

178Next her apron-strings.

FRANCISCUS

179Give me thy hand.

LOLLIO

180Stay, let me put yours in my pocket first. [Puts away the letter] Your hand is true is it not? It will not pick? I partly 181fear it, because I think it does lie.

FRANCISCUS

182Not in a syllable.

LOLLIO

183So; if you love my mistress so well as you have handled 184the matter here, you are like to be cured of your mad- 185ness.

FRANCISCUS

186And none but she can cure it.

LOLLIO

187Well, I’ll give you over then, and she shall cast your water 188next.

FRANCISCUS

189Take for thy pains past.

[Gives him money]

LOLLIO

190I shall deserve more, sir, I hope. My mistress loves you, 191but must have some proof of your love to her.

FRANCISCUS

192There I meet my wishes.

LOLLIO

193That will not serve: you must meet her enemy and yours.

FRANCISCUS

194He’s dead already!

LOLLIO

195Will you tell me that, and I parted but now with him?

FRANCISCUS

196Show me the man.

LOLLIO

197Ay, that’s a right course now: see him before you kill him 198in any case. And yet it needs not go so far neither. ’Tis but 199a fool that haunts the house and my mistress in the shape 200of an idiot. Bang but his fool’s coat well-favouredly, and 201’tis well.

FRANCISCUS

202Soundly, soundly!

LOLLIO

203Only reserve him till the masque be past, and if you find 204him not now in the dance yourself, I’ll show you. In, in! 205My master!

[Dances]

FRANCISCUS

206He handles him like a feather. Hey!

[Exit dancing]

Enter ALIBIUS

ALIBIUS

207Well said. In a readiness, Lollio?

LOLLIO

208Yes, sir.

ALIBIUS

209Away then, and guide them in, Lollio;
210Entreat your mistress to see this sight.

[Exit LOLLIO]

211Hark, is there not one incurable fool
212That might be begged? I have friends.

LOLLIO

213[Within] I have him for you: one that shall deserve it too.

ALIBIUS

214Good boy, Lollio.

[Enter ISABELLA, then LOLLIO with MADMEN and FOOLS]

The MADMEN and FOOLS dance

215’Tis perfect. Well, fit [we] but once these strains,
216We shall have coin and credit for our pains.

Exeunt
