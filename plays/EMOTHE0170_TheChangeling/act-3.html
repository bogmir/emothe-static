I dare not. Quickly! O, I never shall!
102Speak it yet further off, that I may lose
103What has been spoken, and no sound remain on’t.
104I would not hear so much offence again
105For such another deed.

DE FLORES

For such another deed. Soft, lady, soft!
106The last is not yet paid for. O, this act
107Has put me into spirit: I was as greedy on’t
108As the parched earth of moisture, when the clouds weep.
109Did you not mark, I wrought myself into’t,
110Nay, sued and kneeled for’t? Why was all that pains took?
111You see I have thrown contempt upon your gold:
112Not that I want it [not], for I do piteously −
113In order I will come unto’t, and make use on’t −
114But ’twas not held so precious to begin with,
115For I place wealth after the heels of pleasure;
116And were I not resolved in my belief
117That thy virginity were perfect in thee,
118I should but take my recompense with grudging,
119As if I had but half my hopes I agreed for.

BEATRICE

120Why, ’tis impossible thou canst be so wicked,

122To make his death the murderer of my honour!

With any modesty. Push, you forget yourself!
126A woman dipped in blood, and talk of modesty?

BEATRICE

127O misery of sin! Would I had been bound
128Perpetually unto my living hate
129In that Piracquo, than to hear these words!
130Think but upon the distance that creation
131Set ’twixt thy blood and mine, and keep thee there.

DE FLORES

132Look but into your conscience, read me there;
133’Tis a true book, you’ll find me there your equal.
134Push, fly not to your birth, but settle you
135In what the act has made you; y’are no more now.
136You must forget your parentage to me:
137Y’are the deed’s creature; by that name you lost

BEATRICE

And made you one with me. With thee, foul villain?

DE FLORES

150De Flores!

DE FLORES

De Flores! I shall rest from all plagues then;

152Will burn my heart to cinders.

BEATRICE

Will burn my heart to cinders. O sir, hear me!

DE FLORES

153She that in life and love refuses me,
154In death and shame my partner she shall be.

BEATRICE

155Stay, hear me once for all. [Kneels] − I make thee master
156Of all the wealth I have in gold and jewels;
157Let me go poor unto my bed with honour,
158And I am rich in all things.

DE FLORES

And I am rich in all things. Let this silence thee:
159The wealth of all Valencia shall not buy
160My pleasure from me.
161Can you weep fate from its determined purpose?
162So soon may [you] weep me.

BEATRICE

So soon may [you] weep me. Vengeance begins;
163Murder I see is followed by more sins.
164Was my creation in the womb so curst
165It must engender with a viper first?

DE FLORES

166Come, rise and shroud your blushes in my bosom.

[Raises her]

167Silence is one of pleasure’s best receipts:
168Thy peace is wrought forever in this yielding.
169’Las, how the turtle pants! Thou’lt love anon
170What thou so fear’st and faint’st to venture on.
