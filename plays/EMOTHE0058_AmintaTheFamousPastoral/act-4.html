Act IV

Scen. 1.

Daphne, Silvia and Chore of Shepherds.

DAPHNE

1367That wind that brought the sad new’s of thy death,
1368Brought with it likewise in the selfe same breath
1369Thy present and thy future ill, and thou
1370(Thanks be to God) art live and well, when now
1371I deem’d thee dead; Nerina with such greife
1372It told: had she been dumb, or others deaf.

SILVIA

1373The danger sure was great, and she indeed

DAPHNE

1374A just occasion for to tell it, though,
1375She had not, but let me thy danger know,
1376And how thou scap’dst.

SILVIA

And how thou scap’dst. I’ll tell thee, I, in chase
1377Of a fierce wolf, unto the thickest place
1378Came of the wood, so that I quite the track
1379Did of his footsteps loose, but turning back
1380Again, I spi’d him; by my shaft I knew
1381On a dead carkase they had newly slain;
1382That ʼtwas the same wolf first before me flew.
1383He with some others feeding was amain,
1384Of what I could not well discern, but he
1385Leaving his prey came running after me.
1386I stood him; with intent to make him feel,
1387My dart than arrow was the sharper steel;
1388And thou knowst well I’m mistresse of the art,
1389Of brandishing and lacing wel a dart;
1390I seldom use to misse, and so, when nigh
1391Enough I thought him, I my dart let fly,
1392But was it fault of fortune, or of me,
1393I mist the wolf, and stuck it in a tree:
1394The Wolf more fiercely at me runs, and I,
1395Seeing ʼtwas vain to use my bow, did fly:
1396He follows me, (now hear a chance,) my vail
1397Hap’d to untie, and with the murm’ring gale
1398Came through the woods, blew up and down, at last
1399Abot a bough tangled it self so fast,
1400That though my force redoubled was, my strength
1401Could not prevail a rescue, but at length,
1402Seeing no was to’scape, I thought it best
1403Quite to untie it, and there let it rest;
1404Which done, I ran again with all my might,
1405Feare adding thousand wings unto my flight,
1406That he nere joyn’d me, and I came out safe
1407Unto my lodge, where first of all I have
1408Encountred thee, and wondred much to see
1409Thee stand amaz’d, and wondring so at me.

DAPHNE

1410Thou liv’st, alas! but others not.

SILVIA

Thou liv’st, alas! but others not. doth’t greive
1411Thee Daphne then that I am still alive?
1412Hat’st thou me so?

DAPHNE

Hat’st thou me so? no Silvia, I’m full glad
1413Thou liv’st, anothers death ʼtis makes me sad.

SILVIA

1414Whose death?

DAPHNE

Whose death? Amintaʼs.

SILVIA

Whose death? Amintaʼs. Is he dead, and how?

DAPHNE

1415Well how I cannot tell, nor do I know
1416If it be certain.

SILVIA

If it be certain. Strange! but canʼt divine
1417What was the occasion of his death?

DAPHNE

What was the occasion of his death? why? thine.

SILVIA

1418I understand thee not.

DAPHNE

I understand thee not. The tidings sad
1419Brought of thy wretched death, such power had
1420O’re him, poore soul, and o’re his wearied life,
1421That I beleive ʼthas brought him cord or knife.

SILVIA

1422I hope that vaine will this suspect of thine
1423Be of his death, as vain was that of mine;
1424For when men come to the effect, they strive
1425(Let them boast what they wil befare) to live.

DAPHNE

1426Silvia, thou know’st not what the fire of love
1427Can in a heart that’s not of marble move,
1428But thine’s more hard than stone, else thou believe
1429Would’st him, for whom thou yet seem’st not to grieve;
1430And would’st have lov’d him who far more did prize
1431Thy love, than the dear apples of her ey’s.
1432I wel believ’d him, knew, and saw’t, when he
1433Having unloos’d thee (Tigresse) from the tree,
1434An act that would have forc’d thee him to love,
1435Had’st had a heart; but what is’t thee can move?
1436I say I saw him there reverse his dart
1437And with his ful force strive to pierce his heart;
1438Nor did repent him, though at first he did
1439It in his crimson gore; but once more tri’d
1440To make it enter farther, and he sure
1441Had pierc’d his heart, but I could not endure
1442To see’t, so staid him; yet believe the rage
1443Of that small wound did not at all asswage
1444His desp’rate constancy, but onely made
1445A freer passage for his thirsty blade.

SILVIA

1446What dost thou tell?

DAPHNE

What dost thou tell? I saw him too when he
1447First understood news of the death of thee
1448To swound for grief; reviv’d, he fled away
1449In fury with intent himself to slay:
1450And he’l have done it surely.

SILVIA

And he’l have done it surely. Think’st thou so.

DAPHNE

1451I cannot doubt it.

SILVIA

I cannot doubt it. Oh my Daphne, oh
1452Why followedst not to hinder him, with me
1453Come now and seek to find him; for if he,
1454Thinking me dead, resolv’d himself to kill,
1455Sure now I live, remain in life he will.

DAPHNE

1456I follow’d him, and sought him, but in vain,
1457For when he once got out of sight, again
1458I could not find him, nor his footsteps; thou
1459Then whither, prethee, wilt go seek him now?

SILVIA

1460Alas let’s go however, for if we
1461Don’t stay him, he will his own murd’rer be.

DAPHNE

1462Perhaps it grieves thee then that any one
1463Should boast of killing him, but thee alone;
1464Or cruel one dost think it scorn, his heart
1465Should wounded be by any, but thy dart?
1466Content thy self, how e’re he dies, yet he
1467Dies for thy sake, thou wilt his murd’rer be.

SILVIA

1468Ay melthy comfort’s harsh, but now I find
1469That grief of heart, which overswayes my mind;
1470For his sad chance imbittered is the more,
1471By now much I was rigorous before,
1472And the remembrance of my cruelty
1473Torments me now, I call’d it honesty,
1474And so it was, but too severe by far,
1475Too cruel since it did all pity bar;
1476Now I repent me.

DAPHNE

Now I repent me. Strange! what do I hear?
1477Pitiful thou? what can thy flint heart bear
1478The least impress’on? weep’st thou? what can move
1479These tears? are they of pity or of love?

SILVIA

1480They tears of love not, but of pity, are.

DAPHNE

1481Pity to love is alwaies messenger,
1482As lightning to the thunder.

CHORUS

As lightning to the thunder. Oft when rest
1483He hidden seeks within a virgins breast,
1484Who had with too strict honesty before,
1485Against his fly charms shut and barr’d the door:
1486He of mild pitie his fond servant takes
1487The shape and habit, and so entrance makes.

DAPHNE

1488Silvia, these tears of love be, ʼtis too plain
1489Art silent? dost thou love? thou lov’st in vain:
1490O divine force of love! how justly thou
1491Chastisest those who don’t thy pow’r avow!
1492Wretched Aminta! like the Bee who leaves
1493Sorrow to him he stings, but yet bereaves
1494Himself of life: so now thou victor art,
1495And dying wounded hast that stony heart
1496Which living thou ne’r couldst, and if thou be
1497A wandring spir’t, as I believe, then see
1498Her tears, and though to thee all joy be past,
1499Rejoyce that thou hast overcome at last.
1500Lover in life, belov’d in death; if thy
1501Fate ʼtwere not to be lov’d til thou didst die,
1502Or if this cruel one at no lesse price
1503Would fell her love, then to him for it dies;
1504See thou hast given her price she sought,
1505And with thy early death, her love hast bought.

CHORUS

1506Dear price to him that gave it, but more griev-
1507Ous far (alas) to her did it receive.

SILVIA

1508Could I but with my love his life regain,
1509Or with my life call him from death again!

DAPHNE

1510Ah pitiful too late! and too late wife,
1511Pity assists not where fate help denies.

Scen. 2.

Ergasto Chore of Shepherds Silvia Daphne.

ERGASTO

1512So full my breast of pity is, and so
1513Clogd up with horror, that I do not know
1514Whither to turn me, nothing comes to fight,
1515Nothing I hear, which doth not me affright.

CHORUS

1516What newes brings our Ergasto? sure ʼtis bad,
1517His countenance and accent both are sad.

ERGASTO

1518I bring the sad news of Aminta’s death.

SILVIA

1519Ay me!

ERGASTO

Ay me! The noblest shepherd that did breath
1520Within those woods; whose sweet comportments were
1521Unto the nymphs, and muses both, so dear.

CHORUS

1522And is he dead? and how? relate that we
1523May his untimely end lament with thee.

SILVIA

1524I dare not listen to this sad discourse,
1525Nor yet go near to hear it, though of force
1526Hear it I must, wicked relentlesse heart
1527Of mine, what fear’st thou? go receive that dart
1528Which he brings in his tongue; that so there-by
1529May be made known thy heartlesse cruelty.
1530Shepherd I come a partner here to be
1531Of thy grief, which thou promist’s others, me
1532Perhaps it more concerns, and as a due
1533Debt of my rigor I receiv’t from you.

ERGASTO

1534Nymph, I believe thee well; for at his death
1535He with thy name gave up his latest breath.

DAPHNE

1536Now begin this sad story.

ERGASTO

Now begin this sad story. I surround
1537The Mount did with some nets, spread on the ground,
1538When, lo, Amintas passed by, and he
1539Was too much chang’d from what he us’d to be,
1540Too troubled, and too sad; I ran and staid
1541Him, though with much ado; he to me staid:
1542Ergasto I’d intreat a courtesie
1543Of thee, ʼtis this, to come along with me,
1544And be a witnesse of what I shal do,
1545But this I shal requier first of you,
1546That by streight oath your firmest faith you bind
1547Neither to hinder nor oppose my mind;
1548I (for who could have thought so strange a case,
1549Or such mad fury?) let his will take place
1550And did conjure, Pan, Pales, Priapus,
1551And all those gods are honour’d most by us;
1552He at my oaths went forward, and me led
1553There where the cliffy rock hangs o’re it’s head:
1554Road it was none, for there no path at all,
1555But from the mount, a precipice doth fall
1556Into th’adjacent valley; here a stop
1557We made, when I with looking from the top,
1558Such was the height, so level to the ground,
1559That with a giddinesse my head turn’d round;
1560I started back, he smil’d to see me start,
1561Which too much did assure my fearlesse heart.
1562Then to me said, Ergasto, I’d have thee
1563Recount to Nymphs and Pastors what thou’lt see,
1564So looking down began —
1565Had I here ready to my will,
1566Thee teeth of rav’nous wolves to spill
1567My blood, I’d onely choose that death,
1568By which she di’d who was my breath:
1569They tear this body should of mine,
1570As they did that fair corps of thine;
1571But since that heav’n is so unjust
1572As to deny, (though die I must)
1573My death desired, nor will send
1574Fierce beasts to help me to my end;
1575I’l find a death, though not the due,
1576Shall end my life as soon as you.
1577Silvia I come, I come to thee,
1578Disdain not then my company;
1579Contentedly I shall endure
1580The sharpest death, were I but sure
1581ʼTwould not again begin our strife,
1582Thy anger ceas’d were with thy life.
1583Threw himself down; I frozen stood like Ice.

DAPHNE

1584Wretched Aminta!

SILVIA

Wretched Aminta! Ay poor me!

CHORUS

Wretched Aminta! Ay poor me! But why
1585Some way to hinder him didst thou not try?
1586Perhaps thy promis’d oath did thee refrain.

ERGASTO

1587No; for, rejecting oaths in that case vain
1588When I discern’d his purpose, though too late,
1589I ran to stay him; such was his hard fate,
1590But he went with such force it could not bear
1591His body’s weight, and so it did remain
1592Thus broken in my hands.

CHORUS

Thus broken in my hands. But what became
1593Of the unhappy corps?

ERGASTO

Of the unhappy corps? That know not I,
1594I had not heart enough to see it lie:
1595(Pity and horror having fill’d the place)

CHORUS

(Pity and horror having fill’d the place) O strange case!

SILVIA

1596If this newes kil’s not me, my heart’s of stone,
1597Or rather I believe that I have none;
1598Shall the false rumour of my death have such
1599Power o’re his life, whom I did hate so much?
1600And shall not his true death, who life did leave
1601For love of me, me of my life bereave?
1602It shall: and if that griefe can’t do’t, a knife,
1603Or else this girdle shall command my life;
1604This girdle left behind, alone to be
1605The just revenger of my cruelty;
1606Unhappy girdle of a Master farr
1607Unhappier, disdaine not that you are
1608Lodg’d in this justly to thee hatefull brest,
1609Since there thou as an instrument do’st rest
1610Of that revenge, which is in justice due
1611To your so haplesse Master, and to you;
1612For since twas by my cruelty deni’d
1613Aminta should my confort here abide,
1614ʼTis just I should by work of thine be made
1615His confort, in the sweet Elizian shade.

CHORUS

1616Comfort thy selfe poore wretch, thou fault hast none
1617In this, but fault of fortune ʼtis alone.

SILVIA

1618Shepherds, weep not for me, your tears you loose,
1619Those pity don’t deserve, who none did use,
1620Or if we weep, his losse ye weep in vain,
1621Since that deserves a far more dolefull streine;
1622And thou too, Daphne, dry those teares of thine,
1623If from no other cause they come than mine;
1624This I’d intreat thee do, not for my sake,
1625But for his worthy was, the pains to take,
1626To go with me to see those wretched bones,
1627I feare me, crush’d among the rocks and stones,
1628And helpe to bury them, and when that I
1629Have perform’d this, what should I do but dy?
1630This office I’l repay him, since no more,
1631I can, for that great love which he me bore;
1632And though these hands of mine contaminate
1633Will this good work: yet since pernicious fate
1634Permits me only to do this; I know
1635It will be deare to him, he lov’d me so.

DAPHNE

1636Who would their help to such a work deny?
1637But afterwards thou must not think to dy.

SILVIA

1638Till now I liv’d, have to my selfe alone,
1639And to my cruelty, what rests, to none
1640I’l live but to Aminta: and if fade,
1641My hopes do there, I’l live to his cold shade,
1642So long I’l live, till in one point can I
1643Finish his saddest obsequies, and dy;
1644Shepherd direct me, that I may not faile
1645To find the way which leads unto the vale;
1646Where ends the cursed precipice.

ERGASTO

Where ends the cursed precipice. You are
1647Just in the way, nor it is very far.

DAPHNE

1648Come, I’l go with thee, thou my foot steps trace,
1649And I’l thee lead directly to the place.

SILVIA

1650Then Shepherds, Nimphs, Meadows, and Plaines adieu;
1651Woods, Rivers, Fountains, all farewell to you.

ERGASTO

1652Alas! she takes her leave with sighs so sore,
1653As if she were resolv’d to turn no more.

CHORUS

1654What death must yeeld, that love retains,
1655Thou peace’s friend, he wars unkind,
1656Ever his triumphs, vaunts and reignes;
1657And whil’st thoudo’st twofair souls bind;
1658Thou rendr’st earth so like the heavenly sphear,
1659That thou thy selfe deign’st to inhabit here;
1660There is no anger there above
1661And thou hast mortal brest inclin’d
1662By thy divinest art of love;
1663To put all hatred out of mind;
1664And with a pow’r exceeding earthly Kings
1665Thou here below dost govern mortall things.
