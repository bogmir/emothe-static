[Act I, Scene i]

Enter Count LODOVICO, ANTONELLI and GASPARO

LODOVICO

1Banish’d?

ANTONELLI

2It griev’d me much to hear the sentence.

LODOVICO

3Ha, ha, O Democritus thy gods
4That govern the whole world! Fortune’s a right whore.
5If she gives ought, she deals it in small parcels.
6That she may take away all at one swoop.
7This ‘tis to have great enemies, God quite them:
8Your wolf no longer seems to be a wolf
9That when she’s hungry.

GASPARO

10Are men of princely rank

LODOVICO

11Oh I pray for them.
12The violent thunder is adored by those
13Are pash’d in pieces by it.

ANTONELLI

14You are justly doom’d; look but a little back
15Into yout former life: you have in three years
16Ruin’d the noblest cardiom –

GASPARO

17Your followers
18Have swallowed you like mummia, and being sick
19With such unnatural and horrid physic
20Vomit you up I’th’ kennel –

ANTONELLI

21All the damnable degrees
22Of drinkings have you stagger’d through; one citizen
23Is lord of two fair manors, call’d you master
24Only for caviare.

GASPARO

Only for caviare. Those noblemen
25Which were invited to your prodigal feasts,
26Wherein the phoenix scarce could scape your throats,
27Laugh at your misery, as fore deeming you
28An idle meteor which drawn forth the earth
29Would be soon lost I’th’air.

ANTONELLI

Would be soon lost I’th’air. Jest upon you,
30And say you were begotten in an earthquake,
31You have ruin’d such fair lordships.

LODOVICO

You have ruin’d such fair lordships. Very good,
32This well goes with two buckets, I must tend
33The pouring out of either.

GASPARO

The pouring out of either. Worse than these,
34You have acted certain murders here in Rome,
35Bloody and full of horror.

LODOVICO

Bloody and full of horror. ‘Las they were flea-bitings:
36Why took they not my head then?

GASPARO

Why took they not my head then? O my lord
37The law doth sometimes mediate, thinks it good
38Not ever to steep violent sins in blood;
39This gentle penance may both en your crimes,
40And in the example better these bad times.

LODOVICO

41So; but I wonder then some great men scape
42This banishment; there’s Paulo Giordano Orsini,
43The Duke of Brachiano, now lives in Rome,
44And by close panderism seeks to prostitute
45The honour of Vittoria Corombona:
46Vittoria, she that might have got my pardon
47For one kiss to the Duke.

ANTONELLI

48Have a full man within you.
49We see that trees bear no such pleasant fruit
50There where they grew first, as where they are new set.
51Perfumes the more they are chaf’d the more they render
52Their pleasing scents, and so affliction
53Expresseth virtue, fully, whether true,
54Or else adulterate.

LODOVICO

Or else adulterate. Leave your painted conforts.
55I’ll make Italian cut-works in their guts
56If ever I return.

GASPARO

If ever I return. O sir.

LODOVICO

If ever I return. O sir. I am patient.
57I have seen some ready to be executed
58Give pleasant looks, and money, and grown familiar
59With the knave hangman; so do I, I thank them,
60And would account them nobly merciful
61Would they dispatch me quickly.

ANTONELLI

Would they dispatch me quickly. Fare you well,
62We shall find time I doubt not, to repeal
63[A sennet sounds.] Your banishment.

LODOVICO

[A sennet sounds.] Your banishment. I am ever bound to you;
64This is the world’s alms; pray make use of it:
65Great men sell sheep, thus to be cut in pieces,
66When first they have shorn them bare and sold their fleeces.

Exeunt

[Act I, Scene ii]

Enter BRACHIANO, CAMILLO, FLAMINEO, VITTORIA, CORMBONA [and ATTENDANTS]

BRACHIANO

1Your best of rest.

VITTORIA

Your best of rest. Unto my lord the Duke,
2The best of welcome. More lights, attend the Duke.

[Exeunt VITTORIA and CAMILLO]

BRACHIANO

3Flamineo.

FLAMINEO

Flamineo. My lord.

BRACHIANO

Flamineo. My lord. Quite lost Flamineo.

FLAMINEO

4Pursue your noble wishes. I am promt
5As lightning to your service, O my lord!
6(whispers) The fair Vittoria, my happy sister
7Shall give you present audience. Gentelmen
8Let the caroche go on, and ‘tis his pleasure
9You put out all your torches and depart.

[Exeunt ATTENDANTS]

BRACHIANO

10Are we so happy?

FLAMINEO

Are we so happy? Can’t be otherwise?
11Observ’d you not tonight my honour’d lord,
12Wich way so e’er you went she threw her eyes?
13I have dealt already with her chamber-maid
14Zanche the Moor, and she is wondrous proud
15To be the agent for so high a spirit.

BRACHIANO

16We are happy above thought, because ‘bove merit.

FLAMINEO

17‘Bove merit! We may now talk freely: ‘bove merit; what is’t
18you doubt? Her coyness? That’s but the superficies of lust
19most women have; yet why should ladies blush to hear that
20nam’d, which they do not fear to handle? O they are politic!
21They know our desire is increas’d by the difficulty of
22enjoying; whereas satiety is a blunt, weary and drowsy
23passion; if the buttery-hatch at court stood continually
24open there would be nothing so passionate crowding, nor
25hot suit after the brevage,—

BRACHIANO

hot suit after the brevage,— O but her jealous husband.

FLAMINEO

26Hang him, a gilder that hath his brains perish’d with
27Quicksilver is not more cold in the liver. The great barriers
28moulted not more feathers than he hath shed hairs, by the
29confession of his doctor. An Irish gamester that will play
30himself nacked, and then wage all downward, at hazard, is
31not more venturous. So unable to please a woman that
32like a Dutch doublet all his back is shrunk into his breeches.
33Shroud you within this closet, good my lord;
34Some trick now must be thought in to divide
35My brother-in-law from his fair bed-fellow.

BRACHIANO

36O should she fail to come, —

FLAMINEO

37I must not have your lordship thus unwisely amorous; I
38myself have loved a lady and pursued her with a great deal
39of under-age protestation, whom some 3 or 4 gallants that
40have been rid of. ‘Tis just like a summer bird-cage in a
41garden: the birds that are within despair and are in a consumption
42for fear they shall never get out. Away, away my lord,

Enter CAMILLO

43See, here he comes.

[Exit BRACHIANO;]

44[FLAMINEO speaks aside to this fellow by his apparel.] Some men would judge a politician,
45But call his wit in question you shall find it
46[To CAMILLO] How now, brother,
47Merely an ass in’s foot-cloth.
48What, travelling to bed to your kind wife?

CAMILLO

49I assure you brother, no. My voyage lies
50More northerly, in a far colder clime;
51I do not well remember, I protest,
52When I last lay with her.

FLAMINEO

When I last lay with her. Strange you should lose your count.

CAMILLO

53We never lay together bur ere morning
54There grew a flaw between us.

FLAMINEO

There grew a flaw between us. ‘T had been your part
55To have made up that flaw.

CAMILLO

To have made up that flaw. True, but she loathes
56I should be seen in’t.

FLAMINEO

I should be seen in’t. Why sir, what’s the matter?

CAMILLO

57The Duke your master visits me, I thank him,
58And I perceive how like an earnest bowler
59He very passionately leans that way,
60He should have his bowl run.

FLAMINEO

He should have his bowl run. I hope you do not think —

CAMILLO

61That noblemen bowl booty? ‘Faith his cheek
62Hath a most excellent bias; it would fain
63Jump with my mistress.

FLAMINEO

Jump with my mistress. Will you be an ass
64Despite your Aristotle or a cuckold
65Contrary to your ephemerides
66Wich shows you under what a smiling planet
67You were first swaddled?

CAMILLO

You were first swaddled? Per wew, sir tell not me
68Of planets nor of ephemerides.
69A man may be made cuckold in the day-time
70When the stars’ eyes are out.

FLAMINEO

When the stars’ eyes are out. Sir God boy you,
71I do commit you to your pitiful pillow
72Stuff’d with horn-shavings.

CAMILLO

Stuff’d with horn-shavings. Brother?

FLAMINEO

Stuff’d with horn-shavings. Brother? God refuse me,
73Might I advise you now your only course
74Were to lock up your wife.

CAMILLO

Were to lock up your wife. ‘Twere very good.

FLAMINEO

75Bar her the sight of revels.

CAMILLO

Bar her the sight of revels. Excellent.

FLAMINEO

76Let her not go to church, but like a hound
77In leon at your heels.

CAMILLO

In leon at your heels. ‘Twere for her honour.

FLAMINEO

78And so you should be certain in one fortnight,
79Despite her chastity or innocence
80To be cuckolded, which yet is in suspense:
81This is my counsel and I ask no fee for’t.

CAMILLO

Come, you know not where my nightcap wrings me.

FLAMINEO

Wear it o’th’ old fashion, let your large ears come through, it will be more easy; nay, I will be bitter; bar your wife of her entertainment: women are more willingly and more gloriously chaste, when they are least restrained of their liberty. It seems you would be a fine capricious mathematically jealous coxcomb, take the height of your own horns with a Jacob’s staff afore they are up. These politic enclosures for paltry mutton makes more rebellion in the flesh than all the provocative electuaries doctors have uttered since last Jubilee.

CAMILLO

This does not physic me.

FLAMINEO

It seems you are jealous. I’ll show you the error of it by a familiar example: I have seen a pair of spectacles fashion’d with such perspective art, that lay down but one twelve pence o’th’ board, ‘twill appear as if there were twenty; now should you wear a pair of these spectacles, and see your wife tying her shoe, you would imagine twenty hands were taking up of your wife’s clothes, and this would put you into a horrible causeless fury.

CAMILLO

The fault there sir is not in the eyesight—

FLAMINEO

Enter [VITTORIA] COROMBONA True, but they that have the yellow jaundice, think all objects they look on to be yellow. Jealousy is worser, her fits present to a man, like so many bubbles in a basin of water, twenty several crabbed faces; many times majes his own shadow his cuckold-maker. [aside to VITTORIA] my lord attends you in the banqueting-house your husband is wondrous discontented. See she comes; what reason have you to be jealous of this creature? What an ignorant ass or flattering knave might he be counted, that should write sonnets to her eyes, or call her brow the snow of Ida, or ivory of Cornith, or compare her hair to the blackbird’s bill, when ‘tis liker the black- bird’s feather. This is all. Be wise; I will make you, it shall not be your seeking, do you stand upon that by any means; walk you aloof, I would not have you seen in’t. Sister,

VITTORIA

I did nothing to displease him, I carved to him at supper-time.

FLAMINEO

[aside to VITTORIA] You need not have carved him in faith, they say he is a capon already. I must now seemingly fall out with you. –Shall a gentleman so well descended as Camillo- a lousy slave that within this twenty years rode with the black-guard in the Duke’s carriage ‘mongst spits and dripping-pans,—

CAMILLO

Now he benigs to tickle her.

FLAMINEO

An excellent scholar, -one that hath a head fill’d with calves’ brains without any sage in them,-come crouching in the hams to you for a night’s lodging-that hath an itch in’s hams, which like the fire at the glass-house hath not gone out this seven years.-Is he not a courtly gentleman?- When he wears white stain one would take him by his black muzzle to be no other creature than a maggot.-You are a goodly foil, I confess, well set out-but cover’d with a false stone yon counterfeit diamond.

CAMILLO

He will make her know what is in me.

FLAMINEO

Come, my lord attends you; thou shalt go to bed to my lord.

CAMILLO

Now he comes to’t.

FLAMINEO

With a relish as curious as a vintner going to taste new wine. [To CAMILLO] I am opening you case hard.

CAMILLO

A virtuous brother, o’ my credit.

FLAMINEO

He will give thee a ring with a philosopher’s stone in it.

CAMILLO

Indeed I am studying alchemy.

FLAMINEO

Thou shalt lie in a bed stuff’d with turtles’ feathers, swoon in perfumed linen like the fellow was smothered in roses; so perfect shall be thy happiness, that as men at sea think land and trees and ships go that way they go, so both heaven and earth shall seem to go your voyage. Shalt meet him, ‘tis fix’d, with nails of diamonds to inevitable necessity.

VITTORIA

[aside to FLAMINEO] How shall’s rid him hence?

FLAMINEO

I will put breese in’s tail, set him gadding presently. [To CAMILLO] I have almost wrought her to it, I find her coming, but might I advise you now for this night I would not lie with her; I would cross her humour to make her more humble.

CAMILLO

Shall I? Shall I?

FLAMINEO

It will show in you a supremacy of judgement.

CAMILLO

True, and a mind differing from the tumultuary opinion, for quae negate grata.

FLAMINEO

Right: you are the adamant shall draw her to you, though you keep distance off.

CAMILLO

A philosophical reason.

FLAMINEO

Walk by her o’ the nobleman’s fashion, and tell her you will lie with her at the end of the progress.

CAMILLO

Vittoria, I cannot be induc’d or as a man would say incited—

VITTORIA

To do what sir?

CAMILLO

To lie with you tonight; your silkworm useth to fast everythird day, and the next following spins the better. Tomorrow at night I am for you.

VITTORIA

You’ll spin a fair thread, trust to’t.

FLAMINEO

But do you hear, I shall have you steal to her chamber about midnight.

CAMILLO

Do you think so? Why look you brother, because you shall not think I’ll gull you, take the key, lock me into the chamber, and say you shall be sure of me.

FLAMINEO

115In troth I will, I’ll be your jailer once;
116But have you ne’er a false door?

CAMILLO

A pox on’t, as I am a Christian tell me tomorrow how scurvily she takes my unkink parting.

FLAMINEO

118I will.

CAMILLO

119Didst thou not mark the jest of the silkworm?
120Good night: in faith I will use this trick often, —

Exit CAMILLO

FLAMINEO

121Do , do, do.
122So now you are safe. Ha ha ha, thou entanglest thyself

Enter BRACHIANO

123in thine own work like a silkworm.
124Come sister, darkness hides your blush; women are like
125curs’d dogs, civility keeps tied all daytime, but they
126are let loose at midnight; then they do most good or most
127mischief. My lord, my lord.

BRACHIANO

128Give credit: I could wish time would stand still
129And never end this interview, this hour,
130But all delight doth itself soon’st devour

ZANCHE brings out a carpet, spreads it and lays on it two fair cushions. Enter CORELLA [listening, behind].

131Let me into your bosom happy lady,
132Pour out instead of eloquence my vows;
133Loose me not madam, for it you forgo me
134I am lost eternally.

VITTORIA

I am lost eternally. Sir in the way of pity
135I wish you heart-whole.

BRACHIANO

I wish you heart-whole. You are a sweet physician.

VITTORIA

136Sure sir a loathed cruelty in ladies
137Is as to doctors many funerals.
138It takes away their credit.

BRACHIANO

It takes away their credit. Excellent creature.
139We call the cruel fair, what name for you
140That are so merciful?

[Embraces her]

ZANCHE

That are so merciful? See now they close.

FLAMINEO

That are so merciful? See now they close. Most happy union.

CORNELIA

141My fears are fall’n upon me, oh my heart!aparte
142My son the pander: now I find our houseaparte
143Sinking to ruin. Earthquakes leave behind,aparte
144Where they have tyrannized, iron or lead, or stone,aparte
145But, woe to ruin! Violent lust leaves none.aparte

BRACHIANO

146What value is this jewel?

VITTORIA

What value is this jewel? 'Tis the ornament
147Of a weak fortune.

BRACHIANO

148In sooth I'll have it; nay I will but change
149My jewel for your jewel.

FLAMINEO

My jewel for your jewel. Excellent,
150His jewel for her jewel; well put in Duke.

BRACHIANO

151Nay let me see you wear it.

VITTORIA

Nay let me see you wear it. Here sir.

BRACHIANO

152Nay lower, you shall wear my jewel lower.

FLAMINEO

153That's better; she must wear his jewel lower.

VITTORIA

154To pass away the time I'll tell your grace
155A dream I had last night.

BRACHIANO

156Most wishedly.

VITTORIA

Most wishedly. A foolish idle dream:
157Methought I walk'd about the mid of night,
158Into a church-yard, where a goodly yew-tree
159Spread her large root in ground; under that yew,
160As I sat sadly leaning on a grave,
161Checkered with cross-sticks, there came stealling in
162Your Duchess and my husband; one of them
163A pick-axe bore, th'other a rusty spade,
164And in rough terms they gan to challenge me,
165About this yew.

BRACHIANO

About this yew. That tree.

VITTORIA

About this yew. That tree. This harmless yew.
166They told me my intent was to root up
167That well-grown yew, and plant i'th' stead of it
168A withered blackthorn, and for that they vow'd
169To bury me alive: my husband straigh
170With pick-axe gan to dig, and your fell Duchess
171With shovel, like a fury, voided out
172The earth and scattered bones. Lord how methought
173I trembled, and yet for all this terror
174I could not pray.

FLAMINEO

I could not pray. No the devil was in your dream.

VITTORIA

175When to my rescue there arose me thought
176A whirlwind, which let fall a massy arm
177From that strong plant,
178And both were struck dead by that scared yew
179In that base shallow grave that was their due.

FLAMINEO

180Excellent devil.
181She hath taught him in a dream
182To make away his Duchess and her husband.

BRACHIANO

183Sweetly shall I interpret this your dream.
184You are lodged within his arms who shall protect you,
185From all the fevers of a jealous husband,
186From the poor envy of our phlegmatic Duchess;
187I'll seat you above law and above scandal,
188Give to your thoughts the invention of delight
189And the fruition; nor shall government
190Divide me from you longer than a care
191To keep you great: you shall to me at once
192Be dukedom, health, wife, children friends and all.

CORNELIA

193[approaching them] Woe to light hearts, they still forerun our fall.

FLAMINEO

194What fury rais'd thee up? Away, away!

Exit ZANCHE

CORNELIA

195What make you here my lord this dead of night?
196Never dropp'd mildew on a flower here,
197Till now.

FLAMINEO

Till now. I pray you go to bed then,
198Lest you be blasted?

CORNELIA

Lest you be blasted? O that this fair garden,
199Had with all poison'd herbs of Thessaly,
200At first been planted, made a nursery
201For wichcraft; rather than a burial plot
202For both your honours.

VITTORIA

For both your honours. Dearest mother hear me.

CORNELIA

203O thou dost make my brow bend to the earth,
204Sooner than nature; see the curse of children:
205In life they keep us frequently in tears,
206And in the cold grave leave us in pale fears.

BRACHIANO

207Come, come , I will not hear you.

VITTORIA

Come, come , I will not hear you. Dear my lord.

CORNELIA

208Where is thy Duchess now adulterous Duke?
209Thou little dream'd'st this night she is come to Rome.

FLAMINEO

210How? Come to Rome, —

VITTORIA

How? Come to Rome, — The Duchess, —

BRACHIANO

How? Come to Rome, — The Duchess, — She had been better,—

CORNELIA

211The lives of princes should like dials move,
212Whose regular example is so strong,
213They make the times by them go right or wrong.

FLAMINEO

214So, have you done?

CORNELLIA

So, have you done? Unfortunate Camillo.

VITTORIA

215I do protest if any chaste denial,
216If anything but blood could have allayed
217His long suit to me —

CORNELLIA

His long suit to me — I will join with thee,
218To the most woeful end e'er mother kneel'd,
219If thou dishonour thus thy husband's bed,
220Be thy life short as are the funeral tears
221In great men's.

BRACHIANO

In great men's. Fie, fie, the woman's mad.

CORNELIA

222Be thy act Judas-like, betray in kissing;
223May'st thou be envied during his short breath,
224And pitied like a wretch after his death.

VITTORIA

225O me accurs'd.

Exit VITTORIA

FLAMINEO

O me accurs'd. Are you out of your wits, my lord?
226I'll fetch her back again.

BRACHIANO

I'll fetch her back again. No I'll to bed.
227Send Doctor Julio to me presently.
228Uncharitable woman, thy rash tongue
229Hath rais'd a fearful and prodigious storm,
230Be thou the cause of all ensuing harm.

Exit BRACHIANO

FLAMINEO

231Now, you that stand so much upon your honour,
232Is this a fitting time o' night think you,
233To send a duke home without e'er a man?
234I would fain know where lies the mass of wealth
235Which you have hoarded for my maintenance,
236That I may bear my beard out of the level
237Of my lord's stirrup.

CORNELIA

Of my lord's stirrup. What? Because we are poor
238Shall we be vicious?

FLAMINEO

Shall we be vicious? Pray what means have you
239To keep me from the galleys, or the gallows?
240My father prov'd himself a gentleman,
241Sold all's land, and like a fortunate fellow,
242Died ere the money was spent. You brought me up,
243At Padua I confess, where, I protest,
244For want of means, (the university judge me,)
245I have been fain to heel my tutor's stockings
246At least seven years. Conspiring with a beard
247Made me a graduate, then to this Duke's service;
248I visited the court, whence I return'd —
249More courteous, more lecherous by far,
250But not a suit the richer; and shall I,
251Having a path so open and so free
252To my preferment, still retain your milk
253In my pale forehead? No, this face of mine
254I'll arm and fortify with lusty wine
255'Gainst shame and blushing.

CORNELIA

256O that I ne'er had borne thee, —

FLAMINEO

O that I ne'er had borne thee, — So would I.
257I would the common'st courtezan in Rome
258Had been my mother rather than thyself.
259Nature is very pitiful to whores
260To give them but few children, yet those children
261Plurality of fathers; they are sure
262They shall not want. Go, go,
263Complain unto my great lord cardinal,
264Yet may be he will justify the act.
265Lycurgus wond'red much men would provide
266Good stallions for their mares, and yet would suffer
267Their fair wives to be barren, —

CORNELIA

Their fair wives to be barren, — Misery of miseries.

Exit CORNELIA

FLAMINEO

268The Duchess come to court? I like not that;
269We are engag'd to mischief and must on.
270As rivers to find out the ocean
271Flow with crook bendings beneath forced banks,
272Or as we see, to aspire some mountain's top
273The way ascends not straight, but imitates
274The subtle foldings of a winter's snake,
275So who knows policy and her true aspect,
276Shall find her ways winding and indirect.

Exit
