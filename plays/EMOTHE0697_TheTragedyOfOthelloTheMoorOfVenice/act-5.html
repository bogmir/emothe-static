Enter Othello [with a light], and Desdemona in her bed.

Othello

2488It is the cause, it is the cause, my soul.
2489Let me not name it to you, you chaste stars.
2490It is the cause. Yet I’ll not shed her blood,
2491Nor scar that whiter skin of hers than snow
2492And smooth as monumental alabaster—
2493Yet she must die, else she’ll betray more men.
2494Put out the light, and then put out the light—
2495If I quench thee, thou flaming minister,
2496I can again thy former light restore
2497Should I repent me. But once put out thy light,
2498Thou cunning’st pattern of excelling nature,
2499I know not where is that Promethean heat
2500That can thy light relume. When I have plucked thy rose,
2501I cannot give it vital growth again;
2502It needs must wither. I’ll smell thee on the tree.

[He kisses her.]

2503O balmy breath, that dost almost persuade
2504Justice to break her sword—one more, one more.

[He kisses her.]

2505Be thus when thou art dead, and I will kill thee
2506And love thee after—one more, and that’s the last.

[He kisses her.]

2507So sweet was ne’er so fatal. I must weep,
2508But they are cruel tears. This sorrow’s heavenly;
2509It strikes where it doth love. She wakes.

2510Who’s there? Othello?

Desdemona

2746That with this little arm and this good sword
2747I have made my way through more impediments
2748Than twenty times your stop. But—O vain boast!—
2749Who can control his fate? ’Tis not so now.
2750Be not afraid, though you do see me weaponed.
2751Here is my journey’s end, here is my butt
2752And very seamark of my utmost sail.
2753Do you go back dismayed? ’Tis a lost fear.
2754Man but a rush against Othello’s breast
2755And he retires. Where should Othello go?
2756Now, how dost thou look now? O ill-starred wench,
2757Pale as thy smock; when we shall meet at compt,
2758This look of thine will hurl my soul from heaven
2759And fiends will snatch at it. Cold, cold, my girl?
2760Even like thy chastity. O cursèd, cursèd slave!
2761Whip me, ye devils, from the possession of this heavenly sight,
2762Blow me about in winds, roast me in sulfur,
2763Wash me in steep-down gulfs of liquid fire—
2764O Desdemon! Dead Desdemon! Dead—Oh, Oh!

Enter Lodovico, Cassio [in a chair], Montano, and Iago with officers.

Lodovico

2765Where is this rash and most unfortunate man?

Othello

2766That’s he that was Othello; here I am.

Lodovico

2767Where is that viper? Bring the villain forth.

Othello

2768I look down toward his feet, but that’s a fable;
2769If that thou be’st a devil, I cannot kill thee.

[Othello wounds Iago.]

Lodovico

2770Wrench his sword from him.

Iago

Wrench his sword from him. I bleed, sir, but not killed.

Othello

2771I am not sorry neither; I’d have thee live,
2772For in my sense ’tis happiness to die.

Lodovico

2773O thou Othello, that was once so good,
2774Fallen in the practice of a damnèd slave,
2775What shall be said to thee?

Othello

What shall be said to thee? Why, anything—
2776An honorable murderer, if you will,
2777For naught I did in hate, but all in honor.

Lodovico

2778This wretch hath part confessed his villainy.
2779Did you and he consent in Cassio’s death?

Othello

2780Ay.

Cassio

2781Dear general, I never gave you cause.

Othello

2782I do believe it, and I ask your pardon.
2783Will you, I pray, demand that demi-devil
2784Why he hath thus ensnared my soul and body?

Iago

2785Demand me nothing. What you know, you know.
2786From this time forth, I never will speak word.

Lodovico

2787What, not to pray?

Gratiano

What, not to pray? Torments will ope your lips.

Othello

2788Well, thou dost best.

Lodovico

2789Sir, you shall understand what hath befallen,
2790Which, as I think, you know not. Here is a letter
2791Found in the pocket of the slain Roderigo,
2792And here another. The one of them imports
2793The death of Cassio, to be undertook
2794By Roderigo.

Othello

2795O villain!

Cassio

O villain! Most heathenish and most gross!

Lodovico

2796Now here’s another discontented paper
2797Found in his pocket too; and this, it seems,
2798Roderigo meant t’have sent this damnèd villain,
2799But that, belike, Iago in the interim
2800Came in and satisfied him.

Othello

2801O thou pernicious caitiff!
2802How came you, Cassio, by that handkerchief
2803That was my wife’s?

Cassio

That was my wife’s? I found it in my chamber,
2804And he himself confessed it but even now
2805That there he dropped it for a special purpose
2806Which wrought to his desire.

Othello

Which wrought to his desire. O fool, fool, fool!

Cassio

2807There is besides in Roderigo’s letter
2808How he upbraids Iago that he made him
2809Brave me upon the watch, whereon it came
2810That I was cast; and even but now he spake,
2811After long seeming dead, Iago hurt him,
2812Iago set him on.

Lodovico

2813[To Othello] You must forsake this room and go with us.
2814Your power and your command is taken off,
2815And Cassio rules in Cyprus. For this slave,
2816If there be any cunning cruelty
2817That can torment him much and hold him long,
2818It shall be his. You shall close prisoner rest,
2819Till that the nature of your fault be known
2820To the Venetian state. Come, bring away.

Othello

2821Soft you, a word or two before you go.
2822I have done the state some service, and they know’t;
2823No more of that. I pray you in your letters,
2824When you shall these unlucky deeds relate,
2825Speak of me as I am. Nothing extenuate,
2826Nor set down aught in malice. Then must you speak
2827Of one that loved not wisely, but too well;
2828Of one not easily jealous, but, being wrought,
2829Perplexed in the extreme; of one whose hand,
2830Like the base Indian, threw a pearl away
2831Richer than all his tribe; of one whose subdued eyes,
2832Albeit unused to the melting mood,
2833Drops tears as fast as the Arabian trees
2834Their medicinable gum. Set you down this,
2835And say besides that in Aleppo once,
2836Where a malignant and a turbaned Turk
2837Beat a Venetian and traduced the state,
2838I took by th’throat the circumcisèd dog
2839And smote him—thus.

[Othello stabs himself.]

Lodovico

2840O bloody period.

Gratiano

O bloody period. All that is spoke is marred.

Othello

2841I kissed thee ere I killed thee—no way but this,
2842Killing myself, to die upon a kiss.

[Othello kisses Desdemona and] dies.

Cassio

2843This did I fear, but thought he had no weapon,
2844For he was great of heart.

Lodovico

For he was great of heart. O Spartan dog,
2845More fell than anguish, hunger, or the sea,
2846Look on the tragic loading of this bed.
2847This is thy work. The object poisons sight;
2848Let it be hid. Gratiano, keep the house
2849And seize upon the fortunes of the Moor,
2850For they succeed on you. [To Cassio] To you, lord governor,
2851Remains the censure of this hellish villain;
2852The time, the place, the torture, oh, enforce it.
2853Myself will straight aboard, and to the state
2854This heavy act with heavy heart relate.

Exeunt.
