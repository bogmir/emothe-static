2224To beggarly divorcement—love him dearly,
2225Comfort forswear me.
Comfort forswear me. [She stands.] Unkindness may do much,
2226And his unkindness may defeat my life,
2227But never taint my love. I cannot say “whore”.
2228It does abhor me now I speak the word;
2229To do the act that might the addition earn,
2230Not the world’s mass of vanity could make me.

Iago

2231I pray you be content; ’tis but his humor.
2232The business of the state does him offense,
2233And he does chide with you.

Desdemona

2234If ’twere no other—

Iago

If ’twere no other— It is but so, I warrant.

[Trumpets within]

2235Hark how these instruments summon to supper.
2236The messengers of Venice stays the meat.
2237Go in, and weep not; all things shall be well.

(Exeunt Desdemona and Emilia.)

(Enter Roderigo.)

2238How now Roderigo?

Roderigo

2239I do not find that thou deal’st justly with me.

Iago

What in the contrary?

Roderigo

Every day thou doff’st me with some device, Iago, and rather, as it seems to me now, keep’st from me all conveniency, then suppliest me with the least advantage of hope. I will indeed no longer endure it. Nor am I yet persuaded to put up in peace what already I have foolishly suffered.

Iago

Will you hear me, Roderigo?

Roderigo

Faith, I have heard too much, and your words and performances are no kin together.

Iago

You charge me most unjustly.

Roderigo

With naught but truth. I have wasted myself out of my means; the jewels you have had from me to deliver Desdemona would half have corrupted a votarist. You have told me she hath received them, and returned me expectations and comforts of sudden respect and acquaintance, but I find none.

Iago

Well, go to, very well.

Roderigo

“Very well, go to”! I cannot “go to”, man, nor ’tis not “very well”. By this hand, I say ’tis very scurvy, and begin to find myself fopped in it.

Iago

Very well.

Roderigo

I tell you, ’tis not very well! I will make myself known to Desdemona. If she will return me my jewels, I will give over my suit and repent my unlawful solicitation. If not, assure yourself, I will seek satisfaction of you.

Iago

You have said now.

Roderigo

Ay, and said nothing but what I protest intendment of doing.

Iago

Why, now I see there’s mettle in thee, and even from this instant do build on thee a better opinion than ever before. Give me thy hand, Roderigo. Thou hast taken against me a most just exception, but yet I protest I have dealt most directly in thy affair.

Roderigo

It hath not appeared.

Iago

I grant indeed it hath not appeared, and your suspicion is not without wit and judgment. But, Roderigo, if thou hast that in thee indeed which I have greater reason to believe now than ever—I mean purpose, courage, and valor—this night show it. If thou the next night following enjoy not Desdemona, take me from this world with treachery and devise engines for my life.

Roderigo

Well, what is it? Is it within reason and compass?

Iago

Sir, there is especial commission come from Venice to depute Cassio in Othello’s place.

Roderigo

Is that true? Why then Othello and Desdemona return again to Venice.

Iago

Oh no, he goes into Mauritania and taketh away with him the fair Desdemona, unless his abode be lingered here by some accident, wherein none can be so determinate as the removing of Cassio.

Roderigo

How do you mean removing him?

Iago

Why, by making him uncapable of Othello’s place—knocking out his brains.

Roderigo

And that you would have me to do.

Iago

Ay, if you dare do yourself a profit and a right. He sups tonight with a harlotry, and thither will I go to him. He knows not yet of his honorable fortune. If you will watch his going thence—which I will fashion to fall out between twelve and one—you may take him at your pleasure. I will be near to second your attempt, and he shall fall between us. Come, stand not amazed at it, but go along with me; I will show you such a necessity in his death that you shall think yourself bound to put it on him. It is now high supper time, and the night grows to waste. About it.

Roderigo

I will hear further reason for this.

Iago

And you shall be satisfied.

Exeunt.

4.3

Enter Othello,Lodovico, Desdemona, Emilia, and attendants.

Lodovico

2266I do beseech you, sir, trouble yourself no further.

Othello

2267Oh, pardon me; ’twill do me good to walk.

Lodovico

2268Madam, good night; I humbly thank your ladyship.

Desdemona

2269Your honor is most welcome.

Othello

Will you walk, sir? O Desdemona—

Desdemona

My lord.

Othello

Get you to bed on th’instant. I will be returned forthwith. Dismiss your attendant there; look’t be done.

Desdemona

I will, my lord.

Exeunt [Othello, Lodovico, and attendants.]

Emilia

2274How goes it now? He looks gentler than he did.

Desdemona

2275He says he will return incontinent,
2276And hath commanded me to go to bed,
2277And bid me to dismiss you.

Emilia

And bid me to dismiss you. Dismiss me?

Desdemona

2278It was his bidding; therefore, good Emilia,
2279Give me my nightly wearing, and adieu.
2280We must not now displease him.

Emilia

2281I would you had never seen him.

Desdemona

2282So would not I; my love doth so approve him
2283That even his stubbornness, his checks, his frowns—
2284Prithee unpin me—have grace and favor in them.

Emilia

2285I have laid those sheets you bade me on the bed.

Desdemona

2286All’s one. Good faith, how foolish are our minds?
2287If I do die before thee, prithee shroud me
2288In one of these same sheets.

Emilia

In one of these same sheets. Come, come, you talk.

Desdemona

2289My mother had a maid called Barbary;
2290She was in love, and he she loved proved mad
2291And did forsake her. She had a "Song of Willow"—
2292An old thing ’twas, but it expressed her fortune,
2293And she died singing it. That song tonight
2294Will not go from my mind; I have much to do
2295But to go hang my head all at one side
2296And sing it like poor Barbary. Prithee dispatch.

Emilia

2297Shall I go fetch your nightgown?

Desdemona

Shall I go fetch your nightgown? No, unpin me here.
2298This Lodovico is a proper man.

Emilia

2299A very handsome man.

Desdemona

A very handsome man. He speaks well.

Emilia

2300I know a lady in Venice would have walked
2301Barefoot to Palestine for a touch of his nether lip.

Desdemona

2302[Singing] The poor soul sat singing by a sycamore tree,
2303Sing all a green willow;
2304Her hand on her bosom, her head on her knee.
2305Sing willow, willow, willow.
2306The fresh streams ran by her and murmured her moans,
2307Sing willow, willow, willow;
2308Her salt tears fell from her and softened the stones.
2309Sing willow—
2310Lay by these.—
Lay by these.— [Singing] willow, willow.—
2311Prithee, hie thee; he’ll come anon.
2312[Singing] Sing all a green willow must be my garland.
2313Let nobody blame him, his scorn I approve.
2314Nay, that’s not next. Hark, who is’t that knocks?

Emilia

2315It’s the wind.

Desdemona

2316[Singing] I called my love false love, but what said he then?
2317Sing willow, willow, willow;
2318If I court more women, you’ll couch with more men.
2319So get thee gone, goodnight. Mine eyes do itch;
2320Doth that bode weeping?

Emilia

Doth that bode weeping? ’Tis neither here nor there.

Desdemona

2321I have heard it said so. Oh, these men, these men!
2322Dost thou in conscience think—tell me, Emilia—
2323That there be women do abuse their husbands
2324In such gross kind?

Emilia

In such gross kind? There be some such, no question.

Desdemona

2325Wouldst thou do such a deed for all the world?

Emilia

2326Why, would not you?

Desdemona

Why, would not you? No, by this heavenly light.

Emilia

2327Nor I neither, by this heavenly light;
2328I might do’t as well i’th’dark.

Desdemona

2329Wouldst thou do such a deed for all the world?

Emilia

2330The world’s a huge thing; it is a great price
2331For a small vice.

Desdemona

For a small vice. In troth, I think thou wouldst not.

Emilia

In troth, I think I should, and undo’t when I had done. Marry, I would not do such a thing for a joint ring, nor for measures of lawn, nor for gowns, petticoats, nor caps, nor any petty exhibition. But for all the whole world—’ud’s pity!—who would not make her husband a cuckold to make him a monarch? I should venture purgatory for’t.

Desdemona

2333Beshrew me if I would do such a wrong
2334For the whole world.

Emilia

Why, the wrong is but a wrong i’th’world; and having the world for your labor ’tis a wrong in your own world, and you might quickly make it right.

Desdemona

2336I do not think there is any such woman.

Emilia

Yes, a dozen—and as many to th’vantage as would store the world they played for.

2338But I do think it is their husbands’ faults
2339If wives do fall. Say that they slack their duties
2340And pour our treasures into foreign laps;
2341Or else break out in peevish jealousies,
2342Throwing restraint upon us; or say they strike us,
2343Or scant our former having in despite.
2344Why, we have galls; and though we have some grace,
2345Yet have we some revenge. Let husbands know
2346Their wives have sense like them; they see, and smell,
2347And have their palates both for sweet and sour,
2348As husbands have. What is it that they do
2349When they change us for others? Is it sport?
2350I think it is. And doth affection breed it?
2351I think it doth. Is’t frailty that thus errs?
2352It is so too. And have not we affections,
2353Desires for sport, and frailty, as men have?
2354Then let them use us well; else let them know,
2355The ills we do, their ills instruct us so.

Desdemona

2356Good night, good night. God me such uses send,
2357Not to pick bad from bad, but by bad, mend.

Exeunt.
