SCENE I

LELIA (dressed as a boy) and FLAMMINIO

FLAMMINIO:

204It's very disheartening that after all this time you still haven't been able to win a kind word for me form that cruel and ungrateful Isabella. Still, to see you always welcomed so warmly and listened to so willingly makes me think that she doesn't hate me. And as far as I know, I've never done anything to displease her. LELIA:

205I've already told you twenty times.

FLAMMINIO:

206Well, tell me again. What's it to you?

LELIA:

207What's it to me? It makes me sad to see you so downcast; it makes me suffer as much as you. Since I'm your servant, I don't want to give you anything by pleasure, and I worry that her replies will make you unhappy with me.

FLAMMINIO:

208Don't worry, my dear Fabio. I love you like a brother, and I know that you love me, so you can be sure I'll never let you down, as you'll see with time. Say your prayers and don't worry about anything else. But what did she say?

LELIA:

209As I told you already:that the greatest pleasure you could give her in the whole world would be to leave her alone and stop thinking about her because she's fallen in love with someone else; and that she can't stand the sight of you anymore and you're wasting your time courting her, because when all is said and done, you're going to find yourself empty-handed.

210Did it seem to you that she really meant it, or is she upset with me about something? I'm prepared to court her until the day I die. At least I want to see what the future has to offer. What do you think, Fabio? Don't you agree?

LELIA:

211I don't, sir.

212Why not?

213Because if I were in your shoes, I'd want her to be happy with my courtship. What woman would pass up a man like you, noble, accomplished, gentle-mannered, and handsome? FLAMMINIO:

214Of course. There was one among the others called Lelia - in fact, many times I have been tempted to say that you remind me of her. LELIA:

215Master, turn about is fair play. You have ignored someone who loves you; it's only fair that now you are being ignored.

FLAMMINIO:

216What are you trying to say?

LELIA:

217If that poor young woman was your first love and she continues to love you more than ever, why have you abandoned her to run after another? I'm not sure that God can ever forgive such a sin. Heavens, my lord Flamminio! You're clearly very much in the wrong.

I don't know how to, and I'm not cpaable of it. So go talk to her again and see if you can wheedle out of her why she's so upset with me that she doesn't want to see me.

LELIA:

219You're wasting your time.

FLAMMINIO:

220I'm willing to waste this time.

LELIA:

221You're not going to accomplish anything.

FLAMMINIO:

222That's life!

LELIA:

223Give up on her, I say.

FLAMMINIO:

224I can't. Go on, do as I ask.

LELIA:

225I'm going, but ...

FLAMMINIO:

226Come back immediately with her reply. I'll walk on the cathedral.

LELIA:

227As soon as the moment is right, I'll give it my best shot.

[Walks off towards the cathedral]

FLAMMINIO:

228Fabio, if you can do it, I'll be very pleased with you!

229[Aside] We split up just in time, because here comes Pasquella looking for me.

422Forget her, I say. It is possible that in his entire city there isn't some other woman who merits your love as much as she does? Haven't you ever been attracted to another woman?

FLAMMINIO:

423I wish it weren't true! But I'm afraid that that might be the root of all my problems, because I loved with all my heart that Lelia, daughter of Virginio Bellenzini, whom I told you about. And I'm afraid that Isabella may think I'm still in love with her, and that's why she doesn't want to see me. But I'll convince her that I don't love Lelia anymore - in fact, that I hate her and can't stand even to hear her name. And I'll promise her absolutely never to ho near Lelia again. I want you to tell her this clearly.

LELIA:

424Oh dear!

FLAMMINIO:

425What's wrong? You seem about to faint. How do you feel?

LELIA:

426Oh dear!

FLAMMINIO:

427Where does it hurt?

LELIA:

428Oh dear! My heart.

FLAMMINIO:

429How long have you had this? Lean on me a moment. Does your body ache?

LELIA:

430No, sir.

FLAMMINIO:

431Is your stomach upset?

LELIA:

432It's my heart that hurts.

FLAMMINIO:

433Mine too, and perhaps even more than yours. But you've lost your color. Go on home and put some hot towels on your chest and have your back rubbed; maybe that will do the trick. I'll be back soon, and if you need it, I'll send for the doctor to take your pulse and see what's wrong with you. Give me your arm for a moment. You're freezing! ... That's better. Go on, now, slowly. [Aside] Life is so strange! I wouldn't trade this Fabio for all my wealth, for I don't believe there's anyone more able or willing to serve me in all the world or better mannered than this young man. Moreover, he shows me so much love that if he were a woman, I would think that he's ill because he loves me. [To LELIA] Fabio, go on home now and warm your feet. I'll be there shortly. Tell the others to set the table.

[He leaves]

LELIA:

434[Walking off] O poor wretch! Now you have heard with your own ears from the mouth of this ungrateful Flamminio just how much he loves you! Poor, sad Lelia! Why waste any more time serving this cruel man? Your patience, your prayers, your favors have done no good. Now event your deceptions have failed. Poor me! Rejected! Cast aside! Fled! Hated! What am I doing serving the man who rejects me? Why am I staying with the man who cast me aside? Why do I follow the man who flees me? Why do I love the man who hates me? Ah, Flamminio! You have eyes only for Isabella. He wants no one but Isabella! He can have her. Take her! For I'll either leave him or die. I've decided no longer to serve him in these clothes and never to cross his path again so that he won't ever have to see the woman he finds so hateful. I'll go find Clemenzia. She's waiting for me at home. Together we'll plan what to do with my life.

[She runs off]

SCENE VIII

CRIVELLO and FLAMMINIO

CRIVELLO:

435And if it's true, you can have me hung by the neck if cutting my tongue out isn't enough. That's what happened.

FLAMMINIO:

436When did it happen?

CRIVELLO:

437When you sent me to look for him.

FLAMMINIO:

438How? Tell me again, because he told me he didn't get to talk with her today.

CRIVELLO:

439It would be better if he confessed. But I'll tell you that while I was waiting to see if he would show up at her house, I saw him come out. And as he was about to leave, Isabella called him back inside the doorway and then, after looking around to see if there was anyone to see them, not seeing anyone, they kissed each other.

FLAMMINIO:

440How come they didn't see you, then?

CRIVELLO:

441Because I was hidden in that portico across the way, and they couldn't see me.

FLAMMINIO:

442How did you see them, then?

CRIVELLO:

443With my eyes. Do you think that I saw them with my elbows?

FLAMMINIO:

444And he kisses her?

CRIVELLO:

445I don't know if she kissed him or he her, but I believe they kissed each other.

FLAMMINIO:

446Did they each put their faces so close together that they could kiss?

CRIVELLO:

447Their faces no, but their lips yes.

FLAMMINIO:

448Aha! Can two people bring their lips close without doing the same with their faces?

CRIVELLO:

449All right, I suppose if people had their mouths in their ears or in the back of their necks it would be possible, maybe, but given where they are now, you're right.

FLAMMINIO:

450Look, you'd better have seen things, because later you can't say, "Well, it seemed to me." This is a very serious thing that you're saying.

CRIVELLO:

451As great as the giant Mangia, who rings the clock on the tower of Siena.

FLAMMINIO:

452How did you see it?

CRIVELLO:

453Looking, with my eyes wide open, and as I was there to look, I really didn't have anything else to do but see.

FLAMMINIO:

454If this is true, you've killed me!

CRIVELLO:

455It's true. She called him. They came together. She embraced him. She kissed him. So now if you want to die, go ahead.

FLAMMINIO:

456I'm not surprised that the traitor denied being there! Now I understand why the rogue encouraged me to leave her - so that he could have her for himself! I won't deserve to be called a man if I don't launch a vendetta against him that will stand for all time as an example to servants who betray their masters. But in the end, if I don't have better proof, I don't want to believe you. I know that you're not trustworthy and that you must hate his guts, so you're saying this to get me to get rid of him. But in the name of God who I hold dear, I'll make you tell me the truth or kill you! Tell me! Did you really see it?

CRIVELLO:

457Yes, sir.

FLAMMINIO:

458He kissed her?

CRIVELLO:

459They kissed.

FLAMMINIO:

460How many times?

CRIVELLO:

461Two times.

FLAMMINIO:

462Where?

CRIVELLO:

463In the entrance hall.

FLAMMINIO:

464You're a rotten liar! A little while ago you said they were in the doorway.

CRIVELLO:

465I meant to say near the doorway.

FLAMMINIO:

466Tell the truth!

[Beating him]

CRIVELLO:

467Ouch, ouch! I'm beginning to be sorry I told you.

FLAMMINIO:

468Did it really happen?

CRIVELLO:

469Yes, sir. But I forgot:I have a witness.

FLAMMINIO:

470Who is it?

CRIVELLO:

471Scatizza, Virginio's servant.

FLAMMINIO:

472He saw them too?

CRIVELLO:

473Just like me.

FLAMMINIO:

474And if he doesn't confirm what you said?

CRIVELLO:

475Kill me.

FLAMMINIO:

476I will.

CRIVELLO:

477And if he confirms it?

FLAMMINIO:

478I'll have to kill two people?

CRIVELLO:

479Oh, my God, why?

FLAMMINIO:

480Not you two; Isabella and Fabio.

CRIVELLO:

481And then you should burn down that house with Pasquella and everyone in it.

FLAMMINIO:

482Let's find Scatizza. If I don't pay them as they deserve... if I don't make people talk... if the whole city doesn't see... I'll have such a great revenge! ... That traitor! That's what trust gets you.
