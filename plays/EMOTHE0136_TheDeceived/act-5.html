The Deceived

Act V

SCENE I

VIRGINIO [with a large shield and a mail shirt], STRAGUALCIA, SCATIZZA [with a small round shield], several of VIRGINIO's servants [with various makeshift weapons], GHERARDO, the PEDANT, and FABRIZIO

VIRGINIO:

1044All of you, come with me. Stragualcia, you too.

STRAGUALCIA:

1045With weapons or without? I don't have any weapons.

VIRGINIO:

1046Get some from the inn.

[STRAGUALCIA goes into the inn]

SCATIZZA:

1047You know, Master, with a large shield like that you should have a lance.

VIRGINIO:

1048I don't need a lance. This is enough for me.

SCATIZZA:

1049This small round shield would be more noble, since you're wearing a mail shirt.

VIRGINIO:

1050No, this covers me better. Oh! This muttonhead seems to think he's caught me trying to swindle him somehow. And I'm afraid he may have killed my poor daughter.

STRAGUALCIA:

1051[Coming out of the inn with a roast on a spit and a bottle of wine] This is a fine weapon, Master. I'm going to run this spit through him like a woodcock!

SCATIZZA:

1052Oh! But what are you going to do with the roast?

STRAGUALCIA:

1053I have military experience, and I know that the first thing you need to do is be sure you have field rations.

SCATIZZA:

1054And the wine, what's that for?

STRAGUALCIA:

1055To refresh the troops, if the first attrack is rebuffed.

SCATIZZA:

1056Sounds good to me, and it's likely.

STRAGUALCIA:

1057Do you want me to run them all through - the old man, the daughter, the servants, the whole household and all of them, like chicken livers to be roasted? Why, I'll run this spit through side by side like thrushes.

VIRGINIO:

1058The door is open. They may have set some kind of ambush.

STRAGUALCIA:

1059Bushes? That's bad. I'm more afraid of being with switches than swords. But the Tutor is coming out.

PEDANT:

1060[Coming out of the house and speaking to GHERARDO, who remains inside] Leave this to me, and I'll make peace for you, Messer Gherardo.

STRAGUALCIA:

1061Look at that, Master. It's the Tutor, and it seems that he has mutinied and gone over to the enemy. You can't count on his type to stay loyal! Do you want me to start with him, running him through so that I can say "one down"?

PEDANT:

1062Messer Virginio, my master, why are you armed?

STRAGUALCIA:

1063Aha! Didn't I tell you!

VIRGINIO:

1064What has happened to my daughter? Hand her over. I want to take her home. And have you found Fabrizio?

PEDANT:

1065Yes.

VIRGINIO:

1066Where is he?

PEDANT:

1067He's here inside. And he has taken a very beautiful bride, with your permission.

VIRGINIO:

1068A wife, eh? And who would that be?

STRAGUALCIA:

1069[Aside] That was quick. This is rich, rich!

PEDANT:

1070The beautiful and well-mannered daughter of Gherardo.

VIRGINIO:

1071Oh! The same Gherardo who just now wanted to kill me?

PEDANT:

1072Rem omnem a principia audies. Come on into the house, and you'll hear the whole story. Come on out, Messer Gherardo.

GHERARDO:

1073[Coming out of the house] Oh, Virginio, this is the strangest case ever! Come in.

STRAGUALCIA:

1074[Aside] Should I run him through? But his meat is hardly worth roasting.

GHERARDO:

1075Have your men put down their weapons. This is a matter for laughter.

VIRGINIO:

1076Can I trust you?

PEDANT:

1077Yes, certainly, on my word.

VIRGINIO:

1078All right, go on home everyone! And put down your weapons and bring me my vest.

PEDANT:

1079Fabrizio, come out and meet your father.

[FABRIZIO comes out]

VIRGINIO:

1080Oh! Isn't this Lelia?

PEDANT:

1081No, this is Fabrizio.

VIRGINIO:

1082O my dear son!

FABRIZIO:

1083O my father, I've searched so long for you!

VIRGINIO:

1084O my dear son, I've cried so long for you!

GHERARDO:

1085Come in, come in, and you'll hear the whole story. And I should tell you also that your daughter is safe in the house of Clemenzia, her balia.

VIRGINIO:

1086O dear God, how grateful I am to You!

SCENE II

CRIVELLO, FLAMMINIO, and CLEMENZIA

CRIVELLO:

1087I saw him with these eyes and I heard him with these ears in the house of Clemenzia.

FLAMMINIO:

1088Are you sure it was Fabio?

CRIVELLO:

1089Do you think that I don't know him?

FLAMMINIO:

1090Let's go. If I find him ...

CRIVELLO:

1091You'll ruin everything. Be patient, and wait until he comes out.

FLAMMINIO:

1092Not even God could give me enough patience.

CRIVELLO:

1093You're going to ruin the cake.

FLAMMINIO:

1094I am going to ruin myself.

[Knocks fiercely at the door]

CLEMENZIA:

1095Who's there?

FLAMMINIO:

1096A friend of yours. Come down to the door.

CLEMENZIA:

1097Oh! What do you want, Messer Flamminio?

FLAMMINIO:

1098Open the door and I'll tell you.

CLEMENZIA:

1099Wait a second until I come down.

FLAMMINIO:

1100[To CRIVELLO] As soon as she opens the door, go in and see if he's there, then call me.

CRIVELLO:

1101Leave it to me.

CLEMENZIA:

1102[Opening the door] What do you want, Signor Flamminio?

FLAMMINIO:

1103What's my boy doing in your house?

CLEMENZIA:

1104What boy? [Blocking CRIVELLO as he tries to push past her into the house] And you, Crivello, where are you going, you rascal? Do you think you can enter my house by force?

FLAMMINIO:

1105Clemenzia, by the holy, inviolate, sacred body, if you don't hand over that ...

CLEMENZIA:

1106What do you want me to hand over?

FLAMMINIO:

1107My boy, who's hiding in your house.

CLEMENZIA:

1108There's no serving-boy of yours in my house, even if there is a serving-girl

FLAMMINIO:

1109Clemenzia, thi is not the time to fool around.You've always been a friend of mine. You've helped me out, and Iove helped you. But this is too important.

CLEMENZIA:

1110This must be some madness induced by love. Calm down, Flamminio, cool down a bit.

FLAMMINIO:

1111I said, hand over Fabio.

CLEMENZIA:

1112I'll hand him over.

FLAMMINIO:

1113Enough! Make him come down here now.

CLEMENZIA:

1114Oh, calm down, for God's sake! You know, if I were young and you liked me, I wouldn't give you the time of day. By the way, how's Isabella?

FLAMMINIO:

1115I wish she were cut into pieces!

CLEMENZIA:

1116Hah! I don't believe you.

FLAMMINIO:

1117You don't believe me? I can tell you that she's cleared things up for me just fine.

CLEMENZIA:

1118Really! Well, you young colts deserve every setback, because you are the most ungrateful people in the whole world.

FLAMMINIO:

1119You can't say that about me. Every other accusation might fit, but to be called ungrateful, no. There's not a man alive who dislikes such behavior more.

CLEMENZIA:

1120I'm not talking about you. But there was once here in this city a young girl who, realizing that she was admired by a knight – one of your peers here in Modena – fell so in love with him that she lost sight of everything else.

FLAMMINIO:

1121Lucky man! Happy man! I certainly couldn't say as much for myself.

CLEMENZIA:

1122It so happened that her father sent this poor girl, so in love, away from Modena. Fearing that her lover would forget her, she cried so much that it was a wonder to see. And in fact he immediately found another girl, as if she had never existed.

FLAMMINIO:

1123I'd say that such a man should be called a traitor, not a knight.

CLEMENZIA:

1124Listen, it gets worse. When the young girl returned after several months, she found that her lover loved another who didn't love him. Abandoning her house and her father and putting her honor at risk, she decided to serve him. So she dressed herself as a young manservant and took a job with her lover as his servant.

FLAMMINIO:

1125This is happened in Modena?

CLEMENZIA:

1126And you know then both.

FLAMMINIO:

1127I would rather be this lucky lover than the lord of Milan.

CLEMENZIA:

1128Do you want to hear the rest? This lover of hers, not recognizing her, used her as his go-between with his new love. And to make him happy, this poor girl did everything he asked.

FLAMMINIO:

1129Oh, what a virtuous woman! What constant love! This love could serve as a lesson for future centuries. Why can't I be so lucky?

CLEMENZIA:

1130Well, you wouldn't give up Isabella for such a love anyway.

FLAMMINIO:

1131Why, I'd almost be willing to say I'd give up Christ for someone like that. Please, Clemenzia, tell me who this woman is.

CLEMENZIA:

1132I'd be glad to. But first I want you to tell me, on your oath as a gentleman, what you would do with such a poor girl if such a thing happened to you. Would you kick her out when you learned what she'd done? Would you kill her, or would you judge her worthy of some reward?

FLAMMINIO:

1133I swear in the name of the power of the sun that you see in the sky, and with the proviso that I be banned forever from the company of my peers, gentlemen and knights, that I would take her as my wife – even if she were ugly; even if she were poor; even if she were of low birth; even over the daughter of the duke of Ferrara.

CLEMENZIA:

1134That's a big oath. Are you ready to stand by it?

FLAMMINIO:

1135Yes, I'm saying it, and yes, I would do it.

CLEMENZIA:

1136[Turning to CRIVELLO] You're a witness.

CRIVELLO:

1137I heard him, and I know he would do it.

CLEMENZIA:

1138Now I want to introduce you to this woman and to this knight. Fabio, oh, Fabio! Come down here. Your master is looking for you.

FLAMMINIO:

1139What do you make of this, Crivello? Do you think I should kill this traitor? He's at least a good servant.

CRIVELLO:

1140Oh well! I'm hardly surprised! I thought something strange like this would happen. Go ahead, pardon him. What else is there to do? That tart Isabella never really loved you anyway.

FLAMMINIO:

1141You're right.

SCENE III

PASQUELLA, CLEMENZIA, FLAMMINIO, LELIA[dressed as a woman], and CRIVELLO

PASQUELLA:

1142[Comming out of GHERARDO's house and speaking with someone inside] I'll take care of it. I'll tell him what you told me and what I've learned.

[LELIA comes to the door of CLEMENZIA's house where the others are standing]

CLEMENZIA:

1143This, Messer Flamminio, is your Fabio. Take a good look. Do you recognize him? Are you surprised? And this same girl is that young woman I told you about, so loyal and so true to her love. Take a good look, and see if you recognize her. Have you lost your voicie, Flamminio? Well, what do you have to say now? And you, you are that young man who thought so little of the love of his mistress. Don't think that you're the one of the deceived here! You can see for yourself that I'm telling the truth. Now keep your word, or I'll take you before the courts as someone who has broken his pledge.

FLAMMINIO:

1144I don't believe there's ever been a more perfect deception than this one. How could I have been so blind that I never recognized her?

CRIVELLO:

1145I was more blind than anyone. I looked at her closely a thousand times and never realized. I'll be damned! Oh, whay a fool I've been!

PASQUELLA:

1146[Interrupting] Clemenzia, Virginio told me to tell you to come to our house right now because he has married off Fabrizio, his son who returned today. And he needs you to put the house in order, because, as you know, he has no other woman.

CLEMENZIA:

1147What wife? Whom did he marry him to?

PASQUELLA:

1148Isabella, the daughter of Gherardo, my master.

FLAMMINIO:

1149Who? Isabella, the daughter of Gherardo Foiani, your master, or another Isabella?

PASQUELLA:

1150Another? No, it's her. Flamminio, it's like they say: "Some people have all the luck.”

FLAMMINIO:

1151You're sure it's true?

PASQUELLA:

1152Absolutely. I was there for the whole thing. I saw him give her the ring, and then they embraced, they kissed, and they rejoiced. And even before he gave her the ring she gave him ... well, let's just say, I'm sure.

FLAMMINIO:

1153How long ago did this happen?

PASQUELLA:

1154Now, just now. Then they sent me running here to tell Clemenzia and to bring her back.

CLEMENZIA:

1155Tell them I'll be there shortly. Go on, now.

LELIA:

1156[Aside] O God, You've made things come together so well! I'm dying of happiness.

PASQUELLA:

1157Don't take too long, I've so much to do that I'm all in a dither! I need to go now to buy some makeup. Oh! I forgot to ask if Lelia's here, because Gherardo has agreed to marry her.

CLEMENZIA:

1158You know very well that she's here. Do you really want to see her married to that poor old excuse for a man, your master? He ought to be ashamed of himself!

PASQUELLA:

1159You don't really know my master. Why, if you knew how manly he can be, you wouldn't talk about him like that.

CLEMENZIA:

1160Sure, sure, I believe you. I bet you've tested his manliness.

PASQUELLA:

1161Just like you with your master. Anyway, I'm off.

FLAMMINIO:

1162She's to be married to Gherardo?

CLEMENZIA:

1163Yes, woe is me! Isn't this poor girl unlucky?

FLAMMINIO:

1164But he has already lived his life. Look, Clemenzia, I believe that it's clearly the will of God that she not be wasted like this, for He has taken pity on her virtue and on my poor soul. And so, lady Lelia, if you're willing, I don't want any other wife but you, and I swear to you as a knight that if you won't marry me, I'll never marry another.

LELIA:

1165Flamminio, you are my lord. You know exactly what I did and why, and so you know that I've never had any other desire but this.

FLAMMINIO:

1166You've proven it perfectly. Please forgive me if I've made you unhappy; not recognizing you. I'm very, very sorry, and I recognize my error.

LELIA:

1167You could never do anything, Signor Flamminio, that would make me unhappy.

FLAMMINIO:

1168Clemenzia, I don't want to wait any longer and take the chance that something unforeseen might ruin this good fortune. I want to marry her now if she's willing.

LELIA:

1169I'm most willing.

CRIVELLO:

1170Oh, thank God! And you, my master, Lord Flamminio, are you willing? By the way, you should know that I'm a notary. And if you don't believe me, here's my diploma.

FLAMMINIO:

1171I'm very willing and as happy as I've ever been in my life.

CRIVELLO:

1172Give each other your hands in marriage, and then you can go to bed. Oh! But I forgot to tell you that you should kiss her!

[They kiss]

CLEMENZIA:

1173Here's what I think we should do now. You two stay here in my house while I go tell Virginio everything and break the bad news to Gherardo.

FLAMMINIO:

1174Fine, and you can tell Isabella as well.

SCENE IV

[GIGLIO and PASQUELLA.]

GIGLIO:

1175[Aside] By the king's life, it's that old bag Pasquella who's trying to trick me out of my goods. How glad I am to have run into her.

PASQUELLA:

1176[Aside] Damn this pain in the ass! Just my luck to have him underfoot! He can break his neck along with all the others who have come from Spain, as far as I'm concerned! What excuse can I find now?

GIGLIO:

1177Lady Pasquella!

PASQUELLA:

1178[Aside] Things are going well, I've already become a lady.

GIGLIO:

1179You've tricked me and taken my rosary, and you haven't done what you promised.

PASQUELLA:

1180Shush, shush, shush! Be quiet, be quiet!

GIGLIO:

1181Why? Is there someone around to hear us?

PASQUELLA:

1182Shush, shush, shush!

GIGLIO:

1183I don't see anyone here. You're not going to deceive me again! What are you up to?

PASQUELLA:

1184You're trying to ruin me.

GIGLIO:

1185You're trying to tick me.

PASQUELLA:

1186Go away. Leave me alone now, and I'll talk with you later.

GIGLIO:

1187Give me back my rosary, and we can talk whenever you like. I don't want you to be able to say that you've deceived me.

PASQUELLA:

1188I'll give it to you. But do you think I have it here? Do you think it's that important to me? Why, I can have all the rosaries I want!

GIGLIO:

1189Why did you lock me out and then start singing and say such strange things like "Ghost, ghost" and I don't know what prayers and other stuff?

PASQUELLA:

1190Be quiet. You're going to ruin me. I'll explain everything.

GIGLIO:

1191What? Why don't you tell me?

PASQUELLA:

1192Move a little over this way, in this corner, so that my mistress won't see you.

GIGLIO:

1193Are you trying to trick me again?

PASQUELLA:

1194You're so sure that I'm tricking you! Am I really the type of deceive someone? Is that what you think, eh?

GIGLIO:

1195All right, but tell me right now what's going on.

PASQUELLA:

1196Listen. When we were talking before, Isabella, my mistress, came down very quietly and was hidden near me and heard everything. When I was rounding up my chickens, she went into the room there and from a hole in the wall watched everything we did. I, realizing this, pretended that I hadn't seen her. And I pretended that I wanted to deceive you. In the end, when I showed her the rosary, she, believing that I had tricked you, took it away and, laughing, wrapped it around her arm. But I'll get it back tonight and give it to you if you don't want to me to have it.

GIGLIO:

1197Is all this true? You'd better not be trying to deceive me!

PASQUELLA:

1198Dear Giglio, may I never see you again if it isn't true. Do you think I don't cherish your relationship? But you Spaniards don't believe in Christ or anything else!

GIGLIO:

1199Well, why don't we do now what we agreed to earlier?

PASQUELLA:

1200I can't stay here with you because my mistress has just gotten married, and tonight we'll have the celebration. I have so many things to do. Wait a little longer. [Aside] Oh, what a nuisance these Spaniards are!

GIGLIO:

1201Tomorrow! Tomorrow morning, all right?

PASQUELLA:

1202Leave it to me. I'll remember you when the time is right. Don't worry. [Aside as she hurries off] Ugh, ugh, ugh! How disgusting!

GIGLIO:

1203I swear to God, I'll slash your face if you're deceiving me again!

SCENE V

CITINA [Coming out of CLEMENZIA's house]

CITINA:

1204I wonder what that strange noise is in the downstairs room. I hear the bed making a thumping, a drumming sound, as if it were possessed by some spirit. Oh my! I'm really scared. Oh! I hear someone, "My life, my happiness, my hope, my sweet dear wife!" Oh! I can't make out the rest. I tempted to knock. And oh! Now one of them says, "Wait for me." They must be getting ready to leave. And the other one is saying, "Come quickly too." It sounds like they're going to break that bed. Oh, oh, oh! That bed is bouncing so fast, so fast! By all that's ho ... hole ... holeym I'd better go tell Mama.

SCENE VI

ISABELLA, FABRIZIO, and CLEMENZIA

ISABELLA:

1205I was certain that you were the servant of a local knight. You look so much like him that you must be his brother.

FABRIZIO:

1206Others today have mistaken me for someone else. Even the innkeeper seemed confused.

ISABELLA:

1207Here comes Clemenzia, your balia. She'll want to speak with you.

CLEMENZIA:

1208[Aside] This must be him, for he looks exactly like Lelia. [To FABRIZIO] O Fabrizio, my dear child, welcome home! How are you?

FABRIZIO:

1209Excellent, my dear balia. How's Lelia?

CLEMENZIA:

1210Good, good. But let's go into the house, there's a lot I need to explain to you.

SCENE VII

VIRGINIO, CLEMENZIA, and STRAGUALCIA

VIRGINIO:

1211I'm so happy to have found my son that I'm satisfied with everything.

CLEMENZIA:

1212It was all the will of God. It certainly has turned out better than if you had married your daughter off to that broken-down Gherardo. But let me go into the house to see how things are going. I left the newlyweds all alone and very, very close. [She goes in and after a moment calls] Come in, come in. Everything's fine.

[VIRGINIO follows her; STRAGUALCIA stops in the doorway]

STRAGUALCIA:

1213Spectators, don't wait for them to come back out of the house, because that will make a long story even longer. If you want to come to dinner with us, I'll be waiting for you at the Joker. And bring along some money, because no one's treating. But if you don't want to come – and it looks to me like that's the case - be happy and enjoy yourselves. And you members of the Intronati, how about some applause?

[He enters the JOKER]
