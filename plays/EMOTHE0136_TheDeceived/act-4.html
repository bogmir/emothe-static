Act IV

SCENE I

The PEDANT and STRAGUALCIA

PEDANT:

749It would serve you right if he gave you a good beating to teach you that when he goes out, you should go with him instead of getting drunk and then sleeping it off.

STRAGUALCIA:

750And he ought to load you up with kindling, sulfur, pitch, and gunpowder and then set you alight to teach you not to be what you are.

PEDANT:

751Drunkard! Drunkard!

STRAGUALCIA:

752Pedant! Pedant!

PEDANT:

753Wait till I find our master!

STRAGUALCIA:

754Wait till I find his father!

PEDANT:

755Oh! What do you want to tell his father about me?

STRAGUALCIA:

756And you, what can you say about me?

PEDANT:

757That you're a fool, a crook, a fraud, a thug, a madman, a drunkard!

STRAGUALCIA:

758And I can say that you're a thief, a gambler, a gossip, a cheat, a swindler, an impostor, a braggart, a fathead, a disgrace, an ignoramus, a traitor, a sodomite, and an evil person!

PEDANT:

759It seems we've met.

STRAGUALCIA:

760Right.

PEDANT:

761Enough. I don't want to get mixed up with your sort; there's no honor in it for me.

STRAGUALCIA:

762Yes, by God! One finds in you all the nobility of the Maremma. Will you ever be anything but the son of a mule driver? Aren't I from a better family than you? Is it right that this fool should lord it over everybody just because he knows how to say cuius masculini.

PEDANT:

763Alas! "Poor and nude, philosophy journeys through this world." Latin has come on hard times when it comes form the mouth of an ass.

STRAGUALCIA:

764The ass will be you, and I'll give you a good beating if you don't change your tune.

PEDANT:

765You should remember the proverb "Furor sit laesa saepius sapientia." You're about to make me lose my temper, Stragualcia. Back off, you big ugly manure shoveler, you villain, you archvillain!

STRAGUALCIA:

766Hey! Pedant, archpedant, pedant, greatest pedant! Is there any insult worse than "pedant"? Is there a worse type? Is there a greater fraud? Is there a worse profession? Maybe it wouldn't be so bad if they didn't go around all puffed up because others call them "Messer So and So" or "Professor Such and Such," or if they deigned to respond to someone who doffs his hat to them before they're a mile past. "Good day to you, Sir Crap, Sir Turd. Professor Diarrhea, Sir Shit!"

PEDANT:

767Tratant fabrilia fabri. You're talking about exactly what you are.

STRAGUALCIA:

768I'm talking about what you like.

PEDANT:

769Could you get your ass out of my way?

STRAGUALCIA:

770I would never let my ass be in your way, although I imagine you would like to have had it in your way.

PEDANT:

771By the body of- ...

STRAGUALCIA:

772By the body of- ... You'd better watch out if you're going you to start swearing at me [Aside, but said so that the PEDANT can overhear] He ought to realize that I know about all his evil deeds and that if I wanted to, I could have him burned. Yet he still insists on busting my ass.

PEDANT:

773You're lying! I'm not that kind of man.

STRAGUALCIA:

774[Aside] He would be one of the first pedant who wasn't.

PEDANT:

775I've decided, Stragualcia, that this house is not big enough for both of us.

STRAGUALCIA:

776Hoy original! Is this the first time I've heard you say this? Look, you aren't about to leave unless someone chases you out the door with you, to give you a place to sleep, if not this young man who's as good as they come?

PEDANT:

777For God's sake, as if I couldn't find another post if I wanted to. Why, there's even someone who made me an excellent offer.

STRAGUALCIA:

778Come on, come on, do you expect me to believe that crap?

PEDANT:

779Enough, and you would be wise to keep quiet. Now go back to the inn and look after our master's things. We can settle this later.

STRAGUALCIA:

780I'll return to the inn willingly, and I'll take care of things on my own, but you'll pay in the end. [Aside as he leaves] If I didn't threaten this bugger every now and then, I couldn't live with him. He's lower than a rabbit. When I threaten him, he keeps quiet, but if I let him get on top of me he'd do his worst, given his tastes! It's a good thing I know his character.

PEDANT:

781[Alone] Frulla told me that Fabrizio will be someplace around the central square. I'd better get going.

SCENE II

GHERARDO, VIRGINIO, and the PEDANT

GHERARDO:

782As far as the dowry is concerned, what we agreed to is agreed to. I'll give her the dowry you asked for, and you'll add a thousand florins if you don't find your son.

VIRGINIO:

783That's fine.

PEDANT:

784[Aside as he approaches GHERARDO and VIRGINIO] If I'm not mistaken, I've seen this gentleman before. But I don't remember where.

VIRGINIO:

785[To the PEDANT] What are you staring at, my good man?

PEDANT:

786[Aside] Clearly this is my master.

GHERARDO:

787Let him stare at whatever he wishes. He seems to be a stranger here, and as you know, in other places they don't even think twice about staring at anyone they want to.

PEDANT:

788If I stare, it's not sine causa. Tell me, do you know a Messer Virginio Bellenzini who lives in this city?

VIRGINIO:

789Yes, I know him. Actully, I have no closer friend. But what do you want with him? If you're thinking of staying with him, I should tell you that he's busy with other things and he can't put you up. You'd wise to find other lodgings.

PEDANT:

790You are clearly he. Salvete, patronorum optime!

VIRGINIO:

791Could you possibly be Messer Pietro de'Pagliaricci, my son's tutor?

PEDANT:

792Yes, it is I.

VIRGINIO:

793[Beginning to weep] Oh, my son! Alas! What news do you have of him? Where did you leave him? Where did he die? Why did you wait so long to contact me? Did those traitors, those Jews, those dogs kill him? My poor son! He was the only good I had in the world. O my dear Professor, tell me immediately, please!

PEDANT:

794Don't cry, sir, please.

VIRGINIO:

795O Gherardo, my son-in-law, here's the man who taught my poor son while he was alive. O Professor! O my son! Where are you buried? Don't you know? Why don't you tell me? I'm dying to know, and I'm dying of fear that I'll learn what I don't want to.

PEDANT:

796Oh, don't cry, Master. Why are you crying?

VIRGINIO:

797Shouldn't I cry for such a sweet son? So wise, so learned, so well brought up! And those traitors, they've taken him away from me, killed him.

PEDANT:

798May God keep you both well, you and him. Your son is alive and well.

GHERARDO:

799[Aside] I'm the loser, if this is true. I'm out a thousand florins.

VIRGINIO:

800Alive and well? If that were true, he would be with you now.

GHERARDO:

801Virginio, do you know this fellow well? Could this be some kind of trick?

PEDANT:

802Parcius ista viris, tamen obiicienda memento.

VIRGINIO:

803Explain, Professor.

PEDANT:

804Your son was taken prisoner by a certain Captain Orteca during the sack of Rome.

GHERARDO:

805[Aside] Oh boy, now the story begins.

PEDANT:

806And because the captain captured him along with two others, he decided to trick them by secretly sending your son and me to Siena. But a few days later he began to worry that those gentlemen of Siena -who are great lovers of law and fair play and friends of our city and above all else good men- would free your son. So he moved him from Siena to a castle of the lord of Piombino. In the meantime he made us write per usque millies asking for the ransom he had set of one thousand ducats.

VIRGINIO:

807My poor son! Did they torture him as well?

PEDANT:

808No, in fact they treated him like a gentleman.

GHERARDO:

809[Aside] I'm afraid I can see what's coming.

PEDANT:

810We never had any reply to the letters we sent.

GHERARDO:

811[Aside to VIRGINIO] You understand that this is the way he intends to get his hands on your money.

VIRGINIO:

812[To the PEDANT] Go on.

PEDANT:

813Then the Spaniard took us with him on campaign in Correggio, where he was killed. After that the court took control of his property, and we were freed.

VIRGINIO:

814Where is my son, then?

PEDANT:

815Closer that you think.

VIRGINIO:

816In Modena?

PEDANT:

817If you promise me some refreshment, quia omnis labor optat praemium, I'll tell you.

GHERARDO:

818This is what he's after, the fraud!

PEDANT:

819[To GHERARDO] You're mistaken. I, a fraud? Absit!

VIRGINIO:

820I'll promise you anything you want. Where is he?

PEDANT:

821At the inn of the Joker.

GHERARDO:

822[Aside] It's done: the thousand ducats are played and lost. But what does it matter to me? As long as I have Lelia, I'm fine. I'm rich enough as it is.

VIRGINIO:

823Let's go, Professor. I can't wait to see him and embrace him and kiss him and hold him in my arms!

PEDANT:

824Master, oh, quanto mutatus ab illo! He's no longer a child to hold in your arms. You wouldn't recognize him. He's grown up, and I'm sure he won't recognize you either, what with how much you've changed. Praeterrea, you have the beard now which you didn't have before, and if I hadn't heard your voice I wouldn't have recognized you. And Lelia, how is she?

VIRGINIO:

825Well. She's all grown up and rounded out.

GHERARDO:

826Rounded out? You mean she's pregnant? If that's the case, you can keep her! I don't want her.

VIRGINIO:

827Oh no! Oh no! I mean she's become grown, shapely woman. Oh, Tutor, I haven't embraced you yet.

[He takes the PEDANT in his arms and kissed him on both cheeks]

PEDANT:

828Master, not that I want to brag, but the things I have done for your son -I could tell you! And in turn there was never a thing I asked of him that he didn't do immediately.

VIRGINIO:

829How did his studies go?

PEDANT:

830He didn't lose any time, ut licuit per varios casus, per tot discrimina rerum.

VIRGINIO:

831Call him out here, but don't tell him anything. I want to see if he recognizes me.

PEDANT:

832He left the inn a little while ago. Let's see if he has returned.

SCENE III

The PEDANT, STRAGUALCIA, VIRGINIO, and GHERARDO

PEDANT:

833Stragualcia! Oh, Stragualcia! Has Fabrizio returned?

STRAGUALCIA:

834Not yet.

PEDANT:

835Come here. Say something to my oId master. This is Messer Virginio.

STRAGUALCIA:

836[To the PEDANT] Are you still angry?

PEDANT:

837Don't you know that I never stay angry with you?

STRAGUALCIA:

838Good.

PEDANT:

839Now give your hand to Fabrizio's father.

STRAGUALCIA:

840[To the PEDANT] You should give me your hand.

PEDANT:

841Not to me, to this gentleman.

STRAGUALCIA:

842Is this our master's father?

PEDANT:

843Yes.

STRAGUALCIA:

844[Giving VIRGINIO his hand] O magnificent Master, you are just in time to pay the innkeeper. Welcome!

PEDANT:

845This man was a good servant to your son.

STRAGUALCIA:

846Are you suggesting that I won't be in the future?

PEDANT:

847No.

VIRGINIO:

848You are most welcome, my son! I think I ought to reward all those who have given my son such support.

STRAGUALCIA:

849You can reward me with a very little thing.

VIRGINIO:

850Tell me what.

STRAGUALCIA:

851Get me a job as a servant with this innkeeper, who's the greatest guy in the world and the best supplied and the wisest and the one who best understands a traveler's needs of any innkeeper I've ever met. As far as I'm concerned, this is paradise.

GHERARDO:

852He has an excellent reputation as a host.

VIRGINIO:

853Have you eaten?

STRAGUALCIA:

854A little.

VIRGINIO:

855What have you had?

STRAGUALCIA:

856A couple of partridges, six thrushes, a capon, a bit of veal, and only two tankards of wine.

VIRGINIO:

857Frulla, give him what he wants and leave the bill to me.

PEDANT:

858Now are you happy?

STRAGUALCIA:

859[With a Spanish accent] Let me kiss your hands. This is the way masters should be! Tutor, you're too cheap and want everything for yourself. How many people have told you that? Frulla, bring these gentlemen something to drink.

PEDANT:

860That's not necessary.

STRAGUALCIA:

861I know you'll drink. I'll pay. It's no big deal. Some sweet-breads, a slice of salami ... won't you have some? Tutor, come on, have something to drink.

PEDANT:

862All right, to make peace with you.

STRAGUALCIA:

863Oh, he's good, Master. You should love the Tutor, for he loves your son more than his own eyes.

VIRGINIO:

864May God reward him!

STRAGUALCIA:

865First it's your turn, then God's. [To GHERARDO] And you, sir, have some wine.

GHERARDO:

866I really shouldn't.

[Accepts a glass of wine and takes a sip]

STRAGUALCIA:

867Please, come on in until Fabrizio returns, and then, since dinner is all ready, let's eat here this evening.

PEDANT:

868That's not a bad idea.

GHERARDO:

869I'll leave you, as I have a little matter to take care of at home.

VIRGINIO:

870Be careful that she doesn't get away.

GHERARDO:

871That's exactly why I'm going home.

VIRGINIO:

872She's all yours, now. Take care of things as you see fit. As far as I'm concerned, you can do as you please.

[He and the others go into the inn as GHERARDO heads towards his house]

GHERARDO:

873[Alone] In the end one can't have everything. Patience! [Looking offstage] But if I'm seeing clearly, that's Lelia, who has somehow escaped! That silly servant girl has let her get away!

SCENE IV

LELIA (dressed as a boy), CLEMENZIA, and GHERARDO

LELIA:

874[As GHERARDO approaches, unseen] Don't you think, Clemenzia, that Fortune enjoys toying with me?

CLEMENZIA:

875Relax and leave it to me. I'll find some way to make it all work out for you. But go and change those clothes before you're seen like that.

GHERARDO:

876[Aside] I want both to greet her and to find out how she escaped. [To CLEMENZIA and LELIA] May God make you happy, Clemenzia, and you too, Lelia, my sweet bride. Who let you out? The maid, was it? I'm glad you went to your balia's house, but to be seen in these clothes isn't very honorable for me or for you.

LELIA:

877[To GHERARDO] Who are you speaking to? What Lelia? I'm not Lelia.

GHERARDO:

878Oh! Didn't your father and I lock you up with my daughter Isabella a few minutes ago, and didn’t you admit that you were Lelia? And do you think I wouldn't recognize my own wife? Go and change those clothes.

LELIA:

879[As she enters CLEMENZIA'S house] You're crazy if you think I'd want a husband!

CLEMENZIA:

880You go home too, my dear Gherardo. All women are a bit strange, some in one way, some in another. And you should know that there are very few, in fact probably none, who don't do something a little offbeat now and then. Such things, however, are best kept secret.

GHERARDO:

881As far as I'm concerned, it will remain a secret. But how did she get out of my house? I had locked her up with Isabella.

CLEMENZIA:

882Who? Her?

GHERARDO:

883Her.

CLEMENZIA:

884You're deceiving yourself! She's been with me the whole day. And in a playful moment she decided to put on these clothes and asked me if they suited her, as young girls sometimes do.

GHERARDO:

885Are you trying to kid me? I'm telling you, we put her in my house with Isabella!

CLEMENZIA:

886Where are you coming from now, then?

GHERARDO:

887From the inn of the Joker, where I went with Virginio.

CLEMENZIA:

888Did you drink anything?

GHERARDO:

889Just a sip.

CLEMENZIA:

890Well, go and sleep it off. You clearly need to.

GHERARDO:

891Let me speak with Lelia a bit before I leave. I have some good news for her.

CLEMENZIA:

892What?

GHERARDO:

893Her brother has returned safe and sound, and her father is waiting for him at the inn.

CLEMENZIA:

894Who, Fabrizio?

GHERARDO:

895Fabrizio.

CLEMENZIA:

896If it's true, I'll give you a kiss.

GHERARDO:

897How beautiful happiness is! But as far as the kiss is concerned, I would prefer to give it to Lelia.

CLEMENZIA:

898I'm going to run and tell her.

GHERARDO:

899And I'm going to give hell to that imbecile who let her escape!

SCENE V

PASQUELLA, alone

PASQUELLA:

900Oh dear! I had such a scare that I've run out of the house. [To the audience] Ladies, you won't believe what's happened if I don't tell you. So I will tell you, but not those dirty-minded men, who would just laugh. Those two old muttonheaded dolts were absolutely convinced that that young man was a woman, and they locked him up with my mistress, Isabella, in her bedroom and gave me the key. After a while I decided to go in to see what was up, and I found them hugging and kissing! So I decided to find out whether it was a man or a woman. My mistress had the person down on the bed, and she called me to help her while she held his hands. And he was letting her win, so I opened the front of his clothes, and all of a sudden I felt something slap my hand, and I wasn't sure whether it was a large pestle or a big stick or that other thing. But whatever it was, it was in great shape. And when I saw how big it was, I took off, sisters, and locked the door behind me! And I can tell you that as far as I'm concerned, I have no intention of going back in there alone. And if one of you ladies doesn't believe me and wants to see for herself, I'll lend her the key. But here comes Giglio. I want to see if I can get that rosary and make a fool of him; these Spaniards think so much of themselves that they don't believe there's anyone else in the world as clever as they are.

SCENE VI

GIGLIO and PASQUELLA

GIGLIO:

901[Aside] There's Pasquella. I bet she's been getting herself pretty worked up, waiting so long for me. She already know, the slut, how good we Spaniards are with women. Oh, these Italian whores really get off on us!

PASQUELLA:

902[Aside] I've already figured out how to deceive this guy. Leave it to me.

GIGLIO:

903[Aside] This low-life washerwoman, she thinks I'll give her the rosary. The emperor be damned if I don't get her to love me so much that she buys me dozens of socks and shirts and jackets. I'll use her as I please, and afterwards I'll take off with my rosary without a word. Maybe she's already forgotten it.

PASQUELLA:

904[Aside] If he lets me get my hands on that rosary just once, he'll never see it again or you can cut out my eyes. And if he complains, I'll have my Spela give him a beating like he's never had before.

GIGLIO:

905[To PASQUELLA] Oh, blessed is the mother who gave birth to such a beautiful woman, so well formed, so true! I think that you have been longing for me.

PASQUELLA:

906[Aside] What beautiful bull they dish out! [To GIGLIO] I've been waiting here in the doorway for more than half an hour to see if you'd come by, because my master was out and we could have had some time alone together.

GIGLIO:

907My God, I am devastated that I was held up but other business. But let's go in now.

PASQUELLA:

908I'm afraid my master will return; he's been gone for a while. But you've forgotten the rosary, eh?

GIGLIO:

909No, madam. I have it here with me.

PASQUELLA:

910Show me. Oh! You were going to fix it. Why haven't you?

GIGLIO:

911I'll get it fixed later. To tell you the truth, it slipped my mind.

PASQUELLA:

912Well! I see how much I mean to you, womanizer that you are! I'd like to-

GIGLIO:

913[Interrumping] Don't get angry, madam, with your son. You know I don't have any women friends other than you.

PASQUELLA:

914It' hasn't taken me long to catch you in lie. A little while ago you said you had two gentlewomen for friends.

GIGLIO:

915I gave them up for you. I don't want anyone else besides you. Don't you understand me?

PASQUELLA:

916All right. But is that string of beads really a rosary? It seems pretty long to me.

GIGLIO:

917I don't know how many beads there are.

PASQUELLA:

918That's a sign that you don't say it very often. I'm beginning to wonder if you even know your Our Father. Oh well, give it to me for a second, and I'll count the beads.

GIGLIO:

919Take it. [He hands it to her] But let's go into the house.

PASQUELLA:

920Should we? Take a look around to see if anyone would see you come in.

GIGLIO:

921[Looking around] There's no one around.

PASQUELLA:

922Let's go in. [Opening the door a crack] Oops, oh dear, my hens are all here at the door! Wait a second, Giglio. Move over there. If they get away it'll take all day to round them up.

GIGLIO:

923Hurry up!

PASQUELLA:

924Back, back, my pretty ones, my pretty ones, shoo, shoo! May you break your necks! Some of them are getting away. Watch out, watch out, Giglio!

GIGLIO:

925Where are these hens? From here I don't see any hens, or cocks for that matter.

PASQUELLA:

926You don't? They're over here. Move back a little and let me close the door for a bit so that I can put them back where they belong.

[She enters the house, closing and locking the door]

GIGLIO:

927Hey, you're locking the door! Why are you doing that?

PASQUELLA:

928Because I don't want any cocks opening it.

GIGLIO:

929Hurry up before someone comes along and disturbs us.

PASQUELLA:

930It doesn't matter who comes along, as no one is opening anything.

GIGLIO:

931[Aside] Damn her, the old hag. [To PASQUELLA] Tell me why you aren't opening up.

PASQUELLA:

932My dear Giglio, don't you realize that first I must say this whole rosary? You can forget about this evening. And I forgot, I also have a prayer to say that I mustn't overlook.

GIGLIO:

933Who foolishness is this? What rosary, what prayer?

PASQUELLA:

934What prayer? Do you want me to teach it to you? It's a good one: "Ghost, ghost, the wanders day and night, if you have come all hard to me, all hard you'll have to leave. Evil with evil, you've come to me at the wrong time thinking to have me, and you will remain deceived. Amen."

GIGLIO:

935I don't understand your prayer. If you don't want to open the door, give me back my rosary and I'll go my way. [Aside] In the name of all the holy martyrs, if this ugly old babbling untrustworthy bat has tricked me ... [To PASQUELLA] Lady Pasquella, open up at once, if you value your life!

PASQUELLA:

936[Singing] "What is my lover doing that he doesn't come? The love of another girl keeps him away from me." Alas!

GIGLIO:

937What? Don't say that, my lady Pasquella! He's right here, waiting for you to let him in.

PASQUELLA:

938[Singing] "I cannot serve you, my dear lord." Oh dear!

GIGLIO:

939[Aside] More songs, that damn woman. She doesn't realize that I'm out there. I'll knock on the door, I swear to God.

[Knocking]

PASQUELLA:

940Who's there?

GIGLIO:

941Your little boy.

PASQUELLA:

942What do you want? My master's not at home. Do you want to leave a message?

GIGLIO:

943A word.

PASQUELLA:

944Wait a bit, he should be here shortly.

GIGLIO:

945Open up so that I can wait inside. Come on. [Aside] May the whole world be damned if I don't burn down this house if she doesn't give me my rosary!

PASQUELLA:

946[Pounding on the door] Hello! Who's there? Could you be a little more discreet? Forgive me, but who are you? Oh my! Are you trying to break down the door?

GIGLIO:

947I swear by God and event Saint Litany that I'll burn down this door if you don't give me back my rosary!

PASQUELLA:

948Maybe you should look elsewhere. We usually don't grow roses in our garden.

GIGLIO:

949You misunderstand, I'm saying my rosary beads, my Our Fathers.

PASQUELLA:

950What business is it of mine if you say your Our Fathers? Are you suggesting that I should become a false convert like you Spanish Jews, and that I'd need to learn them over again like you?

GIGLIO:

951Damn you, you whore, you old hag! Now you're calling me a false convert?

PASQUELLA:

952You know that? If you don't get away from that door, I'm going to throw water on you.

GIGLIO:

953Go ahead, throw your water. I'm going yo burn down your door. [PASQUELLA dumps a bucket of water on him] Damn you! She has drowned me, this woman, hag, old whore, tramp! To hell with all that's holy!

PASQUELLA:

954Did you get all wet? I didn't notice you. [GHERARDO enters] But here comes my master. If you want something, ask him and don't brother me anymore.

GIGLIO:

955If that old man finds me here, I'll be in big trouble. I'd better get out of here.

SCENE VII

GHERARDO and PASQUELLA

GHERARDO:

956What were you up to with that Spaniard here by the door? What do you have to do with him?

PASQUELLA:

957He was asking about some rose garden. But I didn't pay any attention to him.

GHERARDO:

958Well, you sure did exactly as I told you! I'd like to break your neck.

PASQUELLA:

959Why?

GHERARDO:

960Why did you let Lelia escape? Didn't I tell you not to let her out?

PASQUELLA:

961What do you mean, escape? Isn't she in the room?

GHERARDO:

985What's wrong? Who is that man with my daughter?

986Oh, don't you know? Isn't it Virginio's baby girl?

988My, don't say such things! What's wrong? Isn't it Lelia?

990What? That can't be true. You can't be right!

992How?

GHERARDO:

993On top of my daughter, alas!

995I wish it were a prank!

1005Impossible. I locked him back in. But there's Virginio. Just the man I'm looking for.
