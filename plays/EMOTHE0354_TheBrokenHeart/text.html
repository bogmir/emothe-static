John Ford

The Broken Heart

The Prologue

1Our scene is Sparta. He whose best of art
2Hath drawn this piece calls it The Broken Heart .
3The title lends no expectation here
4Of apish laughter, or of some lame jeer
5At place or persons; no pretended clause
6Of jests fit for a brothel courts applause
7From vulgar admiration: such low songs,
8Tun'd to unchaste ears, suit not modest tongues.
9The Virgin Sisters then deserv'd fresh bays,
10When Innocence and Sweetness crown'd their lays;
11Then vices gasp'd for breath, whose whole commerce
12Was whipp'd to exile by unblushing verse.
13This law we keep in our presentment now,
14Not to take freedom more than we allow.
15What may be here thought a fiction, when time's youth
16Wanted some riper years, was known a truth:
17In which, if words have cloth'd the subject right,
18You may partake a pity with delight.

Actus Primus:

Scæna prima

[House of Crotolon]

Enter Crotolon and Orgilus

Crotolon

1Dally not further; I will know the reason
2That speeds thee to this journey.

Orgilus

That speeds thee to this journey. Reason! good sir,
3I can yield many.

Crotolon

I can yield many. Give me one, a good one;
4Such I expect, and ere we part must have.
5Athens! Pray, why to Athens? You intend not
6To kick against the world, turn Cynic, Stoic?
7Or read the logic lecture? or become
8An Areopagite, and judge in causes
9Touching the commonwealth? for, as I take it,
10The budding of your chin cannot prognosticate
11So grave an honour.

Orgilus

So grave an honour. All this I acknowledge.

Crotolon

12You do! Then, son, if books and love of knowledge
13Inflame you to this travel, here in Sparta
14You may as freely study.

Orgilus

You may as freely study. 'T is not that, sir.

Crotolon

15Not that, sir! As a father, I command thee
16To acquaint me with the truth.

Orgilus

To acquaint me with the truth. Thus I obey ye.
17After so many quarrels as dissension,
18Fury, and rage had broach'd in blood, and sometimes
19With death to such confederates as sided
20With now-dead Thrasus and yourself, my lord;
21Our present king, Amyclas, reconcil'd
22Your eager swords and seal'd a gentle peace.
23Friends you profess'd yourselves; which to confirm,
24A resolution for a lasting league
25Betwixt your families was entertain'd,
26By joining in a Hymenean bond
27Me and the fair Penthea, only daughter
28To Thrasus.

Crotolon

To Thrasus. What of this?

Orgilus

To Thrasus. What of this? Much, much, dear sir.
29A freedom of converse, an interchange
30Of holy and chaste love, so fix'd our souls
31In a firm growth of holy union, that no time
32Can eat into the pledge. We had enjoy'd
33The sweets our vows expected, had not cruelty
34Prevented all those triumphs we prepar'd for
35By Thrasus his untimely death.

Crotolon

By Thrasus his untimely death. Most certain.

Orgilus

36From this time sprouted up that poisonous stalk
37Of aconite, whose ripen'd fruit hath ravish'd
38All health, all comfort of a happy life;
39For Ithocles, her brother, proud of youth,
40And prouder in his power, nourish'd closely
41The memory of former discontents,
42To glory in revenge. By cunning partly,
43Partly by threats, 'a woos at once and forces
44His virtuous sister to admit a marriage
45With Bassanes, a nobleman, in honour
46And riches, I confess, beyond my fortunes.

Crotolon

47All this is no sound reason to importune
48My leave for thy departure.

Orgilus

My leave for thy departure. Now it follows.
49Beauteous Penthea, wedded to this torture
50By an insulting brother, being secretly
51Compell'd to yield her virgin freedom up
52To him who never can usurp her heart,
53Before contracted mine, is now so yok'd
54To a most barbarous thraldom, misery,
55Affliction, that he savours not humanity,
56Whose sorrow melts not into more than pity
57In hearing but her name.

Crotolon

In hearing but her name. As how, pray?

Orgilus

In hearing but her name. As how, pray? Bassanes,
58The man that calls her wife, considers truly
59What heaven of perfections he is lord of
60By thinking fair Penthea his. This thought
61Begets a kind of monster-love, which love
62Is nurse unto a fear so strong and servile
63As brands all dotage with a jealousy:
64All eyes who gaze upon that shrine of beauty,
65He doth resolve, do homage to the miracle;
66Some one, he is assur'd, may now or then,
67If opportunity but sort, prevail.
68So much, out of a self-unworthiness,
69His fears transport him; not that he finds cause
70In her obedience, but his own distrust.

Crotolon

71You spin out your discourse.

Orgilus

You spin out your discourse. My griefs are violent:
72For knowing how the maid was heretofore
73Courted by me, his jealousies grow wild
74That I should steal again into her favours,
75And undermine her virtues; which the gods
76Know I nor dare nor dream of. Hence, from hence
77I undertake a voluntary exile;
78First, by my absence to take off the cares
79Of jealous Bassanes; but chiefly, sir,
80To free Penthea from a hell on earth;
81Lastly, to lose the memory of something
82Her presence makes to live in me afresh.

Crotolon

83Enough, my Orgilus, enough. To Athens!
84I give a full consent. — Alas, good lady! —
85We shall hear from thee often?

Orgilus

We shall hear from thee often? Often.

Crotolon

We shall hear from thee often? Often. See,
86Thy sister comes to give a farewell.

Enter Euphranea

Euphranea

Thy sister comes to give a farewell. Brother!

Orgilus

87Euphranea, thus upon thy cheeks I print
88A brother's kiss; more careful of thine honour,
89Thy health, and thy well-doing, than my life.
90Before we part, in presence of our father,
91I must prefer a suit t' ye.

Euphranea

I must prefer a suit t' ye. You may style it,
92My brother, a command.

Orgilus

My brother, a command. That you will promise
93To pass never to any man, however
94Worthy, your faith, till, with our father's leave,
95I give a free consent.

Crotolon

I give a free consent. An easy motion!
96I'll promise for her, Orgilus.

Orgilus

I'll promise for her, Orgilus. Your pardon;
97Euphranea's oath must yield me satisfaction.

Euphranea

98By Vesta's sacred fires I swear.

Crotolon

By Vesta's sacred fires I swear. And I,
99By great Apollo's beams, join in the vow,
100Not without thy allowance to bestow her
101On any living.

Orgilus

On any living. Dear Euphranea,
102Mistake me not: far, far 't is from my thought,
103As far from any wish of mine, to hinder
104Preferment to an honourable bed
105Or fitting fortune. Thou art young and handsome;
106And 't were injustice, — more, a tyranny, —
107Not to advance thy merit. Trust me, sister,
108It shall be my first care to see thee match'd
109As may become thy choice and our contents.
110I have your oath.

Euphranea

I have your oath. You have. But mean you, brother,
111To leave us, as you say?

Crotolon

To leave us, as you say? Ay, ay, Euphranea.
112He has just grounds direct him. I will prove
113A father and a brother to thee.

Euphranea

A father and a brother to thee. Heaven
114Does look into the secrets of all hearts.
115Gods, you have mercy with ye, else —

Crotolon

Gods, you have mercy with ye, else — Doubt nothing;
116Thy brother will return in safety to us.

Orgilus

117Souls sunk in sorrows never are without 'em;
118They change fresh airs, but bear their griefs about 'em.

Exeunt omnes.

Calantha

Prophilus

Calantha

But Ithocles — Your friend —

Prophilus

43He, in this firmament of honour, stands
44Like a star fix'd, not mov'd with any thunder
45Of popular applause or sudden lightning
46Of self-opinion. He hath serv'd his country,
47And thinks 't was but his duty.

Crotolon

And thinks 't was but his duty. You describe
48A miracle of man.

Amyclas

A miracle of man. Such, Crotolon,
49Flourish. On forfeit of a king's word, thou wilt find him. —
50Hark, warning of his coming! All attend him.

Armostes

Ithocles

Calantha

Requires, for payment? 'A speaks truth.

Ithocles

81Applause runs madding, like the drunken priests
82In Bacchus' sacrifices, without reason
83Voicing the leader-on a demi-god;
84Whenas, indeed, each common soldier's blood
85Drops down as current coin in that hard purchase
86As his whose much more delicate condition
87Hath suck'd the milk of ease. Judgment commands,
88But resolution executes. I use not,
89Before this royal presence, these fit slights
90As in contempt of such as can direct;
91My speech hath other end: not to attribute
92All praise to one man's fortune, which is strengthen'd
93By many hands. For instance, here is Prophilus,
94A gentleman — I cannot flatter truth —
95Of much desert; and, though in other rank,
96Both Lemophil and Groneas were not missing
97To wish their country's peace; for, in a word,
98All there did strive their best, and 't was our duty.

Amyclas

99Courtiers turn soldiers! — We vouchsafe our hand.

100Observe your great example.

Lemophil

Groneas

101Obsequiously and hourly.

Amyclas

Euphranea

On! — Come, my Ithocles. Sir, with your favour,
105I need not a supporter.

Prophilus

I need not a supporter. Fate instructs me.

106With me?

Groneas

Groneas

Lemophil

Philema

Groneas

Philema

126Will hardly yield an interest.

Groneas

127I'll marry thee!

Philema

128Except my mind do alter strangely.

Groneas

Except my mind do alter strangely. One word.

Christalla

129You lie beyond all modesty: — forbear me.

Lemophil

130I'll make thee mistress of a city; 't is
131Mine own by conquest.

Christalla

132In forma pauperis . — City! kennel. — Gallants,
133Off with your feathers, put on aprons, gallants;
134Learn to reel, thrum, or trim a lady's dog,
135And be good quiet souls of peace, hobgoblins!

Lemophil

136Christalla!

Christalla

Christalla! Practise to drill hogs, in hope
137To share in the acorns. — Soldiers! corncutters,
138But not so valiant: they ofttimes draw blood,
139Which you durst never do. When you have practis'd
140More wit or more civility, we'll rank ye
141I' th' list of men: till then, brave things-at-arms,
142Dare not to speak to us, — most potent Groneas! —

Philema

143And Lemophil the hardy! — at your services.

Groneas

144They scorn us as they did before we went.

Lemophil

145Hang 'em! let us scorn them, and be reveng'd.

Groneas

146Shall we?

Lemophil

147We will: and when we slight them thus,
148Instead of following them, they'll follow us;
149It is a woman's nature.

Groneas

Scene 3

[Grove near the palace]

Enter Tecnicus, a philosopher, and Orgilus disguised like a Scholar of his

Tecnicus

1Tempt not the stars; young man, thou canst not play
2With the severity of fate: this change
3Of habit and disguise in outward view
4Hides not the secrets of thy soul within thee
5From their quick-piercing eyes, which dive at all times
6Down to thy thoughts. In thy aspect I note
7A consequence of danger.

Orgilus

A consequence of danger. Give me leave,
8Grave Tecnicus, without foredooming destiny,
9Under thy roof to ease my silent griefs
10By applying to my hidden wounds the balm
11Of thy oraculous lectures. If my fortune
12Run such a crooked by-way as to wrest
13My steps to ruin, yet thy learned precepts
14Shall call me back and set my footings straight.
15I will not court the world.

Tecnicus

I will not court the world. Ah, Orgilus,
16Neglects in young men of delights and life
17Run often to extremities; they care not
18For harms to others who contemn their own.

Orgilus

19But I, most learned artist, am not so much
20At odds with nature that I grutch the thrift
21Of any true deserver; nor doth malice
22Of present hopes so check them with despair
23As that I yield to thought of more affliction
24Than what is incident to frailty: wherefore
25Impute not this retired course of living
26Some little time to any other cause
27Than what I justly render, — the information
28Of an unsettled mind; as the effect
29Must clearly witness.

Tecnicus

Must clearly witness. Spirit of truth inspire thee!
30On these conditions I conceal thy change,
31And willingly admit thee for an auditor. —
32I'll to my study.

Orgilus

I'll to my study. I to contemplations
33In these delightful walks.

[Exit Tecnicus.]

34I may without suspicion hearken after
35Penthea's usage and Euphranea's faith.

37Themselves are not secure in searching out
38The secrets of those flames, which, hidden, waste
39A breast made tributary to the laws

41A remedy to cure a lover's wound. —
42Ha! who are those that cross yon private walk
43Into the shadowing grove in amorous foldings?

150The like from you to me: so can we never,

Euphranea

153Occasion is most favourable; use it.

Calantha

6A fair excuse for absence: as for Bassanes,
7Delights to him are troublesome. Armostes
8Is with the king?

Crotolon

Is with the king? He is.

Calantha

Is with the king? He is. On to the dance! —

12Fall to our dance.

Enter Armostes

Armostes

13(In Calantha's ear.) The king your father 's dead.

Calantha

14To the other change.

Armostes

To the other change. Is 't possible?

Bassanes

To the other change. Is 't possible? [Whispers Calantha.] O, madam!

Calantha

Penthea, poor Penthea 's starv'd. Beshrew thee! —
16Lead to the next.

Bassanes

Lead to the next. Amazement dulls my senses.

Dance again.

Orgilus

17[Whispers Calantha.] Brave Ithocles is murther'd, murther'd cruelly.

Calantha

18How dull this music sounds! Strike up more sprightly;

20Which treads the nimbler measure.

Orgilus

Which treads the nimbler measure. I am thunderstruck.

Last change. Cease music.

Calantha

21So! let us breathe awhile. — Hath not this motion
22Rais'd fresher colour on your cheeks?

Nearchus

Rais'd fresher colour on your cheeks? Sweet princess,
23A perfect purity of blood enamels
24The beauty of your white.

Calantha

The beauty of your white. We all look cheerfully;
25And, cousin, 't is, methinks, a rare presumption
26In any who prefer our lawful pleasures
27Before their own sour censure, to interrupt
28The custom of this ceremony bluntly.

Nearchus

29None dares, lady.

Calantha

30Yes, yes; some hollow voice deliver'd to me
31How that the king was dead.

Armostes

How that the king was dead. The king is dead:
32That fatal news was mine; for in mine arms
33He breath'd his last, and with his crown bequeath'd ye
34Your mother's wedding ring; which here I tender.

Crotolon

35Most strange!

Calantha

36Peace crown his ashes! We are queen, then.

Nearchus

37Long live Calantha! Sparta's sovereign queen!

Omnes

38Long live the queen!

Calantha

Long live the queen! What whisper'd Bassanes?

Bassanes

39That my Penthea, miserable soul,
40Was starv'd to death.

Calantha

Was starv'd to death. She's happy; she hath finish'd
41A long and painful progress. — A third murmur
42Pierc'd mine unwilling ears.

Orgilus

Pierc'd mine unwilling ears. That Ithocles
43Was murther'd; — rather butcher'd, had not bravery
44Of an undaunted spirit, conquering terror,
45Proclaim'd his last act triumph over ruin.

Armostes

46How! murther'd!

Calantha

How! murther'd! By whose hand?

Orgilus

How! murther'd! By whose hand? By mine; this weapon
47Was instrument to my revenge: the reasons
48Are just, and known; quit him of these, and then
49Never liv'd gentleman of greater merit,
50Hope or abiliment to steer a kingdom.

Crotolon

51Fie, Orgilus!

Euphranea

Fie, Orgilus! Fie, brother!

Calantha

Fie, Orgilus! Fie, brother! You have done it?

Bassanes

52How it was done let him report, the forfeit
53Of whose allegiance to our laws doth covet
54Rigour of justice; but that done it is,
55Mine eyes have been an evidence of credit
56Too sure to be convinc'd. Armostes, rent not
57Thine arteries with hearing the bare circumstances
58Of these calamities. Thou 'st lost a nephew,
59A niece, and I a wife: continue man still.
60Make me the pattern of digesting evils,
61Who can outlive my mighty ones, not shrinking
62At such a pressure as would sink a soul
63Into what's most of death, the worst of horrors.
64But I have seal'd a covenant with sadness,
65And enter'd into bonds without condition,
66To stand these tempests calmly. Mark me, nobles:
67I do not shed a tear, not for Penthea!
68Excellent misery!

Calantha

Excellent misery! We begin our reign
69With a first act of justice: thy confession,
70Unhappy Orgilus, dooms thee a sentence;
71But yet thy father's or thy sister's presence
72Shall be excus'd. — Give, Crotolon, a blessing
73To thy lost son; — Euphranea, take a farewell; —
74And both be gone.

Crotolon

And both be gone. [To Orgilus.] Confirm thee, noble sorrow,
75In worthy resolution!

Euphranea

In worthy resolution! Could my tears speak,
76My griefs were slight.

Orgilus

My griefs were slight. All goodness dwell amongst ye!
77Enjoy my sister, Prophilus: my vengeance
78Aim'd never at thy prejudice.

Calantha

Aim'd never at thy prejudice. Now withdraw.

Exeunt Crotolon, Prophilus, and Euphranea.

79Bloody relater of thy stains in blood,
80For that thou hast reported him, whose fortunes
81And life by thee are both at once snatch'd from him,
82With honourable mention, make thy choice
83Of what death likes thee best; there's all our bounty. —
84But to excuse delays, let me, dear cousin,
85Intreat you and these lords see execution
86Instant before ye part.

Nearchus

Instant before ye part. Your will commands us.

Orgilus

87One suit, just queen, my last: vouchsafe your clemency,
88That by no common hand I be divided
89From this my humble frailty.

Calantha

From this my humble frailty. To their wisdoms
90Who are to be spectators of thine end
91I make the reference. Those that are dead
92Are dead; had they not now died, of necessity
93They must have paid the debt they ow'd to nature
94One time or other. — Use dispatch, my lords;
95We'll suddenly prepare our coronation.

Exeunt Calantha, Philema, Christalla.

Armostes

96'T is strange these tragedies should never touch on
97Her female pity.

Bassanes

Her female pity. She has a masculine spirit;
98And wherefore should I pule, and, like a girl,
99Put finger in the eye? Let's be all toughness,
100Without distinction betwixt sex and sex.

Nearchus

101Now, Orgilus, thy choice?

Orgilus

Now, Orgilus, thy choice? To bleed to death.

Armostes

102The executioner?

Orgilus

The executioner? Myself, no surgeon;
103I am well skill'd in letting blood. Bind fast
104This arm, that so the pipes may from their conduits
105[Shows his dagger.] Convey a full stream; here's a skilful instrument.
106Only I am a beggar to some charity
107To speed me in this execution
108By lending th' other prick to th' tother arm,
109When this is bubbling life out.

Bassanes

When this is bubbling life out. I am for 'ee;
110It most concerns my art, my care, my credit. —
111Quick, fillet both his arms.

Orgilus

Quick, fillet both his arms. Gramercy, friendship!
112Such courtesies are real which flow cheerfully
113Without an expectation of requital.
114Reach me a staff in this hand.

[They give him a staff.]

Reach me a staff in this hand. — If a proneness
115Or custom in my nature from my cradle
116Had been inclin'd to fierce and eager bloodshed,
117A coward guilt, hid in a coward quaking,
118Would have betray'd fame to ignoble flight
119And vagabond pursuit of dreadful safety:
120But look upon my steadiness, and scorn not
121The sickness of my fortune, which, since Bassanes
122Was husband to Penthea, had lain bed-rid.
123We trifle time in words: — thus I show cunning
124In opening of a vein too full, too lively.

[Pierces the vein with his dagger.]

Armostes

125Desperate courage!

Orgilus

Desperate courage! Honourable infamy!

Lemophil

126I tremble at the sight.

Groneas

I tremble at the sight. Would I were loose!

Bassanes

127It sparkles like a lusty wine new broach'd;
128The vessel must be sound from which it issues. —
129Grasp hard this other stick — I'll be as nimble —
130But prithee, look not pale — have at ye! stretch out
131Thine arm with vigour and unshook virtue.

[Opens the vein.]

132Good! O, I envy not a rival, fitted
133To conquer in extremities. This pastime
134Appears majestical; some high-tun'd poem
135Hereafter shall deliver to posterity
136The writer's glory and his subject's triumph.
137How is 't, man? Droop not yet.

Orgilus

How is 't, man? Droop not yet. I feel no palsies.
138On a pair-royal do I wait in death;
139My sovereign, as his liegeman; on my mistress,
140As a devoted servant; and on Ithocles,
141As, if no brave, yet no unworthy enemy.
142Nor did I use an engine to entrap
143His life, out of a slavish fear to combat
144Youth, strength, or cunning; but for that I durst not
145Engage the goodness of a cause on fortune,
146By which his name might have outfac'd my vengeance.
147O, Tecnicus, inspir'd with Phœbus' fire!
148I call to mind thy augury: 't was perfect;
149Revenge proves its own executioner .
150When feeble man is bending to his mother,
151The dust 'a was first fram'd on, thus he totters.

Bassanes

152Life's fountain is dri'd up.

Orgilus

Life's fountain is dri'd up. So falls the standard
153Of my prerogative in being a creature!
154A mist hangs o'er mine eyes, the sun's bright splendour
155Is clouded in an everlasting shadow:
156Welcome, thou ice, that sitt'st about my heart!
157No heat can ever thaw thee.

Dies.

Nearchus

No heat can ever thaw thee. Speech hath left him.

Bassanes

158'A has shook hands with time; his funeral urn
159Shall be my charge: remove the bloodless body.
160The coronation must require attendance;
161That past, my few days can be but one mourning.

[A Temple]

An altar covered with white; two lights of virgin wax, during which music of recorders; enter four bearing Ithocles on a hearse, or in a chair, in a rich robe, and a crown on his head; place him on one side of the altar. After him enter Calantha in a white robe and crown'd; Euphranea, Philema, Christalla, in white; Nearchus, Armostes, Crotolon, Prophilus, Amelus, Bassanes, Lemophil, and Groneas.

Calantha goes and kneels before the altar, the rest stand off, the women kneeling behind. Cease recorders, during her devotions. Soft music. Calantha and the rest rise, doing obeisance to the altar.

Calantha

1Our orisons are heard; the gods are merciful. —
2Now tell me, you whose loyalties pay tribute
3To us your lawful sovereign, how unskilful
4Your duties or obedience is to render
5Subjection to the sceptre of a virgin,
6Who have been ever fortunate in princes
7Of masculine and stirring composition.
8A woman has enough to govern wisely
9Her own demeanours, passions, and divisions.
10A nation warlike and inur'd to practice
11Of policy and labour cannot brook
12A feminate authority: we therefore
13Command your counsel, how you may advise us
14In choosing of a husband whose abilities
15Can better guide this kingdom.

Nearchus

Can better guide this kingdom. Royal lady,
16Your law is in your will.

Armostes

Your law is in your will. We have seen tokens
17Of constancy too lately to mistrust it.

Crotolon

18Yet, if your highness settle on a choice
19By your own judgment both allow'd and lik'd of,
20Sparta may grow in power, and proceed
21To an increasing height.

Calantha

To an increasing height. Hold you the same mind?

Bassanes

22Alas, great mistress, reason is so clouded
23With the thick darkness of my infinite woes,
24That I forecast nor dangers, hopes, or safety.
25Give me some corner of the world to wear out
26The remnant of the minutes I must number,
27Where I may hear no sounds but sad complaints
28Of virgins who have lost contracted partners;
29Of husbands howling that their wives were ravish'd
30By some untimely fate; of friends divided
31By churlish opposition; or of fathers
32Weeping upon their children's slaughter'd carcases;
33Or daughters groaning o'er their fathers' hearses:
34And I can dwell there, and with these keep consort
35As musical as theirs. What can you look for
36From an old, foolish, peevish, doting man
37But craziness of age?

Calantha

But craziness of age? Cousin of Argos, —

Nearchus

But craziness of age? Cousin of Argos, — Madam?

Calantha

But craziness of age? Cousin of Argos, — Madam? Were I presently
38To choose you for my lord, I'll open freely
39What articles I would propose to treat on
40Before our marriage.

Nearchus

Before our marriage. Name them, virtuous lady.

Calantha

41I would presume you would retain the royalty
42Of Sparta in her own bounds; then in Argos
43Armostes might be viceroy; in Messene
44Might Crotolon bear sway; and Bassanes —

Bassanes

45I, queen! alas, what I?

Calantha

I, queen! alas, what I? Be Sparta's marshal.
46The multitudes of high employments could not
47But set a peace to private griefs. These gentlemen,
48Groneas and Lemophil, with worthy pensions,
49Should wait upon your person in your chamber. —
50I would bestow Christalla on Amelus, —
51She'll prove a constant wife; and Philema
52Should into Vesta's Temple.

Bassanes

Should into Vesta's Temple. This is a testament!
53It sounds not like conditions on a marriage.

Nearchus

54All this should be perform'd.

Calantha

All this should be perform'd. Lastly, for Prophilus,
55He should be, cousin, solemnly invested
56In all those honours, titles, and preferments
57Which his dear friend and my neglected husband
58Too short a time enjoy'd.

Prophilus

Too short a time enjoy'd. I am unworthy
59To live in your remembrance.

Euphranea

To live in your remembrance. Excellent lady!

Nearchus

60Madam, what means that word, "neglected husband"?

Calantha

61Forgive me: — now I turn to thee, thou shadow
62Of my contracted lord! Bear witness all,
63I put my mother's wedding-ring upon
64His finger; 't was my father's last bequest.

[Places a ring on the finger of Ithocles.]

65Thus I new-marry him whose wife I am;
66Death shall not separate us. O, my lords,
67I but deceiv'd your eyes with antic gesture,
68When one news straight came huddling on another
69Of death, and death, and death! still I danc'd forward;
70But it struck home, and here, and in an instant.
71Be such mere women, who with shrieks and outcries
72Can vow a present end to all their sorrows,
73Yet live to vow new pleasures, and outlive them?
74They are the silent griefs which cut the heartstrings;
75Let me die smiling.

Nearchus

Let me die smiling. 'T is a truth too ominous.

Calantha

All

1Glories, pleasures, pomps, delights, and ease,
2Can but please
3Outward senses when the mind
4Is not untroubled or by peace refin'd.

1 [Voice]

5Crowns may flourish and decay,
6Beauties shine, but fade away.

2 [Voice]

7Youth may revel, yet it must
8Lie down in a bed of dust.

3 [Voice.

9Earthly honours flow and waste,
10Time alone doth change and last.

All

11Sorrows mingled with contents prepare
12Rest for care;
13Love only reigns in death; though art
14Can find no comfort for a broken heart.

Armostes

15Look to the queen!

Bassanes

Look to the queen! Her heart is broke, indeed.
16O, royal maid, would thou hadst miss'd this part!
17Yet 't was a brave one. I must weep to see
18Her smile in death.

Armostes

Her smile in death. Wise Tecnicus! thus said he.
19When youth is ripe, and age from time doth part,
20The Lifeless Trunk shall wed the Broken Heart.
21'T is here fulfill'd.

Nearchus

'T is here fulfill'd. I am your king.

Omnes

'T is here fulfill'd. I am your king. Long live
22Nearchus, King of Sparta!

Nearchus

Nearchus, King of Sparta! Her last will
23Shall never be digress'd from: wait in order
24Upon these faithful lovers, as becomes us. —
25The counsels of the gods are never known
26Till men can call th' effects of them their own.

[Exeunt.]

27WHERE noble judgments and clear eyes are fix'd
28To grace endeavour, there sits truth, not mix'd
29With ignorance: those censures may command
30Belief which talk not till they understand.
31Let some say, "This was flat;" some, "Here the scene
32Fell from its height;" another, that the mean
33Was ill observ'd in such a growing passion
34As it transcended either state or fashion.
35Some few may cry, "'T was pretty well," or so,
36"But —" and there shrug in silence; yet we know
37Our writer's aim was in the whole address'd
38Well to deserve of all, but please the best:
39Which granted, by th' allowance of this strain
40The Broken Heart may be piec'd up again.
