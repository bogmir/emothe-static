Act II

Scene i

[Enter Bosola and Castruchio.]

BOSOLA.

You say you would fain be taken for an eminent courtier?

CASTRUCHIO.

‘Tis the very main of my ambition.

BOSOLA.

Let me see, you have a reasonable good face for’t already, and your nightcap expresses your ears sufficient largely; I would have you learn to twirl the strings of your band with a good grace; and in a set speech, at th’ end of every sentence, to hum, three or four times, or blow your nose, till it smart again, to recover your memory. When you come to be a president in criminal causes, if you smile upon a prisoner, hang him, but if you frown upon him, and threaten him, let him be sure to scape the gallows.

CASTRUCHIO.

I would be a very merry president,―

BOSOLA.

Do not sup a nights; ’twill beget you an admirable wit.

CASTRUCHIO.

Rather it would make me have a good stomach to quarrel, for they say your roaring boys eat meat seldom, and that makes them so valiant: but how shall I know whether the people take me for an eminent fellow?

BOSOLA.

I will teach a trick to know it: give out you lie a- dying, and if you hear the common people curse you, be sure you are taken for one of the prime nightcaps. [Enter Old Lady] You come from painting now?

OLD LADY.

From what?

BOSOLA.

Why, from your scurvy face physic: To behold thee not painted inclines somewhat near a miracle. These in thy face here, were deep ruts and foul sloughs, the last progress. There was a lady in France, that having had the smallpox, flayed the skin off her face, to make it more level; and whereas before she looked like a nutmeg grater, after she resembled an abortive hedgehog.

OLD LADY.

Do you call this painting?

BOSOLA.

No, no but you call it careening of an old mor- phewed lady, to make her disembogue again. There’s rough- cast phrase to your plastic.

OLD LADY.

BOSOLA.

One would suspect it for a shop of witchcraft, to find in it the fat of serpents; spawn of snakes, Jews’ spittle, and their young children’s ordure, and all these for the face. I would sooner eat a dead pigeon, taken from the soles of the feet of one sick of the plague, than kiss one of you fasting. Here are two of you, whose sin of your youth is the very patrimony of the physician, makes him renew his footcloth with the spring, and change his high-prized courtesan with the fall of the leaf: I do wonder you do not loathe yourselves. Scene ii

[Enter Bosola and Old Lady]

BOSOLA.

So, so: there’s no question but her tetchiness and most vulturous eating of the apricots, are apparent signs of breeding, now?

OLD LADY.

I am in haste, sir.

BOSOLA.

OLD LADY.

BOSOLA.

And it was only to know what strange instrument it was, should swell up a glass to the fashion of a woman’s belly.

OLD LADY.

I will hear no more of the glass-house, you are still abusing women?

BOSOLA.

Who, I? no, only, by the way now and then, mention your frailties. The orange tree bears ripe and green fruit and blossoms altogether. And some of you give entertainment for pure love: but more, for more precious reward. The lusty spring smells well: but drooping autumn tastes well. If we have the same golden showers, that rained in the time of Jupiter the Thunderer: you have the same Danaes still, to hold up their laps to receive them: didst thou never study the mathematics?

OLD LADY.

What’s that, sir?

BOSOLA.

Why, to know the trick how to make a many lines meet in one centre. Go, go; give your foster-daughters good counsel: tell them, that the devil takes delight to hang at a woman’s girdle, like a false rusty watch, that she cannot discern how the time passes.

82They pass through whirlpools, and deep woes do shun,
83Who the event weigh, ere the action’s done .

Exit.

Scene v

[Enter] Cardinal, and Ferdinand, with a letter.

FERDINAND.

1I have this night digged up a mandrake.

CARDINAL.

I have this night digged up a mandrake. Say you?

FERDINAND.

2And I am grown mad with’t.

CARDINAL.

And I am grown mad with’t. What’s the prodigy?

FERDINAND.

3Read there, a sister damned, she’s loose i’th’hilts:
4Grown a notorious strumpet.

CARDINAL.

Grown a notorious strumpet. Speak lower.

FERDINAND.

Grown a notorious strumpet. Speak lower. Lower?
5Rogues do not whisper’t now, but seek to publish’t,
6As servants do the bounty of their lords,
7Aloud; and with a covetous searching eye,
8To mark who note them. Oh confusion seize her,
9She hath had most cunning bawds to serve her turn,
10And more secure conveyances for lust,
11Than towns of garrison, for service.

CARDINAL.

Than towns of garrison, for service. Is’t possible?
12Can this be certain?

FERDINAND.

Can this be certain? Rhubarb, oh for rhubarb
13To purge this choler; here’s the cursed day
14To prompt my memory, and here’t shall stick
15Till of her bleeding heart I make a sponge
16To wipe it out.

CARDINAL.

To wipe it out. Why do you make yourself
17So wild a tempest?

FERDINAND.

So wild a tempest? Would I could be one,
18That I might toss her palace ’bout her ears,
19Root up her goodly forests, blast her meads,
20And lay her general territory as waste,
21As she hath done her honour’s.

CARDINAL.

As she hath done her honour’s. Shall our blood?
22The royal blood of Aragon and Castile,
23Be thus attainted?

FERDINAND.

Be thus attainted? Apply desperate physic,
24We must not now use balsamum, but fire,
25The smarting cupping-glass, for that’s the mean
26To purge infected blood, such blood as hers.
27There is a kind of pity in mine eye,
28I’ll give it to my handkercher; and, now ’tis here,
29I’ll bequeath this to her bastard.

CARDINAL.

I’ll bequeath this to her bastard. What to do?

FERDINAND.

30Why, to make soft lint for his mother’s wounds,
31When I have hewed her to pieces.

CARDINAL.

When I have hewed her to pieces. Cursed creature!
32Unequal Nature, to place women’s hearts
33So far upon the left side.

FERDINAND.

So far upon the left side. Foolish men,
34That e’er will trust their honour in a bark,
35Made of so slight, weak bulrush, as is woman,
36Apt every minute to sink it!

CARDINAL.

37Thus ignorance, when it hath purchased honour,
38It cannot wield it.

FERDINAND.

It cannot wield it. Methinks I see her laughing,
39Excellent hyena! Talk to me somewhat, quickly,
40Or my imagination will carry me
41To see her in the shameful act of sin.

CARDINAL.

42With whom?

FERDINAND.

43Happily, with some strong-thighed bargeman;
44Or one o’th’ wood-yard, that can quoit the sledge
45Or toss the bar, or else some lovely squire
46That carries coals up to her privy lodgings.

CARDINAL.

47You fly beyond your reason.

FERDINAND.

You fly beyond your reason. Go to, mistress!
48’Tis not your whore’s milk, that shall quench my wild-fire
49But your whore’s blood.

CARDINAL.

50How idly shows this rage! which carries you,
51As men conveyed by witches, through the air
52On violent whirlwinds: this intemperate noise
53Fitly resembles deaf men’s shrill discourse,
54Who talk aloud, thinking all other men
55To have their imperfection.

FERDINAND.

To have their imperfection. Have not you
56My palsy?

CARDINAL.

My palsy? Yes, I can be angry
57Without this rupture; there is not in nature
58A thing, that makes man so deformed, so beastly
59As doth intemperate anger; chide yourself:
60You have divers men, who never yet expressed
61Their strong desire of rest but by unrest,
62By vexing of themselves. Come, put yourself
63In tune.

FERDINAND.

In tune. So, I will only study to seem
64The thing I am not. I could kill her now,
65In you, or in myself, for I do think
66It is some sin in us, Heaven doth revenge
67By her.

CARDINAL.

By her. Are you stark mad?

FERDINAND.

By her. Are you stark mad? I would have their bodies
68Burnt in a coal-pit, with the ventage stopped,
69That their cursed smoke might not ascend to Heaven:
70Or dip the sheets they lie in, in pitch or sulphur,
71Wrap them in’t, and then light them like a match:
72Or else to boil their bastard to a cullis,
73And give’t his lecherous father, to renew
74The sin of his back.

CARDINAL.

The sin of his back. I’ll leave you.

FERDINAND.

The sin of his back. I’ll leave you. Nay, I have done;
75I am confident, had I been damned in hell,
76And should have heard of this, it would have put me
77Into a cold sweat. In, in, I’ll go sleep:
78Till I know who leaps my sister, I’ll not stir:
79That known, I’ll find scorpions to string my whips,
80And fix her in a general eclipse.

Exeunt.
