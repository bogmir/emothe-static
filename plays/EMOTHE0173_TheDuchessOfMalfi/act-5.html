Scene i

[Enter Antonio and Delio.]

ANTONIO.

1What think you of my hope of reconcilement
2To the Aragonian brethren?

DELIO.

To the Aragonian brethren? I misdoubt it
3For though they have sent their letters of safe conduct
4For your repair to Milan, they appear
5But nets to entrap you. The Marquis of Pescara,
6Under whom you hold certain land in cheat,
7Much ’gainst his noble nature, hath been moved
8To sieze those lands, and some of his dependents
9Are at this instant making it their suit
10To be invested in your revenues.
11I cannot think they mean well to your life,
12That do deprive you of your means of life,
13Your living.

ANTONIO.

Your living. You are still an heretic.
14To any safety I can shape myself.

DELIO.

15Here comes the Marquis. I will make myself
16Petitioner for some part of your land,
17To know whither it is flying.

ANTONIO.

To know whither it is flying. I pray, do.

[Enter Pescara.]

DELIO.

18Sir, I have a suit to you.

PESCARA.

Sir, I have a suit to you. To me?

DELIO.

Sir, I have a suit to you. To me? An easy one:
19There is the citadel of St. Bennet,
20With some demesnes, of late in the possession
21Of Antonio Bologna; please you bestow them on me?

PESCARA.

22You are my friend. But this is such a suit
23Nor fit for me to give, nor you to take.

DELIO.

24No sir?

PESCARA.

No sir? I will give you ample reason for’t
25Soon, in private. Here’s the Cardinal’s mistress.

[Enter Julia.]

JULIA.

26My lord, I am grown your poor petitioner,
27And should be an ill beggar, had I not
28A great man’s letter here, the Cardinal’s,
29To court you in my favour.

[She gives him a letter which he reads.]

PESCARA.

To court you in my favour. He entreats for you
30The citadel of Saint Bennet, that belonged
31To the banished Bologna.

JULIA.

To the banished Bologna. Yes.

PESCARA.

32I could not have thought of a friend I could
33Rather pleasure with it: ’tis yours.

JULIA.

Rather pleasure with it: ’tis yours. Sir, I thank you:
34And he shall know how doubly I am engaged
35Both in your gift, and speediness of giving,
36Which makes your grant the greater.

Exit.

38Themselves with my ruin!

DELIO.

39Little bound to you.

PESCARA.

Little bound to you. Why?

DELIO.

40Because you denied this suit to me, and gave’t
41To such a creature.

PESCARA.

To such a creature. Do you know what it was?
42It was Antonio’s land: not forfeited
43By course of law; but ravished from his throat
44By the Cardinal’s entreaty: it were not fit
45I should bestow so main a piece of wrong
46Upon my friend: ’tis a gratification
47Only due to a strumpet; for it is injustice.
48Shall I sprinkle the pure blood of innocents
49To make those followers I call my friends
50Look ruddier upon me? I am glad
51This land, tane from the owner by such wrong,
52Returns again unto so foul an use,
53As salary for his lust. Learn, good Delio,
54To ask noble things of me, and you shall find
55I’ll be a noble giver.

DELIO.

I’ll be a noble giver. You instruct me well.

ANTONIO.

56Why, here’s a man, now, would fright impudenceaparte
57From sauciest beggars.

PESCARA.

From sauciest beggars. Prince Ferdinand’s come to Milan
58Sick, as they give out, of an apoplexy:
59But some say ’tis a frenzy; I am going
60Exit. To visit him.

ANTONIO.

Exit. To visit him. 'Tis a noble old fellow:

DELIO.

61What course do you mean to take, Antonio?

ANTONIO.

62This night I mean to venture all my fortune,
63Which is no more than a poor ling’ring life,
64To the Cardinal’s worst of malice. I have got
65Private access to his chamber: and intend
66To visit him, about the mid of night,
67As once his brother did our noble Duchess.
68It may be that the sudden apprehension
69Of danger (for I’ll go in mine own shape)
70When he shall see it fraught with love and duty,
71May draw the poison out of him, and work
72A friendly reconcilement: if it fail,
73Yet it shall rid me of this infamous calling,
74For better fall once, than be ever falling.

DELIO.

75I’ll second you in all danger: and, howe’er,
76My life keeps rank with yours.

ANTONIO.

77You are still my lovèd and best friend.

Exeunt.

That answers. I do love these ancient ruins:
10We never tread upon them, but we set
11Our foot upon some reverend history,
12And, questionless, here in this open court,
13Which now lies naked to the injuries
14Of stormy weather, some men lie interred
15Loved the church so well, and gave so largely to’t,
16They thought it should have canopied their bones
17Till doomsday. But all things have their end:
18Churches and cities, which have diseases like to men
19Must have like death that we have.

ECHO.

Must have like death that we have. Like death that we have.

DELIO.

20Now the echo hath caught you.

ANTONIO.

21It groaned, methought, and gave
22A very deadly accent!

ECHO.

A very deadly accent! Deadly accent.

DELIO.

23I told you ’twas a pretty one. You may make it
24A huntsman, or a falconer, a musician
25Or a thing of sorrow.

ECHO.

Or a thing of sorrow. A thing of sorrow.

ANTONIO.

26Ay, sure: that suits it best.

ECHO.

Ay, sure: that suits it best. That suits it best.

ANTONIO.

27’Tis very like my wife's voice.

ECHO.

’Tis very like my wife's voice. Ay, wife's voice.

DELIO.

28Come: let us walk farther from’t:
29I would not have you go to th’ Cardinal’s tonight:
30Do not.

ECHO.

Do not. Do not.

DELIO.

31Wisdom doth not more moderate wasting sorrow
32Than time: take time for’t: be mindful of thy safety.

ECHO.

33Be mindful of thy safety.

ANTONIO.

Be mindful of thy safety. Necessity compels me:
34Make scrutiny throughout the passes
35Of your own life; you’ll find it impossible
36To fly your fate.

ECHO.

To fly your fate. O fly your fate.

DELIO.

37Hark: the dead stones seem to have pity on you
38And give you good counsel.

ANTONIO.

And give you good counsel. Echo, I will not talk with thee;
39For thou art a dead thing.

ECHO.

For thou art a dead thing. Thou art a dead thing.

ANTONIO.

40My Duchess is asleep now,
41And her little ones, I hope sweetly: oh Heaven
42Shall I never see her more?

ECHO.

Shall I never see her more? Never see her more.

ANTONIO.

43I marked not one repetition of the Echo
44But that: and on the sudden, a clear light
45Presented me a face folded in sorrow.

DELIO.

46Your fancy; merely.

ANTONIO.

Your fancy; merely. Come: I’ll be out of this ague;
47For to live thus, is not indeed to live:
48It is a mockery, and abuse of life.
49I will not henceforth save myself by halves;
50Lose all, or nothing.

DELIO.

Lose all, or nothing. Your own virtue save you.
51I’ll fetch your eldest son; and second you:
52It may be that the sight of his° own blood
53Spread in so sweet a figure, may beget
54The more compassion.

ANTONIO.

The more compassion. However, fare you well.
55Though in our miseries Fortune have a part
56Yet, in our noble suff’rings, she hath none:
57Contempt of pain, that we may call our own

Exe[unt].

Scene iv

[Enter] Cardinal, Pescara, Malateste, Roderigo, Grisolan.

CARDINAL.

1You shall not watch tonight by the sick Prince;
2His Grace is very well recoverèd.

MALATESTE.

3Good my lord, suffer us.

CARDINAL.

Good my lord, suffer us. Oh, by no means:
4The noise and change of object in his eye
5Doth more distract him. I pray, all to bed,
6And though you hear him in his violent fit,
7Do not rise, I entreat you.

PESCARA.

Do not rise, I entreat you. So sir, we shall not―

CARDINAL.

8Nay, I must have you promise
9Upon your honours, for I was enjoined to’t
10By himself; and he seemed to urge it sensibly.

PESCARA.

11Let our honours bind this trifle.

CARDINAL.

12Nor any of your followers.

MALATESTE.

Nor any of your followers. Neither.

CARDINAL.

13It may be, to make trial of your promise
14When he’s asleep, myself will rise, and feign
15Some of his mad tricks, and cry out for help,
16And feign myself in danger.

MALATESTE.

And feign myself in danger. If your throat were cutting,
17I’ll’d not come at you, now I have protested against it.

CARDINAL.

18Why, I thank you.

[Withdraws]

GRISOLAN.

Why, I thank you. ’Twas a foul storm tonight.

RODERIGO.

19The Lord Ferdinand’s chamber shook like an osier.

MALATESTE.

20’Twas nothing but pure kindness in the devil,
21To rock his own child.

Exeunt [Roderigo, Malataste, Pescara, Grisolan.]

CARDINAL.

22The reason why I would not suffer these
23About my brother, is because at midnight
24I may with better privacy convey
25Julia’s body to her own lodging. O, my conscience!
26I would pray now: but the devil takes away my heart
27For having any confidence in prayer.
28About this hour I appointed Bosola
29To fetch the body: when he hath served my turn.
30He dies.

Exit. [Enter Bosola.]

BOSOLA.

Ha? ’twas the Cardinals voice. I heard him name Bosola, and my death: listen, I hear one’s footing.

[Enter Ferdinand.]

FERDINAND.

33Strangling is a very quiet death.

BOSOLA.

34Nay then I see, I must stand upon my guard.

FERDINAND.

35What say’ to that? Whisper, softly: do you agree to’t?
36So it must be done i’th’ dark: the Cardinal
37Would not for a thousand pounds the Doctor should see it.

Exit.

BOSOLA.

38My death is plotted; here’s the consequence of murther.
39We value not desert, nor Christian breath
40When we know black deeds must be cured with death.

[Withdraws. Enter Antonio and a Servant.]

SERVANT.

41Here stay sir, and be confident, I pray:
42I’ll fetch you a dark lanthorn.

Exit.

ANTONIO.

I’ll fetch you a dark lanthorn. Could I take him
43At his prayers, there were hope of pardon.

BOSOLA.

44Fall right my sword: [strikes Antonio down from behind.]
45I’ll not give thee so much leisure as to pray.

ANTONIO.

46Oh, I am gone! Thou hast ended a long suit,
47In a minute.

BOSOLA.

In a minute. What art thou?

ANTONIO.

In a minute. What art thou? A most wretched thing
48That only have thy benefit in death,
49To appear myself.

[Enter Servant with a dark lanthorn.]

SERVANT.

To appear myself. Where are you sir?

ANTONIO.

50Very near my home. Bosola?

SERVANT.

Very near my home. Bosola? Oh misfortune!

BOSOLA.

51[to servant] Smother thy pity, thou art dead else. Antonio?
52The man I would have saved ’bove mine own life!
53We are merely the star’s tennis-balls, struck and banded
54Which way please them: oh good Antonio,
55I’ll whisper one thing in thy dying ear,
56Shall make thy heart break quickly. Thy fair Duchess
57And two sweet children—

ANTONIO.

And two sweet children— Their very names
58Kindle a little life in me.

BOSOLA.

Kindle a little life in me. Are murdered!

ANTONIO.

59Some men have wish’s to die
60At the hearing of sad tidings: I am glad
61That I shall do’t in sadness: I would not now
62Wish my wounds balmed, nor healed: for I have no use
63To put my life to. In all our quest of greatness,
64Like wanton boys, whose pastime is their care,
65We follow after bubbles, blown in th’air.
66Pleasure of life, what is’t? only the good hours
67Of an ague: merely a preparative to rest,
68To endure vexation. I do not ask
69The process of my death: only commend me
70To Delio.

BOSOLA.

71Break, heart!

ANTONIO.

72And let my son fly the courts of princes.

[Dies.]

BOSOLA.

73Thou seem’st to have loved Antonio?

SERVANT.

74I brought him hither,
75To have reconciled him to the Cardinal.

BOSOLA.

76I do not ask thee that.
77Take him up, if thou tender thine own life,
78And bear him where the Lady Julia
79Was wont to lodge. Oh, my fate moves swift.
80I have this Cardinal in the forge already,
81Now I’ll bring him to th’ hammer. (O direful misprision!)
82I will not imitate things glorious,
83No more than base: I’ll be mine own example.
84On, on: and look thou represent, for silence,
85The thing thou bear’st.

Exeunt.

Scene v

[Enter] Cardinal (with a book).

CARDINAL.

1I am puzzled in a question about hell:
2He says, in hell there’s one material fire,
3And yet it shall not burn all men alike.
4Lay him by. How tedious is a guilty conscience!
5When I look into the fishponds, in my garden,
6Methinks I see a thing armed with a rake
7That seems to strike at me. Now? Art thou come?

[Enter Bolosa and Servant with Antonio’s body.]

8Thou look’st ghastly:
9There sits in thy face some great determination,
10Mixed with some fear.

BOSOLA.

Mixed with some fear. Thus it lightens into action:
11I am come to kill thee.

CARDINAL.

I am come to kill thee. Ha? Help! our guard!

BOSOLA.

12Thou art deceived:
13They are out of thy howling.

CARDINAL.

14Hold: and I will faithfully divide
15Revenues with thee.

BOSOLA.

Revenues with thee. Thy prayers and proffers
16Are both unseasonable.

CARDINAL.

Are both unseasonable. Raise the watch:
17We are betrayed!

BOSOLA.

We are betrayed! I have confined your flight:
18I’ll suffer your retreat to Julia’s chamber,
19But no further.

CARDINAL.

But no further. Help: we are betrayed!

[Enter Pescara, Malateste, Roderigo and Grisolan, above.]

MALATESTE.

But no further. Help: we are betrayed! Listen.

CARDINAL.

20My dukedom for rescue!

RODERIGO.

My dukedom for rescue! Fie upon his counterfeiting.

MALATESTE.

21Why, ’tis not the Cardinal.

RODERIGO.

Why, ’tis not the Cardinal. Yes, yes, ’tis he:
22But I’ll see him hanged, ere I’ll go down to him.

CARDINAL.

23Here’s a plot upon me; I am assaulted. I am lost,
24Unless some rescue!

GRISOLAN.

Unless some rescue! He doth this pretty well:
25But it will not serve to laugh me out of mine honour.

CARDINAL.

26The sword’s at my throat!

RODERIGO.

The sword’s at my throat! You would not bawl so loud, then.

MALATESTE.

27Come, come: let’s go to bed: he told us thus much aforehand.

PESCARA.

28He wished you should not come at him: but believe’t,
29The accent of the voice sounds not in jest.
30I’ll down to him, howsoever, and with engines
31[Exit.] Force ope the doors.

RODERIGO.

[Exit.] Force ope the doors. Let’s follow him aloof,
32And note how the Cardinal will laugh at him.

[Exeunt above.]

BOSOLA.

33There’s for you first:
34’Cause you shall not unbarricade the door
35To let in rescue.

He kills the Servant.

CARDINAL.

36What cause hast thou to pursue my life?

BOSOLA.

What cause hast thou to pursue my life? Look there.

CARDINAL.

37Antonio!

BOSOLA.

Antonio! Slain by my hand unwittingly.
38Pray, and be sudden: when thou killed’st thy sister,
39Thou took’st from Justice her most equal balance,
40And left her naught but her sword.

CARDINAL.

And left her naught but her sword. O mercy!

BOSOLA.

41Now it seems thy greatness was only outward:
42For thou fall’st faster of thyself than calamity
43Can drive thee. I’ll not waste longer time. There.

[Stabs the Cardinal.]

CARDINAL.

44Thou hast hurt me!

BOSOLA.

Thou hast hurt me! Again!

[Stabs him again.]

CARDINAL.

45Shall I die like a leveret
46Without any resistance? Help, help, help!
47I am slain!

[Enter] Ferdinand.]

FERDINAND.

I am slain! Th’alarum? give me a fresh horse.
48Rally the vaunt-guard; or the day is lost.
49Yield, yield! I give you the honour of arms,
50Shake my sword over you, will you yield?

CARDINAL.

51Help me, I am your brother.

FERDINAND.

Help me, I am your brother. The devil?
52My brother fight upon the adverse party?

(He wounds the Cardinal and, in the scuffle, gives Bosola his death wound.)

53There flies your ransome.

CARDINAL.

There flies your ransome. Oh Justice:
54I suffer now for what hath former bin
55Sorrow is held the eldest child of sin.

FERDINAND.

Now you’re brave fellows. Caesar’s fortune was harder than Pompey’s: Caesar died in the arms of prosperity, Pompey at the feet of disgrace: you both died in the field, the pain’s nothing. Pain many times is taken away with the apprehension of greater, as the tooth- ache with the sight of a barber that comes to pull it out: there’s philosophy for you.

BOSOLA.

63Now my revenge is perfect: sink, thou main cause
64Of my undoing: the last part of my life
65Hath done me best service.

He kills Ferdinand.

FERDINAND.

66Give me some wet hay, I am broken winded.
67I do account this world but a dog-kennel:
68I will vault credit, and affect high pleasures
69Beyond death.

BOSOLA.

Beyond death. He seems to come to himself,
70Now he’s so near the bottom.

FERDINAND.

71My sister, oh! my sister, there’s the cause on’t.
72Whether we fall by ambition, blood, or lust,
73Like diamonds, we are cut with our own dust.

[Dies.]

CARDINAL.

74Thou hast thy payment too.

BOSOLA.

75Yes, I hold my weary soul in my teeth;
76’Tis ready to part from me. I do glory
77That thou, which stood’st like a huge pyramid
78Begun upon a large and ample base,
79Shalt end in a little point, a kind of nothing.

[Enter Pescara, Malateste, Roderigo, and Grisolan.]

PESCARA.

80How now, my lord?

MALATESTE.

How now, my lord? O sad disaster!

RODERIGO.

How now, my lord? O sad disaster! How comes this?

BOSOLA.

81Revenge, for the Duchess of Malfi, murdered
82By th’ Aragonian brethren; for Antonio,
83Slain by this hand; for lustful Julia,
84Poisoned by this man; and lastly, for myself,
85That was an actor in the main of all,
86Much ’gainst mine own good nature, yet i’th’ end
87Neglected.

PESCARA.

Neglected. How now, my lord?

CARDINAL.

Neglected. How now, my lord? Look to my brother:
88He gave us these large wounds, as we were struggling
89Here i’th’rushes. And now, I pray, let me
90Be laid by, and never thought of.

[Dies.]

PESCARA.

91How fatally, it seems, he did withstand
92His own rescue!

MALATESTE.

His own rescue! Thou wretched thing of blood,
93How came Antonio by his death?

BOSOLA.

94In a mist: I know not how;
95Such a mistake as I have often seen
96In a play. Oh, I am gone:
97We are only like dead walls, or vaulted graves
98That, ruined, yields no echo. Fare you well;
99It may be pain: but no harm to me to die
100In so good a quarrel. Oh this gloomy world,
101In what a shadow, or deep pit of darkness
102Doth, womanish, and fearful, mankind live?
103Let worthy minds ne'er stagger in distrust
104To suffer death or shame for what is just:
105Mine is another voyage.

[Dies.]

PESCARA.

106The noble Delio, as I came to th’palace,
107Told me of Antonio’s being here, and showed me
108A pretty gentleman his son and heir.

[Enter Delio with Antonio’s son.]

MALATESTE.

109O sir, you come too late.

DELIO.

O sir, you come too late. I heard so, and
110Was armed for’t ere I came. Let us make noble use
111Of this great ruin; and join all our force
112To establish this young hopeful gentleman
113In’s mother's right. These wretched eminent things
114Leave no more fame behind ’em, than should one
115Fall in a frost, and leave his print in snow,
116As soon as the sun shines, it ever melts
117Both form and matter. I have ever thought
118Nature doth nothing so great for great men,
119As when she’s pleased to make them lords of truth:
120Integrity of life is fame’s best friend,
121Which nobly, beyond death, shall crown the end.

Exeunt.
