Act I

Scene i

[Enter Antonio and Delio.]

DELIO.

1You are welcome to your country, dear Antonio,
2You have been long in France, and you return
3A very formal Frenchman, in your habit.
4How do you like the French court?

ANTONIO.

How do you like the French court? I admire it;
5In seeking to reduce both State and people
6To a fixed order, their judicious King
7Begins at home. Quits first his royal palace
8Of flatt’ring sycophants, of dissolute,
9And infamous persons, which he sweetly terms
10His Master’s master-piece, the work of Heaven,
11Consid’ring duly, that a Prince’s court
12Is like a common fountain, whence should flow
13Pure silver-drops in general. But if’t chance
14Some cursed example poison’t near the head,
15Death and diseases through the whole land spread.
16And what is’t makes this blessed government,
17But a most provident Council, who dare freely
18Inform him, the corruption of the times?
19Though some o’th’court hold it presumption
20To instruct Princes what they ought to do,
21It is a noble duty to inform them
22What they ought to foresee. Here comes Bosola,
23[Enter Bosola] The only court-gall: yet I observe his railing
24Is not for simple love of piety:
25Indeed he rails at those things which he wants,
26Would be as lecherous, covetous, or proud,
27Bloody, or envious, as any man,
28If he had means to be so. Here’s the Cardinal.

[Enter Cardinal.]

BOSOLA.

I do haunt you still.

CARDINAL.

So.

BOSOLA.

I have done you better service than to be slighted thus. Miserable age, where only the reward of doing well, is the doing of it!

CARDINAL.

You enforce your merit too much.

BOSOLA.

I fell into the galleys in your service, where, for two years together, I wore two towels instead of a shirt, with a knot on the shoulder, after the fashion of a Roman mantle. Slighted thus? I will thrive some way: blackbirds fatten best in hard weather: why not I, in these dog days?

CARDINAL.

Would you could become honest,-

BOSOLA.

With all your divinity, do but direct me the way to it. I have known many travel far for it, and yet return as arrant knaves, as they went forth; because they carried [Exit Cardinal] Are themselves always along with them. you gone? Some fellows, they say, are possessed with the evil, but this great fellow were able to possess the greatest devil and make him worse.

ANTONIO.

He hath denied thee some suit?

BOSOLA.

He and his brother are like plum trees, that grow crooked over standing pools, they are rich, and o’erladen with fruit, but none but crows, pies, and caterpillars feed on them. Could I be one of their flatt’ring panders, I would hang on their ears like a horse-leech, till I were full, and then drop off. I pray leave me. Who would rely upon these miserable dependences, in expectation to be advanced tomorrow? What creature ever fed worse, than hoping Tantalus; nor ever died any man more fearfully, than he that hoped for a pardon? There are rewards for hawks, and dogs, when they have done us service; but for a soldier, that hazards his limbs in a battle, nothing but a kind of geo- metry is his last supportation.

DELIO.

Geometry?

BOSOLA.

Ay, to hang in a fair pair of slings, take his latter swing in the world, upon an honourable pair of crutches, from hospital to hospital: fare ye well sir. And yet do not you scorn us, for places in the court are but like beds in the hospital, where this man’s head lies at that man’s foot, and so lower and lower.

[Exit BOSOLA.]

DELIO.

69I knew this fellow seven years in the galleys,
70For a notorious murther, and ’twas thought
71The Cardinal suborned it: he was released
72By the French general, Gaston de Foix
73When he recovered Naples.

ANTONIO.

When he recovered Naples. ’Tis great pity
74He should be thus neglected, I have heard
75He’s very valiant. This foul melancholy
76Will poison all his goodness, for, I’ll tell you,
77If too immoderate sleep be truly said
78To be an inward rust unto the soul;
79It then doth follow want of action
80Breeds all black malcontents, and their close rearing,
81Like moths in cloth, do hurt for want of wearing.

Scene ii

[Enter Castruchio, Silvio, Roderigo, and Grisolan.]

DELIO.

1The presence ’gins to fill. You promised me
2To make me the partaker of the natures
3Of some of your great courtiers.

ANTONIO.

Of some of your great courtiers. The Lord Cardinal’s
4And other strangers’, that are now in court?
5I shall. Here comes the great Calabrian Duke.

[Enter Ferdinand.]

FERDINAND.

6Who took the ring oft’nest?

SILVIO.

Who took the ring oft’nest? Antonio Bologna, my lord.

FERDINAND.

Our sister Duchess’ great master of her house- hold? Give him the jewel: when shall we leave this sportive action, and fall to action indeed?

CASTRUCHIO.

Methinks, my lord, you should not desire to go to war, in person.

FERDINAND.

Now, for some gravity: why, my lord?

CASTRUCHIO.

It is fitting a soldier arise to be a prince, but not necessary a prince descend to be a captain!

FERDINAND.

No?

CASTRUCHIO.

No, my lord, he were far better do it by a deputy.

FERDINAND.

Why should he not as well sleep, or eat, by a deputy? This might take idle, offensive, and base office from him, whereas the other deprives him of honour.

CASTRUCHIO.

Believe my experience: that realm is never long in quiet, where the ruler is a soldier.

FERDINAND.

Thou told’st me thy wife could not endure fighting.

CASTRUCHIO.

True, my lord.

FERDINAND.

And of a jest she broke, of a captain she met full of wounds: I have forgot it.

CASTRUCHIO.

She told him, my lord, he was a pitiful fellow, to lie, like the children of Ismael, all in tents.

FERDINAND.

Why, there’s a wit were able to undo all the chirurgeons o’ the city, for although gallants should quarrel, and had drawn their weapons, and were ready to go to it; yet her persuasions would make them put up.

CASTRUCHIO.

That she would, my lord. How do you like my Spanish jennet?

RODERIGO.

He is all fire.

FERDINAND.

I am of Pliny’s opinion, I think he was begot by the wind; he runs as if he were ballasted with quick- silver.

SILVIO.

True, my lord, he reels from the tilt often.

RODERIGO and DELIO.

Ha, ha, ha!

FERDINAND.

Why do you laugh? Methinks you that are courtiers should be my touchwood, take fire when I give fire; that is, laugh when I laugh, were the subject never so witty,-

CASTRUCHIO.

True, my lord, I myself have heard a very good jest, and have scorned to seem to have so silly a wit, as to understand it.

FERDINAND.

But I can laugh at your fool, my lord.

CASTRUCHIO.

He cannot speak, you know, but he makes faces; my lady cannot abide him.

FERDINAND.

No?

CASTRUCHIO.

Nor endure to be in merry company: for she says too much laughing, and too much company, fills her too full of the wrinkle.

FERDINAND.

I would then have a mathematical instrument made for her face, that she might not laugh out of compass. I shall shortly visit you at Milan, Lord Silvio.

SILVIO.

Your Grace shall arrive most welcome.

FERDINAND.

You are a good horseman, Antonio; you have excellent riders in France, what do you think of good horsemanship?

ANTONIO.

Nobly, my lord: as out of the Grecian horse issued many famous princes: so out of brave horsemanship, arise the first sparks of growing resolution, that raise the mind to noble action.

FERDINAND.

You have bespoke it worthily.

[Enter Duchess, Cardinal, Cariola and Julia.]

SILVIO.

Your brother, the Lord Cardinal, and sister Duchess.

CARDINAL.

Are the galleys come about?

DELIO.

They are, my lord.

FERDINAND.

Here’s the Lord Silvio, is come to take his leave.

DELIO

Now, sir, your promise: what’s that Cardinal? I mean his temper? They say he’s a brave fellow, will play his five thousand crowns at tennis, dance, court ladies, and one that hath fought single combats.

ANTONIO.

Some such flashes superficially hang on him, for form: but observe his inward character: he is a melancholy churchman. The spring in his face is nothing but the engen- d’ring of toads: where he is jealous of any man, he lays worse plots for them, than ever was imposed on Hercules: for he strews in his way flatterers, panders, intelligencers, atheists: and a thousand such political monsters: he should have been Pope: but instead of coming to it by the primitive decency of the Church, he did bestow bribes, so largely, and so impudently, as if he would have carried it away without Heaven’s knowledge. Some good he hath done.

DELIO.

89You have given too much of him: what’s his brother?

ANTONIO.

90The Duke there? a most perverse and turbulent nature;
91What appears in him mirth, is merely outside,
92If he laugh heartily, it is to laugh
93All honesty out of fashion.

DELIO.

All honesty out of fashion. Twins?

ANTONIO.

All honesty out of fashion. Twins? In quality:
94He speaks with others’ tongues, and hears men’s suits
95With others’ ears: will seem to sleep o’th’bench
96Only to entrap offenders in their answers;
97Dooms men to death by information,
98Rewards, by hearsay.

DELIO.

Rewards, by hearsay. Then the law to him
99Is like a foul black cobweb to a spider,
100He makes it his dwelling, and a prison
101To entangle those shall feed him.

ANTONIO.

To entangle those shall feed him. Most true:
102He nev’r pays debts, unless they be shrewd turns,
103And those he will confess, that he doth owe.
104Last: for his brother, there, the Cardinal,
105They that do flatter him most, say oracles
106Hang at his lips: and verily I believe them:
107For the devil speaks in them.
108But for their sister, the right noble Duchess,
109You never fixed your eye on three fair medals,
110Cast in one figure, of so different temper.
111For her discourse, it is so full of rapture
112You only will begin, then to be sorry
113When she doth end her speech: and wish, in wonder,
114She held it less vainglory to talk much
115Than your penance, to hear her: whilst she speaks,
116She throws upon a man so sweet a look,
117That it were able to raise one to a galliard
118That lay in a dead palsy; and to dote
119On that sweet countenance: but in that look
120There speaketh so divine a continence
121As cuts off all lascivious, and vain hope.
122Her days are practiced in such noble virtue,
123That, sure her nights, nay more, her very sleeps,
124Are more in heaven, than other ladies’ shrifts.
125Let all sweet ladies break their flatt’ring glasses,
126And dress themselves in her.

DELIO.

And dress themselves in her. Fie Antonio,
127You play the wire-drawer with her commendations.

ANTONIO.

128I’ll case the picture up: only thus much:
129All her particular worth grows to this sum:
130She stains the time past: lights the time to come.

CARIOLA.

131You must attend my lady, in the gallery,
132Some half an hour hence.

ANTONIO.

Some half an hour hence. I shall.

[Exeunt Antonio and Delio.]

FERDINAND.

133Sister, I have a suit to you.

DUCHESS.

Sister, I have a suit to you. To me, sir?

FERDINAND.

134A gentleman here: Daniel de Bosola:
135One, that was in the galleys.

DUCHESS.

One, that was in the galleys. Yes, I know him.

FERDINAND.

136A worthy fellow h’ is: pray let me entreat for
137The provisorship of your horse.

DUCHESS.

The provisorship of your horse. Your knowledge of him
138Commends him, and prefers him.

FERDINAND.

Commends him, and prefers him. Call him hither.
139[Exit attendant] We are now upon parting. Good Lord Silvio
140Do us commend to all our noble friends
141At the leaguer.

SILVIO.

At the leaguer. Sir, I shall.

DUCHESS.

At the leaguer. Sir, I shall. You are for Milan?

SILVIO.

At the leaguer. Sir, I shall. You are for Milan? I am.

DUCHESS.

142Bring the caroches: we’ll bring you down to the haven.

[Exeunt Dutchess, Cariola, Silvio, Castruchio, Roderigo, Grisolan and Julia.]

CARDINAL.

143Be sure you entertain that Bosola
144For your intelligence: I would not be seen in’t.
145And therefore many times I have slighted him,
146When he did court our furtherance: as this morning.

FERDINAND.

147Antonio, the great master of her household
148Had been far fitter.

CARDINAL.

Had been far fitter. You are deceived in him,
149His nature is too honest for such business.
150He comes: I’ll leave you.

[Enter Bosola]

BOSOLA.

He comes: I’ll leave you. I was lured to you.

[Exit Cardinal.]

FERDINAND.

151My brother here, the Cardinal, could never
152Abide you.

BOSOLA.

Abide you. Never since he was in my debt.

FERDINAND.

153May be some oblique character in your face
154Made him suspect you?

BOSOLA.

Made him suspect you? Doth he study physiognomy?
155There’s no more credit to be given to th’face,
156Than to a sick man’s urine, which some call
157The physician’s whore, because she cozens him.
158He did suspect me wrongfully.

FERDINAND.

He did suspect me wrongfully. For that
159You must give great men leave to take their times:
160Distrust doth cause us seldom be deceived;
161You see, the oft shaking of the cedar tree
162Fastens it more at root.

BOSOLA.

Fastens it more at root. Yet take heed:
163For to suspect a friend unworthily
164Instructs him the next way to suspect you,
165And prompts him to deceive you.

FERDINAND .

And prompts him to deceive you. There’s gold.

BOSOLA.

And prompts him to deceive you. There’s gold. So:
166What follows? (Never rained such showers as these
167Without thunderbolts i’th’tail of them;)
168Whose throat must I cut?

FERDINAND.

169Your inclination to shed blood rides post
170Before my occasion to use you. I give you that
171To live i’th’ court, here: and observe the Duchess,
172To note all the particulars of her haviour:
173What suitors do solicit her for marriage
174And whom she best affects: she’s a young widow,
175I would not have her marry again.

BOSOLA.

I would not have her marry again. No, sir?

FERDINAND.

176Do not you ask the reason: but be satisfied,
177I say I would not.

BOSOLA.

I say I would not. It seems you would create me
178One of your familiars.

FERDINAND .

One of your familiars. Familiar? what’s that?

BOSOLA.

179Why, a very quaint invisible devil in flesh:
180An intelligencer.

FERDINAND .

An intelligencer. Such a kind of thriving thing
181I would wish thee: and ere long, thou mayst arrive
182At a higher place by’t.

BOSOLA.

At a higher place by’t. Take your devils
183Which hell calls angels: these cursed gifts would make
184You a corrupter, me an impudent traitor,
185And should I take these, they’ll’d take me to hell.

FERDINAND.

186Sir, I’ll take nothing from you that I have given.
187There is a place that I procured for you
188This morning, the provisorship o’th’horse,
189Have you heard on’t?

BOSOLA.

Have you heard on’t? No.

FERDINAND .

Have you heard on’t? No. ’Tis yours, is’t not worth thanks?

BOSOLA.

190I would have you curse yourself now, that your bounty,
191Which makes men truly noble, e’er should make
192Me a villain: oh, that to avoid ingratitude
193For the good deed you have done me, I must do
194All the ill man can invent. Thus the devil
195Candies all sins o’er: and what heaven terms vild,
196That names he complemental.

FERDINAND.

That names he complemental. Be yourself:
197Keep your old garb of melancholy: ’twill express
198You envy those that stand above your reach,
199Yet strive not to come near ’em. This will gain
200Access to private lodgings, where yourself
201May, like a politic dormouse,—

BOSOLA.

May, like a politic dormouse,— As I have seen some,
202Feed in a lord’s dish, half asleep, not seeming
203To listen to any talk: and yet these rogues
204Have cut his throat in a dream: what’s my place?
205The provisorship o’th’ horse? say then my corruption
206Grew out of horse dung. I am your creature.

FERDINAND.

Grew out of horse dung. I am your creature. Away!

BOSOLA.

207Let good men, for good deeds, covet good fame,
208Since place and riches oft are bribes of shame;
209Sometimes the devil doth preach.

Exit Bosola.

[Enter Cardinal, Duchess and Cariola.]

CARDINAL.

210We are to part from you: and your own discretion
211Must now be your director.

FERDINAND.

Must now be your director. You are a widow:
212You know already what man is: and therefore
213Let not youth: high promotion, eloquence,—

CARDINAL.

214No, nor anything without the addition, Honour,
215Sway your high blood.

FERDINAND.

Sway your high blood. Marry? They are most luxurious,
216Will wed twice.

CARDINAL.

Will wed twice. Oh, fie!

FERDINAND .

Will wed twice. Oh, fie! Their livers are more spotted
217Than Laban’s sheep.

DUCHESS.

Than Laban’s sheep. Diamonds are of most value
218They say, that have passed through most jewellers’ hands.

FERDINAND.

219Whores, by that rule, are precious.

DUCHESS.

220Will you hear me?
Will you hear me? I’ll never marry―

CARDINAL.

Will you hear me? I’ll never marry― So most widows say:
221But commonly that motion lasts no longer
222Than the turning of an hourglass; the funeral sermon
223And it, end both together.

FERDINAND.

And it, end both together. Now hear me:
224You live in a rank pasture here, i’th’ court,
225There is a kind of honeydew that’s deadly:
226’Twill poison your fame; look to’t; be not cunning:
227For they whose faces do belie their hearts
228Are witches, ere they arrive at twenty years,
229Ay: and give the devil suck.

DUCHESS.

Ay: and give the devil suck. This is terrible good counsel.

FERDINAND.

230Hypocrisy is woven of a fine small thread,
231Subtler than Vulcan’s engine: yet, believe’t,
232Your darkest actions: nay, your privat’st thoughts,
233Will come to light.

CARDINAL.

Will come to light. You may flatter yourself,
234And take your own choice: privately be married
235Under the eaves of night―

FERDINAND.

Under the eaves of night― Think’t the best voyage
236That e’er you made; like the irregular crab,
237Which, though ’t goes backward, thinks that it goes right,
238Because it goes its own way: but observe:
239Such weddings may more properly be said
240To be executed, than celebrated.

CARDINAL.

To be executed, than celebrated. The marriage night
241Is the entrance into some prison.

FERDINAND.

Is the entrance into some prison. And those joys,
242Those lustful pleasures, are like heavy sleeps
243Which do forerun man’s mischief.

CARDINAL.

Which do forerun man’s mischief. Fare you well.
244Wisdom begins at the end: remember it.

[Exit Cardinal.]

DUCHESS.

245I think this speech between you both was studied,
246It came so roundly off.

FERDINAND.

It came so roundly off. You are my sister.
247This was my father’s poniard: do you see,
248I’ll’d be loath to see’t look rusty, ’cause ’twas his.
249I would have you to give o’er these chargeable revels;
250A visor and a mask are whispering-rooms
251That were nev’r built for goodness: fare ye well:
252And women like that part, which, like the lamprey,
253Hath nev’r a bone in’t.

DUCHESS.

Hath nev’r a bone in’t. Fie, sir!

FERDINAND .

Hath nev’r a bone in’t. Fie, sir! Nay,
254I mean the tongue: variety of courtship;
255What cannot a neat knave with a smooth tale
256Make a woman believe? Farewell, lusty widow.

[Exit Ferdinand.]

DUCHESS.

257Shall this move me? If all my royal kindred
258Lay in my way unto this marriage:
259I’ll’d make them my low foot-steps. And even now,
260Even in this hate, (as men in some great battles
261By apprehending danger, have achieved
262Almost impossible actions: I have heard soldiers say so,)
263So I, through frights and threat’nings, will assay
264This dangerous venture. Let old wives report
265I winked, and chose a husband. Cariola,
266To thy known secrecy I have given up
267More than my life, my fame.

CARIOLA.

More than my life, my fame. Both shall be safe:
268For I’ll conceal this secret from the world
269As warily as those that trade in poison,
270Keep poison from their children.

DUCHESS.

Keep poison from their children. Thy protestation
271Is ingenious and hearty. I believe it.
272Is Antonio come?

CARIOLA.

Is Antonio come? He attends you.

DUCHESS.

Is Antonio come? He attends you. Good dear soul,
273Leave me: but place thyself behind the arras,
274Where thou mayst overhear us: wish me good speed,
275For I am going into a wilderness,
276Where I shall find nor path, nor friendly clew
277To be my guide.
To be my guide. [Cariola goes behind the arras; the Dutchess draws the traverse to reveal Antonio.] I sent for you. Sit down:
278Take pen and ink, and write. Are you ready?

ANTONIO.

279Yes.

DUCHESS.

Yes. What did I say?

ANTONIO.

280That I should write somewhat.

DUCHESS.

That I should write somewhat. Oh, I remember:
281After these triumphs and this large expense
282It’s fit, like thrifty husbands, we inquire
283What’s laid up for tomorrow.

ANTONIO.

284So please your beauteous excellence.

DUCHESS.

So please your beauteous excellence. Beauteous?
285Indeed I thank you: I look young for your sake.
286You have tane my cares upon you.

ANTONIO.

You have tane my cares upon you. I’ll fetch your Grace
287The particulars of your revenue and expense.

DUCHESS.

288Oh, you are an upright treasurer: but you mistook,
289For when I said I meant to make inquiry
290What’s laid up for tomorrow: I did mean
291What’s laid up yonder for me.

ANTONIO.

What’s laid up yonder for me. Where?

DUCHESS.

What’s laid up yonder for me. Where? In heaven.
292I am making my will, as ’tis fit princes should
293In perfect memory, and I pray sir, tell me
294Were not one better make it smiling, thus?
295Than in deep groans, and terrible ghastly looks,
296As if the gifts we parted with, procured
297That violent distraction?

ANTONIO.

That violent distraction? Oh, much better.

DUCHESS.

298If I had a husband now, this care were quit:
299But I intend to make you overseer;
300What good deed shall we first remember? Say.

ANTONIO.

301Begin with that first good deed, began i’th’ world,
302After man’s creation, the sacrament of marriage.
303I’ld have you first provide for a good husband,
304Give him all.

DUCHESS.

Give him all. All?

ANTONIO.

Give him all. All? Yes, your excellent self.

DUCHESS.

305In a winding-sheet?

ANTONIO.

In a winding-sheet? In a couple.

DUCHESS.

306St. Winifred! that were a strange will.

ANTONIO.

St. Winifred! that were a strange will. ’Twere strange
307If there were no will in you to marry again.

DUCHESS.

308What do you think of marriage?

ANTONIO.

309I take’t, as those that deny purgatory,
310It locally contains or heaven, or hell;
311There’s no third place in’t.

DUCHESS.

There’s no third place in’t. How do you affect it?

ANTONIO.

312My banishment, feeding my melancholy,
313Would often reason thus: —

DUCHESS.

Would often reason thus: — Pray let’s hear it.

ANTONIO.

314Say a man never marry, nor have children,
315What takes that from him? Only the bare name
316Of being a father, or the weak delight
317To see the little wanton ride a-cock-horse
318Upon a painted stick, or hear him chatter
319Like a taught starling.

DUCHESS.

Like a taught starling. Fie, fie, what’s all this?
320One of your eyes is bloodshot, use my ring to’t,
321They say ’tis very sovereign: ’twas my wedding ring,
322And I did vow never to part with it,
323But to my second husband.

ANTONIO.

But to my second husband. You have parted with it now.

DUCHESS.

324Yes, to help your eyesight.

ANTONIO.

Yes, to help your eyesight. You have made me stark blind.

DUCHESS.

Yes, to help your eyesight. You have made me stark blind. How?

ANTONIO.

325There is a saucy and ambitious devil
326Is dancing in this circle.

DUCHESS.

Is dancing in this circle. Remove him.

ANTONIO.

Is dancing in this circle. Remove him. How?

DUCHESS.

327There needs small conjuration, when your finger
328May do it: thus, is it fit?

[She puts the ring on his finger.] he kneels.

ANTONIO.

May do it: thus, is it fit? What said you?

DUCHESS.

May do it: thus, is it fit? What said you? Sir,
329This goodly roof of yours, is too low built,
330I cannot stand upright in’t, nor discourse,
331Without I raise it higher: raise yourself,
332Or, if you please, my hand to help you: so.

[Raises him.

ANTONIO.

333Ambition, Madam, is a great man’s madness,
334That is not kept in chains, and close-pent rooms,
335But in fair lightsome lodgings, and is girt
336With the wild noise of prattling visitants,
337Which makes it lunatic, beyond all cure.
338Conceive not, I am so stupid, but I aim
339Whereto your favours tend. But he’s a fool
340That, being a-cold, would thrust his hands i’th’ fire
341To warm them.

DUCHESS.

To warm them. So, now the ground’s broke,
342You may discover what a wealthy mine
343I make you lord of.

ANTONIO.

I make you lord of. Oh, my unworthiness!

DUCHESS.

344You were ill to sell yourself;
345This dark’ning of your worth is not like that
346Which tradesmen use i’th’ city; their false lights
347Are to rid bad wares off: and I must tell you
348If you will know where breathes a complete man,
349(I speak it without flattery), turn your eyes,
350And progress through yourself.

ANTONIO.

351Were there nor heaven, nor hell,
352I should be honest: I have long served virtue,
353And nev’r tane wages of her.

DUCHESS.

And nev’r tane wages of her. Now she pays it.
354The misery of us, that are born great,
355We are forced to woo, because none dare woo us:
356And as a tyrant doubles with his words,
357And fearfully equivocates: so we
358Are forced to express our violent passions
359In riddles, and in dreams, and leave the path

361To seem the thing it is not. Go, go brag
362You have left me heartless, mine is in your bosom,

364Make not your heart so dead a piece of flesh

366What is’t distracts you? This is flesh, and blood, sir,

372I use but half a blush in’t.

ANTONIO.

I use but half a blush in’t. Truth speak for me,
373I will remain the constant sanctuary
374Of your good name.

DUCHESS.

Of your good name. I thank you, gentle love,
375And ’cause you shall not come to me in debt,
376Being now my steward, here upon your lips
377I sign your Quietus est . This you should have begged now:
378I have seen children oft eat sweetmeats thus,
379As fearful to devour them too soon.

ANTONIO.

380But for your brothers?

DUCHESS.

But for your brothers? Do not think of them:
381All discord, without this circumference,
382Is only to be pitied, and not feared.
383Yet, should they know it, time will easily
384Scatter the tempest.

ANTONIO.

Scatter the tempest. These words should be mine,
385And all the parts you have spoke, if some part of it

404I would have you lead your fortune by the hand,
405Unto your marriage bed:

(You speak in me this, for we now are one)

406We’ll only lie, and talk together, and plot

408Like the old tale, in Alexander and Lodowick ,

410Oh, let me shroud my blushes in your bosom,
411Since ’tis the treasury of all my secrets.

CARIOLA.

412Whether the spirit of greatness, or of woman
413Reign most in her, I know not, but it shows
414A fearful madness: I owe her much of pity.
