The Shoemaker’s Holiday

[Scene I]

Enter [Sir Roger Oatley, the] Lord Mayor, [and the Earl of] Lincoln.

LINCOLN

1My Lord Mayor, you have sundry times
2Feasted myself and many courtiers more.
3Seldom or never can we be so kind
4To make requital of your courtesy.
5But leaving this, I hear my cousin Lacy
6Is much affected to your daughter Rose.

OATLEY

7True, my good lord; and she loves him so well
8That I mislike her boldness in the chase.

LINCOLN

9Why, my Lord Mayor, think you it then a shame
10To join a Lacy with an Oatley’s name?

OATLEY

11Too mean is my poor girl for his high birth.
12Poor citizens must not with courtiers wed,
13Who will in silks and gay apparel spend
14More in one year than I am worth by far.
15Therefore your honour need not doubt my girl.

LINCOLN

16Take heed, my lord; advise you what you do.
17A verier unthrift lives not in the world
18Than is my cousin; for, I’ll tell you what,
19’Tis now almost a year since he requested
20To travel countries for experience.
21I furnished him with coin, bills of exchange,
22Letters of credit, men to wait on him,
23Solicited my friends in Italy
24Well to respect him. But to see the end:
25Scant had he journeyed through half Germany
26But all his coin was spent, his men cast off,
27His bills embezzled, and my jolly coz,
28Ashamed to show his bankrupt presence here,
29Became a Shoemaker in Wittenberg−
30A goodly science for a gentleman
31Of such descent! Now judge the rest by this:
32Suppose your daughter have a thousand pound,
33He did consume me more in one half-year;
34And make him heir to all the wealth you have,
35One twelve-month’s rioting will waste it all.

37To wed you daughter to.
