MERCUTIO

Farewell, ancient lady. Farewell, lady, lady, lady.

⸢Mercutio and Benvolio⸣ exit.

NURSE

I pray you, sir, what saucy merchant was this that was so full of his ropery?

ROMEO

A gentleman, nurse, that loves to hear himself talk and will speak more in a minute than he will stand to in a month.

NURSE

An he speak anything against me, I’ll take him down, an he were lustier than he is, and twenty such jacks. An if I cannot, I’ll find those that shall. Scurvy knave, I am none of his flirt-gills; I am none of his skains-mates. ⸢To Peter⸣. And thou must stand by too and suffer every knave to use me at his pleasure.

PETER

I saw no man use you at his pleasure. If I had, my weapon should quickly have been out. I warrant you, I dare draw as soon as another man, if I see occasion in a good quarrel, and the law on my side.

NURSE

Now, afore God, I am so vexed that every part about me quivers. Scurvy knave! ⸢To Romeo⸣. Pray you, sir, a word. And, as I told you, my young lady bid me inquire you out. What she bid me say, I will keep to myself. But first let me tell you, if you should lead her in a fool’s paradise, as they say, it were a very gross kind of behavior, as they say. For the gentlewoman is young; and therefore, if you should deal double with her, truly it were an ill thing to be offered to any gentlewoman, and very weak dealing.

ROMEO

Nurse, commend me to thy lady and mistress. I protest unto thee—

NURSE

Good heart, and i’ faith I will tell her as much. Lord, Lord, she will be a joyful woman.

ROMEO

What wilt thou tell her, nurse? Thou dost not mark me.

NURSE

I will tell her, sir, that you do protest, which, as I take it, is a gentlemanlike offer.

ROMEO

958Bid her devise
959Some means to come to shrift this afternoon,
960And there she shall at Friar Lawrence’ cell
961Be shrived and married. Here is for thy pains.

⸢Offering her money.⸣

NURSE

No, truly, sir, not a penny.

ROMEO

Go to, I say you shall.

NURSE

962This afternoon, sir? Well, she shall be there.

ROMEO

963And stay, good nurse, behind the abbey wall.
964Within this hour my man shall be with thee
965And bring thee cords made like a tackled stair,
966Which to the high topgallant of my joy
967Must be my convoy in the secret night.
968Farewell. Be trusty, and I’ll quit thy pains.
969Farewell. Commend me to thy mistress.

NURSE

970Now, God in heaven bless thee! Hark you, sir.

ROMEO

971What sayst thou, my dear nurse?

NURSE

972Is your man secret? Did you ne’er hear say
973"Two may keep counsel, putting one away"?

ROMEO

974Warrant thee, my man’s as true as steel.

NURSE

Well, sir, my mistress is the sweetest lady. Lord, Lord, when ’twas a little prating thing—O, there is a nobleman in town, one Paris, that would fain lay knife aboard, but she, good soul, had as lief see a toad, a very toad, as see him. I anger her sometimes and tell her that Paris is the properer man, but I’ll warrant you, when I say so, she looks as pale as any clout in the versal world. Doth not rosemary and Romeo begin both with a letter?

ROMEO

Ay, nurse, what of that? Both with an R.

NURSE

Ah, mocker, that’s the ⸢dog’s⸣ name. R is for the—No, I know it begins with some other letter, and she hath the prettiest sententious of it, of you and rosemary, that it would do you good to hear it.

ROMEO

Commend me to thy lady.

NURSE

Ay, a thousand times.—Peter.

PETER

Anon.

NURSE

Before and apace.

⸢They⸣ exit.

⸢Scene 5⸣

Enter Juliet.

JULIET

975The clock struck nine when I did send the Nurse.
976In half an hour she promised to return.
977Perchance she cannot meet him. That’s not so.
978O, she is lame! Love’s heralds should be thoughts,
979Which ten times faster glides than the sun’s beams,
980Driving back shadows over louring hills.
981Therefore do nimble-pinioned doves draw Love,
982And therefore hath the wind-swift Cupid wings.
983Now is the sun upon the highmost hill
984Of this day’s journey, and from nine till twelve
985Is ⸢three⸣ long hours, yet she is not come.
986Had she affections and warm youthful blood,
987She would be as swift in motion as a ball;
988My words would bandy her to my sweet love,
989And his to me.
990But old folks, many feign as they were dead,
991Unwieldy, slow, heavy, and pale as lead.

Enter Nurse ⸢and Peter.⸣

992O God, she comes!—O, honey nurse, what news?
993Hast thou met with him? Send thy man away.

NURSE

Peter, stay at the gate.

⸢Peter exits⸣.

JULIET

994Now, good sweet nurse—O Lord, why lookest thou sad?
995Though news be sad, yet tell them merrily.
996If good, thou shamest the music of sweet news
997By playing it to me with so sour a face.

NURSE

998I am aweary. Give me leave awhile.
999Fie, how my bones ache! What a jaunt have I!

JULIET

1000I would thou hadst my bones, and I thy news.
1001Nay, come, I pray thee, speak. Good, good nurse, speak.

NURSE

1002Jesu, what haste! Can you not stay awhile?
1003Do you not see that I am out of breath?

JULIET

1004How art thou out of breath, when thou hast breath
1005To say to me that thou art out of breath?
1006The excuse that thou dost make in this delay
1007Is longer than the tale thou dost excuse.
1008Is thy news good or bad? Answer to that.
1009Say either, and I’ll stay the circumstance.
1010Let me be satisfied; is ’t good or bad?

NURSE

Well, you have made a simple choice. You know not how to choose a man. Romeo? No, not he. Though his face be better than any man’s, yet his leg excels all men’s, and for a hand and a foot and a body, though they be not to be talked on, yet they are past compare. He is not the flower of courtesy, but I’ll warrant him as gentle as a lamb. Go thy ways, wench. Serve God. What, have you dined at home?

JULIET

1011No, no. But all this did I know before.
1012What says he of our marriage? What of that?

NURSE

1013Lord, how my head aches! What a head have I!
1014It beats as it would fall in twenty pieces.
1015My back o’ t’ other side! Ah, my back, my back!
1016Beshrew your heart for sending me about
1017To catch my death with jaunting up and down.

JULIET

1018I’ faith, I am sorry that thou art not well.
1019Sweet, sweet, sweet nurse, tell me, what says my love?

NURSE

Your love says, like an honest gentleman, and a courteous, and a kind, and a handsome, and, I warrant, a virtuous—Where is your mother?

JULIET

1020Where is my mother? Why, she is within.
1021Where should she be? How oddly thou repliest:
1022"Your love says, like an honest gentleman,
1023Where is your mother?"

NURSE

Where is your mother?" O God’s lady dear,
1024Are you so hot? Marry, come up, I trow.
1025Is this the poultice for my aching bones?
1026Henceforward do your messages yourself.

JULIET

1027Here’s such a coil. Come, what says Romeo?

NURSE

1028Have you got leave to go to shrift today?

JULIET

I have.

NURSE

1029Then hie you hence to Friar Lawrence’ cell.
1030There stays a husband to make you a wife.
1031Now comes the wanton blood up in your cheeks;
1032They’ll be in scarlet straight at any news.
1033Hie you to church. I must another way,
1034To fetch a ladder by the which your love
1035Must climb a bird’s nest soon when it is dark.
1036I am the drudge and toil in your delight,
1037But you shall bear the burden soon at night.
1038Go. I’ll to dinner. Hie you to the cell.

JULIET

1039Hie to high fortune! Honest nurse, farewell.

⸢Scene 6⸣

Enter Friar ⸢Lawrence⸣ and Romeo.

FRIAR LAWRENCE

1040So smile the heavens upon this holy act
1041That after-hours with sorrow chide us not.

ROMEO

1042Amen, amen. But come what sorrow can,
1043It cannot countervail the exchange of joy
1044That one short minute gives me in her sight.
1045Do thou but close our hands with holy words,
1046Then love-devouring death do what he dare,
1047It is enough I may but call her mine.

FRIAR LAWRENCE

1048These violent delights have violent ends
1049And in their triumph die, like fire and powder,
1050Which, as they kiss, consume. The sweetest honey
1051Is loathsome in his own deliciousness
1052And in the taste confounds the appetite.
1053Therefore love moderately. Long love doth so.
1054Too swift arrives as tardy as too slow.

Enter Juliet.

1055Here comes the lady. O, so light a foot
1056Will ne’er wear out the everlasting flint.
1057A lover may bestride the gossamers
1058That idles in the wanton summer air,
1059And yet not fall, so light is vanity.

JULIET

1060Good even to my ghostly confessor.

FRIAR LAWRENCE

1061Romeo shall thank thee, daughter, for us both.

JULIET

1062As much to him, else is his thanks too much.

ROMEO

1063Ah, Juliet, if the measure of thy joy
1064Be heaped like mine, and that thy skill be more
1065To blazon it, then sweeten with thy breath
1066This neighbor air, and let rich ⸢music’s⸣ tongue
1067Unfold the imagined happiness that both
1068Receive in either by this dear encounter.

JULIET

1069Conceit, more rich in matter than in words,
1070Brags of his substance, not of ornament.
1071They are but beggars that can count their worth,
1072But my true love is grown to such excess
1073I cannot sum up sum of half my wealth.

FRIAR LAWRENCE

1074Come, come with me, and we will make short work,
1075For, by your leaves, you shall not stay alone
1076Till Holy Church incorporate two in one.
