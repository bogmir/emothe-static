3.1

[Enter] Lovell, Allworth, [and] servants.

LOVELL

1Walk the horses down the hill. Something in private
2I must impart to Allworth.

Exeunt servii.

ALLWORTH

I must impart to Allworth. O my lord,
3What sacrifice of reverence, duty, watching,
4Although I could put off the use of sleep
5And ever wait on your commands to serve 'em,
6What dangers, though in ne'er so horrid shapes,
7Nay, death itself, though I should run to meet it,
8Can I, and with a thankful willingness, suffer,
9But still the retribution will fall short
10Of your bounties show'red upon me!

LOVELL

Of your bounties show'red upon me! Loving youth,
11Till what I purpose be put into act,
12Do not o'erprize it. Since you have trusted me
13With your soul's nearest—nay, her dearest—secret,
14Rest confident 'tis in a cabinet locked
15Treachery shall never open. I have found you
16(For so much to your face I must profess,
17Howe'er you guard your modesty with a blush for't)
18More zealous in your love and service to me
19Than I have been in my rewards.

ALLWORTH

Than I have been in my rewards. Still great ones,
20Above my merit.

LOVELL

Above my merit. Such your gratitude calls 'em;
21Nor am I of that harsh and rugged temper
22As some great men are taxed with, who imagine
23They part from the respect due to their honors
24If they use not all such as follow 'em,
25Without distinction of their births, like slaves.
26I am not so conditioned. I can make
27A fitting difference between my footboy
28And a gentleman by want compelled to serve me.

ALLWORTH

29'Tis thankfully acknowledged. You have been
30More like a father to me than a master.
31Pray you, pardon the comparison.

LOVELL

Pray you, pardon the comparison. I allow it;
32And, to give you assurance I am pleased in't,
33My carriage and demeanor to your mistress,
34Fair Margaret, shall truly witness for me
35I can command my passions.

ALLWORTH

I can command my passions. 'Tis a conquest
36Few lords can boast of when they are tempted.—Oh!

LOVELL

37Why do you sigh? Can you be doubtful of me?
38By that fair name I in the wars have purchased,
39And all my actions hitherto untainted,
40I will not be more true to mine own honor
41Than to my Allworth.

ALLWORTH

Than to my Allworth. As you are the brave Lord Lovell,
42Your bare word only given is an assurance
43Of more validity and weight to me
44Than all the oaths bound up with imprecations
45Which, when they would deceive, most courtiers practice.
46Yet, being a man (for, sure, to style you more
47Would relish of gross flattery), I am forced,
48Against my confidence of your worth and virtues,
49To doubt—nay, more—to fear.

LOVELL

To doubt—nay, more—to fear. So young, and jealous?

ALLWORTH

50Were you to encounter with a single foe,
51The victory were certain; but to stand
52The charge of two such potent enemies,
53At once assaulting you, as wealth and beauty,
54And those too seconded with power, is odds
55Too great for Hercules.

LOVELL

Too great for Hercules. Speak your doubts and fears,
56Since you will nourish 'em, in plainer language,
57That I may understand 'em.

ALLWORTH

That I may understand 'em. What's your will,
58Though I lend arms against myself (provided
59They may advantage you) must be obeyed.
60My much loved lord, were Margaret only fair,
61The cannon of her more than earthly form,
62Though mounted high, commanding all beneath it,
63And rammed with bullets of her sparkling eyes,
64Of all the bulwarks that defend your senses
65Could batter none but that which guards your sight.
66But when the well-tuned accents of her tongue
67Make music to you, and with numerous sounds
68Assault your hearing (such as if Ulysses
69Now lived again, howe'er he stood the Sirens,
70Could not resist), the combat must grow doubtful
71Between your reason and rebellious passions.
72Add this too; when you feel her touch and breath,
73Like a soft western wind when it glides o'er
74Arabia creating gums and spices,
75And, in the van, the nectar of her lips
76Which you must taste, bring the battalia on,
77Well armed, and strongly lined with her discourse
78And knowing manners, to give entertainment—
79Hippolytus himself would leave Diana
80To follow such a Venus.

LOVELL

To follow such a Venus. Love hath made you
81Poetical, Allworth.

ALLWORTH

Poetical, Allworth. Grant all these beat off
82(Which, if it be in man to do, you'll do it),
83Mammon, in Sir Giles Overreach, steps in
84With heaps of ill-got gold, and so much land,
85To make her more remarkable, as would tire
86A falcon's wings in one day to fly over.
87O my good lord, these powerful aids, which would
88Make a misshapen Negro beautiful
89(Yet are but ornaments to give her lustre
90That in herself is all perfection), must
91Prevail for her. I here release your trust.
92'Tis happiness enough for me to serve you
93And sometimes with chaste eyes to look upon her.

LOVELL

94Why, shall I swear?

ALLWORTH

Why, shall I swear? Oh, by no means, my lord;
95And wrong not so your judgment to the world
96As from your fond indulgence to a boy,
97Your page, your servant, to refuse a blessing
98Divers great men are rivals for.

LOVELL

Divers great men are rivals for. Suspend
99Your judgment till the trial. How far is it
100T'Overreach's house?

ALLWORTH

T'Overreach's house? At the most, some half hour's riding;
101You'll soon be there.

LOVELL

You'll soon be there. And you the sooner freed
102From your jealous fears.

ALLWORTH

From your jealous fears. Oh, that I durst but hope it!

Exeunt.

3.2

[Enter] Overreach, Greedy, [and] Marall.

OVERREACH

1Spare for no cost. Let my dressers crack with the weight
2Of curious viands.

GREEDY

Of curious viands. Store indeed's no sore, sir.

OVERREACH

3That proverb fits your stomach, Master Greedy.
4And let no plate be seen but what's pure gold,
5Or such whose workmanship exceeds the matter
6That it is made of. Let my choicest linen
7Perfume the room, and, when we wash, the water,
8With precious powders mixed, so please my lord
9That he may with envy wish to bathe so ever.

MARALL

10'Twill be very chargeable.

OVERREACH

'Twill be very chargeable. Avaunt, you drudge!
11Now all my labored ends are at the stake,
12It's a time to think of thrift? Call in my daughter.

[Exit Marall.]

13And, Master Justice, since you love choice dishes,
14And plenty of 'em—

GREEDY

And plenty of 'em— As I do indeed, sir,
15Almost as much as to give thanks for 'em.

OVERREACH

16I do confer that providence, with my power
17Of absolute command to have abundance,
18To your best care.

GREEDY

To your best care. I'll punctually discharge it
19And give the best directions. Now am I
20In mine own conceit a monarch, at the least
21Archpresident of the boiled, the roast, the baked,
22For which I will eat often, and give thanks,
23When my belly's braced up like a drum—and that's pure justice.

Exit Greedy.

OVERREACH

24It must be so. Should the foolish girl prove modest,
25She may spoil all. She had it not from me,
26But from her mother. I was ever forward,
27As she must be, and therefore I'll prepare her.

[Enter] Margaret.

28Alone—and let your women wait without.

MARGARET

29Your pleasure, sir?

OVERREACH

Your pleasure, sir? Ha, this is a neat dressing!
30These orient pearls, and diamonds well placed too!
31The gown affects me not; it should have been
32Embroidered o'er and o'er with flowers of gold;
33But these rich jewels and quaint fashion help it.
34And how below? Since oft the wanton eye,
35The face observed, descends unto the foot,
36Which, being well proportioned, as yours is,
37Invites as much as perfect white and red,
38Though without art. How like you your new woman,
39The Lady Downfall'n?

MARGARET

The Lady Downfall'n? Well, for a companion;
40Not as a servant.

OVERREACH

Not as a servant. Is she humble, Meg?
41And careful too, her ladyship forgotten?

MARGARET

42I pity her fortune.

OVERREACH

I pity her fortune. Pity her? Trample on her!
43I took her up in an old tamin gown,
44(Even starved for want of twopenny chops) to serve thee;
45And if I understand she but repines
46To do thee any duty, though ne'er so servile,
47I'll pack her to her knight, where I have lodged him,
48Into the Counter, and there let 'em howl together.

MARGARET

49You know your own ways; but, for me, I blush
50When I command her that was once attended
51With persons not inferior to myself
52In birth.

OVERREACH

In birth. In birth? Why, art thou not my daughter?
53The blest child of my industry and wealth?
54Why, foolish girl, was't not to make thee great
55That I have ran, and still pursue, those ways
56That hale down curses on me, which I mind not?
57Part with these humble thoughts, and apt thyself
58To the noble state I labor to advance thee,
59Or, by my hopes to see thee "Honorable,"
60I will adopt a stranger to my heir
61And throw thee from my care. Do not provoke me.

MARGARET

62I will not, sir. Mold me which way you please.

Enter Greedy.

OVERREACH

63How! Interrupted?

GREEDY

How! Interrupted? 'Tis matter of importance.
64The cook, sir, is self-willed and will not learn
65From my experience. There's a fawn brought in, sir,
66And, for my life, I cannot make him roast it
67With a Norfolk dumpling in the belly of it.
68And, sir, we wise men know, without the dumpling
69'Tis not worth threepence.

OVERREACH

'Tis not worth threepence. Would it were whole in thy belly
70To stuff it out! Cook it any way. Prithee leave me.

GREEDY

71Without order for the dumpling?

OVERREACH

Without order for the dumpling? Let it be dumpled
72Which way thou wilt, or tell him I will scald him
73In his own cauldron.

GREEDY

In his own cauldron. I had lost my stomach,
74Had I lost my Mistress Dumpling I'll give thanks for.

Exit Greedy.

OVERREACH

75But to our business, Meg. You have heard who dines here?

MARGARET

76I have, sir.

OVERREACH

I have, sir. 'Tis an honorable man,
77A lord, Meg, and commands a regiment
78Of soldiers, and, what's rare, is one himself,
79A bold and understanding one; and to be
80A lord and a good leader in one volume
81Is granted unto few but such as rise up
82The kingdom's glory.

Enter Greedy.

GREEDY

The kingdom's glory. I'll resign my office
83If I be not better obeyed.

OVERREACH

If I be not better obeyed. 'Slight, art thou frantic?

GREEDY

84Frantic? 'Twould make me a frantic, and stark mad,
85Were I not a Justice of Peace, and Coram too,
86Which this rebellious cook cares not a straw for.
87There are a dozen of woodcocks—

OVERREACH

There are a dozen of woodcocks— Make thyself
88Thirteen, the baker's dozen.

GREEDY

Thirteen, the baker's dozen. I am contented,
89So they may be dressed to my mind. He has found out
90A new device for sauce, and will not dish 'em
91With toasts and butter. My father was a tailor,
92And my name, though a justice, Greedy Woodcock;
93And ere I'll see my lineage so abused,
94I'll give up my commission.

OVERREACH

I'll give up my commission. [to the cook within] Cook! Rogue, obey him!
95[To Greedy] I have given the word; pray you, now remove yourself
96To a collar of brawn, and trouble me no farther.

GREEDY

97I will, and meditate what to eat at dinner.

Exit Greedy.

OVERREACH

98And as I said, Meg, when this gull disturbed us,
99This honorable lord, this colonel,
100I would have thy husband.

MARGARET

I would have thy husband. There's too much disparity
101Between his quality and mine to hope it.

OVERREACH

102I more than hope't, and doubt not to effect it.
103Be thou no enemy to thyself; my wealth
104Shall weigh his titles down, and make you equals.
105Now for the means to assure him thine. Observe me:
106Remember he's a courtier and a soldier
107And not to be trifled with, and therefore when
108He comes to woo you, see you do not coy it.
109This mincing modesty hath spoiled many a match
110By a first refusal, in vain after hoped for.

MARGARET

111You'll have me, sir, preserve the distance that
112Confines a virgin?

OVERREACH

Confines a virgin? Virgin me no virgins!
113I must have you lose that name, or you lose me.
114I will have you private—start not—I say, private.
115If thou art my true daughter, not a bastard,
116Thou wilt venture alone with one man, though he came
117Like Jupiter to Semele, and come off too;
118And therefore when he kisses you, kiss close.

MARGARET

119I have heard this is the strumpet's fashion, sir,
120Which I must never learn.

OVERREACH

Which I must never learn. Learn anything,
121And from any creature that may make thee great—
122From the devil himself.

MARGARET

From the devil himself. This is but devilish doctrine.

OVERREACH

123Or if his blood grow hot, suppose he offer
124Beyond this, do not you stay till it cool,
125But meet his ardor; if a couch be near,
126Sit down on't, and invite him.

MARGARET

Sit down on't, and invite him. In your house?
127Your own house, sir? For heav'n's sake, what are you then?
128Or what shall I be, sir?

OVERREACH

Or what shall I be, sir? Stand not on form.
129Words are no substances.

MARGARET

Words are no substances. Though you could dispense
130With your own honor, cast aside religion,
131The hopes of heaven or fear of hell, excuse me
132In worldly policy, this is not the way
133To make me his wife; his whore, I grant it may do.
134My maiden honor so soon yielded up,
135Nay, prostituted, cannot but assure him
136I that am light to him will not hold weight
137When he is tempted by others; so, in judgment,
138When to his lust I have given up my honor,
139He must and will forsake me.

OVERREACH

He must and will forsake me. How? Forsake thee?
140Do I wear a sword for fashion? Or is this arm
141Shrunk up? Or withered? Does there live a man
142Of that large list I have encountered with
143Can truly say I e'er gave inch of ground
144Not purchased with his blood that did oppose me?
145Forsake thee when the thing is done? He dares not.
146Give me but proof he has enjoyed thy person,
147Though all his captains, echoes to his will,
148Stood armed by his side to justify the wrong,
149And he himself in the head of his bold troop,
150Spite of his lordship and his colonelship,
151Or the judge's favor, I will make him render
152A bloody and a strict account, and force him,
153By marrying thee, to cure thy wounded honor.
154I have said it.

Enter Marall.

MARALL

I have said it. Sir, the man of honor's come
155Newly alighted.

OVERREACH

Newly alighted. [to Margaret] In, without reply,
156And do as I command, or thou art lost.

Exit Margaret.

157Is the loud music I gave order for
158Ready to receive him?

MARALL

Ready to receive him? 'Tis, sir.

OVERREACH

Ready to receive him? 'Tis, sir. Let 'em sound
159A princely welcome.

[Exit Marall.]

A princely welcome. [To himself] Roughness, awhile leave me,
160For fawning now—a stranger to my nature—
161Must make way for me.

Loud music.

Enter Lovell, Greedy, Allworth, [and] Marall.

LOVELL

Must make way for me. Sir, you meet your trouble.

OVERREACH

162What you are pleased to style so is an honor
163Above my worth and fortunes.

ALLWORTH

Above my worth and fortunes. Strange. So humble!aparte

OVERREACH

164A justice of peace, my lord.

Presents Greedy to him.

LOVELL

A justice of peace, my lord. [to Greedy] Your hand, good sir.

GREEDY

165This is a lord, and some think this a favor;aparte
166But I had rather have my hand in my dumpling.aparte

OVERREACH

167Room for my lord!

LOVELL

Room for my lord! I miss, sir, your fair daughter
168To crown my welcome.

OVERREACH

To crown my welcome. May it please my lord
169To taste a glass of Greek wine first, and suddenly
170She shall attend my lord.

LOVELL

She shall attend my lord. You'll be obeyed, sir.

Exeunt omnes praeter Overreach.

OVERREACH

171'Tis to my wish. As soon as come, ask for her!—
172[Calling] Why, Meg! Meg Overreach!

[Enter Margaret.]

[Calling] Why, Meg! Meg Overreach! How! Tears in your eyes?
173Ha! Dry 'em quickly, or I'll dig 'em out.
174Is this a time to whimper? Meet that greatness
175That flies into thy bosom! Think what 'tis
176For me to say, "My honorable daughter,"
177And thou, when I stand bare, to say "Put on,"
178Or, "Father, you forget yourself." No more,
179But be instructed, or expect—He comes!

Enter Lovell, Greedy, Allworth, [and] Marall. They salute.

180A black-browed girl, my lord,

LOVELL

A black-browed girl, my lord, [kissing Margaret] As I live, a rare one.

ALLWORTH

181He's took already! I am lost.aparte

OVERREACH

He's took already! I am lost. That kissaparte
182Came twanging off; I like it.aparte

The rest [Greedy, Allworth, and Marall, go] off.

183A little bashful, my good lord, but you,
184I hope, will teach her boldness.

LOVELL

I hope, will teach her boldness. I am happy
185In such a scholar, but—

OVERREACH

In such a scholar, but— I am past learning,
186And therefore leave you to yourselves.
And therefore leave you to yourselves. (To his daughter) Remember!

Exit Overreach.

LOVELL

187You see, fair lady, your father is solicitous
188To have you change the barren name of virgin
189Into a hopeful wife.

MARGARET

Into a hopeful wife. His haste, my lord,
190Holds no power o'er my will.

LOVELL

Holds no power o'er my will. But o'er your duty.

MARGARET

191Which, forced too much, may break.

LOVELL

Which, forced too much, may break. Bend rather, sweetest.
192Think of your years.

MARGARET

Think of your years. Too few to match with yours;
193And choicest fruits, too soon plucked, rot and wither.

LOVELL

194Do you think I am old?

MARGARET

Do you think I am old? I am sure I am too young.

LOVELL

195I can advance you.

MARGARET

I can advance you. To a hill of sorrow,
196Where every hour I may expect to fall,
197But never hope firm footing. You are noble,
198I of a low descent, however rich;
199And tissues matched with scarlet suit but ill.
200O my good lord, I could say more, but that
201I dare not trust these walls.

LOVELL

I dare not trust these walls. Pray you, trust my ear, then.

[They whisper.]

Enter Overreach, listening.

OVERREACH

202Close at it! Whispering! This is excellent!
203And, by their postures, a consent on both parts.

Enter Greedy. [He and Overreach converse, unnoticed at first by Lovell and Margaret.]

GREEDY

204Sir Giles, Sir Giles!

OVERREACH

Sir Giles, Sir Giles! The great fiend stop that clapper!

GREEDY

205It must ring out, sir, when my belly rings noon.
206The baked meats are run out, the roasts turned powder.

OVERREACH

207I shall powder you.

GREEDY

I shall powder you. Beat me to dust, I care not;
208In such a cause as this, I'll die a martyr.

OVERREACH

209Marry, and shall, you barathrum of the shambles!

Strikes him.

GREEDY

210How! Strike a justice of peace? 'Tis petty treason,
211Edwardi quinto . But that you are my friend,
212I could commit you without bail or mainprize.

OVERREACH

213Leave your bawling, sir, or I shall commit you
214Where you shall not dine today. Disturb my lord
215When he is in discourse?

GREEDY

When he is in discourse? Is't a time to talk
216When we should be munching?

LOVELL

When we should be munching? [hearing something] Ha! I heard some noise.

OVERREACH

217[privately to Greedy] Mum, villain, vanish! Shall we break a bargain
218Almost made up?

Thrust Greedy off [exiting with him].

LOVELL

Almost made up? Lady, I understand you,
219And rest most happy in your choice, believe it;
220I'll be a careful pilot to direct
221Your yet uncertain bark to a port of safety.

MARGARET

222So shall Your Honor save two lives, and bind us
223Your slaves forever.

LOVELL

Your slaves forever. I am in the act rewarded,
224Since it is good; howe'er, you must put on
225An amorous carriage towards me to delude
226Your subtle father.

MARGARET

Your subtle father. I am prone to that.

LOVELL

227Now break we off our conference.—Sir Giles!
228Where is Sir Giles?

Enter Overreach and the rest [Greedy, Marall, and Allworth].

OVERREACH

Where is Sir Giles? My noble lord! And how
229Does Your Lordship find her?

LOVELL

Does Your Lordship find her? Apt, Sir Giles, and coming,
230And I like her the better.

OVERREACH

And I like her the better. So do I too.

LOVELL

231Yet, should we take forts at the first assault,
232'Twere poor in the defendant. I must confirm her
233With a love letter or two, which I must have
234Delivered by my page, and you give way to't.

OVERREACH

235With all my soul! A towardly gentleman.—
236Your hand, good Master Allworth. Know my house
237Is ever open to you.

ALLWORTH

Is ever open to you. 'Twas shut till now.aparte

OVERREACH

238Well done, well done, my honorable daughter!

240And cherish him, my honorable daughter.

MARGARET

241I shall, with my best care.

OVERREACH

I shall, with my best care. A coach!

GREEDY

I shall, with my best care. A coach! More stops
242Before we go to dinner! Oh, my guts!

LADY ALLWORTH

Before we go to dinner! Oh, my guts! [privately to Wellborn] If I find welcome,
243You share in it; if not, I'll back again,
244Now I know your ends, for I come armed for all
245Can be objected.

LOVELL

Can be objected. How! The Lady Allworth!

OVERREACH

246And thus attended!

Lovell salutes the Lady [Allworth]; the Lady salutes Margaret.

MARALL

And thus attended! No, I am a dolt!aparte
247The spirit of lies had ent'red me!aparte

OVERREACH

The spirit of lies had ent'red me! Peace, patch!aparte
248'Tis more than wonder! An astonishmentaparte

LOVELL

That does possess me wholly! Noble lady,
250This is a favor, to prevent my visit,
251The service of my life can never equal.

LADY ALLWORTH

252My lord, I laid wait for you, and much hoped
253You would have made my poor house your first inn;
254And therefore doubting that you might forget me,
255Or too long dwell here, having such ample cause
256In this unequalled beauty for your stay,
257And fearing to trust any but myself
258With the relation of my service to you,
259I borrowed so much from my long restraint
260And took the air in person to invite you.

LOVELL

261Your bounties are so great they rob me, madam,
262Of words to give you thanks.

LADY ALLWORTH

Of words to give you thanks. Good Sir Giles Overreach!

Salutes him.

263How dost thou, Marrall? [To Greedy] Liked you my meat so ill
264You'll dine no more with me?

GREEDY

You'll dine no more with me? I will, when you please,
265And it like Your Ladyship.

LADY ALLWORTH

And it like Your Ladyship. When you please, Master Greedy;
266If meat can do it, you shall be satisfied.—
267And now, my lord, pray take into your knowledge
268This gentleman. (Presents Wellborn.) Howe'er his outside's coarse,
269His inward linings are as fine and fair
270As any man's. Wonder not I speak at large.
271And howsoe'er his humor carries him
272To be thus accoutred, or what taint soever
273For his wild life hath stuck upon his fame,
274He may, ere long, with boldness rank himself
275With some that have contemned him.—Sir Giles Overreach,
276If I am welcome, bid him so.

OVERREACH

If I am welcome, bid him so. My nephew!
277He has been too long a stranger. [To Wellborn] Faith, you have;
278Pray, let it be mended.

MARALL

Though I be beaten dead for't. [privately to Lovell] Let my silence plead

LOVELL

Of my poor fortunes. [privately to Wellborn] I would hear, and help 'em.

OVERREACH

287[aloud] Your dinner waits you.

LOVELL

[aloud] Your dinner waits you. Pray you lead; we follow.

LADY ALLWORTH

GREEDY

289"Dear Master Wellborn"! So she said. Heav'n! Heav'n!

295To stand here prating, and forget my dinner.

Enter Marall.

296Are they set, Marrall?

MARALL

Are they set, Marrall? Long since. Pray you a word, sir.

GREEDY

297No wording now.

MARALL

No wording now. In troth, I must. My master,
298Knowing you are his good friend, makes bold with you
299And does entreat you, more guests being come in
300Than he expected, especially his nephew,
301The table being full too, you would excuse him
302And sup with him on the cold meat.

GREEDY

And sup with him on the cold meat. How! No dinner,
303After all my care?

MARALL

After all my care? 'Tis but a penance for
304A meal. Besides, you broke your fast.

GREEDY

A meal. Besides, you broke your fast. That was
305But a bit to stay my stomach. A man in commission
306Give place to a tatterdemalion?

MARALL

Give place to a tatterdemalion? No bug-words, sir.
307Should His Worship hear you—

GREEDY

Should His Worship hear you— Lose my dumpling too?
308And buttered toasts, and woodcocks?

MARALL

And buttered toasts, and woodcocks? Come, have patience.
309If you will dispense a little with your worship,
310And sit with the waiting-women, you have dumpling,
311Woodcock, and buttered toasts too.

GREEDY

Woodcock, and buttered toasts too. This revives me.
312I will gorge there sufficiently.

MARALL

I will gorge there sufficiently. This is the way, sir.

Exeunt.

3.3

[Enter] Overreach as from dinner.

OVERREACH

1She's caught! Oh, women! She neglects my lord,
2And all her compliments applied to Wellborn!
3The garments of her widowhood laid by,
4She now appears as glorious as the spring.
5Her eyes fixed on him, in the wine she drinks,
6He being her pledge, she sends him burning kisses,
7And sits on thorns till she be private with him.
8She leaves my meat to feed upon his looks,
9And, if in our discourse he be but named,
10From her a deep sigh follows. But why grieve I
11At this? It makes for me; if she prove his,
12All that is hers is mine, as I will work him.

Enter Marall.

MARALL

13Sir, the whole board is troubled at your rising.

OVERREACH

14No matter; I'll excuse it. Prithee, Marrall,
15Watch an occasion to invite my nephew
16To speak with me in private.

MARALL

To speak with me in private. Who! The rogue
17The lady scorned to look on?

OVERREACH

The lady scorned to look on? You are a wag.

Enter Lady [Allworth] and Wellborn.

MARALL

18[privately to Overreach] See, sir, she's come, and cannot be without him.

LADY ALLWORTH

19[to Overreach] With your favor, sir, after a plenteous dinner,
20I shall make bold to walk a turn or two
21In your rare garden.

OVERREACH

In your rare garden. There's an arbor too,
22If Your Ladyship please to use it.

LADY ALLWORTH

If Your Ladyship please to use it. Come, Master Wellborn.

Exeunt Lady [Allworth] and Wellborn.

OVERREACH

23Grosser and grosser! Now I believe the poet
24Feigned not, but was historical, when he wrote
25Pasiphaë was enamored of a bull.
26This lady's lust's more monstrous.—

Enter Lovell, Margaret, and the rest [including Allworth, Greedy, and servants].

This lady's lust's more monstrous.— My good lord,
27Excuse my manners.

LOVELL

Excuse my manners. There needs none, Sir Giles—
28I may ere long say "father," when it pleases
29My dearest mistress to give warrant to it.

OVERREACH

30She shall seal to it, my lord, and make me happy.

MARGARET

31My lady is returned.

Enter Wellborn and the Lady [Allworth].

LADY ALLWORTH

My lady is returned. [to servants] Provide my coach;
32I'll instantly away.—My thanks, Sir Giles,

[Exeunt servants.]

33For my entertainment.

OVERREACH

For my entertainment. 'Tis your nobleness
34To think it such.

LADY ALLWORTH

To think it such. I must do you a further wrong
35In taking away your honorable guest.

LOVELL

36I wait on you, madam.—Farewell, good Sir Giles.

LADY ALLWORTH

37Good Mistress Margaret!—Nay, come, Master Wellborn,
38I must not leave you behind; in sooth, I must not.

OVERREACH

39Rob me not, madam, of all joys at once.
40Let my nephew stay behind; he shall have my coach,
41And, after some small conference between us,
42Soon overtake Your Ladyship.

LADY ALLWORTH

Soon overtake Your Ladyship. [to Wellborn] Stay not long, sir.

LOVELL

43[kissing Margaret] This parting kiss. You shall every day hear from me
44By my faithful page.

ALLWORTH

By my faithful page. 'Tis a service I am proud of.

Exeunt Lovell, Lady Allworth, Marall [and all the others except Overreach and Wellborn].

OVERREACH

45Daughter, to your chamber.

[Exit] Margaret.

46You may wonder, nephew,
47After so long an enmity between us,
48I should desire your friendship.

WELLBORN

I should desire your friendship. So I do, sir.
49'Tis strange to me.

OVERREACH

'Tis strange to me. But I'll make it no wonder,
50And, what is more, unfold my nature to you.
51We worldly men, when we see friends and kinsmen
52Past hope sunk in their fortunes, lend no hand
53To lift 'em up, but rather set our feet
54Upon their heads to press 'em to the bottom—
55As, I must yield, with you I practised it.
56But, now I see you in a way to rise,
57I can and will assist you. This rich lady
58(And I am glad of 't) is enamored of you;
59'Tis too apparent, nephew.

WELLBORN

'Tis too apparent, nephew. No such thing;
60Compassion rather, sir.

OVERREACH

Compassion rather, sir. Well, in a word,
61Because your stay is short, I'll have you seen
62No more in this base shape; nor shall she say
63She married you like a beggar, or in debt.

WELLBORN

64He'll run into the noose and save my labor.aparte

OVERREACH

65You have a trunk of rich clothes, not far hence,
66In pawn; I will redeem 'em. And, that no clamor
67May taint your credit for your petty debts,
68You shall have a thousand pounds to cut 'em off,
69And go a free man to the wealthy lady.

WELLBORN

70This done, sir, out of love, and no ends else—

OVERREACH

71As it is, nephew.

WELLBORN

As it is, nephew. Binds me still your servant.

OVERREACH

72No compliments; you are stayed for. Ere you've supped
73You shall hear from me.—My coach, knaves, for my nephew!—
74Tomorrow I will visit you.

WELLBORN

Tomorrow I will visit you. Here's an uncle
75In a man's extremes! How much they do belie you
76That say you are hard-hearted!

OVERREACH

That say you are hard-hearted! My deeds, nephew,
77Shall speak my love; what men report I weigh not.

Exeunt.
