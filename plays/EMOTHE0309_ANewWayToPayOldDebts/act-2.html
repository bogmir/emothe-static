2.1

[Enter] Overreach [and] Marall.

OVERREACH

1He's gone, I warrant thee; this commission crushed him.

MARALL

2Your Worship have the way on't, and ne'er miss
3To squeeze these unthrifts into air; and yet
4The chap-fall'n justice did his part, returning
5For your advantage the certificate,
6Against his conscience and his knowledge too
7(With your good favor), to the utter ruin
8Of the poor farmer.

OVERREACH

Of the poor farmer. 'Twas for these good ends
9I made him a justice. He that bribes his belly
10Is certain to command his soul.

MARALL

Is certain to command his soul. I wonder
11(Still with your license) why, Your Worship having
12The power to put this thin-gut in commission,
13You are not in't yourself?

OVERREACH

You are not in't yourself? Thou art a fool.
14In being out of office I am out of danger,
15Where, if I were a justice, besides the trouble,
16I might or out of willfulness or error
17Run myself finely into a praemuníre,
18And so become a prey to the informer.
19No, I'll have none of't. 'Tis enough I keep
20Greedy at my devotion; so he serve
21My purposes, let him hang, or damn, I care not.
22Friendship is but a word.

MARALL

Friendship is but a word. You are all wisdom.

OVERREACH

23I would be wordly-wise; for the other wisdom,
24That does prescribe us a well-governed life,
25And to do right to others as ourselves,
26I value not an atom.

MARALL

I value not an atom. What course take you,
27With your good patience, to hedge in the manor
28Of your neighbor Master Frugal? As 'tis said
29He will nor sell nor borrow nor exchange,
30And his land, lying in the midst of your many lordships,
31Is a foul blemish.

OVERREACH

Is a foul blemish. I have thought on't, Marrall,
32And it shall take. I must have all men sellers,
33And I the only purchaser.

MARALL

And I the only purchaser. 'Tis most fit, sir.

OVERREACH

34I'll therefore buy some cottage near his manor,
35Which done, I'll make my men break ope his fences,
36Ride o'er his standing corn, and in the night
37Set fire on his barns, or break his cattle's legs.
38These trespasses draw on suits, and suits expenses,
39Which I can spare, but will soon beggar him.
40When I have harried him thus two or three year,
41Though he sue in forma pauperis , in spite
42Of all his thrift and care he'll grow behindhand.

MARALL

43The best I ever heard! I could adore you.

OVERREACH

44Then with the favor of my man of law,
45I will pretend some title. Want will force him
46To put it to arbitrament; then, if he sell
47For half the value, he shall have ready money,
48And I possess his land.

MARALL

And I possess his land. 'Tis above wonder!
49Wellborn was apt to sell, and needed not
50These fine arts, sir, to hook him in.

OVERREACH

These fine arts, sir, to hook him in. Well thought on.
51This varlet, Marall, lives too long to upbraid me
52With my close cheat put upon him. Will nor cold
53Nor hunger kill him?

MARALL

Nor hunger kill him? I know not what to think on't.
54I have used all means, and the last night I caused
55His host the tapster to turn him out of doors,
56And have been since with all your friends and tenants,
57And on the forfeit of your favour charged 'em,
58Though a crust of moldy bread would keep him from starving,
59Yet they should not relieve him. This is done, sir.

OVERREACH

60That was something, Marall, but thou must go further,
61And suddenly, Marall.

MARALL

And suddenly, Marall. Where and when you please, sir.

OVERREACH

62I would have thee seek him out, and, if thou canst,
63Persuade him that 'tis better steal than beg.
64Then if I prove he has but robbed a henroost,
65Not all the world shall save him from the gallows.
66Do anything to work him to despair,
67And 'tis thy masterpiece.

MARALL

And 'tis thy masterpiece. I will do my best, sir.

OVERREACH

68I am now on my main work with the Lord Lovell,
69The gallant-minded, popular Lord Lovell;
70The minion of the people's love. I hear
71He's come into the country, and my aims are
72To insinuate myself into his knowledge,
73And then invite him to my house.

MARALL

And then invite him to my house. I have you.
74This points at my young mistress.

OVERREACH

This points at my young mistress. She must part with
75That humble title, and write "Honorable,"
76"Right Honorable," Marall, my "Right Honorable" daughter,
77If all I have, or e'er shall get, will do it.
78I will have her well attended; there are ladies
79Of errant knights decayed and brought so low
80That, for cast clothes and meat, will gladly serve her.
81And 'tis my glory, though I come from the city,
82To have their issue, whom I have undone,
83To kneel to mine as bondslaves.

MARALL

To kneel to mine as bondslaves. 'Tis fit state, sir.

OVERREACH

84And therefore, I'll not have a chambermaid
85That ties her shoes, or any meaner office,
86But such whose fathers were Right Worshipful.
87'Tis a rich man's pride, there having ever been
88More than a feud, a strange antipathy
89Between us and true gentry.

Enter Wellborn.

MARALL

Between us and true gentry. See who's here, sir.

OVERREACH

90Hence, monster! Prodigy!

WELLBORN

Hence, monster! Prodigy! Sir, your wife's nephew;
91She and my father tumbled in one belly.

OVERREACH

92Avoid my sight! Thy breath's infectious, rogue.
93I shun thee as a leprosy, or the plague.—
94This is the time to work him.aparte

MARALL

95I warrant you, sir.

Exit Overreach.

WELLBORN

I warrant you, sir. By this light, I think he's mad.

MARALL

96Mad? Had you took compassion on yourself,
97You long since had been mad.

WELLBORN

You long since had been mad. You have took a course,
98Between you and my venerable uncle,
99To make me so.

MARALL

To make me so. The more pale-spirited you,
100That would not be instructed. I swear deeply—

WELLBORN

101By what?

MARALL

By what? By my religion.

WELLBORN

By what? By my religion. Thy religion!
102The devil's creed. But what would you have done?

MARALL

103Had there been but one tree in all the shire,
104Nor any hope to compass a penny halter,
105Before, like you, I had outlived my fortunes,
106A withe had served my turn to hang myself.
107I am zealous in your cause; pray you, hang yourself,
108And presently, as you love your credit.

WELLBORN

And presently, as you love your credit. I thank you.

MARALL

109Will you stay till you die in a ditch, or lice devour you?
110Or if you dare not do the feat yourself,
111But that you'll put the state to charge and trouble,
112Is there no purse to be cut? House to be broken?
113Or market women with eggs that you may murder,
114And so dispatch the business?

WELLBORN

And so dispatch the business? Here's variety,
115I must confess; but I'll accept of none
116Of all your gentle offers, I assure you.

MARALL

117Why, have you hope ever to eat again?
118Or drink? Or be the master of three farthings?
119If you like not hanging, drown yourself. Take some course
120For your reputation.

WELLBORN

For your reputation. 'Twill not do, dear tempter,
121With all the rhetoric the fiend hath taught you.
122I am as far as thou art from despair;
123Nay, I have confidence, which is more than hope,
124To live, and suddenly, better than ever.

MARALL

125Ha! Ha! These castles you build in the air
126Will not persuade me or to give or lend
127A token to you.

WELLBORN

A token to you. I'll be more kind to thee.
128Come, thou shalt dine with me.

MARALL

Come, thou shalt dine with me. With you?

WELLBORN

Come, thou shalt dine with me. With you? Nay, more, dine gratis.

MARALL

129Under what hedge, I pray you? Or at whose cost?
130Are they padders or Abram-men that are your consorts?

WELLBORN

131Thou art incredulous, but thou shalt dine
132Not alone at her house, but with a gallant lady—
133With me and with a lady.

MARALL

With me and with a lady. Lady? What lady?
134With the Lady of the Lake, or Queen of Fairies?
135For I know it must be an enchanted dinner.

WELLBORN

136With the Lady Allworth, knave.

MARALL

With the Lady Allworth, knave. Nay, now there's hope
137Thy brain is cracked.

WELLBORN

Thy brain is cracked. Mark there with what respect
138I am entertained.

MARALL

I am entertained. With choice, no doubt, of dog whips.
139Why, dost thou ever hope to pass her porter?

WELLBORN

140'Tis not far off; go with me. Trust thine own eyes.

MARALL

141Troth, in my hope, or my assurance rather
142To see thee curvet and mount like a dog in a blanket
143If ever thou presume to pass her threshold,
144I will endure thy company.

WELLBORN

I will endure thy company. Come along, then.

Exeunt.

2.2

[Enter] Allworth, Waiting-woman, Chambermaid, Order, Amble, Furnace, [and] Watchall.

WAITING-WOMAN

CHAMBERMAID

2Or half an hour?

CHAMBERMAID

WAITING-WOMAN

ALLWORTH

14To both.

WAITING-WOMAN

CHAMBERMAID

WAITING-WOMAN

2.3

[Enter] Wellborn [and] Marall.

WELLBORN

1I think I am in a good way.

MARALL

I think I am in a good way. Good sir, the best way,
2The certain best way.

WELLBORN

3That men are subject to.

MARALL

4And, as you are already Worshipful,

WELLBORN

MARALL

12When Your Worship's present.

WELLBORN

MARALL

15I know Your Worship's wise, and needs no counsel;
16Yet if, in my desire to do you service,

WELLBORN

MARALL

OVERREACH

MARALL

With him? What lady? The rich Lady Allworth.

OVERREACH

75Thou dolt! How dar'st thou speak this?

MARALL

Thou dolt! How dar'st thou speak this? I speak truth;

MARALL
