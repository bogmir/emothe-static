Act V

[5.1]

Enter Octavius, Antony, and their army.

OCTAVIUS

1928Now, Antony, our hopes are answerèd.
1929You said the enemy would not come down
1930But keep the hills and upper regions.
1931It proves not so: their battles are at hand.
1932They mean to warn us at Philippi here,
1933Answering before we do demand of them.

ANTONY

1934Tut, I am in their bosoms, and I know
1935Wherefore they do it. They could be content
1936To visit other places, and come down
1937With fearful bravery, thinking by this face
1938To fasten in our thoughts that they have courage.
1939But 'tis not so. enger.

Enter a Messenger.

MESSALA

But 'tis not so. enger. Prepare you, generals!
1940The enemy comes on in gallant show.
1941Their bloody sign of battle is hung out,
1942And something to be done immediately.

ANTONY

1943Octavius, lead your battle softly on
1944Upon the left hand of the even field.

OCTAVIUS

1945Upon the right hand, I; keep thou the left.

ANTONY

1946Why do you cross me in this exigent?

OCTAVIUS

1947I do not cross you, but I will do so.

March. Drum. Enter Brutus, Cassius, and their army[: Lucilius, Titinnius, Messala, and others].

BRUTUS

1948They stand and would have parley.

CASSIUS

1949Stand fast, Titinius, we must out and talk.

OCTAVIUS

1950Mark Antony, shall we give sign of battle?

ANTONY

1951No, Caesar, we will answer on their charge.
1952Make forth! The generals would have some words.

OCTAVIUS

1953Stir not until the signal.

BRUTUS

1954Words before blows. Is it so, countrymen?

OCTAVIUS

1955Not that we love words better, as you do.

BRUTUS

1956Good words are better than bad strokes, Octavius.

ANTONY

1957In your bad strokes, Brutus, you give good words.
1958Witness the hole you made in Caesar's heart,
1959Crying,

CASSIUS

Crying, Antony,
1960The posture of your blows are yet unknown;
1961But for your words, they rob the Hybla bees,
1962And leave them honeyless.

ANTONY

And leave them honeyless. Not stingless too?

BRUTUS

1963Oh yes, and soundless too.
1964For you have stol'n their buzzing, Antony,
1965And very wisely threat before you sting.

ANTONY

1966Villains! You did not so, when your vile daggers
1967Hacked one another in the sides of Caesar.
1968You showed your teeth like apes, and fawned like hounds,
1969And bowed like bondmen, kissing Caesar's feet,
1970Whilst damnèd Casca, like a cur, behind
1971Struck Caesar on the neck. Oh, you flatterers!

CASSIUS

1972Flatterers? Now, Brutus, thank yourself!
1973This tongue had not offended so today,
1974If Cassius might have ruled.

OCTAVIUS

1975Come, come, the cause. If arguing make us sweat,
1976The proof of it will turn to redder drops.
1977Look, I draw a sword against conspirators!
1978When think you that the sword goes up again?
1979Never till Caesar's three-and-thirty wounds
1980Be well avenged, or till another Caesar
1981Have added slaughter to the sword of traitors.

BRUTUS

1982Caesar, thou canst not die by traitors' hands,
1983Unless thou bring'st them with thee.

OCTAVIUS

Unless thou bring'st them with thee. So I hope.
1984I was not born to die on Brutus' sword.

BRUTUS

1985Oh, if thou wert the noblest of thy strain,
1986Young man, thou couldst not die more honorable.

CASSIUS

1987A peevish schoolboy, worthless of such honor,
1988Joined with a masker and a reveler.

ANTONY

1989Old Cassius still.

OCTAVIUS

Old Cassius still. Come, Antony! Away!
1990Defiance, traitors, hurl we in your teeth!
1991If you dare fight today, come to the field;
1992If not, when you have stomachs!

Exeunt Octavius, Antony, and army.

CASSIUS

1993Why, now blow wind! Swell billow! And swim bark!
1994The storm is up, and all is on the hazard!

BRUTUS

1995Ho, Lucilius! Hark, a word with you.

Lucilius stands forth.

LUCILIUS

1996My lord?

[Brutus speaks apart with Lucilius.]

CASSIUS

1997[Messala stands forth.] Messala.

MESSALA

[Messala stands forth.] Messala. What says my general?

CASSIUS

[Messala stands forth.] Messala. What says my general? Messala,
1998This is my birthday, as this very day
1999Was Cassius born. Give me thy hand, Messala,
2000Be thou my witness, that against my will,
2001As Pompey was, am I compelled to set
2002Upon one battle all our liberties.
2003You know that I held Epicurus strong
2004And his opinion; now I change my mind,
2005And partly credit things that do presage.
2006Coming from Sardis, on our former ensign
2007Two mighty eagles fell, and there they perched,
2008Gorging and feeding from our soldiers' hands,
2009Who to Philippi here consorted us.
2010This morning are they fled away and gone,
2011And in their steads do ravens, crows, and kites
2012Fly o'er our heads and downward look on us
2013As we were sickly prey. Their shadows seem
2014A canopy most fatal, under which
2015Our army lies ready to give up the ghost.

MESSALA

2016Believe not so.

CASSIUS

Believe not so. I but believe it partly,
2017For I am fresh of spirit and resolved
2018To meet all perils very constantly.

BRUTUS

2019Even so, Lucilius.

CASSIUS

Even so, Lucilius. Now, most noble Brutus,
2020The gods today stand friendly, that we may,
2021Lovers in peace, lead on our days to age.
2022But since the affairs of men rest still uncertain,
2023Let's reason with the worst that may befall.
2024If we do lose this battle, then is this
2025The very last time we shall speak together.
2026What are you then determinèd to do?

BRUTUS

2027Even by the rule of that philosophy
2028By which I did blame Cato for the death
2029Which he did give himself, I know not how,
2030But I do find it cowardly and vile,
2031For fear of what might fall, so to prevent
2032The time of life, arming myself with patience
2033To stay the providence of some high powers
2034That govern us below.

CASSIUS

That govern us below. Then, if we lose this battle,
2035You are contented to be led in triumph
2036Thorough the streets of Rome?

BRUTUS

2037No Cassius, no. Think not, thou noble Roman,
2038That ever Brutus will go bound to Rome;
2039He bears too great a mind. But this same day
2040Must end that work the ides of March begun,
2041And whether we shall meet again I know not.
2042Therefore our everlasting farewell take.
2043Forever and forever, farewell Cassius!
2044If we do meet again, why we shall smile;
2045If not, why then this parting was well made.

CASSIUS

2046Forever and forever, farewell Brutus!
2047If we do meet again, we'll smile indeed;
2048If not, 'tis true, this parting was well made.

BRUTUS

2049Why then, lead on. Oh, that a man might know
2050The end of this day's business ere it come!
2051But it sufficeth that the day will end,
2052And then the end is known. Come ho, away!

Exeunt.

[5.2]

Alarum. Enter Brutus and Messala.

BRUTUS

2053Ride, ride, Messala! Ride, and give these bills
2054Unto the legions, on the other side.

Loud alarum.

2055Let them set on at once, for I perceive
2056But cold demeanor in Octavio's wing,
2057And sudden push gives them the overthrow.
2058Ride, ride, Messala! Let them all come down.

Exeunt.

[5.3]

Alarums. Enter Cassius and Titinius.

CASSIUS

2059O look, Titinius! Look! The villains fly!
2060Myself have to mine own turned enemy!
2061This ensign here of mine was turning back;
2062I slew the coward and did take it from him.

TITINIUS

2063O Cassius, Brutus gave the word too early,
2064Who having some advantage on Octavius,
2065Took it too eagerly. His soldiers fell to spoil,
2066Whilst we by Antony are all enclosed.

Enter Pindarus.

PINDARUS

2067Fly further off, my Lord! Fly further off!
2068Mark Antony is in your tents, my Lord!
2069Fly, therefore, noble Cassius! Fly far off!

CASSIUS

2070This hill is far enough. Look, look, Titinius!
2071Are those my tents, where I perceive the fire?

TITINIUS

2072They are, my Lord.

CASSIUS

They are, my Lord. Titinius, if thou lovest me,
2073Mount thou my horse, and hide thy spurs in him
2074Till he have brought thee up to yonder troops
2075And here again, that I may rest assured
2076Whether yond troops are friend or enemy.

TITINIUS

2077I will be here again even with a thought.

Exit.

CASSIUS

2078Go, Pindarus, get higher on that hill!
2079My sight was ever thick. Regard Titinius,
2080And tell me what thou not'st about the field.

[Pindarus goes up.]

2081This day I breathèd first. Time is come round,
2082And where I did begin, there shall I end.
2083My life is run his compass. Sirrah, what news?

PINDARUS

2084(Above.) O my Lord!

CASSIUS

2085What news?

PINDARUS

2086Titinius is enclosèd round about
2087With horsemen that make to him on the spur,
2088Yet he spurs on. Now they are almost on him!
2089Now, Titinius! Now some light. Oh, he lights too.
2090He's ta'en!

Shout.

CASSIUS

He's ta'en! Come down. Behold no more.
2091Oh, coward that I am to live so long,
2092To see my best friend ta'en before my face.

Enter Pindarus [from above].

2093Come hither, sirrah.
2094In Parthia did I take thee prisoner,
2095And then I swore thee, saving of thy life,
2096That whatsoever I did bid thee do,
2097Thou should'st attempt it. Come now, keep thine oath,
2098Now be a free man, and with this good sword
2099That ran through Caesar's bowels, search this bosom.
2100Stand not to answer. Here, take thou the hilts,
2101And when my face is cover'd, as 'tis now,
2102Guide thou the sword—
Guide thou the sword— [Pindarus kills him.] Caesar, thou art revenged,
2103Even with the sword that killed thee.

PINDARUS

2104So, I am free, yet would not so have been
2105Durst I have done my will. O Cassius,
2106Far from this country Pindarus shall run,
2107Where never Roman shall take note of him.

[Exit.]

Enter Titinius and Messala.

MESSALA

2108It is but change, Titinius, for Octavius
2109Is overthrown by noble Brutus' power,
2110As Cassius' legions are by Antony.

TITINIUS

2111These tidings will well comfort Cassius.

MESSALA

2112Where did you leave him?

TITINIUS

Where did you leave him? All disconsolate,
2113With Pindarus his bondman, on this hill.

MESSALA

2114Is not that he that lies upon the ground?

TITINIUS

2115He lies not like the living. Oh, my heart!

MESSALA

2116Is not that he?

TITINIUS

Is not that he? No, this was he, Messala,
2117But Cassius is no more. O setting sun,
2118As in thy red rays thou doest sink tonight,
2119So in his red blood Cassius' day is set.
2120The sun of Rome is set. Our day is gone;
2121Clouds, dews, and dangers come; our deeds are done.
2122Mistrust of my success hath done this deed.

MESSALA

2123Mistrust of good success hath done this deed.
2124O hateful Error, Melancholy's child,
2125Why dost thou show to the apt thoughts of men
2126The things that are not? O Error, soon conceived,
2127Thou never comst unto a happy birth,
2128But kill'st the mother that engendered thee.

TITINIUS

2129What, Pindarus! Where art thou, Pindarus?

MESSALA

2130Seek him, Titinius, whilst I go to meet
2131The noble Brutus, thrusting this report
2132Into his ears. I may say "thrusting" it,
2133For piercing steel and darts envenomed
2134Shall be as welcome to the ears of Brutus
2135As tidings of this sight.

TITINIUS

As tidings of this sight. Hie you, Messala,
2136And I will seek for Pindarus the while.

[Exit Messala.]

2137Why didst thou send me forth, brave Cassius?
2138Did I not meet thy friends, and did not they
2139Put on my brows this wreath of victory,
2140And bid me give it thee? Did'st thou not hear their shouts?
2141Alas, thou hast misconstrued everything.
2142But hold thee, take this garland on thy brow.
2143Thy Brutus bid me give it thee, and I
2144Will do his bidding. Brutus, come apace,
2145And see how I regarded Caius Cassius.
2146By your leave, gods. This is a Roman's part.
2147Come Cassius' sword, and find Titinius' heart.

Dies.

Alarum. Enter Brutus, Messala, young Cato, Strato, Volumnius, and Lucilius.

BRUTUS

2148Where, where, Messala, doth his body lie?

MESSALA

2149Lo yonder, and Titinius mourning it.

BRUTUS

2150Titinius' face is upward.

CATO

Titinius' face is upward. He is slain.

BRUTUS

2151O Julius Caesar, thou art mighty yet!
2152Thy spirit walks abroad and turns our swords
2153Low Alarums. In our own proper entrails.

CATO

Low Alarums. In our own proper entrails. Brave Titinius!

Low Alarums.

2154Look, whe'er he have not crowned dead Cassius.

BRUTUS

2155Are yet two Romans living such as these?
2156The last of all the Romans, fare thee well!
2157It is impossible that ever Rome
2158Should breed thy fellow. Friends, I owe more tears
2159To this dead man than you shall see me pay.
2160I shall find time, Cassius, I shall find time.
2161Come therefore, and to Thasos send his body.
2162His funerals shall not be in our camp,
2163Lest it discomfort us. Lucilius, come,
2164And come young Cato. Let us to the field.
2165Labio and Flavio set our battles on.
2166'Tis three o'clock, and Romans, yet ere night,
2167We shall try fortune in a second fight.

Exeunt.

[5.4]

Alarum. Enter Brutus, Messala, young Cato, Lucilius, and Flavius.

BRUTUS

2168Yet, countrymen, oh yet, hold up your heads!

[Exit fighting, followed by Messala and Flavius.]

CATO

2169What bastard doth not? Who will go with me?
2170I will proclaim my name about the field.
2171I am the son of Marcus Cato, ho!
2172A foe to tyrants, and my country's friend.
2173I am the son of Marcus Cato, ho!

Enter soldiers and fight.

LUCILIUS

2174And I am Brutus, Marcus Brutus, I!
2175Brutus, my country's friend! Know me for Brutus!

[Young Cato is killed.]

2176O young and noble Cato, art thou down?
2177Why now thou diest as bravely as Titinius
2178And mayst be honor'd, being Cato's son.

1 SOLDIER

2179Yield, or thou diest!

LUCILIUS

Yield, or thou diest! Only I yield to die.
2180There is so much that thou wilt kill me straight.
2181Kill Brutus, and be honored in his death.

1 SOLDIER

2182We must not. A noble prisoner!

Enter Antony.

2 SOLDIER

2183Room, ho! Tell Antony, Brutus is ta'en.

1 SOLDIER

2184I'll tell the news. Here comes the general.
2185Brutus is ta'en! Brutus is ta'en, my lord!

ANTONY

2186Where is he?

LUCILIUS

2187Safe, Antony. Brutus is safe enough.
2188I dare assure thee that no enemy
2189Shall ever take alive the noble Brutus.
2190The gods defend him from so great a shame!
2191When you do find him, or alive or dead,
2192He will be found like Brutus, like himself.

ANTONY

2193This is not Brutus, friend, but I assure you,
2194A prize no less in worth. Keep this man safe;
2195Give him all kindness. I had rather have
2196Such men my friends than enemies. Go on,
2197And see whe'er Brutus be alive or dead,
2198And bring us word unto Octavius' tent
2199How everything is chanced.

Exeunt.

[5.5]

Enter Brutus, Dardanius, Clitus, Strato, and Volumnius.

BRUTUS

2200Come, poor remains of friends, rest on this rock.

CLITUS

2201Statilius showed the torchlight, but, my lord,
2202He came not back. He is or ta'en or slain.

BRUTUS

2203Sit thee down, Clitus. Slaying is the word;
2204It is a deed in fashion. Hark thee, Clitus.

CLITUS

2205What I, my Lord? No, not for all the world!

BRUTUS

2206Peace then, no words.

CLITUS

Peace then, no words. I’ll rather kill myself.

BRUTUS

2207Hark thee, Dardanius.

DARDANIUS

Hark thee, Dardanius. Shall I do such a deed?

CLITUS

2208O Dardanius!

DARDANIUS

2209O Clitus!

CLITUS

2210What ill request did Brutus make to thee?

DARDANIUS

2211To kill him, Clitus. Look, he meditates.

CLITUS

2212Now is that noble vessel full of grief,
2213That it runs over even at his eyes.

BRUTUS

2214Come hither, good Volumnius. List a word.

VOLUMNIUS

2215What says my lord?

BRUTUS

What says my lord? Why this, Volumnius.
2216The ghost of Caesar hath appeared to me
2217Two several times by night: at Sardis once,
2218And this last night here in Philippi fields.
2219I know my hour is come.

VOLUMNIUS

I know my hour is come. Not so, my lord.

BRUTUS

2220Nay, I am sure it is, Volumnius.
2221Thou see'st the World, Volumnius, how it goes. Our enemies have beat us to the pit.

Low alarums.

2222It is more worthy to leap in ourselves
2223Then tarry till they push us. Good Volumnius,
2224Thou know'st that we two went to school together.
2225Even for that, our love of old, I prithee,
2226Hold thou my sword hilts whilst I run on it.

VOLUMNIUS

2227That's not an office for a friend, my lord.

Alarum still.

CLITUS

2228Fly! Fly, my Lord! There is no tarrying here!

BRUTUS

2229Farewell to you, and you, and you, Volumnius.
2230Strato, thou hast been all this while asleep.
2231Farewell to thee too, Strato. Countrymen,
2232My heart doth joy that yet in all my life
2233I found no man but he was true to me.
2234I shall have glory by this losing day
2235More then Octavius and Mark Antony
2236By this vile conquest shall attain unto.
2237So fare you well at once, for Brutus' tongue
2238Hath almost ended his life's history.
2239Night hangs upon mine eyes; my bones would rest,
2240That have but laboured to attain this hour.

Alarum. Cry within, "Fly! Fly! Fly!"

CLITUS

2241Fly, my Lord! Fly!.

[Exeunt Clitus, Dardanius, and Volumnius.]

BRUTUS

2242I prithee, Strato, stay thou by thy lord.
2243Thou art a fellow of a good respect;
2244Thy life hath had some smatch of honor in it.
2245Hold then my sword, and turn away thy face,
2246While I do run upon it. Wilt thou, Strato?

STRATO

2247Give me your hand first. Fare you well, my lord.

BRUTUS

2248Farewell, good Strato.
Farewell, good Strato. [Runs on his sword.] —Caesar, now be still.
2249I killed not thee with half so good a will.

Dies.

Alarum. Retreat. Enter Antony, Octavius, Messala, Lucilius, and the army.

OCTAVIUS

2250What man is that?

MESSALA

2251My master's man. Strato, where is thy master?

STRATO

2252Free from the bondage you are in, Messala.
2253The conquerors can but make a fire of him,
2254For Brutus only overcame himself,
2255And no man else hath honor by his death.

LUCILIUS

2256So Brutus should be found. I thank thee, Brutus,
2257That thou hast proved Lucilius' saying true.

OCTAVIUS

2258All that served Brutus, I will entertain them.
2259Fellow, wilt thou bestow thy time with me?

STRATO

2260Ay, if Messala will prefer me to you.

OCTAVIUS

2261Do so, good Messala.

MESSALA

Do so, good Messala. How died my master, Strato?

STRATO

2262I held the sword, and he did run on it.

MESSALA

2263Octavius, then take him to follow thee,
2264That did the latest service to my master.

ANTONY

2265This was the noblest Roman of them all.
2266All the conspirators save only he
2267Did that they did in envy of great Caesar;
2268He only in a general honest thought
2269And common good to all made one of them.
2270His life was gentle, and the elements
2271So mixed in him that nature might stand up,
2272And say to all the world, "This was a man!"

OCTAVIUS

2273According to his virtue, let us use him,
2274With all respect and rites of burial.
2275Within my tent his bones tonight shall lie,
2276Most like a soldier, ordered honorably:
2277So call the field to rest, and let's away,
2278To part the glories of this happy day.

Exeunt omnes.
