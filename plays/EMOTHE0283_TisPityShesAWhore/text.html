Tis Pity She's a Whore

[I.i]

Enter Friar and Giovanni.

FRIAR.

1Dispute no more in this, for know, young man,
2These are no school-points; nice philosophy
3May tolerate unlikely arguments,
4But Heaven admits no jest: wits that presum’d
5On wit too much, by striving how to prove
6There was no God, with foolish grounds of art,
7Discover’d first the nearest way to hell,
8And fill’d the world with devilish atheism.
9Such questions, youth, are fond; for better ’tis
10To bless the sun than reason why it shines,
11Yet He thou talk’st of is above the sun.
12No more; I may not hear it.

GIOVANNI.

No more; I may not hear it. Gentle father,
13To you I have unclasp’d my burdened soul,
14Emptied the storehouse of my thoughts and heart,
15Made myself poor of secrets; have not left
16Another word untold, which hath not spoke
17All what I ever durst or think or know;
18And yet is here the comfort I shall have,
19Must I not do what all men else may, love?

FRIAR.

20Yes, you may love, fair son.

GIOVANNI.

Yes, you may love, fair son. Must I not praise
21That beauty which, if fram’d anew, the gods
22Would make a god of, if they had it there,
23And kneel to it, as I do kneel to them?

FRIAR.

24Why, foolish madman–

GIOVANNI.

Why, foolish madman– Shall a peevish sound,
25A customary form, from man to man,
26Of brother and of sister, be a bar
27’Twixt my perpetual happiness and me?
28Say that we had one father, say one womb
29(Curse to my joys) gave both us life and birth;
30Are we not therefore each to other bound
31So much the more by nature, by the links
32Of blood, of reason- nay, if you will have’t,
33Even of religion –to be ever one,
34One soul, one flesh, one love, one heart, one all?

FRIAR.

35Have done, unhappy youth, for thou art lost.

GIOVANNI.

36Shall then, for that I am her brother born,
37My joys be ever banish’d from her bed?
38No, father; in your eyes I see the change
39Of pity and compassion; from your age,
40As from a sacred oracle, distils
41The life of counsel: tell me, holy man
42What cure shall give me ease in these extremes.

FRIAR.

43Repentance, son, and sorrow for this sin:
44For thou hast mov’d a Majesty above
45With thy unranged-almost blasphemy.

GIOVANNI.

46O do not speak of that, dear confessor!

FRIAR.

47Art thou, my son, that miracle of wit
48Who once, within these three months, wert esteem’d
49A wonder of thine age, throughout Bononia?
50How did the University applaud
51Thy government, behaviour, learning, speech,
52Sweetness, and all that could make up a man!
53I was proud of my tutelage, and chose
54Rather to leave my books than part with thee.
55I did so: but the fruits of all my hopes
56Are lost in thee, as thou art in thyself.
57O, Giovanni, hast thou left the schools
58Of knowledge to converse with lust and death?
59For death waits on thy lust. Look through the world,
60And thou shalt see a thousand faces shine
61More glorious than this idol thou ador’st:
62Leave her, and take thy choice, ’tis much less sin,
63Though in such games as those they lose that win.

GIOVANNI.

64It were more ease to stop the ocean
65From floats and ebbs than to dissuade my vows.

FRIAR.

66Then I have done, and in thy wilful flames
67Already see thy ruin; Heaven is just.
68Yet hear my counsel.

GIOVANNI.

Yet hear my counsel. As a voice of life.

FRIAR.

69Hie to thy father’s house, there lock thee fast
70Alone within thy chamber, then fall down
71On both thy knees, and grovel on the ground:
72Cry to thy heart, wash every word thou utter’st
73In tears, and (if’t be possible) of blood:
74Beg Heaven to cleanse the leprosy of lust
75That rots thy soul, acknowledge what thou art,
76A wretch, a worm, a nothing: weep, sigh, pray
77Three times a day, and three times every night.
78For seven days’ space do this, then if thou find’st
79No change in thy desires, return to me:
80I’ll think on remedy. Pray for thyself
81At home, whilst I pray for thee here. –Away,
82My blessing with thee, we have need to pray.

GIOVANNI.

83All this I’ll do, to free me from the rod
84Of vengeance; else I’ll swear my fate’s my god.

Exeunt.

[I.ii]

Enter Grimaldi and Vasques ready to fight.

VASQUES.

Come, sir, stand to your tackling; if you prove craven, I’ll make you run quickly.

GRIMALDI.

Thou art no equal match for me.

VASQUES.

Indeed I never went to the wars to bring home news, nor cannot play the mountebank for a meal’s meat, and swear I got my wounds in the field. See you these grey hairs? They’ll not flinch for a bloody nose. Wilt thou to this gear?

GRIMALDI.

Why, slave, think’st thou I’ll balance my reputation with a cast-suit? Call thy master, he shall know that I dare–

VASQUES.

Scold like a cot-quean, that’s your profession. Thou poor shadow of a soldier, I will make thee know my master keeps servants thy betters in quality and performance. Com’st thou to fight or prate?

GRIMALDI.

Neither, with thee. I am a Roman and a gentleman, one that have got mine honor with expense of blood.

VASQUES.

You are a lying coward and a fool; fight, or by these hilts I’ll kill thee. –Brave my lord! You’ll fight?

GRIMALDI.

Provoke me not, for if thou dost–

VASQUES.

Have at you!

They fight, Grimaldi hath the worst.

FLORIO.

20What mean these sudden broils so near my doors?

23Must I be haunted still with such unrest
24As not to eat or sleep in peace at home?

DONADO.

FLORIO.

SORANZO.

29That, with your patience, signors, I’ll resolve:
30This gentleman, whom fame reports a soldier,
31(for else I know not) rivals me in love
32To Signor Florio’s daughter, to whose ears
33He still prefers his suit, to my disgrace,
34Thinking the way to recommend himself

37My equal in thy blood, yet this bewrays

39Thou wouldst as much disdain as I do thee

41I will’d my servant to correct his tongue,

VASQUES.

GRIMALDI.

I’ll be reveng’d, Soranzo.

VASQUES.

On a dish of warm broth to stay your stomach –do, honest innocence, do; spoon-meat is a wholesomer diet than a Spanish blade.

GRIMALDI.

Remember this!

SORANZO.

I fear thee not, Grimaldi.

Exit Grimaldi.

FLORIO.

52My Lord Soranzo, this is strange to me,

54Owing her heart, what need you doubt her ear?
55Losers may talk by law of any game.

VASQUES.

FLORIO.

59Be you more silent.

62Vasques, put up, let’s end this fray in wine.

Exeunt [Florio, Donado, Soranzo and Vasques].

PUTANA.

How like you this, child? Here’s threat’ning, challenging, quarrelling, and fighting, on every side, and all is for your sake; you had need look to yourself, charge, you’ll be stol’n away sleeping else shortly.

ANNABELLA.

67But tut’ress, such a life gives no content
68To me, my thoughts are fix’d on other ends;
69Would you would leave me.

PUTANA.

Leave you? No marvel else. Leave me no leaving, charge; this is love outright. Indeed I blame you not, you have choice fit for the best lady in Italy.

ANNABELLA.

Pray do not talk so much.

PUTANA.

Take the worst with the best, there’s Grimaldi the soldier, a very well-timber’d fellow: they say he is a Roman, nephew to the Duke Montferrato, they say he did good service in the wars against the Milanese, but ’faith, charge, I do not like him, an’t be for nothing but for being a soldier; not one amongst twenty of your skirmishing captains but have some privy maim or other that mars their standing upright. I like him the worse, he crinkles so much in the hams; though he might serve if there were no more men, yet he’s not the man I would choose.

ANNABELLA.

Fie, how thou prat’st.

PUTANA.

As I am a very woman, I like Signor Soranzo well; he is wise, and what is more, rich; and what is more than that, kind, and what is more than all this, a nobleman; such a one, were I the fair Annabella myself, I would wish and pray for. Then he is bountiful; besides, he is handsome, and by my troth, I think wholesome (and that’s news in a gallant of three and twenty); liberal, that I know; loving, that you know; and a man sure, else he could never ha’ purchas’d such a good name with Hippolita, the lusty widow, in her husband’s lifetime: and ’twere but for that report, sweet- heart, would ’a were thine. Commend a man for his qualities, but take a husband as he is a plain-sufficient, naked man: such a one is for your bed, and such a one is Signor Soranzo, my life for’t.

ANNABELLA.

Sure the woman took her morning’s draught too soon.

Enter Bergetto and Poggio.

PUTANA.

But look, sweetheart, look what thing comes now: here’s another of your ciphers to fill up the number. O brave old ape in a silken coat! Observe.

BERGETTO.

Didst thou think, Poggio, that I would spoil my new clothes, and leave my dinner, to fight?

POGGIO.

No, sir, I did not take you for so arrant a baby.

BERGETTO.

I am wiser than so: for I hope, Poggio, thou never heardst of an elder brother that was a coxcomb. Didst, Poggio?

POGGIO.

Never indeed, sir, as long as they had either land or money left them to inherit.

BERGETTO.

Is it possible, Poggio? O monstrous! Why, I’ll undertake with a handful of silver to buy a headful of wit at any time; but sirrah, I have another purchase in hand, I shall have the wench, mine uncle says. I will but wash my face, and shift socks, and then have at her i’faith! Mark my pace, Poggio.

[Walks affectedly.]

POGGIO.

I have seen an ass and a mule trot the Spanish pavin with a better grace, I know not how often.

Exeunt [Bergetto and Poggio].

ANNABELLA.

This idiot haunts me too.

PUTANA.

Ay, ay, he needs no description; the rich magnifico that is below with your father, charge, Signor Donado his uncle, for that he means to make this his cousin a golden calf, thinks that you will be a right Israelite and fall down to him presently: but I hope I have tutor’d you better. Enter Florio and Donado.

FLORIO.

1Signor Donado, you have said enough,
2I understand you; but would have you know
3I will not force my daughter ‘gainst her will.
4You see I have but two, a son and her;
5And he is so devoted to his book,
6As I must tell you true, I doubt his health:
7Should he miscarry, all my hopes rely
8Upon my girl; as for worldly fortune,
9I am, I thank my stars, blest with enough.
10My care is how to match her to her liking:
11I would not have her marry wealth, but love,
12And if she like your nephew, let him have her,
13Here’s all that I can say.

DONADO.

Here’s all that I can say. Sir, you say well,
14Like a true father, and for my part, I,
15If the young folks can like (’twixt you and me),
16Will promise to assure my nephew presently
17Three thousand florins yearly during life,
18And after I am dead, my whole estate.

FLORIO.

19’Tis a fair proffer, sir; meantime your nephew
20Shall have free passage to commence his suit:

GIOVANNI.

1Come, Annabella: no more sister now,
2But love, a name more gracious; do not blush,
3Beauty’s sweet wonder, but be proud to know
4That yielding thou hast conquer’d, and inflam’d
5A heart whose tribute is thy brother’s life.

ANNABELLA.

6And mine is his. O, how these stol’n contents
7Would print a modest crimson on my cheeks,
8Had any but my heart’s delight prevail’d!

GIOVANNI.

9I marvel why the chaster of your sex
10Should think this pretty toy call’d maidenhead
11So strange a loss, when, being lost, ’tis nothing,
12And you are still the same.

ANNABELLA.

And you are still the same. ’Tis well for you;
13Now you can talk.

GIOVANNI.

14In th’ ear, as in the playing.

ANNABELLA.

91So noble in his quality, condition,
92Learning, behaviour, entertainment, love,

[II.v]

Enter Friar and Giovanni.

FRIAR.

1Peace! Thou hast told a tale, whose every word
2Threatens eternal slaughter to the soul.
3I’m sorry I have heard it; would mine ears
4Had been one minute deaf, before the hour
5That thou cam’st to me. O young man castaway,
6By the religious number of mine order,
7I day and night have wak’d my aged eyes,
8Above my strength, to weep on thy behalf:
9But Heaven is angry, and be thou resolv’d,
10Thou art a man remark’d to taste a mischief:
11Look for’t; though it come late, it will come sure.

GIOVANNI.

12Father, in this you are uncharitable;
13What I have done, I’ll prove both fit and good.
14It is a principle, which you have taught
15When I was yet your scholar, that the frame
16And composition of the mind doth follow
17The frame and composition of the body:
18So where the body’s furniture is beauty,
19The mind’s must needs be virtue; which allowed,
20Virtue itself is reason but refin’d,
21And love the quintessence of that. This proves
22My sister’s beauty being rarely fair
23Is rarely virtuous; chiefly in her love,
24And chiefly in that love, her love to me.
25If hers to me, then so is mine to her;
26Since in like causes are effects alike.

FRIAR.

27O ignorance in knowledge! Long ago,
28How often have I warn’d thee this before?
29Indeed, if we were sure there were no deity,
30Nor Heaven nor hell, then to be led alone
31By nature’s light, as were philosophers
32Of elders times, might instance some defense.
33But ’tis not so; then, madmam, thou wilt find
34That nature is in Heaven’s positions blind.

GIOVANNI.

35Your age o’errules you; had you youth like mine,
36You’d make her love your Heaven, and her divine.

FRIAR.

37Nay then, I see thou’rt too far sold to hell,
38It lies not in the compass of my prayers
39To call thee back; yet let me counsel thee:
40Persuade thy sister to some marriage.

GIOVANNI.

41Marriage? Why, that’s to damn her! That’s to prove
42Her greedy of variety of lust.

FRIAR.

43O fearful! If thou wilt not, give me leave
44To shrive her, lest she should die unabsolv’d.

GIOVANNI.

45At your best leisure, father: then she’ll tell you
46How dearly she doth prize my matchless love.
47Then you will know what pity ’twere we two
48Should have been sunder’d from each other’s arms.
49View well her face, and in that little round
50You may observe a world of variety:
51For color, lips: for sweet perfumes, her breath;
52For jewels, eyes; for threads of purest gold,
53Hair; for delicious choice of flowers, cheeks;
54Wonder in every portion of that throne:
55Hear her but speak, and you will swear the spheres
56Make music to the citizens in Heaven.
57But, father, what is else for pleasure fram’d,
58Lest I offend your ears, shall go unnam’d.

FRIAR.

59The more I hear, I pity thee the more,
60That one so excellent should give those parts
61All to a second death; what I can do
62Is but to pray: and yet I could advise thee,
63Wouldst thou be rul’d.

GIOVANNI.

Wouldst thou be rul’d. In what?

FRIAR.

Wouldst thou be rul’d. In what? Why, leave her yet;
64The throne of mercy is above your trespass,
65Yet time is left you both –

GIOVANNI.

Yet time is left you both – To embrace each other,
66Else let all time be struck quite out of number.
67She is like me, and I like her, resolv’d.

FRIAR.

68No more! I’ll visit her; this grieves me most,
69Things being thus, a pair of souls are lost.

Exeunt.

9The heyday of your luxury, be fed
10Up to a surfeit, and could none but I

38Who had not been but human, as was I,

50Tell me his name.

SORANZO.

So, this was somewhat to the matter; what do you think of your heaven of happiness now, sir?

SORANZO.

149I carry hell about me: all my blood
150Is fir’d in swift revenge.

156I’ll make her tell herself, or–

162Delay in vengeance gives a heavier blow.

Me? Why, he makes a dog of me. But if some were of my mind, I know what we would do; as sure as I am an honest man, he will go near to kill my lady with unkindness. Say she be with child, is that such a matter for a young woman of her years to be blam’d for?

Alas, good heart, it is against her will full sore.

VASQUES.

PUTANA.

Do you think so?

VASQUES.

Foh, I know’t; provided that he did not win her to’t by force. He was once in a mind that you could tell, and meant to have wrung it out of you, but I somewhat pacified him for that; yet sure you know a great deal.

PUTANA.

Heaven forgive us all! I know a little, Vasques.

VASQUES.

PUTANA.

Not for all the world, by my faith and troth, Vasques.

VASQUES.

PUTANA.

Dost think so, Vasques?

VASQUES.

Nay, I know’t; sure ’twas some near and entire friend.

PUTANA.

’Twas a dear friend indeed; but–

VASQUES.

But what? Fear not to name him; my life between you and danger. Faith, I think ’twas no base fellow.

PUTANA.

Thou wilt stand between me and harm?

VASQUES.

’Ud’s pity, what else? You shall be rewarded too, trust me.

PUTANA.

’Twas even no worse than her own brother.

VASQUES.

Her brother Giovanni, I warrant ’ee!

PUTANA.

Even he, Vasques; as brave a gentleman as ever kiss’d fair lady. O, they love most perpetually!

VASQUES.

A brave gentleman indeed; why, therein I commend her choice. –Better and better! –You are sure ’twas he?

PUTANA.

Sure; and you shall see he will not be long from her too.

VASQUES.

He were to blame if he would: but may I believe thee?

PUTANA.

Believe me! Why, dost think I am a Turk or a Jew? No, Vasques, I have known their dealings too long to belie them now.

VASQUES.

Where are you there? Within, sirs!

[V.iii]

Enter Giovanni.

GIOVANNI.

1Busy opinion is an idle fool,
2That as a school-rod keeps a child in awe,
3Frights the unexperienc’d temper of the mind:
4So did it me; who, ere my precious sister
5Was married, thought all taste of love would die
6In such a contract; but I find no change
7Of pleasure in this formal law of sports.
8She is still one to me, and every kiss
9As sweet and as delicious as the first
10I reap’d, when yet the privilege of youth
11Entitled her a virgin. O the glory
12Of two united hearts like hers and mine!
13Let poring book-men dream of other worlds,
14My world, and all of happiness, is here,
15And I’d not change it for the best to come:
16A life of pleasure is Elysium.

17Father, you enter on the jubilee

19The hell you oft have prompted is nought else
20But slavish and fond superstitious fear;

FRIAR.

28Some petty devil factor ’twixt my love
29And your religion-masked sorceries.

FRIAR.

31Else thou wouldst stoop to warning.

GIOVANNI.

37The devil we are; which way is’t possible?
38Are we grown traitors to our own delights?
39Confusion take such dotage, ’tis but forg’d;
40This is your peevish chattering, weak old man.

60To show a matchless beauty to the world,

62The jealous Destinies requir’d again.

67Ye blessed angels, guard me!

70The laws of conscience and of civil use

72Our loves, that love will wipe away that rigor
73Which would in other incests be abhorr’d.
74Give me your hand; how sweetly life doth run
75In these well-colored veins! How constantly
76These palms do promise health! But I could chide
77With nature for this cunning flattery.
78Kiss me again–forgive me.

ANNABELLA.

GIOVANNI.

79Farewell.

ANNABELLA.

GIOVANNI.

81May not behold a deed will turn their splendor
82More sooty than the poets feign their Styx!
83One other kiss, my sister.

ANNABELLA.

One other kiss, my sister. What means this?

GIOVANNI.

84To save thy fame, and kill thee in a kiss.

86Revenge is mine; honor doth love command.

95That in her womb receiv’d its life from me
96Hath had from me a cradle and a grave.

98In all her best, bore her alive and dead.
99Soranzo, thou hast miss’d thy aim in this;
100I have prevented now thy reaching plots,
101And kill’d a love, for whose each drop of blood
102I would have pawn’d my heart. Fair Annabella,
103How over-glorious art thou in thy wounds,
104Triumphing over infamy and hate!
105Shrink not, courageous hand, stand up, my heart,
106And boldly act my last and greater part.

GIOVANNI.

FLORIO.

41Since I first thoroughly view’d and truly lov’d
42Your daughter and my sister.

FLORIO.

43My lords, he’s a frantic madman!

GIOVANNI.

My lords, he’s a frantic madman! Father, no.
44For nine month’s space in secret I enjoy’d
45Sweet Annabella’s sheets; nine months I liv’d
46A happy monarch of her heart and her.
47Soranzo, thou know’st this; thy paler cheek
48Bears the confounding print of thy disgrace,
49For her too fruitful womb too soon bewray’d
50The happy passage of our stol’n delights,
51And made her mother to a child unborn.

CARDINAL.

52Incestuous villain!

FLORIO.

GIOVANNI.

SORANZO.

VASQUES.

BANDITTI.

91To see my wrongs reveng’d on that black devil.
92O Vasques, to thy bosom let me give
93My last of breath; let not that lecher live–O!–

Dies.

VASQUES.

The reward of peace and rest be with him, my ever dearest lord and master.

GIOVANNI.

Whose hand gave me this wound?

VASQUES.

Mine, sir, I was your first man; have you enough?

GIOVANNI.

98I thank thee; thou hast done for me but what
99I would have else done on myself. Art sure
100Thy lord is dead?

VASQUES.

O impudent slave! As sure as I am sure to see thee die.

CARDINAL.

102Think on thy life and end, and call for mercy.

GIOVANNI.

103Mercy? Why, I have found it in this justice.

CARDINAL.

104Strive yet to cry to Heaven.

GIOVANNI.

Strive yet to cry to Heaven. O, I bleed fast.
105Death, thou art a guest long look’d for; I embrace
106Thee and thy wounds; O, my last minute comes!
107Where’er I go, let me enjoy this grace,
108Freely to view my Annabella’s face.

Dies.

DONADO.

Strange miracle of justice!

CARDINAL.

110Raise up the city; we shall be murdered all!

VASQUES.

You need not fear, you shall not; this strange task being ended, I have paid the duty to the son which I have vowed to the father.

CARDINAL.

114Speak, wretched villain, what incarnate fiend
115Hath led thee on to this?

VASQUES.

Honesty, and pity of my master’s wrongs; for know, my lord, I am by birth a Spaniard, brought forth my country in my youth by Lord Soranzo’s father, whom whilst he liv’d I serv’d faithfully; since whose death I have been to this man as I was to him. What I have done was duty, and I repent nothing but that the loss of my life had not ransom’d his.

CARDINAL.

123Say, fellow, know’st thou any yet unnam’d
124Of counsel in this incest?

VASQUES.

Yes, and old woman, sometimes guardian to his murdered lady.

CARDINAL.

And what’s become of her?

VASQUES.

Within this room she is; whose eyes, after her confession, I caus’d to be put out, but kept alive, to confirm what from Giovanni’s own mouth you have heard. Now, my lord, what I have done you may judge of, and let your own wisdom be a judge in your own reason.

CARDINAL.

133Peace! First this woman, chief in these effects:
134My sentence is, that forthwith she be ta’en
135Out of the city, for example’s sake,
136There to be burnt to ashes.

DONADO.

There to be burnt to ashes. ’Tis most just.

CARDINAL.

137Be it your charge, Donado, see it done.

DONADO.

138I shall.

VASQUES.

What for me? If death, ’tis welcome; I have been honest to the son as I was to the father.

CARDINAL.

141Fellow, for thee: since what thou didst was done
142Not for thyself, being no Italian,
143We banish thee forever, to depart
144Within three days; in this we do dispense
145With grounds of reason, not of thine offense.

VASQUES.

’Tis well; this conquest is mine, and I rejoice that a Spaniard outwent an Italian in revenge.

Exit Vasques.

CARDINAL.

148Take up these slaughtered bodies, see them buried;
149And all the gold and jewels, or whatsoever
150Confiscate by the canons of the church,
151We seize upon to the Pope’s proper use.

RICHARDETTO.

152[discovers himself]. Your grace’s pardon! Thus long I liv’d disguis’d
153To see the effect of pride and lust at once
154Brought both to shameful ends.

CARDINAL.

155What, Richardetto whom we thought for dead?

DONADO.

156Sir, was it you–

RICHARDETTO.

Sir, was it you– Your friend.

CARDINAL.

Sir, was it you– Your friend. We shall have time
157To talk at large of all; but never yet
158Incest and murder have so strangely met.
159Of one so young, so rich in nature’s store,
160Who could not say, ’tis pity she’s a whore?

Exeunt.

FINIS
