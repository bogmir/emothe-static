Tis Pity She's a Whore

[V.i]

Enter Annabella above.

ANNABELLA.

1Pleasures, farewell, and all ye thriftless minutes
2Wherein false joys have spun a weary life!
3To these my fortunes now I take my leave.
4Thou, precious Time, that swiftly rid’st in post
5Over the world, to finish up the race
6Of my last fate, here stay thy restless course,
7And bear to ages that are yet unborn
8A wretched, woeful woman’s tragedy.
9My conscience now stands up against my lust
10With depositions character’d in guilt,

Enter Friar [below].

11And tells me I am lost: now I confess
12Beauty that clothes the outside of the face
13Is cursed if it be not cloth’d with grace.
14Here like a turtle mew’d up in a cage,
15Unmated, I converse with air and walls,
16And descant on my vile unhappiness.
17O Giovanni, that hast had the spoil
18Of thine own virtues and my modest fame,
19Would thou hadst been less subject to those stars
20That luckless reign’d at my nativity:
21O would the scourge due to my black offense
22Might pass from thee, that I alone might feel
23The torment of an uncontrolled flame!

FRIAR.

24What’s this I hear?aparte

ANNABELLA.

What’s this I hear? That man, that blessed friar,
25Who join’d in ceremonial knot my hand
26To him whose wife I now am, told me oft
27I trod the path to death, and showed me how.
28But they who sleep in lethargies of lust
29Hug their confusion, making Heaven unjust,
30And so did I.

FRIAR.

And so did I. Here’s music to the soul!aparte

ANNABELLA.

31Forgive me, my good genius, and this once
32Be helpful to my ends; let some good man
33Pass this way, to whose trust I may commit
34This paper double-lin’d with tears and blood:
35Which being granted, here I sadly vow
36Repentance, and a leaving of that life
37I long have died in.

FRIAR.

I long have died in. Lady, Heaven hath heard you,
38And hath by providence ordain’d that I
39Should be his minister for your behoof.

ANNABELLA.

40Ha, what are you?

FRIAR.

Ha, what are you? Your brother’s friend, the friar;
41Glad in my soul that I have liv’d to hear
42This free confession ’twixt your peace and you.
43What would you, or to whom? Fear not to speak.

ANNABELLA.

44Is Heaven so bountiful? Then I have found
45More favour than I hop’d. Here, holy man–

Throws a letter.

46Commend me to my brother; give him that,
47That letter; bid him read it and repent.
48Tell him that I, imprison’d in my chamber,
49Barr’d of all company, even of my guardian,
50Who gives me cause of much suspect, have time
51To blush at what hath pass’d; bid him be wise,
52And not believe the friendship of my lord.
53I fear much more than I can speak: good father,
54The place is dangerous, and spies are busy;
55I must break off–you’ll do’t?

FRIAR.

I must break off–you’ll do’t? Be sure I will;
56And fly with speed–my blessing ever rest
57With thee, my daughter: live, to die more blessed!

Exit Friar.

ANNABELLA.

58Thanks to the Heavens, who have prolong’d my breath
59To this good use: now I can welcome death.

Exit.

[V.ii]

Enter Soranzo and Vasques.

VASQUES.

Am I to be believ’d now? First marry a strumpet that cast herself away upon you but to laugh at your horns, to feast on your disgrace, riot in your vexations, cuckold you in your bride-bed, waste your estate upon panders and bawds!

SORANZO.

No more, I say, no more!

VASQUES.

A cuckold is a goodly tame beast, my lord.

SORANZO.

7I am resolv’d; urge not another word.
8My thoughts are great, and all as resolute
9As thunder; in mean time I’ll cause our lady
10To deck herself in all her bridal robes,
11Kiss her, and fold her gently in my arms.
12Begone–yet hear you, are the banditti ready
13To wait in ambush?

VASQUES.

Good sir, trouble not yourself about other business than your own resolution; remember that time lost cannot be recall’d.

SORANZO.

17With all the cunning words thou canst, invite
18The states of Parma to my birthday’s feast;
19Haste to my brother-rival and his father,
20Entreat them gently, bid them not to fail.
21Be speedy, and return.

VASQUES.

Let not your pity betray you till my coming back; think upon incest and cuckoldry.

SORANZO.

24Revenge is all the ambition I aspire:
25To that I’ll climb or fall; my blood’s on fire.

Exeunt.

[V.iii]

Enter Giovanni.

GIOVANNI.

1Busy opinion is an idle fool,
2That as a school-rod keeps a child in awe,
3Frights the unexperienc’d temper of the mind:
4So did it me; who, ere my precious sister
5Was married, thought all taste of love would die
6In such a contract; but I find no change
7Of pleasure in this formal law of sports.
8She is still one to me, and every kiss
9As sweet and as delicious as the first
10I reap’d, when yet the privilege of youth
11Entitled her a virgin. O the glory
12Of two united hearts like hers and mine!
13Let poring book-men dream of other worlds,
14My world, and all of happiness, is here,
15And I’d not change it for the best to come:
16A life of pleasure is Elysium.

Enter Friar.

17Father, you enter on the jubilee
18Of my retir’d delights; now I can tell you,
19The hell you oft have prompted is nought else
20But slavish and fond superstitious fear;
21And I could prove it too–

FRIAR.

And I could prove it too– Thy blindness slays thee.
22Look there, ’tis writ to thee.

Gives the letter.

GIOVANNI.

23From whom?

FRIAR.

24Unrip the seals and see;
25The blood’s yet seething hot, that will anon
26Be frozen harder than congeal’d coral.
27Why d’ee change color, son?

GIOVANNI.

Why d’ee change color, son? ’Fore Heaven, you make
28Some petty devil factor ’twixt my love
29And your religion-masked sorceries.
30Where had you this?

FRIAR.

Where had you this? Thy conscience, youth, is sear’d,
31Else thou wouldst stoop to warning.

GIOVANNI.

Else thou wouldst stoop to warning. ’Tis her hand,
32I know’t; and ’tis all written in her blood.
33She writes I know not what. Death? I’ll not fear
34An armed thunderbolt aim’d at my heart.
35She writes, we are discovered –pox on dreams
36Of low faint-hearted cowardice! Discovered?
37The devil we are; which way is’t possible?
38Are we grown traitors to our own delights?
39Confusion take such dotage, ’tis but forg’d;
40This is your peevish chattering, weak old man.

Enter Vasques.

Now, sir, what news bring you?

VASQUES.

My lord, according to his yearly custom keeping this day a feast in honor of his birthday, by me invites you thither. Your worthy father, with the Pope’s reverend nuncio, and other magnificoes of Parma, have promis’d their presence; will’t please you to be of the number?

GIOVANNI.

Yes, tell him I dare come.

VASQUES.

“Dare come”?

GIOVANNI.

49So I said; and tell him more, I will come.

VASQUES.

These words are strange to me.

GIOVANNI.

Say I will come.

VASQUES.

You will not miss?

GIOVANNI.

Yet more? I’ll come! Sir, are you answer’d?

VASQUES.

So I’ll say. –My service to you.

Exit Vasques.

FRIAR.

55You will not go, I trust.

GIOVANNI.

You will not go, I trust. Not go! For what?

FRIAR.

56O, do not go! This feast, I’ll gage my life,
57Is but a plot to train you to your ruin;
58Be rul’d, you sha’ not go.

GIOVANNI.

Be rul’d, you sha’ not go. Not go? Stood Death
59Threat’ning his armies of confounding plagues,
60With hosts of dangers hot as blazing stars,
61I would be there. Not go? Yes, and resolve
62To strike as deep in slaughter as they all.
63For I will go.

FRIAR.

For I will go. Go where thou wilt; I see
64The wildness of thy fate draws to an end,
65To a bad fearful end. I must not stay
66To know thy fall; back to Bononia I
67With speed will haste, and shun this coming blow.
68Parma, farewell; would I had never known thee,
69Or aught of thine! Well, young man, since no prayer
70Can make thee safe, I leave thee to despair.

Exit Friar.

GIOVANNI.

71Despair, or tortures of a thousand hells,
72All’s one to me; I have set up my rest.
73Now, now, work serious thoughts on baneful plots,
74Be all a man, my soul; let not the curse
75Of old prescription rend from me the gall
76Of courage, which enrols a glorious death.
77If I must totter like a well-grown oak,
78Some under-shrubs shall in my weighty fall
79Be crush’d to splits: with me they all shall perish.

Exit.

[V.iv]

Enter Soranzo, Vasques, and Banditti.

SORANZO.

1You will not fail, or shrink in the attempt?

VASQUES.

I will undertake for their parts. Be sure, my masters, to be bloody enough, and as unmerciful as if you were preying upon a rich booty on the very mountains of Liguria; for your pardons, trust to my lord, but for reward you shall trust none but your own pockets.

BANDITTI OMNES.

We’ll make a murder.

SORANZO.

8Here’s gold, here’s more; want nothing; what you do
9Is noble, and an act of brave revenge.
10I’ll make ye rich banditti, and all free.

OMNES.

Liberty, liberty!

VASQUES.

Hold, take every man a vizard; when ye are withdrawn, keep as much silence as you can possibly. You know the watchword; till which be spoken, move not, but when you hear that, rush in like a stormy flood; I need not instruct ye in your own profession.

OMNES.

No, no, no.

VASQUES.

In, then: your ends are profit and preferment. –Away!

Exeunt Banditti.

SORANZO.

The guests will all come, Vasques?

VASQUES.

Yes, sir. And now let me a little edge your resolution. You see nothing is unready to this great work, but a great mind in you: call to your remembrance your disgraces, your loss of honor, Hippolita’s blood, and arm your courage in your own wrongs; so shall you best right those wrongs in vengeance, which you may truly call your own.

SORANZO.

26’Tis well; the less I speak, the more I burn,
27And blood shall quench that flame.

VASQUES.

Now you begin to turn Italian. This beside–when my young incest-monger comes, he will be sharp set on his old bit: give him time enough, let him have your chamber and bed at liberty; let my hot hare have law ere he be hunted to his death, that if it be possible, he may post to hell in the very act of his damnation.

Enter Giovanni.

SORANZO.

34It shall be so; and see, as we would wish,
35He comes himself first. Welcome, my much-lov’d brother!
36Now I perceive you honor me; y’are welcome.
37But where’s my father?

GIOVANNI.

But where’s my father? With the other states,
38Attending on the nuncio of the Pope,
39To wait upon him hither. How’s my sister?

SORANZO.

40Like a good housewife, scarcely ready yet;
41Y’are best walk to her chamber.

GIOVANNI.

Y’are best walk to her chamber. If you will.

SORANZO.

42I must expect my honourable friends;
43Good brother, get her forth.

GIOVANNI.

Good brother, get her forth. You are busy, sir.

Exit Giovanni.

VASQUES.

Even as the great devil himself would have it; let him go and glut himself in his own destruction. Flourish. Hark, the nuncio is at hand; good sir, be ready to receive him.

Enter Cardinal, Florio, Donado, Richardetto, and Attendants.

SORANZO.

48Most reverend lord, this grace hath made me proud,
49That you vouchsafe my house; I ever rest
50Your humble servant for this noble favour.

CARDINAL.

51You are our friend, my lord; his Holiness
52Shall understand how zealously you honor
53Saint Peter’s vicar in his substitute.
54Our special love to you.

SORANZO.

Our special love to you. Signors, to you
55My welcome, and my ever best of thanks
56For this so memorable courtesy.
57Pleaseth your grace to walk near?

CARDINAL.

Pleaseth your grace to walk near? My lord, we come
58To celebrate your feast with civil mirth,
59As ancient custom teacheth: we will go.

SORANZO.

60Attend his grace there! Signors, keep your way.

Exeunt.

[V.v]

Enter Giovanni and Annabella lying on a bed.

GIOVANNI.

1What, chang’d so soon? Hath your new sprightly lord
2Found out a trick in night-games more than we
3Could know in our simplicity? Ha! Is’t so?
4Or does the fit come on you, to prove treacherous
5To your past vows and oaths?

ANNABELLA.

To your past vows and oaths? Why should you jest
6At my calamity, without all sense
7Of the approaching dangers you are in?

GIOVANNI.

8What danger’s half so great as thy revolt?
9Thou art a faithless sister, else thou know’st
10Malice, or any treachery beside,
11Would stoop to my bent brows; why, I hold fate
12Clasp’d in my fist, and could command the course
13Of time’s eternal motion, hadst thou been
14One thought more steady than an ebbing sea.
15And what? You’ll now be honest, that’s resolv’d?

ANNABELLA.

16Brother, dear brother, know what I have been,
17And know that now there’s but a dining-time
18’Twixt us and our confusion: let’s not waste
19These precious hours in vain and useless speech.
20Alas, these gay attires were not put on
21But to some end; this sudden solemn feast
22Was not ordain’d to riot in expense;
23I, that have now been chamber’d here alone,
24Barr’d of my guardian, or of any else,
25Am not for nothing at an instant freed
26To fresh access. Be not deceiv’d, my brother:
27This banquet is an harbinger of death
28To you and me; resolve yourself it is,
29And be prepar’d to welcome it.

GIOVANNI.

And be prepar’d to welcome it. Well, then;
30The schoolmen teach that all this globe of earth
31Shall be consum’d to ashes in a minute.

ANNABELLA.

32So I have read too.

GIOVANNI.

So I have read too. But ’twere somewhat strange
33To see the water burn: could I believe
34This might be true, I could believe as well
35There might be hell or Heaven.

ANNABELLA.

There might be hell or Heaven. That’s most certain.

GIOVANNI.

36A dream, a dream! Else in this other world
37We should know one another.

ANNABELLA.

We should know one another. So we shall.

GIOVANNI.

38Have you heard so?

ANNABELLA.

Have you heard so? For certain.

GIOVANNI.

Have you heard so? For certain. But d’ee think
39That I shall see you there? –You look on me?
40May we kiss one another, prate or laugh,
41Or do as we do here?

ANNABELLA.

Or do as we do here? I know not that.
42But good, for the present, what d’ee mean
43To free yourself from danger? Some way think
44How to escape; I’m sure the guests are come.

GIOVANNI.

45Look up, look here; what see you in my face?

ANNABELLA.

46Distraction and a troubled countenance.

GIOVANNI.

47Death, and a swift repining wrath–yet look,
48What see you in mine eyes?

ANNABELLA.

What see you in mine eyes? Methinks you weep.

GIOVANNI.

49I do indeed; these are the funeral tears
50Shed on your grave; these furrowed up my cheeks
51When first I lov’d and knew not how to woo.
52Fair Annabella, should I here repeat
53The story of my life, we might lose time.
54Be record all the spirits of the air,
55And all things else that are, that day and night,
56Early and late, the tribute which my heart
57Hath paid to Annabella’s sacred love
58Hath been these tears, which are her mourners now.
59Never till now did Nature do her best
60To show a matchless beauty to the world,
61Which in an instant, ere it scarce was seen,
62The jealous Destinies requir’d again.
63Pray, Annabella, pray; since we must part,
64Go thou, white in thy soul, to fill a throne
65Of innocence and sanctity in Heaven.
66Pray, pray, my sister!

ANNABELLA.

Pray, pray, my sister! Then I see your drift–
67Ye blessed angels, guard me!

GIOVANNI.

Ye blessed angels, guard me! So say I.
68Kiss me; if ever after-times should hear
69Of our fast-knit affections, though perhaps
70The laws of conscience and of civil use
71May justly blame us, yet when they but know
72Our loves, that love will wipe away that rigor
73Which would in other incests be abhorr’d.
74Give me your hand; how sweetly life doth run
75In these well-colored veins! How constantly
76These palms do promise health! But I could chide
77With nature for this cunning flattery.
78Kiss me again–forgive me.

ANNABELLA.

Kiss me again–forgive me. With my heart.

GIOVANNI.

79Farewell.

ANNABELLA.

Farewell. Will you be gone?

GIOVANNI.

Farewell. Will you be gone? Be dark, bright sun,
80And make this midday night, that thy gilt rays
81May not behold a deed will turn their splendor
82More sooty than the poets feign their Styx!
83One other kiss, my sister.

ANNABELLA.

One other kiss, my sister. What means this?

GIOVANNI.

84To save thy fame, and kill thee in a kiss.
85Thus die, and die by me, and by my hand!
86Revenge is mine; honor doth love command.

Stabs her.

ANNABELLA.

87O brother, by your hand?

GIOVANNI.

O brother, by your hand? When thou art dead
88I’ll give my reasons for’t; for to dispute
89With thy (even in thy death) most lovely beauty,
90Would make me stagger to perform this act,
91Which I most glory in.

ANNABELLA.

92Forgive him, Heaven–and me my sins; farewell.
93Brother unkind, unkind!–Mercy, great Heaven–O!–O!–

Dies.

GIOVANNI.

94She’s dead, alas, good soul! The hapless fruit
95That in her womb receiv’d its life from me
96Hath had from me a cradle and a grave.
97I must not dally. This sad marriage-bed,
98In all her best, bore her alive and dead.
99Soranzo, thou hast miss’d thy aim in this;
100I have prevented now thy reaching plots,
101And kill’d a love, for whose each drop of blood
102I would have pawn’d my heart. Fair Annabella,
103How over-glorious art thou in thy wounds,
104Triumphing over infamy and hate!
105Shrink not, courageous hand, stand up, my heart,
106And boldly act my last and greater part.

Exit with the body.

[V. vi]

A Banquet. Enter Cardinal, Florio, Donado, Soranzo, Richardetto, Vasques, and Attendants; they take their places.

VASQUES.

Remember, sir, what you have to do, be wise and resolute.

SORANZO.

2Enough –my heart is fix’d.–Pleaseth your grace
3To taste these coarse confections; though the use
4Of such set entertainments more consists
5In custom than in cause, yet, reverend sir,
6I am still made your servant by your presence.

CARDINAL.

7And we your friend.

SORANZO.

8But where’s my brother Giovanni?

Enter Giovanni with a heart upon his dagger.

GIOVANNI.

9Here, here, Soranzo; trimm’d in reeking blood,
10That triumphs over death; proud in the spoil
11Of love and vengeance! Fate or all the powers
12That guide the motions of immortal souls
13Could not prevent me.

CARDINAL.

What means this?

FLORIO.

Son Giovanni!

SORANZO.

Shall I be forestall’d?

GIOVANNI.

17Be not amaz’d; if your misgiving hearts
18Shrink at an idle sight, what bloodless fear
19Of coward passion would have seiz’d your senses,
20Had you beheld the rape of life and beauty
21Which I have acted? My sister, O my sister!

FLORIO.

22Ha! What of her?

GIOVANNI.

Ha! What of her? The glory of my deed
23Darkn’d the midday sun, made noon as night.
24You came to feast, my lords, with dainty fare;
25I came to feast too; but I digg’d for food
26In a much richer mine than gold or stone
27Of any value balanc’d; ’tis a heart,
28A heart, my lords, in which is mine entomb’d:
29Look well upon’t; d’ee know’t?

VASQUES.

What strange riddle’s this?

GIOVANNI.

31’Tis Annabella’s heart, ’tis; why d’ee startle?
32I vow ’tis hers; this dagger’s point plough’d up
33Her fruitful womb, and left to me the fame
34Of a most glorious executioner.

FLORIO.

35Why, madman, art thyself?

GIOVANNI.

36Yes, father; and that times to come may know
37How as my fate I honor’d my revenge,
38List, father, to your ears I will yield up
39How much I have deserv’d to be your son.

FLORIO.

40What is’t thou say’st?

GIOVANNI.

What is’t thou say’st? Nine moons have had their changes
41Since I first thoroughly view’d and truly lov’d
42Your daughter and my sister.

FLORIO.

Your daughter and my sister. How! –Alas,
43My lords, he’s a frantic madman!

GIOVANNI.

My lords, he’s a frantic madman! Father, no.
44For nine month’s space in secret I enjoy’d
45Sweet Annabella’s sheets; nine months I liv’d
46A happy monarch of her heart and her.
47Soranzo, thou know’st this; thy paler cheek
48Bears the confounding print of thy disgrace,
49For her too fruitful womb too soon bewray’d
50The happy passage of our stol’n delights,
51And made her mother to a child unborn.

CARDINAL.

52Incestuous villain!

FLORIO.

Incestuous villain! O, his rage belies him.

GIOVANNI.

53It does not, ’tis the oracle of truth;
54I vow it is so.

SORANZO.

I vow it is so. I shall burst with fury;
55Bring the strumpet forth!

VASQUES.

56I shall, sir.

Exit Vasques.

GIOVANNI.

I shall, sir. Do, sir! Have you all no faith
57To credit yet my triumphs? Here I swear
58By all that you call sacred, by the love
59I bore my Annabella whilst she liv’d,
60These hands have from her bosom ripp’d this heart.

Entes Vasques.

61Is’t true or no, sir?

VASQUES.

Is’t true or no, sir? ’Tis most strangely true.

FLORIO.

62Cursed man! –Have I liv’d to–

Dies.

CARDINAL.

Cursed man! –Have I liv’d to– Hold up, Florio.
63Monster of children, see what thou hast done,
64Broke thy old father’s heart! Is none of you
65Dares venture on him?

GIOVANNI.

Dares venture on him? Let ‘em! O, my father,
66How well his death becomes him in his griefs!
67Why, this was done with courage; now survives
68None of our house but I, guilt in the blood
69Of a fair sister and a hapless father.

SORANZO.

70Inhuman scorn of men, hast thou a thought
71T’outlive thy murders?

GIOVANNI.

T’outlive thy murders? Yes, I tell thee, yes;
72For in my fists I bear the twists of life.
73Soranzo, see this heart, which was thy wife’s;
74Thus I exchange it royally for thine,

[Stabs him.]

75And thus and thus! Now brave revenge is mine.

VASQUES.

I cannot hold any longer. –You, sir, are you grown insolent in your butcheries? Have at you!

[They] fight.

GIOVANNI.

Come, I am arm’d to meet thee.

VASQUES.

No, will it not be yet? If this will not, another shall. Not yet? I shall fit you anon. –Vengeance!

Enter Banditti [and fight Giovanni].

GIOVANNI.

81Welcome, come more of you whate’er you be,
82I dare your worst–
83O, I can stand no longer! Feeble arms,
84Have you so soon lost strength?

VASQUES.

Now you are welcome, sir! –Away, my masters, all is done, shift for yourselves! Your reward is your own: shift for yourselves.

BANDITTI.

Away, away!

Exeunt Banditti.

VASQUES.

How d’ee, my lord: see you this? How is’t?

SORANZO.

90Dead; but in death well pleased that I have liv’d
91To see my wrongs reveng’d on that black devil.
92O Vasques, to thy bosom let me give
93My last of breath; let not that lecher live–O!–

Dies.

VASQUES.

The reward of peace and rest be with him, my ever dearest lord and master.

GIOVANNI.

Whose hand gave me this wound?

VASQUES.

Mine, sir, I was your first man; have you enough?

GIOVANNI.

98I thank thee; thou hast done for me but what
99I would have else done on myself. Art sure
100Thy lord is dead?

VASQUES.

O impudent slave! As sure as I am sure to see thee die.

CARDINAL.

102Think on thy life and end, and call for mercy.

GIOVANNI.

103Mercy? Why, I have found it in this justice.

CARDINAL.

104Strive yet to cry to Heaven.

GIOVANNI.

Strive yet to cry to Heaven. O, I bleed fast.
105Death, thou art a guest long look’d for; I embrace
106Thee and thy wounds; O, my last minute comes!
107Where’er I go, let me enjoy this grace,
108Freely to view my Annabella’s face.

Dies.

DONADO.

Strange miracle of justice!

CARDINAL.

110Raise up the city; we shall be murdered all!

VASQUES.

You need not fear, you shall not; this strange task being ended, I have paid the duty to the son which I have vowed to the father.

CARDINAL.

114Speak, wretched villain, what incarnate fiend
115Hath led thee on to this?

VASQUES.

Honesty, and pity of my master’s wrongs; for know, my lord, I am by birth a Spaniard, brought forth my country in my youth by Lord Soranzo’s father, whom whilst he liv’d I serv’d faithfully; since whose death I have been to this man as I was to him. What I have done was duty, and I repent nothing but that the loss of my life had not ransom’d his.

CARDINAL.

123Say, fellow, know’st thou any yet unnam’d
124Of counsel in this incest?

VASQUES.

Yes, and old woman, sometimes guardian to his murdered lady.

CARDINAL.

And what’s become of her?

VASQUES.

Within this room she is; whose eyes, after her confession, I caus’d to be put out, but kept alive, to confirm what from Giovanni’s own mouth you have heard. Now, my lord, what I have done you may judge of, and let your own wisdom be a judge in your own reason.

CARDINAL.

133Peace! First this woman, chief in these effects:
134My sentence is, that forthwith she be ta’en
135Out of the city, for example’s sake,
136There to be burnt to ashes.

DONADO.

There to be burnt to ashes. ’Tis most just.

CARDINAL.

137Be it your charge, Donado, see it done.

DONADO.

138I shall.

VASQUES.

What for me? If death, ’tis welcome; I have been honest to the son as I was to the father.

CARDINAL.

141Fellow, for thee: since what thou didst was done
142Not for thyself, being no Italian,
143We banish thee forever, to depart
144Within three days; in this we do dispense
145With grounds of reason, not of thine offense.

VASQUES.

’Tis well; this conquest is mine, and I rejoice that a Spaniard outwent an Italian in revenge.

Exit Vasques.

CARDINAL.

148Take up these slaughtered bodies, see them buried;
149And all the gold and jewels, or whatsoever
150Confiscate by the canons of the church,
151We seize upon to the Pope’s proper use.

RICHARDETTO.

152[discovers himself]. Your grace’s pardon! Thus long I liv’d disguis’d
153To see the effect of pride and lust at once
154Brought both to shameful ends.

CARDINAL.

155What, Richardetto whom we thought for dead?

DONADO.

156Sir, was it you–

RICHARDETTO.

Sir, was it you– Your friend.

CARDINAL.

Sir, was it you– Your friend. We shall have time
157To talk at large of all; but never yet
158Incest and murder have so strangely met.
159Of one so young, so rich in nature’s store,
160Who could not say, ’tis pity she’s a whore?

Exeunt.

FINIS
