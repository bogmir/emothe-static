[III.i]

Enter Bergetto and Poggio.

BERGETTO.

Does my uncle think to make me a baby still? No, Poggio, he shall know I have a sconce now.

POGGIO.

Ay, let him not bob you off like an ape with an apple.

BERGETTO.

’Sfoot, I will have the wench if he were ten uncles, in despite of his nose, Poggio.

POGGIO.

Hold him to the grindstone and give not a jot of ground. She hath in a manner promised you already.

BERGETTO.

True, Poggio, and her uncle the doctor swore I should marry her.

POGGIO.

He swore, I remember.

BERGETTO.

And I will have her, that’s more; didst see the codpiece- point she gave me and the box of marmalade?

POGGIO.

Very well; and kiss’d you, that my chops water’d at the sight on’t. There’s no way but to clap up a marriage in hugger-mugger.

BERGETTO.

I will do’t; for I tell thee, Poggio, I begin to grow valiant methinks, and my courage begins to rise.

POGGIO.

Should you be afraid of your uncle?

BERGETTO.

Hang him, old doting rascal! No, I say I will have her.

POGGIO.

Lose no time then.

BERGETTO.

I will beget a race of wise men and constables, that shall cart whores at their own charges, and break the duke’s peace ere I have done myself. –Come away.

Exeunt.

[III.ii]

SORANZO.

85She hath and she hath not; I’m full of grief,
86But what she said I’ll tell thee as we go.

[III.iii]

Enter Giovanni and Putana.

PUTANA.

O sir, we are all undone, quite undone, utterly undone, and sham’d forever; your sister, O your sister!

GIOVANNI.

What of her? For Heaven’s sake, speak, how does she?

PUTANA.

O that ever I was born to see this day!

GIOVANNI.

She is not dead, ha? Is she?

PUTANA.

Dead? No, she is quick; ’tis worse, she is with child. You know what you have done; Heaven forgive ’ee! ’Tis too late to repent now, Heaven help us.

GIOVANNI.

With child? How dost thou know’t?

PUTANA.

How do I know’t? Am I at these years ignorant what the meanings of qualms and water-pangs be? Of changing of colors, queasiness of stomachs, pukings, and another thing that I could name? Do not, for her and your credit’s sake, spend the time in asking how, and which way, ’tis so; she is quick, upon my word: if you let a physician see her water, y’are undone.

GIOVANNI.

But in what case is she?

PUTANA.

Prettily amended; ’twas but a fit which I soon espied, and she must look for often henceforward.

GIOVANNI.

20Commend me to her, bid her take no care;
21Let not the doctor visit her, I charge you,
22Make some excuse, till I return. –O me!
23I have a world of business in my head.
24Do not discomfort her.–
25How does this news perplex me! –If my father
26Come to her, tell him she’s recover’d well,
27Say ’twas but some ill diet; d’ee hear, woman?
28Look you to’t.

PUTANA.

I will, sir.

Exeunt.

[III.iv]

Enter Florio and Richardetto.

FLORIO.

1And how d’ee find her, sir?

RICHARDETTO.

And how d’ee find her, sir? Indifferent well;
2I see no danger, scarce perceive she’s sick,
3But that she told me, she had lately eaten
4Melons, and, as she thought, those disagreed
5With her young stomach.

FLORIO.

With her young stomach. Did you give her aught?

RICHARDETTO.

6An easy surfeit-water, nothing else.
7You need not doubt her health; I rather think
8Her sickness is a fulness of her blood –
9You understand me?

FLORIO.

You understand me? I do; you counsel well,
10And once, within these few days, will so order’t
11She shall be married ere she know the time.

RICHARDETTO.

12Yet let not haste, sir, make unworthy choice;
13That were dishonor.

FLORIO.

That were dishonor. Master Doctor, no;
14I will not do so neither; in plain words,
15My Lord Soranzo is the man I mean.

RICHARDETTO.

16A noble and a virtuous gentleman.

FLORIO.

17As any is in Parma. Not far hence
18Dwells Father Bonaventure, a grave friar,
19Once tutor to my son; now at his cell
20I’ll have ’em married.

RICHARDETTO.

I’ll have ’em married. You have plotted wisely.

FLORIO.

21I’ll send one straight to speak with him tonight.

RICHARDETTO.

22Soranzo’s wise, he will delay no time.

FLORIO.

23It shall be so.

Enter Friar and Giovanni.

FRIAR.

It shall be so. Good peace be here and love.

FLORIO.

24Welcome, religious friar; you are one
25That still bring blessing to the place you come to.

GIOVANNI.

26Sir, with what speed I could, I did my best
27To draw this holy man from forth his cell
28To visit my sick sister, that with words
29Of ghostly comfort, in this time of need,
30He might absolve her, whether she live or die.

FLORIO.

31’Twas well done, Giovanni; thou herein
32Hast showed a Christian’s care, a brother’s love.
33Come, father, I’ll conduct you to her chamber,
34And one thing would entreat you.

FRIAR.

And one thing would entreat you. Say on, sir.

FLORIO.

35I have a father’s dear impression,
36And wish, before I fall into my grave,
37That I might see her married, as ’tis fit;
38A word from you, grave man, will win her more
39Than all our best persuasions.

FRIAR.

Than all our best persuasions. Gentle sir,
40All this I’ll say, that Heaven may prosper her.

Exeunt.

[III.v]

Enter Grimaldi.

GRIMALDI.

1Now if the doctor keep his word, Soranzo,
2Twenty to one you miss your bride; I know
3’Tis an unnoble act, and not becomes
4A soldier’s valor, but in terms of love,
5Where merit cannot sway, policy must.
6I am resolv’d; if this physician
7Play not on both hands, then Soranzo falls.

Enter Richardetto.

RICHARDETTO.

8You are come as I could wish; this very night
9Soranzo, ’tis ordain’d, must be affied
10To Annabella, and, for aught I know,
11Married.

GRIMALDI.

Married. How!

RICHARDETTO.

Married. How! Yet your patience.
12The place, ’tis Friar Bonaventure’s cell.
13Now I would wish you to bestow this night
14In watching thereabouts; ’tis but a night:
15If you miss now, tomorrow I’ll know all.

GRIMALDI.

16Have you the poison?

RICHARDETTO.

Have you the poison? Here ’tis in this box.
17Doubt nothing, this will do’t; in any case,
18As you respect your life, be quick and sure.

GRIMALDI.

19I’ll speed him.

RICHARDETTO.

I’ll speed him. Do; away! For ’tis not safe
20You should be seen much here. –Ever my love!

GRIMALDI.

21And mine to you.

Exit Grimaldi.

RICHARDETTO.

22So; if this hit, I’ll laugh and hug revenge,
23And they that now dream of a wedding-feast
24May chance to mourn the lusty bridegroom’s ruin.
25But to my other business. –Niece Philotis!

Enter Philotis.

PHILOTIS.

26Uncle?

RICHARDETTO.

27My lovely niece!
28You have bethought ’ee?

PHILOTIS.

You have bethought ’ee? Yes, and, as you counsel’d,
29Fashion’d my heart to love him; but he swears
30He will tonight be married, for he fears
31His uncle else, if he should know the drift,
32Will hinder all, and call his coz to shrift.

RICHARDETTO.

33Tonight? Why, best of all! –but let me see,
34I –ha–yes: so it shall be; in disguise
35We’ll early to the friar’s, I have thought on’t.

Enter Bergetto and Poggio.

PHILOTIS.

36Uncle, he comes.

RICHARDETTO.

Uncle, he comes. Welcome, my worthy coz.

BERGETTO.

Lass, pretty lass, come buss, lass! –Aha, Poggio!

[Kisses her.]

POGGIO.

There’s hope of this yet!

RICHARDETTO.

39You shall have time enough; withdraw a little,
40We must confer at large.

BERGETTO.

Have you not sweetmeats or dainty devices for me?

PHILOTIS.

You shall have enough, sweetheart.

BERGETTO.

Sweetheart! Mark that, Poggio! By my troth, I cannot choose but kiss thee once more for that word “sweetheart.” – Poggio, I have a monstrous swelling about my stomach, whatsoever the matter be.

POGGIO.

You shall have physic for’t, sir.

RICHARDETTO.

48Time runs apace.

BERGETTO.

Time’s a blockhead.

RICHARDETTO.

50Be rul’d; when we have done what’s fit to do,
51Then you may kiss your fill, and bed her too.

Exeunt.

[III.vi]

Enter the Friar sitting in a chair, Annabella kneeling and whispering to him: a table before them and wax-lights: she weeps and wrings her hands.

FRIAR.

1I am glad to see this penance; for, believe me,
2You have unripp’d a soul so foul and guilty
3As I must tell you true, I marvel how
4The earth hath borne you up: but weep, weep on,
5These tears may do you good; weep faster yet,
6Whiles I do read a lecture.

ANNABELLA.

Whiles I do read a lecture. Wretched creature!

FRIAR.

7Ay, you are wretched, miserably wretched,
8Almost condemn’d alive. There is a place –
9List, daughter –in a black and hollow vault,
10Where day is never seen; there shines no sun,
11But flaming horror of consuming fires,
12A lightless sulphur, chok’d with smoky fogs
13Of an infected darkness; in this place
14Dwell many thousand thousand sundry sorts
15Of never-dying deaths; there damned souls
16Roar without pity; there are gluttons fed
17With toads and adders; there is burning oil
18Pour’d down the drunkard’s throat; the usurer
19Is forc’d to sup whole draughts of molten gold;
20There is the murderer forever stabb’d,
21Yet can he never die; there lies the wanton
22On racks of burning steel, whiles in his soul
23He feels the torment of his raging lust.

ANNABELLA.

24Mercy, O mercy!

FRIAR.

Mercy, O mercy! There stands these wretched things
25Who have dream’d out whole years in lawless sheets
26And secret incests, cursing one another:
27Then you will wish each kiss your brother gave
28Had been a dagger’s point; then you shall hear
29How he will cry, “O would my wicked sister
30Had first been damn’d, when she did yield to lust!”–
31But soft, methinks I see repentance work
32New motions in your heart; say, how is’t with you?

ANNABELLA.

33Is there no way left to redeem my miseries?

FRIAR.

34There is, despair not; Heaven is merciful,
35And offers grace even now. ’Tis thus agreed,
36First, for your honor’s safety, that you marry
37The Lord Soranzo; next, to save your soul,
38Leave off this life, and henceforth live to him.

ANNABELLA.

39Ay me!

FRIAR.

Ay me! Sigh not; I know the baits of sin
40Are hard to leave–O, ’tis a death to do’t.
41Remember what must come. Are you content?

ANNABELLA.

42I am.

FRIAR.

I am. I like it well; we’ll take the time.
43Who’s near us there?

Enter Florio and Giovanni.

FLORIO.

44Did you call, father?

FRIAR.

45Is Lord Soranzo come?

FLORIO.

Is Lord Soranzo come? He stays below.

FRIAR.

46Have you acquainted him at full?

FLORIO.

Have you acquainted him at full? I have,
47And he is overjoy’d.

FRIAR.

And he is overjoy’d. And so are we;
48Bid him come near.

GIOVANNI.

Bid him come near. My sister weeping, ha?aparte
49I fear this friar’s falsehood.aparte

Exit.

FLORIO.

50Daughter, are you resolv’d?

ANNABELLA.

Daughter, are you resolv’d? Father, I am.

Enter Giovanni, Soranzo, and Vasques.

FLORIO.

51My Lord Soranzo, here
52Give me your hand; for that I give you this.

[Joins their hands.]

SORANZO.

53Lady, say you so too?

ANNABELLA.

Lady, say you so too? I do, and vow
54To live with you and yours.

FRIAR.

To live with you and yours. Timely resolv’d:
55My blessing rest on both; more to be done,
56You may perform it on the morning sun.

Exeunt.

PHILOTIS.

35O, he is dead!

POGGIO.

RICHARDETTO.

O, he is dead! How! Dead! He’s dead indeed.
36’Tis now too late to weep; let’s have him home
37And with what speed we may, find out the murderer.

POGGIO.

Exeunt.

[III-viii]

Enter Vasques and Hippolita.

HIPPOLITA.

Betroth’d?

VASQUES.

I saw it.

HIPPOLITA.

And when’s the marriage-day?

VASQUES.

Some two days hence.

HIPPOLITA.

5Two days! Why, man, I would but wish two hours
6To send him to his last and lasting sleep;
7And, Vasques, thou shalt see I’ll do it bravely.

VASQUES.

I do not doubt your wisdom, nor, I trust, you my secrecy; I am infinitely yours.

HIPPOLITA.

10I will be thine in spite of my disgrace.
11So soon? O, wicked man, I durst be sworn,
12He’d laugh to see me weep.

VASQUES.

And that’s a villainous fault in him.

HIPPOLITA.

14No, let him laugh, I’m arm’d in my resolves;
15Be thou still true.

VASQUES.

I should get little by treachery against so hopeful a prefer- ment as I am like to climb to.

HIPPOLITA.

18Even to my bosom, Vasques. Let my youth
19Revel in these new pleasures; if we thrive,
20He now hath but a pair of days to live.

Exeunt.

[III.ix]

Enter Florio, Donado, Richardetto, Poggio, and Officers.

FLORIO.

1’Tis bootles now to show yourself a child,
2Signor Donado; what is done, is done.
3Spend not the time in tears, but seek for justice.

RICHARDETTO.

4I must confess, somewhat I was in fault
5That had not first acquainted you what love
6Pass’d ’twixt him and my niece; but, as I live,
7His fortune grieves me as it were mine own.

DONADO.

8Alas, poor creature, he meant no man harm,
9That I am sure of.

FLORIO.

That I am sure of. I believe that too.
10But stay, my masters, are you sure you saw
11The murderer pass here?

OFFICER.

And it please you, sir, we are sure we saw a ruffian, with a naked weapon in his hand all bloody, get into my lord cardinal’s grace’s gate, that we are sure of; but for fear of his grace, bless us, we durst go no further.

DONADO.

Know you what manner of man he was?

OFFICER.

Yes, sure, I know the man, they say ’a is a soldier; he that lov’d your daughter, sir, an’t please ye; ’twas he for certain.

FLORIO.

19Grimaldi, on my life!

OFFICER.

Grimaldi, on my life! Ay, ay, the same.

RICHARDETTO.

20The cardinal is noble; he no doubt
21Will give true justice.

DONADO.

Knock someone at the gate.

POGGIO.

I’ll knock, sir.

Poggio knocks.

SERVANT.

(within) What would ’ee?

FLORIO.

25We require speech with the lord cardinal
26About some present business; pray inform
27His grace that we are here.

Enter Cardinal and Grimaldi.

CARDINAL.

28Why, how now, friends! What saucy mates are you,
29That know nor duty nor civility?
30Are we a person fit to be your host,
31Or is our house become your common inn,
32To beat our doors at pleasure? What such haste
33Is yours as that it cannot wait fit times?
34Are you the masters of this commonwealth,
35And know no more discretion? O, your news
36Is here before you; you have lost a nephew,
37Donado, last night by Grimaldi slain:
38Is that your business? Well, sir, we have knowledge on’t.
39Let that suffice.

GRIMALDI.

Let that suffice. In presence of your grace,
40In thought I never meant Bergetto harm.
41But Florio, you can tell, with how much scorn
42Soranzo, back’d with his confederates,
43Hath often wrong’d me; I to be reveng’d,
44(For that I could not win him else to fight)
45Had thought by way of ambush to have kill’d him,
46But was unlucklily therein mistook,
47Else he had felt what late Bergetto did:
48And though my fault to him were merely chance,
49Yet humbly I submit me to your grace,
50To do with me as you please.

[Kneels.]

CARDINAL.

To do with me as you please. Rise up, Grimaldi.
51You citizens of Parma, if you seek
52For justice, know, as nuncio from the pope,
53For this offense I here receive Grimaldi
54Into his holiness’ protection.
55He is no common man, but nobly born;
56Of princes’ blood, though you, Sir Florio,
57Thought him too mean a husband for your daughter.
58If more you seek for, you must go to Rome,
59For he shall thither; learn more wit, for shame.
60Bury your dead. –Away, Grimaldi–leave ’em!

Exeunt Cardinal and Grimaldi.

DONADO.

61Is this a churchman’s voice? Dwells justice here?

FLORIO.

62Justice is fled to Heaven and comes no nearer.
63Soranzo! Was’t for him? O impudence!
64Had he the face to speak it, and not blush?
65Come, come, Donado, there’s no help in this,
66When cardinals think murder’s not amiss.
67Great men may do their wills, we must obey;
68But Heaven will judge them for’t another day.

Exeunt.
