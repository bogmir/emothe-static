John Ford

Tis Pity She's a Whore

← Act Act →

[IV.i]

A Banquet. Hautboys. Enter the Friar, Giovanni, Annabella, Philotis, Soranzo, Donado, Florio, Richardetto, Putana, and Vasques.

FRIAR.

1These holy rites perform’d, now take your times
2To spend the remnant of the day in feast;
3Such fit repasts are pleasing to the saints,
4Who are your guests, though not with mortal eyes
5To be beheld. –Long prosper in this day,
6You happy couple, to each other’s joy!

SORANZO.

7Father, your prayer is heard; the hand of goodness
8Hath been a shield for me against my death,
9And, more to bless me, hath enrich’d my life
10With this most precious jewel; such a prize
11As earth hath not another like to this.
12Cheer up, my love, and gentlemen, my friends,
13Rejoice with me in mirth; this day we’ll crown
14With lusty cups to Annabella’s health.

GIOVANNI.

15O torture! Were the marriage yet undone,aparte
16Ere I’d endure this sight, to see my loveaparte
17Clipp’d by another, I would dare confusion,aparte
18And stand the horror of ten thousand deaths.aparte

VASQUES.

19Are you not well, sir?

GIOVANNI.

Are you not well, sir? Prithee, fellow, wait;
20I need not thy officious diligence.

FLORIO.

21Signor Donado, come, you must forget
22Your late mishaps, and drown your cares in wine.

SORANZO.

23Vasques!

VASQUES.

Vasques! My lord?

SORANZO.

Vasques! My lord? Reach me that weighty bowl.
24Here, brother Giovanni, here’s to you;
25Your turn comes next, though now a bachelor.
26Here’s to your sister’s happiness and mine!

GIOVANNI.

27I cannot drink.

SORANZO.

I cannot drink. What!

GIOVANNI.

I cannot drink. What! ’Twill indeed offend me.

ANNABELLA.

28Pray do not urge him, if he be not willing.

Hautboys.

FLORIO.

How now, what noise is this?

VASQUES.

O, sir, I had forgot to tell you; certain young maidens of Parma, in honor to Madam Annabella’s marriage, have sent their loves to her in a masque, for which they humbly crave your patience and silence.

SORANZO.

34We are much bound to them, so much the more
35As it comes unexpected; guide them in.

Enter Hippolita and Ladies in [masks and] white robes, with garlands of willows. Music and a dance.

36Thanks, lovely virgins; now might we but know
37To whom we have been beholding for this love,
38We shall acknowledge it.

HIPPOLITA.

We shall acknowledge it. Yes, you shall know; [Unmasks.] What think you now?

OMNES.

Hippolita!

HIPPOLITA.

’Tis she,
39Be not amaz’d; nor blush, young lovely bride,
40I come not to defraud you of your man.
41’Tis now no time to reckon up the talk
42What Parma long hath rumor’d of us both:
43Let rash report run on; the breath that vents it
44Will, like a bubble, break itself at last.
45But now to you, sweet creature: lend’s your hand;
46Perhaps it hath been said that I would claim
47Some interest in Soranzo, now your lord.
48What I have right to do, his soul knows best:
49But in my duty to your noble worth,
50Sweet Annabella, and my care of you,
51Here take, Soranzo, take this hand from me:
52I’ll once more join what by the holy church
53Is finish’d and allow’d; have I done well?

SORANZO.

54You have too much engag’d us.

HIPPOLITA.

You have too much engag’d us. One thing more.
55That you may know my single charity,
56Freely I here remit all interest
57I e’er could claim, and give you back your vows;
58And to confirm’t–reach me a cup of wine–
59My Lord Soranzo, in this draught I drink
60Long rest t’ee!–Look to it, Vasques.

VASQUES.

Fear nothing.

He gives her a poison’d cup: she drinks.

SORANZO.

62Hippolita, I thank you, and will pledge
63This happy union as another life;
64Wine, there!

VASQUES.

You shall have none, neither shall you pledge her.

HIPPOLITA.

How!

VASQUES.

Know now, Mistress She-Devil, your own mischievous Treachery hath kill’d you; I must not marry you.

HIPPOLITA.

Villain!

OMNES.

What’s the matter?

VASQUES.

Foolish woman, thou art now like a firebrand that hath kindled others and burnt thyself; troppo sperar, inganna , thy vain hope hath deceived thee, thou art but dead; if thou hast any grace, pray.

HIPPOLITA.

Monster!

VASQUES.

Die in charity, for shame! This thing of malice, this woman, had privately corrupted me with promise of marriage, under this politic reconciliation, to poison my lord, whiles she might laugh at his confusion on his marriage day. I promise’d her fair, but I knew what my reward should have been; and would willingly have spar’d her life, but that I was acquainted with the danger of her disposition, and now have fitted her a just payment in her own coin. There she is, she hath yet––and end thy days in peace, vile woman; as for life there’s no hope, think not on’t.

OMNES.

87Wonderful justice!

RICHARDETTO.

Wonderful justice! Heaven, thou art righteous.

HIPPOLITA.

88O, ’tis true;
89I feel my minute coming. Had that slave
90Kept promise (O, my torment!), thou this hour
91Hadst died, Soranzo –heat above hell fire!–
92Yet ere I pass away –cruel, cruel flames!–
93Take here my curse amongst you; may thy bed
94Of marriage be a rack unto thy heart,
95Burn blood and boil in vengeance–O my heart,
96My flame’s intolerable!–Mayst thou live
97To father bastards, may her womb bring forth
98Monsters, and die together in your sins,
99Hated, scorn’d, and unpitied!–O!–O!–

Dies.

FLORIO.

100Was e’er so vile a creature?

RICHARDETTO.

Was e’er so vile a creature? Here’s the end
101Of lust and pride.

ANNABELLA.

Of lust and pride. It is a fearful sight.

SORANZO.

102Vasques, I know thee now a trusty servant,
103And never will forget thee. –Come, my love,
104We’ll home, and thank the Heavens for this escape.
105Father and friends, we must break up this mirth;
106It is too sad a feast.

DONADO.

It is too sad a feast. Bear hence the body.

FRIAR.

107Here’s an ominous change;
108Mark this, my Giovanni, and take heed.
109I fear the event; that marriage seldom’s good,
110Where the bride-banquet so begins in blood.

Exeunt.

[IV.ii]

Enter Richardetto and Philotis.

RICHARDETTO.

1My wretched wife, more wretched in her shame
2Than in her wrongs to me, hath paid too soon
3The forfeit of her modesty and life;
4And I am sure, my niece, though vengeance hover,
5Keeping aloof yet from Soranzo’s fall,
6Yet he will fall, and sink with his own weight.
7I need not now–my heart persuades me so–
8To further his confusion; there is One
9Above begins to work, for, as I hear,
10Debates already ’twixt his wife and him
11Thicken and run to head; she, as ’tis said,
12Slightens his love, and he abandons hers.
13Much talk I hear; since things go thus, my niece,
14In tender love and pity of your youth,
15My counsel is, that you should free your years
16From hazard of these woes by flying hence
17To fair Cremona, there to vow your soul
18In holiness a holy votaress:
19Leave me to see the end of these extremes.
20All human worldly courses are uneven;
21No life is blessed but the way to Heaven.

PHILOTIS.

22Uncle, shall I resolve to be a nun?

RICHARDETTO.

23Ay, gentle niece, and in your hourly prayers
24Remember me, your poor unhappy uncle.
25Hie to Cremona now, as fortune leads,
26Your home your cloister, your best friends your beads.
27Your chaste and single life shall crown your birth;
28Who dies a virgin lives a saint on earth.

PHILOTIS.

29Then farewell, world, and worldly thoughts, adieu!
30Welcome, chaste vows; myself I yield to you.

Exeunt.

[IV.iii]

Enter Soranzo unbrac’d, and Annabella dragg’d in.

SORANZO.

1Come, strumpet, famous whore! Were every drop
2Of blood that runs in thy adulterous veins
3A life, this sword–dost see’t?–should in one blow
4Confound them all. Harlot, rare, notable harlot,
5That with thy brazen face maintains thy sin,
6Was there no man in Parma to be bawd
7To your loose cunning whoredom else but I?
8Must your hot itch and pleurisy of lust,
9The heyday of your luxury, be fed
10Up to a surfeit, and could none but I
11Be pick’d out to be cloak to your close tricks,
12Your belly-sports? Now I must be the dad
13To all that gallimaufry that’s stuff’d
14In thy corrupted bastard-bearing womb,
15Say, must I?

ANNABELLA.

Say, must I? Beastly man! Why, ’tis thy fate.
16I sued not to thee; for, but that I thought
17Your over-loving lordship would have run
18Mad on denial, had ye lent me time,
19I would have told ‘ee in what case I was.
20But you would needs be doing.

SORANZO.

But you would needs be doing. Whore of whores!
21Dar’st thou tell me this?

ANNABELLA.

Dar’st thou tell me this? O yes, why not?
22You were deceiv’d in me; ’twas not for love
23I choose you, but for honor; yet know this,
24Would you be patient yet, and hide your shame,
25I’d see whether I could love you.

SORANZO.

I’d see whether I could love you. Excellent quean!
26Why, art thou not with child?

ANNABELLA.

Why, art thou not with child? What needs all this
27When ’tis superfluous? I confess I am.

SORANZO.

28Tell me by whom.

ANNABELLA.

Tell me by whom. Soft, sir, ’twas not in my bargain.
29Yet somewhat, sir, to stay your longing stomach,
30I’m content t’acquaint you with; the man,
31The more than man, that got this sprightly boy–
32For ’tis a boy; that’s for your glory, sir,
33Your heir shall be a son–

SORANZO.

Your heir shall be a son– Damnable monster!

ANNABELLA.

34Nay, and you will not hear, I’ll speak no more.

SORANZO.

35Yes, speak, and speak thy last.

ANNABELLA.

Yes, speak, and speak thy last. A match, a match!
36This noble creature was in every part
37So angel-like, so glorious, that a woman
38Who had not been but human, as was I,
39Would have kneel’d to him, and have begg’d for love.
40You! Why, you are not worthy once to name
41His name without true worship, or, indeed,
42Unless you kneel’d, to hear another name him.

SORANZO.

43What was he call’d?

ANNABELLA.

What was he call’d? We are not come to that.
44Let it suffice that you shall have the glory
45To father what so brave a father got.
46In brief, had not this chance fall’n out as’t doth,
47I never had been troubled with a thought
48That you had been a creature; but for marriage,
49I scarce dream yet of that.

SORANZO.

50Tell me his name.

ANNABELLA.

Tell me his name. Alas, alas, there’s all!
51Will you believe?

SORANZO.

Will you believe? What?

ANNABELLA.

Will you believe? What? You shall never know.

SORANZO.

52How!

ANNABELLA.

How! Never; if you do, let me be curs’d.

SORANZO.

53Not know it, strumpet! I’ll rip up thy heart,
54And find it there.

ANNABELLA.

And find it there. Do, do!

SORANZO.

And find it there. Do, do! And with my teeth
55Tear the prodigious lecher joint by joint.

ANNABELLA.

56Ha, ha, ha, the man’s merry!

SORANZO.

Ha, ha, ha, the man’s merry! Dost thou laugh?
57Come, whore, tell me your lover, or, by truth,
58I’ll hew thy flesh to shreds; who is’t?

ANNABELLA.

59(sings). Che morte piu dolce che morire per amore?

SORANZO.

60Thus will I pull thy hair, and thus I’ll drag
61Thy lust-be-leper’d body through the dust.
62Yet tell his name.

ANNABELLA.

63(sings). Morendo in gratia Dei, morirei senza dolore.

SORANZO.

64Dost thou triumph? The treasure of the earth
65Shall not redeem thee; were there kneeling kings;
66Did beg thy life, or angels did come down
67To plead in tears, yet should not all prevail
68Against my rage! Dost thou not tremble yet?

ANNABELLA.

69At what? To die? No, be a gallant hangman.
70I dare thee to the worst: strike, and strike home;
71I leave revenge behind, and thou shalt feel’t.

SORANZO.

72Yet tell me ere thou diest, and tell me truly,
73Knows thy old father this?

ANNABELLA.

Knows thy old father this? No, by my life.

SORANZO.

74Wilt thou confess, and I will spare thy life?

ANNABELLA.

75My life! I will not buy my life so dear.

SORANZO.

76I will not slack my vengeance.

Enter Vasques.

VASQUES.

What d’ee mean, sir?

SORANZO.

78Forbear, Vasques; such a damned whore
79Deserves no pity.

VASQUES.

Now the gods forfend! And would you be her executioner, and kill her in your rage too? O, ’twere most unmanlike. She is your wife: what faults hath been done by her before she married you, were not against you; alas, poor lady, what hath she committed which any lady in Italy in the like case would not? Sir, you must be ruled by your reason and not by your fury, that were unhuman and beastly.

SORANZO.

She shall not live.

VASQUES.

Come, she must. You would have her confess the author of her present misfortunes, I warrant’ee; ’tis an unconscion- able demand, and she should lose the estimation that I, for my part, hold of her worth, if she had done it. Why, sir, you ought not all men living to know it: good sir, be reconciled; alas, good gentlewoman!

ANNABELLA.

94Pish, do not beg for me: I prize my life
95As nothing; if the man will needs be mad,
96Why, let him take it.

SORANZO.

Why, let him take it. Vasques, hear’st thou this?

VASQUES.

Sir, in any case smother your revenge; leave the scenting-out your wrongs to me; be rul’d, as you respect your honor, or you mar all.

SORANZO.

106O Vasques, Vasques, in this piece of flesh,
107This faithless face of hers, had I laid up
108The treasure of my heart! –Hadst thou been virtuous,
109Fair, wicked woman, not the matchless joys
110Of life itself had made me wish to live
111With any saint but thee; deceitful creature,
112How hast thou mock’d my hopes, and in the shame
113Of thy lewd womb even buried me alive!
114I did too dearly love thee.

VASQUES.

This is well; follow this temper with some passion. Be brief and moving; ’tis for the purpose.

SORANZO.

117Be witness to my words thy soul and thoughts,
118And tell me, didst not think that in my heart
119I did too superstitiously adore thee?

ANNABELLA.

120I must confess I know you lov’d me well.

SORANZO.

121And wouldst thou use me thus? O, Annabella,
122Be thou assur’d, whatsoe’er the villain was
123That thus hath tempted thee to this disgrace,
124Well he might lust, but never lov’d like me.
125He doted on the picture that hung out
126Upon thy cheeks, to please his humorous eye;
127Not on the part I lov’d, which was thy heart,
128And, as I thought, thy virtues.

ANNABELLA.

And, as I thought, thy virtues. O my lord!
129These words wound deeper than your sword could do.

VASQUES.

Let me not ever take comfort, but I begin to weep myself, so much I pity him; why, madam, I knew when his rage was over-past, what it would come to.

SORANZO.

133Forgive me, Annabella: though thy youth
134Hath tempted thee above thy strength to folly,
135Yet will not I forget what I should be,
136And what I am, a husband; in that name
137Is hid divinity; if I do find
138That thou wilt yet be true, here I remit
139All former faults, and take thee to my bosom.

VASQUES.

By my troth, and that’s a point of noble charity.

ANNABELLA.

141Sir, on my knees–

[Kneels.]

SORANZO.

Sir, on my knees– Rise up, you shall not kneel.
142Get you to your chamber, see you make no show
143Of alteration; I’ll be with you straight.
144My reason tells me now that ’tis as common
145To err in frailty as to be a woman.
146Go to your chamber.

Exit Annabella.

VASQUES.

So, this was somewhat to the matter; what do you think of your heaven of happiness now, sir?

SORANZO.

149I carry hell about me: all my blood
150Is fir’d in swift revenge.

VASQUES.

That may be, but know you how, or on whom? Alas, to marry a great woman, being made great in the stock to your hand, is a usual sport in these says; but to know what ferret it was that haunted your cony-berry, there’s the cunning.

SORANZO.

156I’ll make her tell herself, or–

VASQUES.

Or what? You must not do so. Let me yet persuade your sufferance a little while; go to her, use her mildly, win her if it be possible to a voluntary, to a weeping tune; for the rest, if all hit, I will not miss my mark. Pray, sir, go in; the next news I tell you shall be wonders.

SORANZO.

162Delay in vengeance gives a heavier blow.

Exit.

VASQUES.

Ah, sirrah, here’s work for the nonce! I had a suspicion of a bad matter in my head a pretty whiles ago; but after my madam’s scurvy looks here at home, her waspish perverseness and loud fault-finding, then I remember’d the proverb, that where hens crow and cocks hold their peace there are sorry houses. ’Sfoot, if the lower parts of a she-tailor’s cunning can cover such a swelling in the stomach, I’ll never blame a false stitch in a shoe whiles I live again. Up and up so quick? And so quickly too? ’Twere a fine policy to learn by whom this must be known; and I have thought on’t –here’s the way, or none. Enter Putana. What, crying, old mistress! Alas, alas, I cannot blame ’ee, we have a lord, Heaven help us, is so mad as the devil himself, the more shame for him.

PUTANA.

O Vasques, that ever I was born to see this day! Doth he use thee so too, sometimes, Vasques?

VASQUES.

Me? Why, he makes a dog of me. But if some were of my mind, I know what we would do; as sure as I am an honest man, he will go near to kill my lady with unkindness. Say she be with child, is that such a matter for a young woman of her years to be blam’d for?

PUTANA.

Alas, good heart, it is against her will full sore.

VASQUES.

I durst be sworn, all his madness is for that she will not confess whose ’tis, which he will know, and when he doth know it, I am so well acquainted with his humor, that he will forget all straight. Well, I could wish she would in plain terms tell all, for that’s the way indeed.

PUTANA.

Do you think so?

VASQUES.

Foh, I know’t; provided that he did not win her to’t by force. He was once in a mind that you could tell, and meant to have wrung it out of you, but I somewhat pacified him for that; yet sure you know a great deal.

PUTANA.

Heaven forgive us all! I know a little, Vasques.

VASQUES.

Why should you not? Who else should? Upon my conscience, she loves you dearly, and you would not betray her to any affliction for the world.

PUTANA.

Not for all the world, by my faith and troth, Vasques.

VASQUES.

’Twere pity of your life if you should, but in this you should both relieve her present discomforts, pacify my lord, and gain yourself everlasting love and preferment.

PUTANA.

Dost think so, Vasques?

VASQUES.

Nay, I know’t; sure ’twas some near and entire friend.

PUTANA.

’Twas a dear friend indeed; but–

VASQUES.

But what? Fear not to name him; my life between you and danger. Faith, I think ’twas no base fellow.

PUTANA.

Thou wilt stand between me and harm?

VASQUES.

’Ud’s pity, what else? You shall be rewarded too, trust me.

PUTANA.

’Twas even no worse than her own brother.

VASQUES.

Her brother Giovanni, I warrant ’ee!

PUTANA.

Even he, Vasques; as brave a gentleman as ever kiss’d fair lady. O, they love most perpetually!

VASQUES.

A brave gentleman indeed; why, therein I commend her choice. –Better and better! –You are sure ’twas he?

PUTANA.

Sure; and you shall see he will not be long from her too.

VASQUES.

He were to blame if he would: but may I believe thee?

PUTANA.

Believe me! Why, dost think I am a Turk or a Jew? No, Vasques, I have known their dealings too long to belie them now.

VASQUES.

Where are you there? Within, sirs!

Enter Banditti.

PUTANA.

How now, what are these?

VASQUES.

You shall know presently. Come, sirs, take me this old damnable hag, gag her instantly, and put out her eyes. Quickly, quickly!

PUTANA.

Vasques, Vasques!

VASQUES.

Gag her, I say! ’Sfoot, d’ee suffer her to prate? What d’ee fumble about? Let me come to her; I’ll help your old gums, you toad-bellied bitch. Sirs, carry her closely into the coalhouse, and put out her eyes instantly; if she roars, slit her nose: d’ee hear, be speedy and sure. Why, this is excellent and above expectation. Exeunt [Banditti] with Putana. Her own brother! O horrible! To what a height of liberty in damnation hath the devil train’d our age, her brother! Well, there’s yet but a beginning: I must to my lord, and tutor him better in his points of vengeance; now I see how a smooth tale goes beyond a smooth tail. But soft– What thing comes next? Enter Giovanni. Giovanni! As I would wish; my belief is strengthen’d, ’tis as firm as winter and summer.

GIOVANNI.

Where’s my sister?

VASQUES.

Troubled with a new sickness, my lord; she’s somewhat ill.

GIOVANNI.

Took too much of the flesh, I believe.

VASQUES.

Troth, sir, and you, I think, have e’en hit it. But my virtuous lady–

GIOVANNI.

Where’s she?

VASQUES.

In her chamber; please you visit her, she is alone. [Giovanni gives him money.] Your liberality hath doubly made me your servant, and ever shall, ever. Exit Giovanni. Enter Soranzo. Sir, I am made a man, I have plied my cue with cunning and success; I beseech you let’s be private.

SORANZO.

252My lady’s brother’s come; now he’ll know all.

VASQUES.

Let him know’t: I have made some of them fast enough. How have you dealt with my lady?

SORANZO.

255Gently, as thou hast counsel’d. O, my soul
256Runs circular in sorrow for revenge!
257But, Vasques, thou shalt know–

VASQUES.

Nay, I will know no more, for now comes your turn to know; I would not talk so openly with you. Let my young master take time enough, and go at pleasure; he is sold to death, and the devil shall not ransom him. Sir, I beseech you, your privacy.

SORANZO.

263No conquest can gain glory of my fear.

Exeunt.
