The Prologue to the Stage, at the Cock-pit

13We know not how our play may pass this stage,
14But by the best of poets in that age
15The Malta Jew had being, and was made;
16And he, then by the best of actors played.
17In Hero and Leander, one did gain
18A lasting memory; in Tamburlaine,
19This Jew, with others many, th’ other wan
20The attribute of peerless, being a man
21Whom we may rank with (doing no one wrong)
22Proteus for shapes, and Roscius for a tongue,
23So could he speak, so vary; nor is ’t hate
24To merit in him who doth personate
25Our Jew this day, nor is it his ambition
26To exceed, or equal, being of condition
27More modest; this is all that he intends
28(And that, too, at the urgence of some friends):
29To prove his best, and if none here gainsay it,
30The part he hath studied, and intends to play it.
