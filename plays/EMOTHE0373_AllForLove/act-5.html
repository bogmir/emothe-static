Dolabella.

1457Why would you shift it from yourself on me?
1458Can you not tell her, you must part?

Antony.

1459I could pull out an eye, and bid it go,
1460And t'other should not weep. O Dolabella,
1461How many deaths are in this word, Depart!
1462I dare not trust my tongue to tell her so:
1463One look of hers would thaw me into tears,

1466He's rough by nature.

1882Which a feigned love first got; for injured me,
1883Though my just sense of wrongs forbid my stay,
1884My duty shall be yours.
1885To the dear pledges of our former love
1886My tenderness and care shall be transferred,
1887And they shall cheer, by turns, my widowed nights:
1888So, take my last farewell; for I despair
1889To have you whole, and scorn to take you half.

[Exit.]

Ventidius.

1890I combat Heaven, which blasts my best designs:
1891My last attempt must be to win her back;
1892But oh! I fear in vain.

[Exit.]

Antony.

1893Why was I framed with this plain, honest heart,
1894Which knows not to disguise its griefs and weakness,
1895But bears its working outward to the world?
1896I should have kept the mighty anguish in,
1897And forced a smile at Cleopatra's falsehood:
1898Octavia had believed it, and had stayed.
1899But I am made a shallow-forded stream,
1900Seen to the bottom: all my clearness scorned,
1901And all my faults exposed. –See where he comes,

Enter DOLABELLA.

1902Who has profaned the sacred name of friend,
1903And worn it into vileness!
1904With how secure a brow, and specious form
1905He gilds the secret villain! Sure that face
1906Was meant for honesty; but Heaven mismatched it,
1907And furnished treason out with nature's pomp,
1908To make its work more easy.

Dolabella.

To make its work more easy. O my friend!

Antony.

1909Well, Dolabella, you performed my message?

Dolabella.

1910I did, unwillingly.

Antony.

I did, unwillingly. Unwillingly?
1911Was it so hard for you to bear our parting?
1912You should have wished it.

Dolabella.

You should have wished it. Why?

Antony.

You should have wished it. Why? Because you love me.
1913And she received my message with as true,
1914With as unfeigned a sorrow as you brought it?

Dolabella.

1915She loves you, even to madness.

Antony.

She loves you, even to madness. Oh, I know it.
1916You, Dolabella, do not better know
1917How much she loves me. And should I
1918Forsake this beauty? This all-perfect creature?

Dolabella.

1919I could not, were she mine.

Antony.

I could not, were she mine. And yet you first
1920Persuaded me: How come you altered since?

Dolabella.

1921I said at first I was not fit to go:
1922I could not hear her sighs, and see her tears,
1923But pity must prevail: And so, perhaps,
1924It may again with you; for I have promised,
1925That she should take her last farewell: And, see,
1926She comes to claim my word.

Enter CLEOPATRA.

Antony.

She comes to claim my word. False Dolabella!

Dolabella.

1927What's false, my lord?

Antony.

What's false, my lord? Why, Dolabella's false,
1928And Cleopatra's false; both false and faithless.
1929Draw near, you well-joined wickedness, you serpents,
1930Whom I have in my kindly bosom warmed,
1931Till I am stung to death.

Dolabella.

Till I am stung to death. My lord, have I
1932Deserved to be thus used?

Cleopatra.

Deserved to be thus used? Can Heaven prepare
1933A newer torment? Can it find a curse
1934Beyond our separation?

Antony.

Beyond our separation? Yes, if fate
1935Be just, much greater: Heaven should be ingenious
1936In punishing such crimes. The rolling stone
1937And gnawing vulture were slight pains, invented
1938When Jove was young, and no examples known
1939Of mighty ills; but you have ripened sin,
1940To such a monstrous growth, 'twill pose the gods
1941To find an equal torture. Two, two such! –
1942Oh, there's no further name, –two such! to me,
1943To me, who locked my soul within your breasts,
1944Had no desires, no joys, no life, but you;
1945When half the globe was mine, I gave it you
1946In dowry with my heart; I had no use,
1947No fruit of all, but you; A friend and mistress
1948Was what the world could give. O Cleopatra!
1949O Dolabella! how could you betray
1950This tender heart, which with an infant fondness
1951Lay lulled betwixt your bosoms, and there slept,
1952Secure of injured faith?

Dolabella.

1953If she has wronged you,
1954Heaven, hell, and you revenge it.

Antony.

1955If she has wronged me!
1956Thou wouldst evade thy part of guilt; but swear
1957Thou lov'st not her.

Dolabella.

Thou lov'st not her. Not so as I love you.

Antony.

1958Not so? Swear, swear, I say, thou dost not love her.

Dolabella.

1959No more than friendship will allow.

Antony.

No more than friendship will allow. No more?
1960Friendship allows thee nothing: Thou art perjured –
1961And yet thou didst not swear thou lov'st her not;
1962But not so much, no more. O trifling hypocrite,
1963Who dar'st not own to her, thou dost not love,
1964Nor own to me, thou dost! Ventidius heard it;
1965Octavia saw it.

Cleopatra.

Octavia saw it. They are enemies.

Antony.

1966Alexas is not so: He, he confessed it;
1967He, who, next hell, best knew it, he avowed it.

[To DOLABELLA.]

1968Why do I seek a proof beyond yourself?
1969You, whom I sent to bear my last farewell,
1970Returned, to plead her stay.

Dolabella.

Returned, to plead her stay. What shall I answer?
1971If to have loved be guilt, then I have sinned;
1972But if to have repented of that love
1973Can wash away my crime, I have repented.
1974Yet, if I have offended past forgiveness,
1975Let not her suffer: She is innocent.

Cleopatra.

1976Ah, what will not a woman do, who loves?
1977What means will she refuse, to keep that heart,
1978Where all her joys are placed? 'Twas I encouraged,
1979'Twas I blew up the fire that scorched his soul,
1980To make you jealous, and by that regain you.
1981But all in vain; I could not counterfeit:
1982In spite of all the dams my love broke o'er,
1983And drowned my heart again: fate took the occasion;
1984And thus one minute's feigning has destroyed
1985My whole life's truth.

Antony.

My whole life's truth. Thin cobweb arts of falsehood;
1986Seen, and broke through at first.

Dolabella.

Seen, and broke through at first. Forgive your mistress.

Cleopatra.

1987Forgive your friend.

Antony.

Forgive your friend. You have convinced yourselves.
1988You plead each other's cause: What witness have you,
1989That you but meant to raise my jealousy?

Cleopatra.

1990Ourselves, and Heaven.

Antony.

1991Guilt witnesses for guilt. Hence, love and friendship!
1992You have no longer place in human breasts,
1993These two have driven you out: Avoid my sight!
1994I would not kill the man whom I have loved,
1995And cannot hurt the woman; but avoid me:
1996I do not know how long I can be tame;
1997For, if I stay one minute more, to think
1998How I am wronged, my justice and revenge
1999Will cry so loud within me, that my pity
2000Will not be heard for either.

Dolabella.

Will not be heard for either. Heaven has but
2001Our sorrow for our sins; and then delights
2002To pardon erring man: Sweet mercy seems
2003Its darling attribute, which limits justice;
2004As if there were degrees in infinite,
2005And infinite would rather want perfection
2006Than punish to extent.

Antony.

Than punish to extent. I can forgive
2007A foe; but not a mistress and a friend.
2008Treason is there in its most horrid shape,
2009Where trust is greatest; and the soul resigned,
2010Is stabbed by its own guards: I'll hear no more;
2011Hence from my sight for ever!

Cleopatra.

Hence from my sight for ever! How? for ever!
2012I cannot go one moment from your sight,
2013And must I go for ever?
2014My joys, my only joys, are centred here:
2015What place have I to go to? My own kingdom?
2016That I have lost for you: Or to the Romans?
2017They hate me for your sake: Or must I wander
2018The wide world o'er, a helpless, banished woman,
2019Banished for love of you; banished from you?
2020Ay, there's the banishment! Oh, hear me; hear me,
2021With strictest justice: For I beg no favour;
2022And if I have offended you, then kill me,
2023But do not banish me.

Antony.

But do not banish me. I must not hear you.
2024I have a fool within me takes your part;
2025But honour stops my ears.

Cleopatra.

But honour stops my ears. For pity hear me!
2026Would you cast off a slave who followed you?
2027Who crouched beneath your spurn? –He has no pity!
2028See, if he gives one tear to my departure;
2029One look, one kind farewell: O iron heart!
2030Let all the gods look down, and judge betwixt us,
2031If he did ever love!

Antony.

If he did ever love! No more: Alexas!

Dolabella.

2032A perjured villain!

Antony.

A perjured villain! [to Cleopatra.] Your Alexas; yours.

Cleopatra.

2033Oh, 'twas his plot; his ruinous design,
2034To engage you in my love by jealousy.
2035Hear him; confront him with me; let him speak.

Antony.

2036I have; I have.

Cleopatra.

I have; I have. And if he clear me not –

Antony.

2037Your creature! one who hangs upon your smiles!
2038Watches your eye, to say or to unsay,
2039Whate'er you please! I am not to be moved.

Cleopatra.

2040Then must we part? Farewell, my cruel lord!
2041The appearance is against me; and I go,
2042Unjustified, for ever from your sight.
2043How I have loved, you know; how yet I love,
2044My only comfort is, I know myself:
2045I love you more, even now you are unkind,
2046Than when you loved me most; so well, so truly
2047I'll never strive against it; but die pleased,
2048To think you once were mine.

Antony.

2049Good heaven, they weep at parting!
2050Must I weep too? that calls them innocent.
2051I must not weep; and yet I must, to think
2052That I must not forgive. –
2053Live, but live wretched; 'tis but just you should,
2054Who made me so: Live from each other's sight:
2055Let me not hear you meet. Set all the earth,
2056And all the seas, betwixt your sundered loves:
2057View nothing common but the sun and skies.
2058Now, all take several ways;
2059And each your own sad fate, with mine, deplore;
2060That you were false, and I could trust no more.

[Exeunt severally.]
