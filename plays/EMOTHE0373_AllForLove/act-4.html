ACT III

SCENE I

At one door enter CLEOPATRA, CHARMION, IRAS, and ALEXAS, a Train of Egyptians: at the other ANTONY and Romans. The entrance on both sides is prepared by music; the trumpets first sounding on ANTONY's part: then answered by timbrels, etc., on CLEOPATRA's. CHARMION and IRAS hold a laurel wreath betwixt them. A Dance of Egyptians. After the ceremony, CLEOPATRA crowns ANTONY.

Antony.

965I thought how those white arms would fold me in,
966And strain me close, and melt me into love;
967So pleased with that sweet image, I sprung forwards,
968And added all my strength to every blow.

Cleopatra.

969Come to me, come, my soldier, to my arms!
970You've been too long away from my embraces;
971But, when I have you fast, and all my own,
972With broken murmurs, and with amorous sighs,
973I'll say, you were unkind, and punish you,
974And mark you red with many an eager kiss.

Antony.

975My brighter Venus!

Cleopatra.

My brighter Venus! O my greater Mars!

Antony.

976Thou join'st us well, my love!
977Suppose me come from the Phlegræan plains,
978Where gasping giants lay, cleft by my sword,
979And mountain-tops pared off each other blow,
980To bury those I slew. Receive me, goddess!
981Let Cæsar spread his subtle nets, like Vulcan;
982In thy embraces I would be beheld
983By heaven and earth at once;

1390A name that makes and can unmake a queen.

Cleopatra.

1391Your lord, the man who serves me, is a Roman.

Octavia.

1392He was a Roman, till he lost that name,
1393To be a slave in Egypt; but I come
1394To free him thence.

Cleopatra.

To free him thence. Peace, peace, my lover's Juno.
1395When he grew weary of that household clog,
1396He chose my easier bonds.

Octavia.

He chose my easier bonds. I wonder not
1397Your bonds are easy: you have long been practised
1398In that lascivious art: He's not the first
1399For whom you spread your snares: Let Cæsar witness.

Cleopatra.

1400I loved not Cæsar; 'twas but gratitude
1401I paid his love: The worst your malice can,
1402Is but to say the greatest of mankind
1403Has been my slave. The next, but far above him
1404In my esteem , is he whom law calls yours,
1405But whom his love made mine.

Octavia.

But whom his love made mine. [Coming up close to her.] I would view nearer
1406That face, which has so long usurped my right,
1407To find the inevitable charms, that catch
1408Mankind so sure, that ruined my dear lord.

Cleopatra.

1409Oh, you do well to search; for had you known
1410But half these charms, you had not lost his heart.

Octavia.

1411Far be their knowledge from a Roman lady,
1412Far from a modest wife! Shame of our sex,
1413Dost thou not blush to own those black endearments,
1414That make sin pleasing?

Cleopatra.

1415You may blush, who want them.
1416If bounteous nature, if indulgent Heaven
1417Have given me charms to please the bravest man,
1418Should I not thank them? Should I be ashamed,
1419And not be proud? I am, that he has loved me;
1420And, when I love not him, Heaven change this face
1421For one like that.

Octavia.

For one like that. Thou lov'st him not so well.

Cleopatra.

1422I love him better, and deserve him more.

Octavia.

1423You do not; cannot: You have been his ruin.
1424Who made him cheap at Rome, but Cleopatra?
1425Who made him scorned abroad, but Cleopatra?
1426At Actium, who betrayed him? Cleopatra.
1427Who made his children orphans, and poor me
1428A wretched widow? only Cleopatra.

Cleopatra.

1429Yet she, who loves him best, is Cleopatra.
1430If you have suffered, I have suffered more.
1431You bear the specious title of a wife,
1432To gild your cause, and draw the pitying world
1433To favour it: the world condemns poor me.
1434For I have lost my honour, lost my fame,
1435And stained the glory of my royal house,
1436And all to bear the branded name of mistress.
1437There wants but life, and that too I would lose
1438For him I love.

Octavia.

For him I love. Be't so, then; take thy wish.

[Exit with her Train.]

Cleopatra.

1439And 'tis my wish,
1440Now he is lost for whom alone I lived.
1441My sight grows dim, and every object dances,
1442And swims before me, in the maze of death.
1443My spirits, while they were opposed, kept up;
1444They could not sink beneath a rival's scorn!
1445But now she's gone, they faint.

Alexas.

But now she's gone, they faint. Mine have had leisure
1446To recollect their strength, and furnish counsel,
1447To ruin her, who else must ruin you.

Cleopatra.

1448Vain promiser!
1449Lead me, my Charmion; nay, your hand too, Iras.
1450My grief has weight enough to sink you both.
1451Conduct me to some solitary chamber,
1452And draw the curtains round;
1453Then leave me to myself, to take alone
1454My fill of grief:
1455There I till death will his unkindness weep;
1456As harmless infants moan themselves asleep.

[Exeunt.]
