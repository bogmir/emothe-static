ACT V

SCENE I

Enter CLEOPATRA, CHARMION, and IRAS.

Charmion.

2061Be juster, Heaven; such virtue punished thus,
2062Will make us think that chance rules all above,
2063And shuffles, with a random hand, the lots
2064Which man is forced to draw.

Cleopatra.

2065I could tear out these eyes that gained his heart,
2066And had not power to keep it. O the curse
2067Of doting on, even when I find it dotage!
2068Bear witness, gods, you heard him bid me go;
2069You, whom he mocked with imprecating vows
2070Of promised faith! –I'll die; I will not bear it.
2071You may hold me –

[She pulls out her dagger, and they hold her.]

2072But I can keep my breath; I can die inward,
2073And choke this love.

Enter ALEXAS.

Iras.

And choke this love. Help, O Alexas, help!
2074The queen grows desperate; her soul struggles in her
2075With all the agonies of love and rage,
2076And strives to force its passage.

Cleopatra.

And strives to force its passage. Let me go.
2077Art thou there, traitor! –O,
2078O for a little breath, to vent my rage,
2079Give, give me away, and let me loose upon him.

Alexas.

2080Yes, I deserve it, for my ill-timed truth.
2081Was it for me to prop
2082The ruins of a falling majesty?
2083To place myself beneath the mighty flaw,
2084Thus to be crushed, and pounded into atoms,
2085By its o'erwhelming weight? 'Tis too presuming
2086For subjects to preserve that wilful power,
2087Which courts its own destruction.

Cleopatra.

Which courts its own destruction. I would reason
2088More calmly with you. Did not you o'errule,
2089And force my plain, direct, and open love,
2090Into these crooked paths of jealousy?
2091Now, what's the event? Octavia is removed;
2092But Cleopatra's banished. Thou, thou villain,
2093Hast pushed my boat to open sea; to prove,
2094At my sad cost, if thou canst steer it back.
2095It cannot be; I'm lost too far; I'm ruined:
2096Hence, thou impostor, traitor, monster, devil! –
2097I can no more: Thou, and my griefs, have sunk
2098Me down so low, that I want voice to curse thee.

Alexas.

2099Suppose some shipwrecked seaman near the shore,
2100Dropping and faint with climbing up the cliff,
2101If, from above, some charitable hand
2102Pull him to safety, hazarding himself,
2103To draw the other's weight; would he look back,
2104And curse him for his pains? The case is yours;
2105But one step more, and you have gained the height.

Cleopatra.

2106Sunk, never more to rise.

Alexas.

2107Octavia's gone, and Dolabella banished.
2108Believe me, madam, Antony is yours.
2109His heart was never lost, but started off
2110To jealousy, love's last retreat and covert;
2111Where it lies hid in shades, watchful in silence,
2112And listening for the sound that calls it back.
2113Some other, any man ('tis so advanced),
2114May perfect this unfinished work, which I
2115(Unhappy only to myself) have left
2116So easy to his hand.

Cleopatra.

So easy to his hand. Look well thou do't; else –

Alexas.

2117Else, what your silence threatens. – Antony
2118Is mounted up the Pharos; from whose turret,
2119He stands surveying our Egyptian galleys,
2120Engaged with Cæsar's fleet. Now death or conquest!
2121If the first happen, fate acquits my promise;
2122If we o'ercome, the conqueror is yours.

[A distant shout within.]

Charmion.

2123Have comfort, madam: Did you mark that shout?

[Second shout nearer.]

Iras.

2124Hark! they redouble it.

Alexas.

Hark! they redouble it. 'Tis from the port.
2125The loudness shows it near: Good news, kind heavens!

Cleopatra.

2126Osiris make it so!

Enter SERAPION.

Serapion.

Osiris make it so! Where, where's the queen?

Alexas.

2127How frightfully the holy coward stares
2128As if not yet recovered of the assault,
2129When all his gods, and, what's more dear to him,
2130His offerings, were at stake.

Serapion.

His offerings, were at stake. O horror, horror!
2131Egypt has been; our latest hour has come:
2132The queen of nations, from her ancient seat,
2133Is sunk for ever in the dark abyss:
2134Time has unrolled her glories to the last,
2135And now closed up the volume.

Cleopatra.

And now closed up the volume. Be more plain:
2136Say, whence thou comest; though fate is in thy face,
2137Which from thy haggard eyes looks wildly out,
2138And threatens ere thou speakest.

Serapion.

And threatens ere thou speakest. I came from Pharos;
2139From viewing (spare me, and imagine it)
2140Our land's last hope, your navy –

Cleopatra.

Our land's last hope, your navy – Vanquished?

Serapion.

Our land's last hope, your navy – Vanquished? No:
2141They fought not.

Cleopatra.

They fought not. Then they fled.

Serapion.

They fought not. Then they fled. Nor that. I saw,
2142With Antony, your well-appointed fleet
2143Row out; and thrice he waved his hand on high,
2144And thrice with cheerful cries they shouted back:
2145'Twas than false Fortune, like a fawning strumpet,
2146About to leave the bankrupt prodigal,
2147With a dissembled smile would kiss at parting,
2148And flatter to the last; the well-timed oars,
2149Now dipt from every bank, now smoothly run
2150To meet the foe; and soon indeed they met,
2151But not as foes. In few, we say their caps
2152On either side thrown up; the Egyptians galleys,
2153Received like friends, passed through, and fell behind
2154The Roman rear: And now, they all come forward,
2155And ride within the port.

Cleopatra.

And ride within the port. Enough, Serapion:
2156I've heard my doom. –This needed not, you gods:
2157When I lost Antony, your work was done;
2158'Tis buy superfluous malice. –Where's my lord?
2159How bears he this last blow?

Serapion.

2160His fury cannot be expressed by words:
2161Thrice he attempted headlong to have fallen
2162Full on his foes, and aimed at Cæsar's galley:
2163Withheld, he raves on you; cries, he's betrayed.
2164Should he now find you –

Alexas.

Should he now find you – Shun him; seek your safety,
2165Till you can clear your innocence.

Cleopatra.

Till you can clear your innocence. I'll stay.

Alexas.

2166You must not; haste you to your monument,
2167While I make speed to Cæsar.

Cleopatra.

While I make speed to Cæsar. Cæsar! No,
2168I have no business with him.

Alexas.

I have no business with him. I can work him
2169To spare your life, and let this madam perish.

Cleopatra.

2170Base fawning wretch! wouldst thou betray him too?
2171Hence from my sight! I will not hear a traitor;
2172'Twas thy design brought all this ruin on us. –
2173Serapion, thou art honest; counsel me:
2174But haste, each moment's precious.

Serapion.

2175Retire; you must not yet see Antony.
2176He who began this mischief,
2177'Tis just he tempt the danger; let him clear you:
2178And, since he offered you his servile tongue,
2179To gain a poor precarious life from Cæsar,
2180Let him expose that fawning eloquence,
2181And speak to Antony.

Alexas.

And speak to Antony. O heavens! I dare not;
2182I meet my certain death.

Cleopatra.

I meet my certain death. Slave, thou deservest it. –
2183Not that I fear my lord, will I avoid him;
2184I know him noble: when he banished me,
2185And thought me false, he scorned to take my life;
2186But I'll be justified, and then die with him.

Alexas.

2187O pity me, and let me follow you.

Cleopatra.

2188To death, if thou stir hence. Speak, if thou canst,
2189Now for thy life, which basely thou wouldst save;
2190While mine I prize at –this! Come, good Serapion.

[Exeunt CLEOPATRA, SERAPION, CHARMION, and IRAS.]

Alexas.

2191O that I less could fear to lose this being,
2192Which, like a snowball in my coward hand,
2193The more 'tis grasped, the faster melts away.
2194Poor reason! what a wretched aid art thou!
2195For still, in spite of thee,
2196These two long lovers, soul and body, dread
2197Their final separation. Let me think:
2198What can I say, to save myself from death?
2199No matter what becomes of Cleopatra.

Antony.

2200[Within.] Which way? where?

Ventidius.

[Within.] Which way? where? [Within.] This leads to the monument.

Alexas.

2201Ah me! I hear him; yet I'm unprepared:
2202My gift of lying's gone;
2203And this court-devil, which I so oft have raised,
2204Forsakes me at my need. I dare not stay;
2205Yet cannot far go hence.

[Exit.]

Enter ANTONY and VENTIDIUS.

Antony.

2206O happy Cæsar! thou hast men to lead:
2207Think not 'tis thou hast conquered Antony;
2208But Rome has conquered Egypt. I'm betrayed.

Ventidius.

2209Curse on this treacherous train!
2210Their soil and heaven infect them all with baseness:
2211And their young souls come tainted to the world
2212With the first breath they draw.

Antony.

2213The original villain sure no god created;
2214He was a bastard of the sun, by Nile,
2215Aped into man; with all his mother's mud
2216Crusted about his soul.

Ventidius.

Crusted about his soul. The nation is
2217One universal traitor; and their queen
2218The very spirit and extract of them all.

Antony.

2219Is there yet left
2220A possibility of aid from valour?
2221Is there one god unsworn to my destruction?
2222The least unmortgaged hope? for, if there be,
2223Methinks I cannot fall beneath the fate
2224Of such a boy as Cæsar.
2225The world's one half is yet in Antony;
2226And from each limb of it that's hewed away,
2227The soul comes back to me.

Ventidius.

The soul comes back to me. There yet remain
2228Three legions in the town. The last assault
2229Lopt off the rest; if death be your design, –
2230As I must wish it now, –these are sufficient
2231To make a heap about us of dead foes,
2232An honest pile for burial.

Antony.

An honest pile for burial. They are enough.
2233We'll not divide our stars; but, side by side,
2234Fight emulous, and with malicious eyes
2235Survey each other's acts: So every death
2236Thou giv'st, I'll take on me, as a just debt,
2237And pay thee back a soul.

Ventidius.

2238Now you shall see I love you. Not a word
2239Of chiding more. By my few hours of life,
2240I am so pleased with this brave Roman fate,
2241That I would not be Cæsar, to outlive you.
2242When we put off this flesh, and mount together,
2243I shall be shown to all the ethereal crowd, –
2244Lo, this is he who died with Antony!

Antony.

2245Who knows, but we may pierce through all their troops,
2246And reach my veterans yet? 'tis worth the 'tempting,
2247To o'erleap this gulf of fate,
2248And leave our wond'ring destinies behind.

Enter ALEXAS, trembling.

Ventidius.

2249See, see, that villain!
2250See Cleopatra stamped upon that face,
2251With all her cunning, all her arts of falsehood!
2252How she looks out through those dissembling eyes!
2253How he has set his countenance for deceit,
2254And promises a lie, before he speaks!
2255Let me despatch him first.

[Drawing.]

Alexas.

Let me despatch him first. O spare me, spare me!

Antony.

2256Hold; he's not worth your killing. –On thy life,
2257Which thou may'st keep, because I scorn to take it,
2258No syllable to justify thy queen;
2259Save thy base tongue its office.

Alexas.

Save thy base tongue its office. Sir, she is gone,
2260Where she shall never be molested more
2261By love, or you.

Antony.

By love, or you. Fled to her Dolabella!
2262Die, traitor! I revoke my promise! die!

[Going to kill him.]

Alexas.

2263O hold! she is not fled.

Antony.

O hold! she is not fled. She is: my eyes
2264Are open to her falsehood; my whole life
2265Has been a golden dream of love and friendship;
2266But, now I wake, I'm like a merchant, roused
2267From soft repose, to see his vessel sinking,
2268And all his wealth cast over. Ungrateful woman!
2269Who followed me, but as the swallow summer,
2270Hatching her young ones in my kindly beams,
2271Singing her flatteries to my morning wake:
2272But, now my winter comes, she spreads her wings,
2273And seeks the spring of Cæsar.

Alexas.

And seeks the spring of Cæsar. Think not so:
2274Her fortunes have, in all things, mixed with yours.
2275Had she betrayed her naval force to Rome,
2276How easily might she have gone to Cæsar,
2277Secure by such a bribe!

Ventidius.

Secure by such a bribe! She sent it first,
2278To be more welcome after.

Antony.

To be more welcome after. 'Tis too plain;
2279Else would she have appeared, to clear herself.

Alexas.

2280Too fatally she has: she could not bear
2281To be accused by you; but shut herself
2282Within her monument; looked down and sighed;
2283While, from her unchanged face, the silent tears
2284Dropt, as they had not leave, but stole their parting.
2285Some undistinguished words she inly murmured;
2286Al last, she raised her eyes; and, with such looks
2287As dying Lucrece cast –

Antony.

As dying Lucrece cast – My heart forebodes –

Ventidius.

2288All for the best: –Go on.

Alexas.

All for the best: –Go on. She snatched her poniard,
2289And, ere we could prevent the fatal blow,
2290Plunged it within her breast; then turned to me:
2291Go, bear my lord, said she, my last farewell;
2292And ask him, if he yet suspect my faith.
2293More she was saying, but death rushed betwixt.
2294She half pronounced your name with her last breath,
2295And buried half within her.

Ventidius.

And buried half within her. Heaven be praised!

Antony.

2296Then art thou innocent, my poor dear love,
2297And art thou dead?
2298O those two words! their sound should be divided:
2299Hadst thou been false, and died; or hadst thou lived,
2300And hadst been true –But innocence and death!
2301This shows not well above. Then what am I,
2302The murderer of this truth, this innocence!
2303Thoughts cannot form themselves in words so horrid
2304As can express my guilt!

Ventidius.

2305Is't come to this? The gods have been too gracious;
2306And thus you thank them for it!

Antony.

2307[to ALEXAS.] Why stayest thou here?
2308Is it for thee to spy upon my soul,
2309And see its inward mourning? Get thee hence;
2310Thou art not worthy to behold, what now
2311Becomes a Roman emperor to perform.

Alexas.

2312He loves her still:
2313His grief betrays it. Good! the joy to find
2314She's yet alive, completes the reconcilement.
2315I've saved myself, and her. But, oh! the Romans!
2316Fate comes too fast upon my wit,
2317Hunts me too hard, and meets me at each double.

[Exit.]

Ventidius.

2318Would she had died a little sooner, though!aparte
2319Before Octavia went, you might have treated:
2320Now 'twill look tame, and would not be received.
2321Come, rouse yourself, and let's die warm together.

Antony.

2322I will not fight: there's no more work for war.
2323The business of my angry hours is done.

Ventidius.

2324Cæsar is at your gates.

Antony.

Cæsar is at your gates. Why, let him enter;
2325He's welcome now.

Ventidius.

He's welcome now. What lethargy has crept into your soul?

Antony.

2326'Tis but a scorn of life, and just desire
2327To free myself from bondage.

Ventidius.

2328Do it bravely.

Antony.

2329I will; but not by fighting. O Ventidius!
2330What should I fight for now? –my queen is dead.
2331I was but great for her; my power, my empire,
2332Were but my merchandise to buy her love;
2333And conquered kings, my factors. Now she's dead,
2334Let Cæsar take the world, –
2335An empty circle, since the jewel's gone
2336Which made it worth my strife: my being's nauseous;
2337For all the bribes of life are gone away.

Ventidius.

2338Would you be taken?

Antony.

Would you be taken? Yes, I would be taken;
2339But, as a Roman ought, –dead, my Ventidius:
2340For I'll convey my soul from Cæsar's reach,
2341And lay down life myself. 'Tis time the world
2342Should have a lord, and know whom to obey.
2343We two have kept its homage in suspense,
2344And bent the globe, on whose each side we trod,
2345Till it was dented inwards. Let him walk
2346Alone upon't: I'm weary of my part.
2347My torch is out; and the world stands before me,
2348Like a black desert at the approach of night:
2349I'll lay me down, and stray no farther on.

Ventidius.

2350I could be grieved,
2351But that I'll not outlive you: choose your death;
2352For, I have seen him in such various shapes,
2353I care not which I take: I'm only troubled,
2354The life I bear is worn to such a rag,
2355'Tis scarce worth giving. I could wish, indeed,
2356We threw it from us with a better grace;
2357That, like two lions taken in the toils,
2358We might at least thrust out our paws, and wound
2359The hunters that enclose us.

Antony.

2360I have thought on it.
2361Ventidius, you must live.

Ventidius.

Ventidius, you must live. I must not, sir.

Antony.

2362Wilt thou not live, to speak some good of me?
2363To stand by my fair fame, and guard the approaches
2364From the ill tongues of men?

Ventidius.

From the ill tongues of men? Who shall guard mine,
2365For living after you?

Antony.

For living after you? Say, I command it.

Ventidius.

2366If we die well, our deaths will speak themselves
2367And need no living witness.

Antony.

And need no living witness. Thou hast loved me,
2368And fain I would reward thee. I must die;
2369Kill me, and take the merit of my death,
2370To make thee friends with Cæsar.

Ventidius.

To make thee friends with Cæsar. Thank your kindness.
2371You said I loved you; and in recompense,
2372You bid me turn a traitor: Did I think
2373You would have used me thus? –that I should die
2374With a hard thought of you?

Antony.

With a hard thought of you? Forgive me, Roman.
2375Since I have heard of Cleopatra's death,
2376My reason bears no rule upon my tongue,
2377But lets my thoughts break all at random out.
2378I've thought better; do not deny me twice.

Ventidius.

2379By Heaven I will not.
2380Let it not be to outlive you.

Antony.

2381Kill me first,
2382And then die thou; for 'tis but just thou serve
2383Thy friend before thyself.

Ventidius.

Thy friend before thyself. Give me your hand.
2384We soon shall meet again. Now, farewell, emperor! –

[Embrace.]

2385Methinks that word's too cold to be my last:
2386Since death sweeps all distinctions, farewell, friend!
2387That's all –
2388I will not make a business of a trifle;
2389And yet I cannot look on you, and kill you;
2390Pray turn your face.

Antony.

Pray turn your face. I do: strike home, be sure.

Ventidius.

2391Home as my sword will reach.

[Kills himself.]

Antony.

Home as my sword will reach. Oh, thou mistak'st;
2392That wound was none of thine; give it me back;
2393Thou robb'st me of my death.

Ventidius.

Thou robb'st me of my death. I do indeed;
2394But think 'tis the first time I e'er deceived you,
2395If that may plead my pardon. –And you, gods,
2396Forgive me, if you will; for I die perjured,
2397Rather than kill my friend.

[Dies.]

Antony.

2398Farewell! Ever my leader, even in death!
2399My queen and thou have got the start of me,
2400And I'm the lag of honour . –Gone so soon?
2401Is Death no more? he used him carelessly,
2402With a familiar kindness: ere he knocked,
2403Ran to the door, and took him in his arms,
2404As who should say –You're welcome at all hours,
2405A friend need give no warning. Books had spoiled him;
2406For all the learned are cowards by profession.
2407'Tis not worth
2408My further thought; for death, for aught I know,
2409Is but to think no more. Here's to be satisfied.

[Falls on his sword.]

2410I've missed my heart. O unperforming hand!
2411Thou never couldst have erred in a worse time.
2412My fortune jades me to the last; and death,
2413Like a great man, takes state, and makes me wait
2414For my admittance. –

[Trampling within.]

2415Some, perhaps, from Cæsar:
2416If he should find me living, and suspect
2417That I played booty with my life! I'll mend
2418My work, ere they can reach me.

[Rises upon his knees.]

Enter CLEOPATRA, CHARMION, and IRAS.

Cleopatra.

2419Where is my lord? where is he?

Charmion.

Where is my lord? where is he? There he lies,
2420And dead Ventidius by him.

Cleopatra.

2421My fears were prophets; I am come too late.
2422O that accursed Alexas!

[Runs to him.]

Antony.

O that accursed Alexas! Art thou living?
2423Or am I dead before I knew, and thou
2424The first kind ghost that meets me?

Cleopatra.

The first kind ghost that meets me? Help me seat him.
2425Send quickly, send for help!

[They place him in a chair.]

Antony.

Send quickly, send for help! I am answered.
2426We live both. Sit thee down, my Cleopatra:
2427I'll make the most I can of life, to stay
2428A moment more with thee.

Cleopatra.

A moment more with thee. How is it with you?

Antony.

2429'Tis as with a man
2430Removing in a hurry; all packed up,
2431But one dear jewel that his haste forgot;
2432And he, for that, returns upon the spur:
2433So I come back for thee.

Cleopatra.

2434Too long, ye heavens, you have been cruel to me:
2435Now show your mended faith, and give me back
2436His fleeting life!

Antony.

His fleeting life! It will not be, my love;
2437I keep my soul by force.
2438Say but, thou art not false.

Cleopatra.

Say but, thou art not false. 'Tis now too late
2439To say I'm true: I'll prove it, and die with you.
2440Unknown to me, Alexas feigned my death:
2441Which, when I knew, I hasted to prevent
2442This fatal consequence. My fleet betrayed
2443Both you and me.

Antony.

Both you and me. And Dolabella –

Cleopatra.

Both you and me. And Dolabella – Scarce
2444Esteemed before he loved; but hated now.

Antony.

2445Enough: my life's not long enough for more.
2446Thou say'st, thou wilt come after: I believe thee;
2447For I can now believe whate'er thou sayest,
2448That we may part more kindly.

Cleopatra.

That we may part more kindly. I will come:
2449Doubt not, my life, I'll come, and quickly too:
2450Cæsar shall triumph o'er no part of thee.

Antony.

2451But grieve not, while thou stayest,
2452My last disastrous times:
2453Think we have had a clear and glorious day
2454And Heaven did kindly to delay the storm,
2455Just till our close of evening. Ten years' love,
2456And not a moment lost, but all improved
2457To the utmost joys, –what ages have we lived?
2458And now to die each other's; and, so dying,
2459While hand in hand we walk in groves below,
2460Whole troops of lovers' ghosts shall flock about us,
2461And all the train be ours.

Cleopatra.

2462Your words are like the notes of dying swans,
2463Too sweet to last. Were there so many hours
2464For your unkindness, and not one for love?

Antony.

2465No, not a minute. –This one kiss –more worth
2466Than all I leave to Cæsar.

Cleopatra.

2467O tell me so again,
2468And take ten thousand kisses for that word.
2469My lord, my lord! speak, if you yet have being;
2470Sign to me, if you cannot speak; or cast
2471One look! Do anything that shows you live.

Iras.

2472He's gone too far to hear you;
2473And this you see, a lump of senseless clay,
2474The leavings of a soul.

Charmion.

The leavings of a soul. Remember, madam,
2475He charged you not to grieve.

Cleopatra.

He charged you not to grieve. And I'll obey him.
2476I have not loved a Roman, not to know
2477What should become his wife; his wife, my Charmion!
2478For 'tis to that high title I aspire;
2479And now I'll not die less. Let dull Octavia
2480Survive, to mourn him dead: My nobler fate
2481Shall knit our spousals with a tie too strong
2482For Roman laws to break.

Iras.

For Roman laws to break. Will you then die?

Cleopatra.

2483Why shouldst thou make that question?

Iras.

Why shouldst thou make that question? Cæsar is merciful.

Cleopatra.

2484Let him be so
2485To those that want his mercy: My poor lord
2486Made no such covenant with him, to spare me
2487When he was dead. Yield me to Cæsar's pride?
2488What! to be led in triumph through the streets,
2489A spectacle to base plebeian eyes;
2490While some dejected friend of Antony's,
2491Close in a corner, shakes his head, and mutters
2492A secret curse on her who ruined him!
2493I'll none of that.

Charmion.

I'll none of that. Whatever you resolve,
2494I'll follow, even to death.

Iras.

I'll follow, even to death. I only feared
2495For you; but more should fear to live without you.

Cleopatra.

2496Why, now, 'tis as it should be, Quick, my friends,
2497Despatch; ere this, the town's in Cæsar's hands:
2498My lord looks down concerned, and fears my stay,
2499Lest I should be surprising;
2500Keep him not waiting for his love too long.
2501You, Charmion, bring my crown and richest jewels;
2502With them, the wreath of victory I made
2503(Vain augury!) for him, who now lies dead:
2504You, Iras, bring the cure of all our ills.

Iras.

2505The aspics, madam?

Cleopatra.

The aspics, madam? Must I bid you twice?

[Exit CHARMION and IRAS.]

2506'Tis sweet to die, when they would force life on me,
2507To rush into the dark abode of death,
2508And seize him first; if he be like my love,
2509He is not frightful, sure.
2510We're now alone, in secrecy and silence;
2511And is not this like lovers? I may kiss
2512These pale, cold lips; Octavia does not see me:
2513And, oh! 'tis better far to have him thus,
2514Than see him in her arms. –Oh, welcome, welcome!

Enter CHARMION and IRAS.

Charmion.

2515What must be done?

Cleopatra.

What must be done? Short ceremony, friends;
2516But yet it must be decent. First, this laurel
2517Shall crown my hero's head: he fell not basely,
2518Nor left his shield behind him. –Only thou
2519Couldst triumph o'er thyself; and thou alone
2520Wert worthy so to triumph.

Charmion.

Wert worthy so to triumph. To what end
2521These ensigns of your pomp and royalty?

Cleopatra.

2522Dull, that thou art! why 'tis to meet my love;
2523As when I saw him first, on Cydnus' bank,
2524All sparkling, like a goddess: so adorned,
2525I'll find him once again; my second spousals
2526Shall match my first in glory. Haste, haste, both,
2527And dress the bride of Antony.

Charmion.

And dress the bride of Antony. 'Tis done.

Cleopatra.

2528Now seat me by my lord. I claim this place;
2529For I must conquer Cæsar too, like him,
2530And win my share of the world. –Hail, you dear relics
2531Of my immortal love!
2532O let no impious hand remove you hence:
2533But rest for ever here! Let Egypt give
2534His death that peace, which it denied his life. –
2535Reach me the casket.

Iras.

Reach me the casket. Underneath the fruit
2536The aspic lies.

Cleopatra.

The aspic lies. Welcome, thou kind deceiver!

[Putting aside the leaves.]

2537Thou best of thieves; who, with an easy key,
2538Dost open life, and, unperceived by us,
2539Even steal us from ourselves; discharging so
2540Death's dreadful office, better than himself;
2541Touching our limbs so gently into slumber,
2542That Death stands by, deceived by his own image,
2543And thinks himself but sleep.

Serapion.

And thinks himself but sleep. [Within.] The queen, where is she?
2544The town is yielded, Cæsar's at the gates.

Cleopatra.

2545He comes too late to invade the rights of death.
2546Haste, bare my arm, and rouse the serpent's fury.

[Holds out her arm, and draws it back.]

2547Coward flesh,
2548Wouldst thou conspire with Cæsar to betray me,
2549As thou wert none of mine? I'll force thee to it,
2550And not be sent by him.
2551But bring, myself, my soul to Antony.

[Turns aside, and then shows her arm bloody.]

2552Take hence; the work is done.

Serapion.

Take hence; the work is done. [Within.] Break ope the door,
2553And guard the traitor well.

Charmion.

And guard the traitor well. The next is ours.

Iras.

2554Now, Charmion, to be worthy
2555Of our great queen and mistress.

[They apply the aspics.]

Cleopatra.

2556Already, death, I feel thee in my veins:
2557I go with such a will to find my lord,
2558That we shall quickly meet.
2559A heavy numbness creeps through every limb,
2560And now 'tis at my head: My eyelids fall
2561And my dear love is vanish'd in a mist.
2562Where shall I find him, where? O turn me to him,
2563And lay me on his breast! – Cæsar, thy worst;
2564Now part us, if thou canst.

[Dies.]

[IRAS sinks down at her feet, and dies; CHARMION stands behind her chair, as dressing her head.]

Enter SERAPION, two Priests, ALEXAS bound, Egyptians.

Priest.

Now part us, if thou canst. Behold, Serapion,
2565What havoc death has made!

Serapion.

What havoc death has made! 'Twas what I feared. –
2566Charmion, is this well done?

Charmion.

2567Yes, 'tis well done, and like a queen, the last
2568Of her great race: I follow her.

[Sinks down: dies.]

Alexas.

Of her great race: I follow her. 'Tis true,
2569She has done well: Much better thus to die,
2570Than live to make a holiday in Rome.

Serapion.

2571See, see how the lovers sit in state together,
2572As they were giving laws to half mankind!
2573The impression of a smile, left in her face,
2574Shows she died pleased with him for whom she lived,
2575And went to charm him in another world.
2576Cæsar's just entering: grief has now no leisure.
2577Secure that villain, as our pledge of safety,
2578To grace the imperial triumph. –Sleep, blest pair,
2579Secure from human chance, long ages out,
2580While all the storms of fate fly o'er your tomb;
2581And fame to late posterity shall tell,
2582No lovers lived so great, or died so well.

[Exeunt.]
