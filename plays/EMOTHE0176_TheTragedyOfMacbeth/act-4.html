1219In the poisoned entrails throw.

1221Days and nights has thirty-one

Scene 2

Enter Macduff’s Wife, her Son, and Ross.

LADY MACDUFF

1371What had he done to make him fly the land?

ROSS

1372You must have patience, madam.

LADY MACDUFF

You must have patience, madam. He had none.
1373His flight was madness. When our actions do not,
1374Our fears do make us traitors.

ROSS

Our fears do make us traitors. You know not
1375Whether it was his wisdom or his fear.

LADY MACDUFF

1376Wisdom? To leave his wife, to leave his babes,
1377His mansion and his titles in a place
1378From whence himself does fly? He loves us not;
1379He wants the natural touch; for the poor wren,
1380The most diminutive of birds, will fight,
1381Her young ones in her nest, against the owl.
1382All is the fear, and nothing is the love,
1383As little is the wisdom, where the flight
1384So runs against all reason.

ROSS

So runs against all reason. My dearest coz,
1385I pray you school yourself. But for your husband,
1386He is noble, wise, judicious, and best knows
1387The fits o’ th’ season. I dare not speak much further;
1388But cruel are the times when we are traitors
1389And do not know ourselves; when we hold rumor
1390From what we fear, yet know not what we fear,
1391But float upon a wild and violent sea
1392Each way and move —I take my leave of you.
1393Shall not be long but I’ll be here again.
1394Things at the worst will cease or else climb upward
1395To what they were before. —My pretty cousin,
1396Blessing upon you.

LADY MACDUFF

1397Fathered he is, and yet he’s fatherless.

ROSS

1398I am so much a fool, should I stay longer
1399It would be my disgrace and your discomfort.
1400I take my leave at once.

Ross exits.

LADY MACDUFF

1401Sirrah, your father’s dead.
1402And what will you do now? How will you live?

SON

1403As birds do, mother.

LADY MACDUFF

As birds do, mother. What, with worms and flies?

SON

1404With what I get, I mean; and so do they.

LADY MACDUFF

1405Poor bird, thou ’dst never fear the net nor lime,
1406The pitfall nor the gin.

SON

1407Why should I, mother? Poor birds they are not set for.
1408My father is not dead, for all your saying.

LADY MACDUFF

1409Yes, he is dead. How wilt thou do for a father?

SON

1410Nay, how will you do for a husband?

LADY MACDUFF

1411Why, I can buy me twenty at any market.

SON

1412Then you’ll buy ’em to sell again.

LADY MACDUFF

1413Thou speak’st with all thy wit,
1414And yet, i’ faith, with wit enough for thee.

SON

Was my father a traitor, mother?

LADY MACDUFF

Ay, that he was.

SON

What is a traitor?

LADY MACDUFF

Why, one that swears and lies.

SON

And be all traitors that do so?

LADY MACDUFF

Every one that does so is a traitor

and must be hanged.

SON

And must they all be hanged that swear and lie?

LADY MACDUFF

Every one.

SON

Who must hang them?

LADY MACDUFF

Why, the honest men.

SON

Then the liars and swearers are fools, for there

are liars and swearers enough to beat the honest

men and hang up them.

LADY MACDUFF

Now God help thee, poor monkey! But

how wilt thou do for a father?

SON

If he were dead, you’d weep for him. If you would

not, it were a good sign that I should quickly have a

new father.

LADY MACDUFF

Poor prattler, how thou talk’st!

Enter a Messenger.

MESSENGER

1415Bless you, fair dame. I am not to you known,
1416Though in your state of honor I am perfect.
1417I doubt some danger does approach you nearly.
1418If you will take a homely man’s advice,
1419Be not found here. Hence with your little ones!
1420To fright you thus methinks I am too savage;
1421To do worse to you were fell cruelty,
1422Which is too nigh your person. Heaven preserve you!
1423I dare abide no longer.

Messenger exits.

LADY MACDUFF

I dare abide no longer. Whither should I fly?
1424I have done no harm. But I remember now
1425I am in this earthly world, where to do harm
1426Is often laudable, to do good sometime
1427Accounted dangerous folly. Why then, alas,
1428Do I put up that womanly defense
1429To say I have done no harm?

Enter Murderers.

MURDERER

1430Where is your husband?

LADY MACDUFF

1431I hope in no place so unsanctified
1432Where such as thou mayst find him.

MURDERER

SON

1433Thou liest, thou shag-eared villain!

MURDERER

Scene 3

Enter Malcolm and Macduff.

MALCOLM

1436Let us seek out some desolate shade and there
1437Weep our sad bosoms empty.

MACDUFF

Weep our sad bosoms empty. Let us rather
1438Hold fast the mortal sword and, like good men,
1439Bestride our ⸢downfall’n⸣ birthdom. Each new morn
1440New widows howl, new orphans cry, new sorrows
1441Strike heaven on the face, that it resounds
1442As if it felt with Scotland, and yelled out
1443Like syllable of dolor.

MALCOLM

1444What I believe, I’ll wail;
1445What know, believe; and what I can redress,
1446As I shall find the time to friend, I will.
1447What you have spoke, it may be so, perchance.
1448This tyrant, whose sole name blisters our tongues,
1449Was once thought honest. You have loved him well.
1450He hath not touched you yet. I am young, but something
1451You may ⸢deserve⸣ of him through me, and wisdom
1452To offer up a weak, poor, innocent lamb
1453T’ appease an angry god.

MACDUFF

1454I am not treacherous.

MALCOLM

I am not treacherous. But Macbeth is.
1455A good and virtuous nature may recoil
1456In an imperial charge. But I shall crave your pardon.
1457That which you are, my thoughts cannot transpose.
1458Angels are bright still, though the brightest fell.
1459Though all things foul would wear the brows of grace,
1460Yet grace must still look so.

MACDUFF

Yet grace must still look so. I have lost my hopes.

MALCOLM

1461Perchance even there where I did find my doubts.
1462Why in that rawness left you wife and child,
1463Those precious motives, those strong knots of love,
1464Without leave-taking? I pray you,
1465Let not my jealousies be your dishonors,
1466But mine own safeties. You may be rightly just,
1467Whatever I shall think.

MACDUFF

Whatever I shall think. Bleed, bleed, poor country!
1468Great tyranny, lay thou thy basis sure,
1469For goodness dare not check thee. Wear thou thy wrongs;
1470The title is affeered. —Fare thee well, lord.
1471I would not be the villain that thou think’st
1472For the whole space that’s in the tyrant’s grasp,
1473And the rich East to boot.

MALCOLM

And the rich East to boot. Be not offended.
1474I speak not as in absolute fear of you.
1475I think our country sinks beneath the yoke.
1476It weeps, it bleeds, and each new day a gash
1477Is added to her wounds. I think withal
1478There would be hands uplifted in my right;
1479And here from gracious England have I offer
1480Of goodly thousands. But, for all this,
1481When I shall tread upon the tyrant’s head
1482Or wear it on my sword, yet my poor country
1483Shall have more vices than it had before,
1484More suffer, and more sundry ways than ever,
1485By him that shall succeed.

MACDUFF

By him that shall succeed. What should he be?

MALCOLM

1486It is myself I mean, in whom I know
1487All the particulars of vice so grafted
1488That, when they shall be opened, black Macbeth
1489Will seem as pure as snow, and the poor state
1490Esteem him as a lamb, being compared
1491With my confineless harms.

MACDUFF

With my confineless harms. Not in the legions
1492Of horrid hell can come a devil more damned
1493In evils to top Macbeth.

MALCOLM

In evils to top Macbeth. I grant him bloody,
1494Luxurious, avaricious, false, deceitful,
1495Sudden, malicious, smacking of every sin
1496That has a name. But there’s no bottom, none,
1497In my voluptuousness. Your wives, your daughters,
1498Your matrons, and your maids could not fill up
1499The cistern of my lust, and my desire
1500All continent impediments would o’erbear
1501That did oppose my will. Better Macbeth
1502Than such an one to reign.

MACDUFF

Than such an one to reign. Boundless intemperance
1503In nature is a tyranny. It hath been
1504Th’ untimely emptying of the happy throne
1505And fall of many kings. But fear not yet
1506To take upon you what is yours. You may
1507Convey your pleasures in a spacious plenty
1508And yet seem cold —the time you may so hoodwink.
1509We have willing dames enough. There cannot be
1510That vulture in you to devour so many
1511As will to greatness dedicate themselves,
1512Finding it so inclined.

MALCOLM

1513In my most ill-composed affection such
1514A stanchless avarice that, were I king,
1515I should cut off the nobles for their lands,
1516Desire his jewels, and this other’s house;
1517And my more-having would be as a sauce
1518To make me hunger more, that I should forge
1519Quarrels unjust against the good and loyal,
1520Destroying them for wealth.

MACDUFF

1521Sticks deeper, grows with more pernicious root
1522Than summer-seeming lust, and it hath been
1523The sword of our slain kings. Yet do not fear.
1524Scotland hath foisons to fill up your will
1525Of your mere own. All these are portable,
1526With other graces weighed.

MALCOLM

1527But I have none. The king-becoming graces,
1528As justice, verity, temp’rance, stableness,
1529Bounty, perseverance, mercy, lowliness,
1530Devotion, patience, courage, fortitude,
1531I have no relish of them but abound
1532In the division of each several crime,
1533Acting it many ways. Nay, had I power, I should
1534Pour the sweet milk of concord into hell,
1535Uproar the universal peace, confound
1536All unity on earth.

MACDUFF

MALCOLM

1538I am as I have spoken.

MACDUFF

1539No, not to live. —O nation miserable,
1540With an untitled tyrant bloody-sceptered,

1545Was a most sainted king. The queen that bore thee,
1546Oft’ner upon her knees than on her feet,
1547Died every day she lived. Fare thee well.
1548These evils thou repeat’st upon thyself

1550Thy hope ends here!

1551Child of integrity, hath from my soul
1552Wiped the black scruples, reconciled my thoughts
1553To thy good truth and honor. Devilish Macbeth

1556From overcredulous haste. But God above

1559Unspeak mine own detraction, here abjure
1560The taints and blames I laid upon myself

1562Unknown to woman, never was forsworn,
1563Scarcely have coveted what was mine own,

MACDUFF

1574Such welcome and unwelcome things at once
1575’Tis hard to reconcile.

MALCOLM

1584A most miraculous work in this good king,

1589The mere despair of surgery, he cures,
1590Hanging a golden stamp about their necks,
1591Put on with holy prayers; and, ’tis spoken,
1592To the succeeding royalty he leaves
1593The healing benediction. With this strange virtue,
1594He hath a heavenly gift of prophecy,
1595And sundry blessings hang about his throne
1596That speak him full of grace.

Enter Ross.

MACDUFF

That speak him full of grace. See who comes here.

MALCOLM

1597My countryman, but yet I know him ⸢not.⸣

MACDUFF

1598My ever-gentle cousin, welcome hither.

MALCOLM

1599I know him now. —Good God betimes remove
1600The means that makes us strangers!

ROSS

The means that makes us strangers! Sir, amen.

MACDUFF

1601Stands Scotland where it did?

ROSS

1602Almost afraid to know itself. It cannot
1603Be called our mother, but our grave, where nothing
1604But who knows nothing is once seen to smile;
1605Where sighs and groans and shrieks that rent the air
1606Are made, not marked; where violent sorrow seems
1607A modern ecstasy. The dead man’s knell
1608Is there scarce asked for who, and good men’s lives
1609Expire before the flowers in their caps,
1610Dying or ere they sicken.

MACDUFF

1611O relation too nice and yet too true!

MALCOLM

1612What’s the newest grief?

ROSS

1614Each minute teems a new one.

ROSS

1615Why, well.

MACDUFF

ROSS

1617Well too.

MACDUFF

1631An older and a better soldier none

1633This comfort with the like. But I have words
1634That would be howled out in the desert air,
1635Where hearing should not latch them.

1637Due to some single breast?

ROSS

Due to some single breast? No mind that’s honest
1638But in it shares some woe, though the main part
1639Pertains to you alone.

MACDUFF

Pertains to you alone. If it be mine,
1640Keep it not from me. Quickly let me have it.

ROSS

1641Let not your ears despise my tongue forever,
1642Which shall possess them with the heaviest sound
1643That ever yet they heard.

MACDUFF

That ever yet they heard. Hum! I guess at it.

ROSS

1644Your castle is surprised, your wife and babes
1645Savagely slaughtered. To relate the manner
1646Were on the quarry of these murdered deer
1647To add the death of you.

MALCOLM

To add the death of you. Merciful heaven!—
1648What, man, ne’er pull your hat upon your brows.
1649Give sorrow words. The grief that does not speak
1650Whispers the o’erfraught heart and bids it break.

MACDUFF

1651My children too?

ROSS

1652Wife, children, servants, all that could be found.

MACDUFF

1653And I must be from thence? My wife killed too?

ROSS

1654I have said.

MALCOLM

1655Be comforted.
1656Let’s make us med’cines of our great revenge
1657To cure this deadly grief.

MACDUFF

1658He has no children. All my pretty ones?
1659Did you say “all”? O hell-kite! All?
1660What, all my pretty chickens and their dam
1661At one fell swoop?

MALCOLM

1662Dispute it like a man.

MACDUFF

1663I shall do so,
1664But I must also feel it as a man.
1665I cannot but remember such things were
1666That were most precious to me. Did heaven look on
1667And would not take their part? Sinful Macduff,
1668They were all struck for thee! Naught that I am,
1669Not for their own demerits, but for mine,
1670Fell slaughter on their souls. Heaven rest them now.

MALCOLM

1671Be this the whetstone of your sword. Let grief
1672Convert to anger. Blunt not the heart; enrage it.

MACDUFF

1673O, I could play the woman with mine eyes
1674And braggart with my tongue! But, gentle heavens,
1675Cut short all intermission! Front to front
1676Bring thou this fiend of Scotland and myself.
1677Within my sword’s length set him. If he ’scape,
1678Heaven forgive him too.

MALCOLM

Heaven forgive him too. This ⸢tune⸣ goes manly.
1679Come, go we to the King. Our power is ready;
1680Our lack is nothing but our leave. Macbeth
1681Is ripe for shaking, and the powers above
1682Put on their instruments. Receive what cheer you may.
1683The night is long that never finds the day.

They exit.
