Act III

Scene 1

Enter Banquo.

BANQUO

754Thou hast it now —King, Cawdor, Glamis, all
755As the Weïrd Women promised, and I fear
756Thou played’st most foully for ’t. Yet it was said
757It should not stand in thy posterity,
758But that myself should be the root and father
759Of many kings. If there come truth from them
760(As upon thee, Macbeth, their speeches shine)
761Why, by the verities on thee made good,
762May they not be my oracles as well,
763And set me up in hope? But hush, no more.

Sennet sounded.

Enter Macbeth as King, Lady ⸢Macbeth,⸣ Lennox, Ross, Lords, and Attendants.

MACBETH

764Here’s our chief guest.

LADY MACBETH

Here’s our chief guest. If he had been forgotten,
765It had been as a gap in our great feast
766And all-thing unbecoming.

MACBETH

767Tonight we hold a solemn supper, sir,
768And I’ll request your presence.

BANQUO

And I’ll request your presence. Let your Highness
769Command upon me, to the which my duties
770Are with a most indissoluble tie
771Forever knit.

MACBETH

772Ride you this afternoon?

BANQUO

773Ay, my good lord.

MACBETH

774We should have else desired your good advice
775(Which still hath been both grave and prosperous)
776In this day’s council, but we’ll take tomorrow.
777Is ’t far you ride?

BANQUO

778As far, my lord, as will fill up the time
779’Twixt this and supper. Go not my horse the better,
780I must become a borrower of the night
781For a dark hour or twain.

MACBETH

For a dark hour or twain. Fail not our feast.

BANQUO

782My lord, I will not.

MACBETH

784In England and in Ireland, not confessing
785Their cruel parricide, filling their hearers
786With strange invention. But of that tomorrow,
787When therewithal we shall have cause of state

789Till you return at night. Goes Fleance with you?

BANQUO

790Ay, my good lord. Our time does call upon ’s.

792And so I do commend you to their backs.
793Farewell.

SECOND MURDERER

865So weary with disasters, tugged with fortune,

867To mend it or be rid on ’t.

MACBETH

868Know Banquo was your enemy.

⸢MURDERERS⸣

MACBETH

871Against my near’st of life. And though I could
872With barefaced power sweep him from my sight

894I’ll call upon you straight. Abide within.

⸢Murderers exit.⸣

903Where our desire is got without content.
904’Tis safer to be that which we destroy

906How now, my lord, why do you keep alone,
907Of sorriest fancies your companions making,
908Using those thoughts which should indeed have died
909With them they think on? Things without all remedy
910Should be without regard. What’s done is done.

MACBETH

911We have scorched the snake, not killed it.
912She’ll close and be herself whilst our poor malice
913Remains in danger of her former tooth.
914But let the frame of things disjoint, both the worlds suffer,
915Ere we will eat our meal in fear, and sleep
916In the affliction of these terrible dreams
917That shake us nightly. Better be with the dead,
918Whom we, to gain our peace, have sent to peace,
919Than on the torture of the mind to lie
920In restless ecstasy. Duncan is in his grave.
921After life’s fitful fever he sleeps well.
922Treason has done his worst; nor steel nor poison,
923Malice domestic, foreign levy, nothing
924Can touch him further.

LADY MACBETH

Can touch him further. Come on, gentle my lord,
925Sleek o’er your rugged looks. Be bright and jovial
926Among your guests tonight.

MACBETH

Among your guests tonight. So shall I, love,
927And so I pray be you. Let your remembrance
928Apply to Banquo; present him eminence
929Both with eye and tongue: unsafe the while that we
930Must lave our honors in these flattering streams
931And make our faces vizards to our hearts,
932Disguising what they are.

LADY MACBETH

Disguising what they are. You must leave this.

MACBETH

933O, full of scorpions is my mind, dear wife!
934Thou know’st that Banquo and his Fleance lives.

LADY MACBETH

935But in them nature’s copy’s not eterne.

MACBETH

936There’s comfort yet; they are assailable.
937Then be thou jocund. Ere the bat hath flown
938His cloistered flight, ere to black Hecate’s summons
939The shard-born beetle with his drowsy hums
940Hath rung night’s yawning peal, there shall be done
941A deed of dreadful note.

LADY MACBETH

A deed of dreadful note. What’s to be done?

MACBETH

942Be innocent of the knowledge, dearest chuck,
943Till thou applaud the deed. —Come, seeling night,
944Scarf up the tender eye of pitiful day
945And with thy bloody and invisible hand
946Cancel and tear to pieces that great bond
947Which keeps me pale. Light thickens, and the crow
948Makes wing to th’ rooky wood.
949Good things of day begin to droop and drowse,
950Whiles night’s black agents to their preys do rouse. —
951Thou marvel’st at my words, but hold thee still.
952Things bad begun make strong themselves by ill.
953So prithee go with me.

They exit.
