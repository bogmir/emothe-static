Act II

Scene 1

Enter Banquo, and Fleance with a torch before him.

BANQUO

How goes the night, boy?

FLEANCE

466The moon is down. I have not heard the clock.

BANQUO

And she goes down at twelve.

FLEANCE

I take ’t ’tis later, sir.

BANQUO

467Hold, take my sword.

⸢He gives his sword to Fleance.⸣

Hold, take my sword. There’s husbandry in heaven;
468Their candles are all out. Take thee that too.
469A heavy summons lies like lead upon me,
470And yet I would not sleep. Merciful powers,
471Restrain in me the cursèd thoughts that nature
472Gives way to in repose.

Enter Macbeth, and a Servant with a torch.

Gives way to in repose. Give me my sword. —Who’s there?

MACBETH

473A friend.

BANQUO

474What, sir, not yet at rest? The King’s abed.
475He hath been in unusual pleasure, and
476Sent forth great largess to your offices.
477This diamond he greets your wife withal,
478By the name of most kind hostess, and shut up
479In measureless content.

⸢He gives Macbeth a diamond.⸣

MACBETH

In measureless content. Being unprepared,
480Our will became the servant to defect,
481Which else should free have wrought.

BANQUO

482All’s well.
483I dreamt last night of the three Weïrd Sisters.
484To you they have showed some truth.

MACBETH

To you they have showed some truth. I think not of them.
485Yet, when we can entreat an hour to serve,
486We would spend it in some words upon that business,
487If you would grant the time.

BANQUO

If you would grant the time. At your kind’st leisure.

MACBETH

488If you shall cleave to my consent, when ’tis,
489It shall make honor for you.

BANQUO

It shall make honor for you. So I lose none
490In seeking to augment it, but still keep
491My bosom franchised and allegiance clear,
492I shall be counseled.

MACBETH

I shall be counseled. Good repose the while.

BANQUO

493Thanks, sir. The like to you.

Banquo ⸢and Fleance⸣ exit.

MACBETH

494Go bid thy mistress, when my drink is ready,
495She strike upon the bell. Get thee to bed.

⸢Servant⸣ exits.

496Is this a dagger which I see before me,
497The handle toward my hand? Come, let me clutch thee.
498I have thee not, and yet I see thee still.
499Art thou not, fatal vision, sensible
500To feeling as to sight? Or art thou but
501A dagger of the mind, a false creation
502Proceeding from the heat-oppressèd brain?
503I see thee yet, in form as palpable
504As this which now I draw.

⸢He draws his dagger.⸣

505Thou marshal’st me the way that I was going,
506And such an instrument I was to use.
507Mine eyes are made the fools o’ th’ other senses
508Or else worth all the rest. I see thee still,
509And, on thy blade and dudgeon, gouts of blood,
510Which was not so before. There’s no such thing.
511It is the bloody business which informs
512Thus to mine eyes. Now o’er the one-half world
513Nature seems dead, and wicked dreams abuse
514The curtained sleep. Witchcraft celebrates
515Pale Hecate’s off’rings, and withered murder,
516Alarumed by his sentinel, the wolf,
517Whose howl’s his watch, thus with his stealthy pace,
518With Tarquin’s ravishing ⸢strides,⸣ towards his design
519Moves like a ghost. Thou ⸢sure⸣and firm-set earth,
520Hear not my steps, which ⸢way they⸣ walk, for fear
521Thy very stones prate of my whereabouts
522And take the present horror from the time,
523Which now suits with it. Whiles I threat, he lives.
524Words to the heat of deeds too cold breath gives.

A bell rings.

525I go, and it is done. The bell invites me.
526Hear it not, Duncan, for it is a knell
527That summons thee to heaven or to hell.

He exits.

Scene 2

Enter Lady ⸢Macbeth.⸣

LADY MACBETH

528That which hath made them drunk hath made me bold.
529What hath quenched them hath given me fire. Hark! —Peace.
530It was the owl that shrieked, the fatal bellman,
531Which gives the stern’st good-night. He is about it.
532The doors are open, and the surfeited grooms
533Do mock their charge with snores. I have drugged their possets,
534That death and nature do contend about them
535Whether they live or die.

MACBETH,

Whether they live or die. ⸢within⸣ Who’s there? what, ho!

LADY MACBETH

536Alack, I am afraid they have awaked,
537And ’tis not done. Th’ attempt and not the deed
538Confounds us. Hark! —I laid their daggers ready;
539He could not miss ’em. Had he not resembled
540My father as he slept, I had done ’t.

Enter Macbeth ⸢with bloody daggers.⸣

541My husband?

MACBETH

542I have done the deed. Didst thou not hear a noise?

LADY MACBETH

543I heard the owl scream and the crickets cry.
544Did not you speak?

MACBETH

Did not you speak? When?

LADY MACBETH

545Now.

MACBETH

Now. As I descended?

LADY MACBETH

546Ay.

MACBETH

547Hark! —Who lies i’ th’ second chamber?

LADY MACBETH

548Donalbain.

MACBETH

549This is a sorry sight.

LADY MACBETH

550A foolish thought, to say a sorry sight.

MACBETH

551There’s one did laugh in ’s sleep, and one cried “Murder!”
552That they did wake each other. I stood and heard them.
553But they did say their prayers and addressed them
554Again to sleep.

LADY MACBETH

Again to sleep. There are two lodged together.

MACBETH

555One cried “God bless us” and “Amen” the other,
556As they had seen me with these hangman’s hands,
557List’ning their fear. I could not say “Amen”
558When they did say “God bless us.”

LADY MACBETH

559Consider it not so deeply.

MACBETH

560But wherefore could not I pronounce “Amen”?
561I had most need of blessing, and “Amen”
562Stuck in my throat.

LADY MACBETH

Stuck in my throat. These deeds must not be thought
563After these ways; so, it will make us mad.

MACBETH

564Methought I heard a voice cry “Sleep no more! Macbeth does murder sleep” —the innocent sleep,
565Sleep that knits up the raveled sleave of care,
566The death of each day’s life, sore labor’s bath,
567Balm of hurt minds, great nature’s second course,
568Chief nourisher in life’s feast.

LADY MACBETH

Chief nourisher in life’s feast. What do you mean?

MACBETH

569Still it cried “Sleep no more!” to all the house.
570“Glamis hath murdered sleep, and therefore Cawdor
571Shall sleep no more. Macbeth shall sleep no more.”

LADY MACBETH

572Who was it that thus cried? Why, worthy thane,
573You do unbend your noble strength to think
574So brainsickly of things. Go get some water
575And wash this filthy witness from your hand. —
576Why did you bring these daggers from the place?
577They must lie there. Go, carry them and smear
578The sleepy grooms with blood.

MACBETH

The sleepy grooms with blood. I’ll go no more.
579I am afraid to think what I have done.
580Look on ’t again I dare not.

LADY MACBETH

Look on ’t again I dare not. Infirm of purpose!
581Give me the daggers. The sleeping and the dead
582Are but as pictures. ’Tis the eye of childhood
583That fears a painted devil. If he do bleed,
584I’ll gild the faces of the grooms withal,
585For it must seem their guilt.

She exits ⸢with the daggers.⸣

Knock within.

MACBETH

For it must seem their guilt. Whence is that knocking?
586How is ’t with me when every noise appalls me?
587What hands are here! Ha, they pluck out mine eyes.
588Will all great Neptune’s ocean wash this blood
589Clean from my hand? No, this my hand will rather
590The multitudinous seas incarnadine,
591Making the green one red.

Enter Lady ⸢Macbeth.⸣

LADY MACBETH

592My hands are of your color, but I shame
593To wear a heart so white.

Knock.

To wear a heart so white. I hear a knocking
594At the south entry. Retire we to our chamber.
595A little water clears us of this deed.
596How easy is it, then! Your constancy
597Hath left you unattended.

Knock.

Hath left you unattended. Hark, more knocking.
598Get on your nightgown, lest occasion call us
599And show us to be watchers. Be not lost
600So poorly in your thoughts.

MACBETH

601To know my deed ’twere best not know myself.

Knock.

602Wake Duncan with thy knocking. I would thou couldst.

They exit.

Scene 3

Knocking within.

Enter a Porter.

PORTER

Here’s a knocking indeed! If a man were porter

of hell gate, he should have old turning the

key. (Knock.) Knock, knock, knock! Who’s there, i’

th’ name of Beelzebub? Here’s a farmer that hanged

himself on th’ expectation of plenty. Come in time!

Have napkins enough about you; here you’ll sweat

for ’t. (Knock.) Knock, knock! Who’s there, in th’

other devil’s name? Faith, here’s an equivocator

that could swear in both the scales against either

scale, who committed treason enough for God’s

sake yet could not equivocate to heaven. O, come in,

equivocator. (Knock.) Knock, knock, knock! Who’s

there? Faith, here’s an English tailor come hither for

stealing out of a French hose. Come in, tailor. Here

you may roast your goose. (Knock.) Knock, knock!

Never at quiet. —What are you? —But this place is

too cold for hell. I’ll devil-porter it no further. I had

thought to have let in some of all professions that go

the primrose way to th’ everlasting bonfire.

(Knock.) Anon, anon!

⸢The Porter opens the door to ⸣ Macduff and Lennox.

I pray you, remember the porter.

MACDUFF

603Was it so late, friend, ere you went to bed
604That you do lie so late?

PORTER

Faith, sir, we were carousing till the second

cock, and drink, sir, is a great provoker of three

things.

MACDUFF

What three things does drink especially

provoke?

PORTER

Marry, sir, nose-painting, sleep, and urine.

Lechery, sir, it provokes and unprovokes. It provokes

the desire, but it takes away the performance.

Therefore much drink may be said to be an

equivocator with lechery. It makes him, and it

mars him; it sets him on, and it takes him off; it

persuades him and disheartens him; makes him

stand to and not stand to; in conclusion, equivocates

him in a sleep and, giving him the lie, leaves

him.

MACDUFF

I believe drink gave thee the lie last night.

PORTER

That it did, sir, i’ th’ very throat on me; but I

requited him for his lie, and, I think, being too

strong for him, though he took up my legs sometime,

yet I made a shift to cast him.

MACDUFF

605Is thy master stirring?

Enter Macbeth.

606Our knocking has awaked him. Here he comes.

⸢Porter exits.⸣

LENNOX

607Good morrow, noble sir.

MACBETH

Good morrow, noble sir. Good morrow, both.

MACDUFF

608Is the King stirring, worthy thane?

MACBETH

Is the King stirring, worthy thane? Not yet.

MACDUFF

609He did command me to call timely on him.
610I have almost slipped the hour.

MACBETH

I have almost slipped the hour. I’ll bring you to him.

MACDUFF

611I know this is a joyful trouble to you,
612But yet ’tis one.

MACBETH

613The labor we delight in physics pain.
614This is the door.

MACDUFF

This is the door. I’ll make so bold to call,
615For ’tis my limited service.

Macduff exits.

LENNOX

616Goes the King hence today?

MACBETH

617He does. He did appoint so.

LENNOX

618The night has been unruly. Where we lay,
619Our chimneys were blown down and, as they say,
620Lamentings heard i’ th’ air, strange screams of death,
621And prophesying, with accents terrible,
622Of dire combustion and confused events
623New hatched to th’ woeful time. The obscure bird
624Clamored the livelong night. Some say the earth
625Was feverous and did shake.

MACBETH

Was feverous and did shake. ’Twas a rough night.

LENNOX

626My young remembrance cannot parallel
627A fellow to it.

Enter Macduff.

MACDUFF

A fellow to it. O horror, horror, horror!
628Tongue nor heart cannot conceive nor name thee!

MACBETH AND LENNOX

629What’s the matter?

MACDUFF

630Confusion now hath made his masterpiece.
631Most sacrilegious murder hath broke ope
632The Lord’s anointed temple and stole thence
633The life o’ th’ building.

MACBETH

The life o’ th’ building. What is ’t you say? The life?

LENNOX

634Mean you his Majesty?

MACDUFF

635Approach the chamber and destroy your sight
636With a new Gorgon. Do not bid me speak.
637See and then speak yourselves.

Macbeth and Lennox exit.

See and then speak yourselves. Awake, awake!
638Ring the alarum bell. —Murder and treason!
639Banquo and Donalbain, Malcolm, awake!
640Shake off this downy sleep, death’s counterfeit,
641And look on death itself. Up, up, and see
642The great doom’s image. Malcolm, Banquo,
643As from your graves rise up and walk like sprites
644To countenance this horror. —Ring the bell.

Bell rings.

Enter Lady ⸢Macbeth.⸣

LADY MACBETH

645What’s the business,
646That such a hideous trumpet calls to parley
647The sleepers of the house? Speak, speak!

MACDUFF

648O gentle lady,
649’Tis not for you to hear what I can speak.
650The repetition in a woman’s ear
651Would murder as it fell.

Enter Banquo.

Would murder as it fell. O Banquo, Banquo,
652Our royal master’s murdered.

LADY MACBETH

Our royal master’s murdered. Woe, alas!
653What, in our house?

BANQUO

What, in our house? Too cruel anywhere. —
654Dear Duff, I prithee, contradict thyself
655And say it is not so.

Enter Macbeth, Lennox, and Ross.

MACBETH

656Had I but died an hour before this chance,
657I had lived a blessèd time; for from this instant
658There’s nothing serious in mortality.
659All is but toys. Renown and grace is dead.
660The wine of life is drawn, and the mere lees
661Is left this vault to brag of.

Enter Malcolm and Donalbain.

DONALBAIN

662What is amiss?

MACBETH

663You are, and do not know ’t.
664The spring, the head, the fountain of your blood
665Is stopped; the very source of it is stopped.

MACDUFF

666Your royal father’s murdered.

MALCOLM

Your royal father’s murdered. O, by whom?

LENNOX

667Those of his chamber, as it seemed, had done ’t.
668Their hands and faces were all badged with blood.
669So were their daggers, which unwiped we found
670Upon their pillows. They stared and were distracted.
671No man’s life was to be trusted with them.

MACBETH

672O, yet I do repent me of my fury,
673That I did kill them.

MACDUFF

That I did kill them. Wherefore did you so?

MACBETH

674Who can be wise, amazed, temp’rate, and furious,
675Loyal, and neutral, in a moment? No man.
676Th’ expedition of my violent love
677Outrun the pauser, reason. Here lay Duncan,
678His silver skin laced with his golden blood,
679And his gashed stabs looked like a breach in nature
680For ruin’s wasteful entrance; there the murderers,
681Steeped in the colors of their trade, their daggers
682Unmannerly breeched with gore. Who could refrain
683That had a heart to love, and in that heart
684Courage to make ’s love known?

LADY MACBETH

Courage to make ’s love known? Help me hence, ho!

MACDUFF

685Look to the lady.

MALCOLM,

Look to the lady. Why do we hold our tongues,aparte
686That most may claim this argument for ours?aparte

DONALBAIN,

687What should be spoken here, where our fate,aparte
688Hid in an auger hole, may rush and seize us?aparte
689Let’s away. Our tears are not yet brewed.aparte

MALCOLM,

690Nor our strong sorrow upon the foot of motion.aparte

BANQUO

691Look to the lady.

⸢Lady Macbeth is assisted to leave.⸣

692And when we have our naked frailties hid,
693That suffer in exposure, let us meet
694And question this most bloody piece of work
695To know it further. Fears and scruples shake us.
696In the great hand of God I stand, and thence
697Against the undivulged pretense I fight
698Of treasonous malice.

MACDUFF

Of treasonous malice. And so do I.

ALL

Of treasonous malice. And so do I. So all.

MACBETH

699Let’s briefly put on manly readiness
700And meet i’ the hall together.

ALL

And meet i’ the hall together. Well contented.

⸢All but Malcolm and Donalbain⸣ exit.

MALCOLM

701What will you do? Let’s not consort with them.
702To show an unfelt sorrow is an office
703Which the false man does easy. I’ll to England.

DONALBAIN

704To Ireland I. Our separated fortune
705Shall keep us both the safer. Where we are,
706There’s daggers in men’s smiles. The near in blood,
707The nearer bloody.

MALCOLM

The nearer bloody. This murderous shaft that’s shot
708Hath not yet lighted, and our safest way
709Is to avoid the aim. Therefore to horse,
710And let us not be dainty of leave-taking
711But shift away. There’s warrant in that theft
712Which steals itself when there’s no mercy left.

They exit.

Scene 4

Enter Ross with an Old Man.

OLD MAN

713Threescore and ten I can remember well,
714Within the volume of which time I have seen
715Hours dreadful and things strange, but this sore night
716Hath trifled former knowings.

ROSS

Hath trifled former knowings. Ha, good father,
717Thou seest the heavens, as troubled with man’s act,
718Threatens his bloody stage. By th’ clock ’tis day,
719And yet dark night strangles the traveling lamp.
720Is ’t night’s predominance or the day’s shame
721That darkness does the face of earth entomb
722When living light should kiss it?

OLD MAN

When living light should kiss it? ’Tis unnatural,
723Even like the deed that’s done. On Tuesday last
724A falcon, tow’ring in her pride of place,
725Was by a mousing owl hawked at and killed.

ROSS

726And Duncan’s horses (a thing most strange and certain),
727Beauteous and swift, the minions of their race,
728Turned wild in nature, broke their stalls, flung out,
729Contending ’gainst obedience, as they would
730Make war with mankind.

OLD MAN

Make war with mankind. ’Tis said they eat each other.

ROSS

731They did so, to th’ amazement of mine eyes
732That looked upon ’t.

Enter Macduff.

That looked upon ’t. Here comes the good Macduff. —
733How goes the world, sir, now?

MACDUFF

How goes the world, sir, now? Why, see you not?

ROSS

734Is ’t known who did this more than bloody deed?

MACDUFF

735Those that Macbeth hath slain.

ROSS

Those that Macbeth hath slain. Alas the day,
736What good could they pretend?

MACDUFF

What good could they pretend? They were suborned.
737Malcolm and Donalbain, the King’s two sons,
738Are stol’n away and fled, which puts upon them
739Suspicion of the deed.

ROSS

Suspicion of the deed. ’Gainst nature still!
740Thriftless ambition, that will ravin up
741Thine own lives’ means. Then ’tis most like
742The sovereignty will fall upon Macbeth.

MACDUFF

743He is already named and gone to Scone
744To be invested.

ROSS

To be invested. Where is Duncan’s body?

MACDUFF

745Carried to Colmekill,
746The sacred storehouse of his predecessors
747And guardian of their bones.

ROSS

And guardian of their bones. Will you to Scone?

MACDUFF

748No, cousin, I’ll to Fife.

ROSS

No, cousin, I’ll to Fife. Well, I will thither.

MACDUFF

749Well, may you see things well done there. Adieu,
750Lest our old robes sit easier than our new.

ROSS

751Farewell, father.

OLD MAN

752God’s benison go with you and with those
753That would make good of bad and friends of foes.

All exit.
