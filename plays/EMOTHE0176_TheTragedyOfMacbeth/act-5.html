Act V

Scene 1

Enter a Doctor of Physic and a Waiting-Gentlewoman.

DOCTOR

I have two nights watched with you but can

perceive no truth in your report. When was it she

last walked?

GENTLEWOMAN

Since his Majesty went into the field, I

have seen her rise from her bed, throw her nightgown

upon her, unlock her closet, take forth paper,

fold it, write upon ’t, read it, afterwards seal it, and

again return to bed; yet all this while in a most fast

sleep.

DOCTOR

A great perturbation in nature, to receive at

once the benefit of sleep and do the effects of

watching. In this slumb’ry agitation, besides her

walking and other actual performances, what at any

time have you heard her say?

GENTLEWOMAN

That, sir, which I will not report after

her.

DOCTOR

You may to me, and ’tis most meet you

should.

GENTLEWOMAN

Neither to you nor anyone, having no

witness to confirm my speech.

Enter Lady ⸢Macbeth⸣ with a taper.

Lo you, here she comes. This is her very guise and,

upon my life, fast asleep. Observe her; stand close.

DOCTOR

How came she by that light?

GENTLEWOMAN

Why, it stood by her. She has light by

her continually. ’Tis her command.

DOCTOR

You see her eyes are open.

GENTLEWOMAN

Ay, but their sense are shut.

DOCTOR

What is it she does now? Look how she rubs

her hands.

GENTLEWOMAN

It is an accustomed action with her to

seem thus washing her hands. I have known her

continue in this a quarter of an hour.

LADY MACBETH

Yet here’s a spot.

DOCTOR

Hark, she speaks. I will set down what comes

from her, to satisfy my remembrance the more

strongly.

LADY MACBETH

Out, damned spot, out, I say! One. Two.

Why then, ’tis time to do ’t. Hell is murky. Fie, my

lord, fie, a soldier and afeard? What need we fear

who knows it, when none can call our power to

account? Yet who would have thought the old man

to have had so much blood in him?

DOCTOR

Do you mark that?

LADY MACBETH

The Thane of Fife had a wife. Where is

she now? What, will these hands ne’er be clean? No

more o’ that, my lord, no more o’ that. You mar all

with this starting.

DOCTOR

Go to, go to. You have known what you should

not.

GENTLEWOMAN

She has spoke what she should not,

I am sure of that. Heaven knows what she has known.

LADY MACBETH

Here’s the smell of the blood still. All

the perfumes of Arabia will not sweeten this little

hand. O, O, O!

DOCTOR

What a sigh is there! The heart is sorely

charged.

GENTLEWOMAN

I would not have such a heart in my

bosom for the dignity of the whole body.

DOCTOR

Well, well, well.

GENTLEWOMAN

Pray God it be, sir.

DOCTOR

This disease is beyond my practice. Yet I have

known those which have walked in their sleep,

who have died holily in their beds.

LADY MACBETH

Wash your hands. Put on your nightgown.

Look not so pale. I tell you yet again, Banquo’s

buried; he cannot come out on ’s grave.

DOCTOR

Even so?

LADY MACBETH

To bed, to bed. There’s knocking at the

gate. Come, come, come, come. Give me your

hand. What’s done cannot be undone. To bed, to

bed, to bed.

Lady ⸢Macbeth⸣ exits.

DOCTOR

Will she go now to bed?

GENTLEWOMAN

Directly.

DOCTOR

1684Foul whisp’rings are abroad. Unnatural deeds
1685Do breed unnatural troubles. Infected minds
1686To their deaf pillows will discharge their secrets.
1687More needs she the divine than the physician.
1688God, God forgive us all. Look after her.
1689Remove from her the means of all annoyance
1690And still keep eyes upon her. So, good night.
1691My mind she has mated, and amazed my sight.
1692I think but dare not speak.

GENTLEWOMAN

I think but dare not speak. Good night, good doctor.

They exit.

Scene 2

Drum and Colors. Enter Menteith, Caithness, Angus, Lennox, ⸢and⸣ Soldiers.

MENTEITH

1693The English power is near, led on by Malcolm,
1694His uncle Siward, and the good Macduff.
1695Revenges burn in them, for their dear causes
1696Would to the bleeding and the grim alarm
1697Excite the mortified man.

ANGUS

Excite the mortified man. Near Birnam Wood
1698Shall we well meet them. That way are they coming.

CAITHNESS

1699Who knows if Donalbain be with his brother?

LENNOX

1700For certain, sir, he is not. I have a file
1701Of all the gentry. There is Siward’s son
1702And many unrough youths that even now
1703Protest their first of manhood.

MENTEITH

Protest their first of manhood. What does the tyrant?

CAITHNESS

1704Great Dunsinane he strongly fortifies.
1705Some say he’s mad; others that lesser hate him
1706Do call it valiant fury. But for certain
1707He cannot buckle his distempered cause
1708Within the belt of rule.

ANGUS

Within the belt of rule. Now does he feel
1709His secret murders sticking on his hands.
1710Now minutely revolts upbraid his faith-breach.
1711Those he commands move only in command,
1712Nothing in love. Now does he feel his title
1713Hang loose about him, like a giant’s robe
1714Upon a dwarfish thief.

MENTEITH

Upon a dwarfish thief. Who, then, shall blame
1715His pestered senses to recoil and start
1716When all that is within him does condemn
1717Itself for being there?

CAITHNESS

Itself for being there? Well, march we on
1718To give obedience where ’tis truly owed.
1719Meet we the med’cine of the sickly weal,
1720And with him pour we in our country’s purge
1721Each drop of us.

LENNOX

Each drop of us. Or so much as it needs
1722To dew the sovereign flower and drown the weeds.
1723Make we our march towards Birnam.

They exit marching.

Scene 3

Enter Macbeth, ⸢the⸣ Doctor, and Attendants.

MACBETH

1724Bring me no more reports. Let them fly all.
1725Till Birnam Wood remove to Dunsinane
1726I cannot taint with fear. What’s the boy Malcolm?
1727Was he not born of woman? The spirits that know
1728All mortal consequences have pronounced me thus:
1729“Fear not, Macbeth. No man that’s born of woman
1730Shall e’er have power upon thee.” Then fly, false thanes,
1731And mingle with the English epicures.
1732The mind I sway by and the heart I bear
1733Shall never sag with doubt nor shake with fear.

Enter Servant.

1734The devil damn thee black, thou cream-faced loon!
1735Where got’st thou that goose-look?

SERVANT

1736There is ten thousand —

MACBETH

1737Geese, villain?

SERVANT

1738Soldiers, sir.

MACBETH

1739Go prick thy face and over-red thy fear,
1740Thou lily-livered boy. What soldiers, patch?
1741Death of thy soul! Those linen cheeks of thine
1742Are counselors to fear. What soldiers, whey-face?

SERVANT

1743The English force, so please you.

MACBETH

1744Take thy face hence.

⸢Servant exits.⸣

Take thy face hence. Seyton! —I am sick at heart
1745When I behold —Seyton, I say! —This push
1746Will cheer me ever or ⸢disseat⸣ me now.
1747I have lived long enough. My way of life
1748Is fall’n into the sere, the yellow leaf,
1749And that which should accompany old age,
1750As honor, love, obedience, troops of friends,
1751I must not look to have, but in their stead
1752Curses, not loud but deep, mouth-honor, breath
1753Which the poor heart would fain deny and dare not. —
1754Seyton!

Enter Seyton.

SEYTON

1755What’s your gracious pleasure?

MACBETH

What’s your gracious pleasure? What news more?

SEYTON

1756All is confirmed, my lord, which was reported.

MACBETH

1757I’ll fight till from my bones my flesh be hacked.
1758Give me my armor.

SEYTON

1759’Tis not needed yet.

MACBETH

1760I’ll put it on.
1761Send out more horses. Skirr the country round.
1762Hang those that talk of fear. Give me mine armor. —
1763How does your patient, doctor?

DOCTOR

1764Not so sick, my lord,
1765As she is troubled with thick-coming fancies
1766That keep her from her rest.

MACBETH

That keep her from her rest. Cure ⸢her⸣ of that.
1767Canst thou not minister to a mind diseased,
1768Pluck from the memory a rooted sorrow,
1769Raze out the written troubles of the brain,
1770And with some sweet oblivious antidote
1771Cleanse the stuffed bosom of that perilous stuff
1772Which weighs upon the heart?

DOCTOR

Which weighs upon the heart? Therein the patient
1773Must minister to himself.

MACBETH

1774Throw physic to the dogs. I’ll none of it. —
1775Come, put mine armor on. Give me my staff.

⸢Attendants begin to arm him.⸣

1776Seyton, send out. —Doctor, the thanes fly from me. —
1777Come, sir, dispatch. —If thou couldst, doctor, cast
1778The water of my land, find her disease,
1779And purge it to a sound and pristine health,
1780I would applaud thee to the very echo
1781That should applaud again —Pull ’t off, I say. —
1782What rhubarb, senna, or what purgative drug
1783Would scour these English hence? Hear’st thou of them?

DOCTOR

1784Ay, my good lord. Your royal preparation
1785Makes us hear something.

MACBETH

Makes us hear something. Bring it after me. —
1786I will not be afraid of death and bane
1787Till Birnam Forest come to Dunsinane.

DOCTOR,

1788Were I from Dunsinane away and clear,aparte
1789Profit again should hardly draw me here.aparte

They exit.

Scene 4

Drum and Colors. Enter Malcolm, Siward, Macduff, Siward’s son, Menteith, Caithness, Angus, and Soldiers, marching.

MALCOLM

1790Cousins, I hope the days are near at hand
1791That chambers will be safe.

MENTEITH

That chambers will be safe. We doubt it nothing.

SIWARD

1792What wood is this before us?

MENTEITH

What wood is this before us? The Wood of Birnam.

MALCOLM

1793Let every soldier hew him down a bough
1794And bear ’t before him. Thereby shall we shadow
1795The numbers of our host and make discovery
1796Err in report of us.

SOLDIER

Err in report of us. It shall be done.

SIWARD

1797We learn no other but the confident tyrant
1798Keeps still in Dunsinane and will endure
1799Our setting down before ’t.

MALCOLM

Our setting down before ’t. ’Tis his main hope;
1800For, where there is advantage to be given,
1801Both more and less have given him the revolt,
1802And none serve with him but constrainèd things
1803Whose hearts are absent too.

MACDUFF

Whose hearts are absent too. Let our just censures
1804Attend the true event, and put we on
1805Industrious soldiership.

SIWARD

Industrious soldiership. The time approaches
1806That will with due decision make us know
1807What we shall say we have and what we owe.
1808Thoughts speculative their unsure hopes relate,
1809But certain issue strokes must arbitrate;
1810Towards which, advance the war.

They exit marching.

Scene 5

Enter Macbeth, Seyton, and Soldiers, with Drum and Colors.

MACBETH

1811Hang out our banners on the outward walls.
1812The cry is still “They come!” Our castle’s strength
1813Will laugh a siege to scorn. Here let them lie
1814Till famine and the ague eat them up.
1815Were they not forced with those that should be ours,
1816We might have met them dareful, beard to beard,
1817And beat them backward home.

A cry within of women.

And beat them backward home. What is that noise?

SEYTON

1818It is the cry of women, my good lord.

⸢He exits.⸣

MACBETH

1819I have almost forgot the taste of fears.
1820The time has been my senses would have cooled
1821To hear a night-shriek, and my fell of hair
1822Would at a dismal treatise rouse and stir
1823As life were in ’t. I have supped full with horrors.
1824Direness, familiar to my slaughterous thoughts,
1825Cannot once start me.

⸢Enter Seyton.⸣

Cannot once start me. Wherefore was that cry?

SEYTON

1826The Queen, my lord, is dead.

MACBETH

1827She should have died hereafter.
1828There would have been a time for such a word.
1829Tomorrow and tomorrow and tomorrow
1830Creeps in this petty pace from day to day
1831To the last syllable of recorded time,
1832And all our yesterdays have lighted fools
1833The way to dusty death. Out, out, brief candle!
1834Life’s but a walking shadow, a poor player
1835That struts and frets his hour upon the stage
1836And then is heard no more. It is a tale
1837Told by an idiot, full of sound and fury,
1838Signifying nothing.

Enter a Messenger.

1839Thou com’st to use thy tongue: thy story quickly.

MESSENGER

1840Gracious my lord,
1841I should report that which I say I saw,
1842But know not how to do ’t.

MACBETH

But know not how to do ’t. Well, say, sir.

MESSENGER

1843As I did stand my watch upon the hill,
1844I looked toward Birnam, and anon methought
1845The Wood began to move.

MACBETH

The Wood began to move. Liar and slave!

MESSENGER

1846Let me endure your wrath if ’t be not so.
1847Within this three mile may you see it coming.
1848I say, a moving grove.

MACBETH

I say, a moving grove. If thou speak’st false,
1849Upon the next tree shall thou hang alive
1850Till famine cling thee. If thy speech be sooth,
1851I care not if thou dost for me as much. —
1852I pull in resolution and begin
1853To doubt th’ equivocation of the fiend,
1854That lies like truth. “Fear not till Birnam Wood
1855Do come to Dunsinane,” and now a wood
1856Comes toward Dunsinane. —Arm, arm, and out! —
1857If this which he avouches does appear,
1858There is nor flying hence nor tarrying here.
1859I ’gin to be aweary of the sun
1860And wish th’ estate o’ th’ world were now undone. —
1861Ring the alarum bell. —Blow wind, come wrack,
1862At least we’ll die with harness on our back.

They exit.

Scene 6

Drum and Colors. Enter Malcolm, Siward, Macduff, and their army, with boughs.

MALCOLM

1863Now near enough. Your leafy screens throw down
1864And show like those you are. —You, worthy uncle,
1865Shall with my cousin, your right noble son,
1866Lead our first battle. Worthy Macduff and we
1867Shall take upon ’s what else remains to do,
1868According to our order.

SIWARD

According to our order. Fare you well.
1869Do we but find the tyrant’s power tonight,
1870Let us be beaten if we cannot fight.

MACDUFF

1871Make all our trumpets speak; give them all breath,
1872Those clamorous harbingers of blood and death.

They exit.

Alarums continued.

Scene 7

Enter Macbeth.

MACBETH

1873They have tied me to a stake. I cannot fly,
1874But, bear-like, I must fight the course. What’s he
1875That was not born of woman? Such a one
1876Am I to fear, or none.

Enter young Siward.

YOUNG SIWARD

1877What is thy name?

MACBETH

1878Thou ’lt be afraid to hear it.

YOUNG SIWARD

1879No, though thou call’st thyself a hotter name
1880Than any is in hell.

MACBETH

Than any is in hell. My name’s Macbeth.

YOUNG SIWARD

1881The devil himself could not pronounce a title
1882More hateful to mine ear.

MACBETH

More hateful to mine ear. No, nor more fearful.

YOUNG SIWARD

1883Thou liest, abhorrèd tyrant. With my sword
1884I’ll prove the lie thou speak’st.

⸢They fight,⸣ and young Siward ⸢is⸣ slain.

MACBETH

I’ll prove the lie thou speak’st. Thou wast born of woman.
1885But swords I smile at, weapons laugh to scorn,
1886Brandished by man that’s of a woman born.

He exits.

Alarums.

Enter Macduff.

MACDUFF

1887That way the noise is. Tyrant, show thy face!
1888If thou beest slain, and with no stroke of mine,
1889My wife and children’s ghosts will haunt me still.
1890I cannot strike at wretched kerns, whose arms
1891Are hired to bear their staves. Either thou, Macbeth,
1892Or else my sword with an unbattered edge
1893I sheathe again undeeded. There thou shouldst be;
1894By this great clatter, one of greatest note
1895Seems bruited. Let me find him, Fortune,
1896And more I beg not.

He exits.

Alarums.

Enter Malcolm and Siward.

SIWARD

1897This way, my lord. The castle’s gently rendered.
1898The tyrant’s people on both sides do fight,
1899The noble thanes do bravely in the war,
1900The day almost itself professes yours,
1901And little is to do.

MALCOLM

And little is to do. We have met with foes
1902That strike beside us.

SIWARD

That strike beside us. Enter, sir, the castle.

They exit.

Alarum.

⸢Scene 8⸣

Enter Macbeth.

MACBETH

1903Why should I play the Roman fool and die
1904On mine own sword? Whiles I see lives, the gashes
1905Do better upon them.

Enter Macduff.

MACDUFF

Do better upon them. Turn, hellhound, turn!

MACBETH

1906Of all men else I have avoided thee.
1907But get thee back. My soul is too much charged
1908With blood of thine already.

MACDUFF

With blood of thine already. I have no words;
1909My voice is in my sword, thou bloodier villain
1910Than terms can give thee out.

Fight. Alarum.

MACBETH

Than terms can give thee out. Thou losest labor.
1911As easy mayst thou the intrenchant air
1912With thy keen sword impress as make me bleed.
1913Let fall thy blade on vulnerable crests;
1914I bear a charmèd life, which must not yield
1915To one of woman born.

MACDUFF

To one of woman born. Despair thy charm,
1916And let the angel whom thou still hast served
1917Tell thee Macduff was from his mother’s womb
1918Untimely ripped.

MACBETH

1919Accursèd be that tongue that tells me so,
1920For it hath cowed my better part of man!
1921And be these juggling fiends no more believed
1922That palter with us in a double sense,
1923That keep the word of promise to our ear
1924And break it to our hope. I’ll not fight with thee.

MACDUFF

1925Then yield thee, coward,
1926And live to be the show and gaze o’ th’ time.
1927We’ll have thee, as our rarer monsters are,
1928Painted upon a pole, and underwrit
1929“Here may you see the tyrant.”

MACBETH

“Here may you see the tyrant.” I will not yield
1930To kiss the ground before young Malcolm’s feet
1931And to be baited with the rabble’s curse.
1932Though Birnam Wood be come to Dunsinane
1933And thou opposed, being of no woman born,
1934Yet I will try the last. Before my body
1935I throw my warlike shield. Lay on, Macduff,
1936And damned be him that first cries “Hold! Enough!”

They exit fighting.

Retreat and flourish.

Enter, with Drum and Colors, Malcolm, Siward, Ross, Thanes, and Soldiers.

MALCOLM

1937I would the friends we miss were safe arrived.

SIWARD

1938Some must go off; and yet by these I see
1939So great a day as this is cheaply bought.

MALCOLM

1940Macduff is missing, and your noble son.

ROSS

1941Your son, my lord, has paid a soldier’s debt.
1942He only lived but till he was a man,
1943The which no sooner had his prowess confirmed
1944In the unshrinking station where he fought,
1945But like a man he died.

SIWARD

But like a man he died. Then he is dead?

ROSS

1946Ay, and brought off the field. Your cause of sorrow
1947Must not be measured by his worth, for then
1948It hath no end.

SIWARD

It hath no end. Had he his hurts before?

ROSS

1949Ay, on the front.

SIWARD

Ay, on the front. Why then, God’s soldier be he!
1950Had I as many sons as I have hairs,
1951I would not wish them to a fairer death;
1952And so his knell is knolled.

MALCOLM

1953He’s worth more sorrow, and that I’ll spend for him.

SIWARD

1954He’s worth no more.
1955They say he parted well and paid his score,
1956And so, God be with him. Here comes newer comfort.

Enter Macduff with Macbeth’s head.

MACDUFF

1957Hail, King! for so thou art. Behold where stands
1958Th’ usurper’s cursèd head. The time is free.
1959I see thee compassed with thy kingdom’s pearl,
1960That speak my salutation in their minds,
1961Whose voices I desire aloud with mine.
1962Hail, King of Scotland!

ALL

1963Hail, King of Scotland!

Flourish.

MALCOLM

1964We shall not spend a large expense of time
1965Before we reckon with your several loves
1966And make us even with you. My thanes and kinsmen,
1967Henceforth be earls, the first that ever Scotland
1968In such an honor named. What’s more to do,
1969Which would be planted newly with the time,
1970As calling home our exiled friends abroad
1971That fled the snares of watchful tyranny,
1972Producing forth the cruel ministers
1973Of this dead butcher and his fiend-like queen
1974(Who, as ’tis thought, by self and violent hands,
1975Took off her life) —this, and what needful else
1976That calls upon us, by the grace of grace,
1977We will perform in measure, time, and place.
1978So thanks to all at once and to each one,
1979Whom we invite to see us crowned at Scone.

Flourish.

All exit.
