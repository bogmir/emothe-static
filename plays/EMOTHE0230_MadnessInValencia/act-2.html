Act II

Scene 1

Enter FLORIANO.

FLORIANO

901Fortune must have its little joke,
902piling despair upon despair
903so that the will eventually snaps.
904Sane or mad, we’re fortune’s playthings;
905I was snatched from the gates of death,
906and now I’m more dead than ever,
907tossed like a leaf in the heart’s deep storm.
908She looked with a goddess’s gaze,
909in her eyes flared madness and fire,
910and like a moth I flew too close,
911spinning madly into the flame.
912And when the storm in head and heart
913had fanned the fire into white heat,
914when I most needed time to think,
915to freeze the molten flow of thought,
916then the earth opened up below me.
917My greatest and dearest friend
918has been used by cruel fortune
919as her most outrageous arrow.
920Valerio, who gave me new hope,
921without whom I would now be dead,
922says that his life hangs from a thread,
923and that whether she’s mad or sane
924he means to have her for his own.
925If he pretends she’s a relative,
926then he’ll be granted full licence
927to take her home. If I lose her,
928whether she’s mad or if she’s sane,
929then the only thing left to me
930will be the madness of the storm.

FEDRA
