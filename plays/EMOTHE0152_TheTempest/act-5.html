Enter Prospero, in his magic robes, and Ariel.

PROSPERO

1234Now does my project gather to a head:
1235My charms crack not, my spirits obey, and time
1236Goes upright with his carriage. [To Ariel] How's the day?

ARIEL

1237On the sixth hour — at which time, my Lord,
1238You said our work should cease.

PROSPERO

You said our work should cease. I did say so
1239When first I raised the tempest. Say, my spirit,
1240How fares the King and's followers?

ARIEL

How fares the King and's followers? Confined together
1241In the same fashion as you gave in charge,
1242Just as you left them — all prisoners, sir,
1243In the lime-grove which weather-fends your cell;
1244They cannot budge till your release. The King,
1245His brother, and yours abide, all three distracted,
1246And the remainder mourning over them,
1247Brimful of sorrow and dismay — but chiefly
1248Him that you termed, sir, the good old lord Gonzalo:
1249His tears runs down his beard like winter's drops
1250From eaves of reeds. Your charm so strongly works 'em
1251That if you now beheld them, your affections
1252Would become tender.

PROSPERO

Would become tender. Dost thou think so, spirit?

ARIEL

1253Mine would, sir, were I human.

PROSPERO

Mine would, sir, were I human. And mine shall.
1254Hast thou, which art but air, a touch, a feeling
1255Of their afflictions, and shall not myself,
1256One of their kind, that relish all as sharply
1257Passion as they, be kindlier moved than thou art?
1258Though with their high wrongs I am struck to th'quick,
1259Yet with my nobler reason 'gainst my fury
1260Do I take part. The rarer action is
1261In virtue than in vengeance; they being penitent,
1262The sole drift of my purpose doth extend
1263Not a frown further. Go, release them, Ariel:
1264My charms I'll break, their senses I'll restore,
1265And they shall be themselves.

ARIEL

And they shall be themselves. I'll fetch them, sir.

Exit [Ariel while Prospero traces a magic circle on the stage].

PROSPERO

1266Ye elves of hills, brooks, standing lakes, and groves,
1267And ye that on the sands with printless foot
1268Do chase the ebbing Neptune, and do fly him
1269When he comes back; you demi-puppets that
1270By moonshine do the green sour ringlets make
1271Whereof the ewe not bites; and you whose pastime
1272Is to make midnight-mushrooms that rejoice
1273To hear the solemn curfew, by whose aid —
1274Weak masters though ye be — I have bedimmed
1275The noontide sun, called forth the mutinous winds,
1276And 'twixt the green sea and the azured vault
1277Set roaring war; to the dread-rattling thunder
1278Have I given fire, and rifted Jove's stout oak
1279With his own bolt! The strong-based promontory
1280Have I made shake, and by the spurs plucked up
1281The pine and cedar. Graves at my command
1282Have waked their sleepers, ope'd, and let 'em forth
1283By my so potent art. But this rough magic
1284I here abjure, and when I have required
1285Some heavenly music, which even now I do,
1286To work mine end upon their senses that
1287This ayrie charm is for, I'll break my staff,
1288Bury it certain fathoms in the earth;
1289And deeper than did ever plummet sound,
1290I'll drown my book.

GONZALO

1338Inhabits here! Some heavenly power guide us
1339Out of this fearful country!

PROSPERO

Out of this fearful country! [To Alonso] Behold, Sir King,
1340The wrongèd Duke of Milan, Prospero.
1341For more assurance that a living prince
1342Does now speak to thee, I embrace thy body,

[Embraces Alonso.]

1343And to thee and thy company I bid

ALONSO

1345Or some enchanted trifle to abuse me

1347Beats as of flesh and blood, and since I saw thee,
1348Th'affliction of my mind amends, with which
1349I fear a madness held me. This must crave

GONZALO

PROSPERO

1366Thy rankest fault (all of them), and require

ALONSO

Thou must restore. If thou be'st Prospero,
1369Give us particulars of thy preservation,
1370How thou hast met us here, whom three hours since

PROSPERO

ALONSO

PROSPERO

ALONSO

PROSPERO

ALONSO

1383A daughter?

1398For 'tis a chronicle of day by day,
1399Not a relation for a breakfast, nor
1400Befitting this first meeting. [To Alonso] Welcome, sir.
1401This cell's my court; here have I few attendants —
1402And subjects none abroad. Pray you, look in.

1404I will requite you with as good a thing,

Here Prospero discovers Ferdinand and Miranda playing at chess.

MIRANDA

1407Sweet Lord, you play me false!

FERDINAND

Sweet Lord, you play me false! No, my dearest love,
1408I would not for the world.

MIRANDA

1409Yes, for a score of kingdoms you should wrangle,
1410And I would call it fair play.

ALONSO

And I would call it fair play. If this prove
1411A vision of the island, one dear son
1412Shall I twice lose.

SEBASTIAN

Shall I twice lose. A most high miracle!

[Ferdinand sees Alonso and the others.]

FERDINAND

1413Though the seas threaten, they are merciful;
1414I have cursed them without cause.

ALONSO

I have cursed them without cause. Now all the blessings
1415Of a glad father compass thee about:
1416Arise, and say how thou cam'st here.

MIRANDA

Arise, and say how thou cam'st here. O wonder!
1417How many goodly creatures are there here!
1418How beauteous mankind is! O brave new world
1419That has such people in't!

PROSPERO

That has such people in't! 'Tis new to thee.

ALONSO

1420What is this maid with whom thou wast at play?
1421Your eld'st acquaintance cannot be three hours.
1422Is she the goddess that hath severed us
1423And brought us thus together?

FERDINAND

And brought us thus together? Sir, she is mortal,
1424But by immortal providence, she's mine.
1425I chose her when I could not ask my father
1426For his advice, nor thought I had one. She
1427Is daughter to this famous Duke of Milan,
1428Of whom so often I have heard renown,
1429But never saw before, of whom I have
1430Received a second life, and second father
1431This lady makes him to me.

ALONSO

This lady makes him to me. I am hers.
1432But oh, how oddly will it sound that I
1433Must ask my child forgiveness.

PROSPERO

Must ask my child forgiveness. There, sir, stop.
1434Let us not burden our remembrances with
1435A heaviness that's gone.

GONZALO

A heaviness that's gone. I have inly wept,
1436Or should have spoke ere this: look down, you gods,
1437And on this couple drop a blessèd crown,
1438For it is you that have chalked forth the way
1439Which brought us hither.

ALONSO

Which brought us hither. I say amen, Gonzalo.

GONZALO

1445And Ferdinand, her brother, found a wife
1446Where he himself was lost; Prospero, his dukedom

GONZALO
