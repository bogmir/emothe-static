Act III

[3.1]

Enter Ferdinand, bearing a log.

FERDINAND

757There be some sports are painful, and their labor
758Delight in them set off. Some kinds of baseness
759Are nobly undergone, and most poor matters
760Point to rich ends; this, my mean task,
761Would be as heavy to me, as odious, but
762The mistress which I serve quickens what's dead
763And makes my labors pleasures. Oh, she is
764Ten times more gentle than her father's crabbed,
765And he's composed of harshness. I must remove
766Some thousands of these logs and pile them up
767Upon a sore injunction. My sweet mistress
768Weeps when she sees me work, and says such baseness
769Had never like executor. I forget —
770But these sweet thoughts do even refresh my labors
771Enter Miranda and Prospero [he, at a distance, unseen]. Most busiliest when I do it.

MIRANDA

Enter Miranda and Prospero [he, at a distance, unseen]. Most busiliest when I do it. Alas, now pray you,
772Work not so hard. I would the lightning had
773Burnt up those logs that you are enjoined to pile.
774Pray, set it down and rest you — when this burns,
775'Twill weep for having wearied you. My father
776Is hard at study; pray now, rest yourself.
777He's safe for these three hours.

FERDINAND

He's safe for these three hours. O most dear mistress,
778The sun will set before I shall discharge
779What I must strive to do.

MIRANDA

What I must strive to do. If you'll sit down,
780I'll bear your logs the while. Pray, give me that;
781I'll carry it to the pile.

FERDINAND

I'll carry it to the pile. No, precious creature;
782I had rather crack my sinews, break my back
783Than you should such dishonor undergo
784While I sit lazy by.

MIRANDA

While I sit lazy by. It would become me
785As well as it does you, and I should do it
786With much more ease, for my good will is to it,
787And yours it is against.

PROSPERO

And yours it is against. Poor worm, thou art infected;aparte
788This visitation shows it.aparte

MIRANDA

This visitation shows it. You look wearily.

FERDINAND

789No, noble mistress, 'tis fresh morning with me
790When you are by at night. I do beseech you
791(Chiefly that I might set it in my prayers),
792What is your name?

MIRANDA

What is your name? O my father,aparte
793I have broke your hest to say so!aparte

FERDINAND

I have broke your hest to say so! Admired Miranda,
794Indeed the top of admiration, worth
795What's dearest to the world: full many a lady
796I have eyed with best regard, and many a time
797Th'harmony of their tongues hath into bondage
798Brought my too diligent ear. For several virtues
799Have I liked several women — never any
800With so full soul, but some defect in her
801Did quarrel with the noblest grace she owed
802And put it to the foil. But you, O you
803So perfect and so peerless, are created
804Of every creature's best.

MIRANDA

Of every creature's best. I do not know
805One of my sex, no woman's face remember —
806Save, from my glass, mine own. Nor have I seen
807More that I may call men than you, good friend,
808And my dear father. How features are abroad
809I am skilless of, but by my modesty
810(The jewel in my dower), I would not wish
811Any companion in the world but you,
812Nor can imagination form a shape,
813Besides yourself, to like of — but I prattle
814Something too wildly, and my father's precepts
815I therein do forget.

FERDINAND

I therein do forget. I am, in my condition,
816A prince, Miranda, I do think a King
817(I would not so), and would no more endure
818This wooden slavery than to suffer
819The flesh-fly blow my mouth. Hear my soul speak:
820The very instant that I saw you did
821My heart fly to your service, there resides
822To make me slave to it, and for your sake
823Am I this patient log man.

MIRANDA

Am I this patient log man. Do you love me?

FERDINAND

824O heaven, O earth, bear witness to this sound,
825And crown what I profess with kind event
826If I speak true; if hollowly, invert
827What best is boaded me to mischief. I,
828Beyond all limit of what else i'th'world,
829Do love, prize, honor you.

MIRANDA

Do love, prize, honor you. I am a fool
830To weep at what I am glad of.

PROSPERO

To weep at what I am glad of. Fair encounteraparte
831Of two most rare affections! Heavens rain graceaparte
832On that which breeds between 'em.aparte

FERDINAND

On that which breeds between 'em. Wherefore weep you?

MIRANDA

833At mine unworthiness, that dare not offer
834What I desire to give, and much less take
835What I shall die to want. But this is trifling,
836And all the more it seeks to hide itself,
837The bigger bulk it shows. Hence, bashful cunning,
838And prompt me, plain and holy innocence:
839I am your wife if you will marry me —
840If not, I'll die your maid. To be your fellow
841You may deny me, but I'll be your servant
842Whether you will or no.

FERDINAND

Whether you will or no. My mistress dearest,
843And I thus humble ever.

MIRANDA

And I thus humble ever. My husband then?

FERDINAND

844Ay, with a heart as willing
845As bondage ere of freedom: here's my hand.

MIRANDA

846And mine, with my heart in't; and now, farewell
847Till half an hour hence.

FERDINAND

Till half an hour hence. A thousand, thousand.

Exit [Miranda and Ferdinand].

PROSPERO

848So glad of this as they I cannot be,
849Who are surprised with all, but my rejoicing
850At nothing can be more. I'll to my book,
851For yet ere suppertime must I perform
852Much business appertaining.

Exit.

[3.2]

Enter Caliban, Stephano, and Trinculo.

STEPHANO

[To Trinculo] Tell not me! When the butt is out, we will drink water, not a drop before: therefore bear up and board 'em. [To Caliban] Servant monster, drink to me!

TRINCULO

Servant monster? The folly of this island! They say there's but five upon this isle; we are three of them. If the other two be brained like us, the state totters.

STEPHANO

Drink, servant monster, when I bid thee; thy eyes are almost set in thy head.

TRINCULO

Where should they be set else? He were a brave monster indeed if they were set in his tail.

STEPHANO

My man-monster hath drowned his tongue in sack. For my part, the sea cannot drown me. I swam, ere I could recover the shore, five and thirty leagues off and on. By this light, thou shalt be my lieutenant monster — or my standard.

TRINCULO

Your lieutenant, if you list; he's no standard.

STEPHANO

We'll not run, Monsieur Monster.

TRINCULO

Nor go, neither — but you'll lie like dogs and yet say nothing, neither.

STEPHANO

Mooncalf: speak once in thy life, if thou be'st a good mooncalf.

CALIBAN

How does thy honor? Let me lick thy shoe. I'll not serve him; he is not valiant.

TRINCULO

Thou liest, most ignorant monster. I am in case to jostle a constable. Why, thou deboshed fish thou, was there ever man a coward that hath drunk so much sack as I today? Wilt thou tell a monstrous lie, being but half a fish and half a monster?

CALIBAN

Lo, how he mocks me! Wilt thou let him, my Lord?

TRINCULO

"Lord," quoth he! — that a monster should be such a natural.

CALIBAN

Lo, lo, again! Bite him to death, I prithee.

STEPHANO

Trinculo: keep a good tongue in your head. If you prove a mutineer, the next tree! The poor monster's my subject, and he shall not suffer indignity.

CALIBAN

I thank my noble Lord. Wilt thou be pleased to hearken once again to the suit I made to thee?

STEPHANO

Marry will I: kneel and repeat it. I will stand and so shall Trinculo.

Enter Ariel, invisible.

CALIBAN

As I told thee before, I am subject to a tyrant —a sorcerer — that by his cunning hath cheated me of the island.

ARIEL

Thou liest.

CALIBAN

[To Trinculo] Thou liest, thou jesting monkey thou! I would my valiant master would destroy thee. I do not lie.

STEPHANO

Trinculo, if you trouble him any more in's tale, by this hand I will supplant some of your teeth.

TRINCULO

Why, I said nothing.

STEPHANO

Mum, then, and no more. Proceed.

CALIBAN

853I say by sorcery he got this isle.
854From me he got it! If thy greatness will
855Revenge it on him — for I know thou dar'st,
856But this thing dare not.

STEPHANO

That's most certain.

CALIBAN

857Thou shalt be lord of it, and I'll serve thee.

STEPHANO

How now shall this be compassed? Canst thou bring me to the party?

CALIBAN

Yea, yea, my Lord; I'll yield him thee asleep, where thou mayst knock a nail into his head.

ARIEL

Thou liest: thou canst not.

CALIBAN

858What a pied ninny's this! Thou scurvy patch!
859I do beseech thy greatness, give him blows
860And take his bottle from him. When that's gone,
861He shall drink nought but brine, for I'll not show him
862Where the quick freshes are.

STEPHANO

Trinculo, run into no further danger. Interrupt the monster one word further, and by this hand I'll turn my mercy out of doors and make a stockfish of thee.

TRINCULO

Why, what did I? I did nothing. I'll go farther off.

STEPHANO

Didst thou not say he lied?

ARIEL

Thou liest.

STEPHANO

Do I so? Take thou that! As you like this, give me the lie another time!

TRINCULO

I did not give the lie! Out of your wits and hearing too? A pox on your bottle — this can sack and drinking do. A murrain on your monster, and the devil take your fingers!

CALIBAN

Ha ha ha!

STEPHANO

Now, forward with your tale. [To Trinculo] Prithee, stand further off!

CALIBAN

Beat him enough! After a little time, I'll beat him too.

STEPHANO

Stand farther. Come, proceed.

CALIBAN

863Why, as I told thee, 'tis a custom with him
864I'th'afternoon to sleep: there thou mayst brain him,
865Having first seized his books, or with a log
866Batter his skull, or paunch him with a stake,
867Or cut his weasand with thy knife. Remember
868First to possess his books, for without them
869He's but a sot, as I am, nor hath not
870One spirit to command; they all do hate him
871As rootedly as I. Burn but his books;
872He has brave utensils, for so he calls them,
873Which, when he has a house, he'll deck withal.
874And that most deeply to consider is
875The beauty of his daughter — he himself
876Calls her a nonpareil. I never saw a woman
877But only Sycorax, my dam, and she,
878But she as far surpasseth Sycorax
879As great'st does least.

STEPHANO

As great'st does least. Is it so brave a lass?

CALIBAN

880Ay, Lord, she will become thy bed, I warrant,
881And bring thee forth brave brood.

STEPHANO

Monster, I will kill this man. His daughter and I will be King and Queen, save our graces, and Trinculo and thyself shall be viceroys. Dost thou like the plot, Trinculo?

TRINCULO

Excellent.

STEPHANO

Give me thy hand. I am sorry I beat thee; but while thou liv'st, keep a good tongue in thy head.

CALIBAN

882Within this half hour will he be asleep.
883Wilt thou destroy him then?

STEPHANO

Wilt thou destroy him then? Ay, on mine honor.

ARIEL

This will I tell my master.

CALIBAN

884Thou mak'st me merry; I am full of pleasure.
885Let us be jocund! Will you troll the catch
886You taught me but whilere?

STEPHANO

887At thy request, monster, I will do reason,
888Any reason. Come on, Trinculo, let us sing.

Sings

889Flout'em and cout'em; and skout'em and flout'em:
890Thought is free.

CALIBAN

That's not the tune!

Ariel plays the tune on a tabor and pipe.

STEPHANO

What is this same?

TRINCULO

This is the tune of our catch, played by the picture of Nobody.

STEPHANO

891If thou be'st a man, show thyself in thy likeness;
892If thou be'st a devil, take't as thou list.

TRINCULO

O forgive me my sins!

STEPHANO

He that dies pays all debts. I defy thee! Mercy upon us!

CALIBAN

Art thou afeard?

STEPHANO

No, monster, not I.

CALIBAN

893Be not afeard, the isle is full of noises,
894Sounds, and sweet ayres that give delight and hurt not.
895Sometimes a thousand twangling instruments
896Will hum about mine ears, and sometimes voices —
897That if I then had waked after long sleep,
898Will make me sleep again — and then, in dreaming,
899The clouds methought would open and show riches
900Ready to drop upon me, that when I waked
901I cried to dream again.

STEPHANO

This will prove a brave kingdom to me, where I shall have my music for nothing!

CALIBAN

When Prospero is destroyed.

STEPHANO

That shall be by and by; I remember the story.

TRINCULO

The sound is going away. Let's follow it, and after do our work.

STEPHANO

Lead, monster: we'll follow. I would I could see this taborer; he lays it on.

TRINCULO

[To Caliban] Wilt come? [To Stephano] I'll follow, Stephano.

Exeunt.

Enter Alonso, Sebastian, Antonio, Gonzalo, Adrian, Francisco, and others.

GONZALO

902By'r lakin, I can go no further, sir;
903My old bones aches. Here's a maze trod indeed
904Through fourth-rights and meanders. By your patience,
905I needs must rest me.

ALONSO

I needs must rest me. Old lord, I cannot blame thee,
906Who am myself attached with weariness
907To th'dulling of my spirits. Sit down and rest.
908Even here I will put off my hope and keep it
909No longer for my flatterer. He is drowned
910Whom thus we stray to find, and the sea mocks
911Our frustrate search on land. Well, let him go.

ANTONIO

912I am right glad that he's so out of hope.aparte
913Do not for one repulse forgo the purposeaparte
914That you resolved t'effect.aparte

SEBASTIAN

That you resolved t'effect. The next advantageaparte
915Will we take throughly.aparte

ANTONIO

916For now they are oppressed with travail; they
917Will not nor cannot use such vigilance
918As when they are fresh.

Solemn and strange music, and Prospero on the top [i.e., on the upper stage], invisible. Enter several strange shapes bringing in a banquet, and dance about it with gentle actions of salutations, and, inviting the King [and others] to eat, they depart.

ALONSO

919What harmony is this, my good friends? Hark!

GONZALO

920Marvelous sweet music!

ALONSO

921Give us kind keepers, heavens! What were these?

SEBASTIAN

922A living drollery! Now I will believe
923That there are unicorns, that in Arabia
924There is one tree, the phoenix' throne — one phoenix
925At this hour reigning there.

ANTONIO

At this hour reigning there. I'll believe both —
926And what does else want credit, come to me,
927And I'll be sworn 'tis true. Travelers ne'er did lie,
928Though fools at home condemn 'em.

GONZALO

Though fools at home condemn 'em. If in Naples
929I should report this now, would they believe me?
930If I should say I saw such islanders
931(For certes, these are people of the island),
932Who, though they are of monstrous shape, yet note
933Their manners are more gentle, kind, than of
934Our human generation you shall find
935Many — nay, almost any.

PROSPERO

Many — nay, almost any. Honest Lord,aparte
936Thou hast said well, for some of you there presentaparte
937Are worse than devils.aparte

ALONSO

Are worse than devils. I cannot too much muse
938Such shapes, such gesture, and such sound expressing
939(Although they want the use of tongue) a kind
940Of excellent dumb discourse.

PROSPERO

Of excellent dumb discourse. Praise in departing.aparte

FRANCISCO

941They vanished strangely.

SEBASTIAN

They vanished strangely. No matter, since
942They have left their viands behind, for we have stomachs.
943Will't please you taste of what is here?

ALONSO

Will't please you taste of what is here? Not I.

GONZALO

944Faith, sir, you need not fear. When we were boys,
945Who would believe that there were mountaineers
946Dewlapped like bulls, whose throats had hanging at 'em
947Wallets of flesh? Or that there were such men
948Whose heads stood in their breasts, which now we find
949Each putter-out of five for one will bring us
950Good warrant of?

ALONSO

Good warrant of? I will stand to and feed,
951Although my last. No matter, since I feel
952The best is past. Brother, my Lord the Duke:
953Stand to and do as we.

Thunder and lightning. Enter Ariel like a harpy, claps his wings upon the table, and with a quaint device, the banquet vanishes.

ARIEL

954You are three men of sin, whom Destiny
955(That hath to instrument this lower world,
956And what is in't) the never surfeited sea
957Hath caused to belch up you; and on this island,
958Where man doth not inhabit, you 'mongst men,
959Being most unfit to live, I have made you mad.
960And even with such-like valor, men hang and drown
961Their proper selves.

[Alonso, Sebastian, and others draw their swords.]

Their proper selves. You fools! I and my fellows
962Are ministers of Fate. The elements
963Of whom your swords are tempered may as well
964Wound the loud winds, or with bemocked-at stabs
965Kill the still-closing waters, as diminish
966One dowl that's in my plume. My fellow ministers
967Are like invulnerable; if you could hurt,
968Your swords are now too massy for your strengths
969And will not be uplifted. But remember,
970For that's my business to you, that you three
971From Milan did supplant good Prospero,
972Exposed unto the sea (which hath requite it)
973Him and his innocent child, for which foul deed
974The powers (delaying, not forgetting) have
975Incensed the seas and shores (yea, all the creatures!)
976Against your peace. Thee of thy son, Alonso,
977They have bereft, and do pronounce by me:
978Ling'ring perdition, worse than any death
979Can be at once, shall step by step attend
980You and your ways; whose wraths to guard you from
981(Which here, in this most desolate isle, else falls
982Upon your heads) is nothing but heart's sorrow
983And a clear life ensuing.

He vanishes in thunder; then, to soft music, enter the shapes again and dance with mocks and mows and carrying out the table.

PROSPERO

984Bravely the figure of this harpy hast thou
985Performed, my Ariel. A grace it had, devouring!
986Of my instruction hast thou nothing bated
987In what thou hadst to say; so with good life
988And observation strange, my meaner ministers
989Their several kinds have done. My high charms' work,
990And these, mine enemies, are all knit up
991In their distractions. They now are in my power,
992And in these fits I leave them while I visit
993Young Ferdinand (whom they suppose is drowned)
994And his and mine loved darling.

GONZALO

995I'th'name of something holy, sir, why stand you
996In this strange stare?

ALONSO

In this strange stare? Oh, it is monstrous, monstrous!
997Methought the billows spoke and told me of it;
998The winds did sing it to me, and the thunder,
999That deep and dreadful organ pipe, pronounced
1000The name of Prosper — it did bass my trespass.
1001Therefore, my son i'th'ooze is bedded; and
1002I'll seek him deeper than e'er plummet sounded,
1003And with him there lie mudded.

Exit [Alonso].

SEBASTIAN

1004But one fiend at a time,
1005I'll fight their legions o'er.

ANTONIO

I'll fight their legions o'er. I'll be thy second.

Exit [Sebastian, Antonio, and Francisco].

GONZALO

1006All three of them are desperate. Their great guilt
1007(Like poison given to work a great time after)
1008Now 'gins to bite the spirits. I do beseech you,
1009That are of suppler joints, follow them swiftly,
1010And hinder them from what this ecstasy
1011May now provoke them to.

ADRIAN

May now provoke them to. [To remaining others] Follow, I pray you.

Exeunt omnes.
