Act I

[1.1]

A tempestuous noise of thunder and lightning heard. Enter a Shipmaster and a Boatswain.

SHIPMASTER

Boatswain!

BOATSWAIN

Here, master. What cheer?

SHIPMASTER

Good. Speak to the mariners. Fall to it yarely or we run ourselves aground. Bestir! Bestir!

Exit [Shipmaster].

Enter Mariners.

BOATSWAIN

Heigh, my hearts! Cheerly, cheerly, my hearts! Yare, yare. Take in the topsail! Tend to the master's whistle. [To the storm] Blow till thou burst thy wind if room enough!

Enter Alonso, Sebastian, Antonio, Ferdinand, Gonzalo, and others.

ALONSO

Good boatswain, have care. Where's the master? Play the men.

BOATSWAIN

I pray now, keep below.

ANTONIO

Where is the master, boatswain?

BOATSWAIN

Do you not hear him? You mar our labor. Keep your cabins! You do assist the storm.

GONZALO

Nay, good, be patient.

BOATSWAIN

When the sea is. Hence! What cares these roarers for the name of King? To cabin! Silence: trouble us not.

GONZALO

Good, yet remember whom thou hast aboard.

BOATSWAIN

None that I more love than myself. You are a counselor — if you can command these elements to silence and work the peace of the present, we will not hand a rope more. Use your authority; if you cannot, give thanks you have lived so long, and make yourself ready in your cabin for the mischance of the hour if it so hap. [To Mariners] Cheerly, good hearts! [To Courtiers] Out of our way, I say!

Exit [Boatswain].

GONZALO

I have great comfort from this fellow. Methinks he hath no drowning mark upon him; his complexion is perfect gallows. Stand fast, good Fate, to his hanging. Make the rope of his destiny our cable, for our own doth little advantage. If he be not born to be hanged, our case is miserable.

Exit [Sebastian, Antonio, and Gonzalo].

Enter Boatswain.

BOATSWAIN

Down with the topmast! Yare: lower, lower. Bring her to try with main-course!

A cry within. Enter Sebastian, Antonio, and Gonzalo.

BOATSWAIN

A plague upon this howling; they are louder than the weather or our office. Yet again? What do you here? Shall we give over and drown? Have you a mind to sink?

SEBASTIAN

A pox on your throat, you bawling, blasphemous, uncharitable dog!

BOATSWAIN

Work you, then!

ANTONIO

Hang, cur. Hang, you whoreson, insolent noisemaker! We are less afraid to be drowned than thou art.

GONZALO

I'll warrant him for drowning, though the ship were no stronger than a nutshell and as leaky as an unstanched wench.

BOATSWAIN

Lay her ahold, ahold: set her two courses off to sea again. Lay her off!

Enter Mariners, wet.

MARINERS

All lost! To prayers, to prayers! All lost!

BOATSWAIN

What, must our mouths be cold?

GONZALO

The King and prince at prayers. Let's assist them, for our case is as theirs.

SEBASTIAN

I am out of patience.

ANTONIO

We are merely cheated of our lives by drunkards. This wide-chopped rascal! [To Boatswain] Would thou mightst lie drowning the washing of ten tides.

GONZALO

1He'll be hanged yet,
2Though every drop of water swear against it
3And gape at wid'st to glut him.

A confused noise within

GONZALO

And gape at wid'st to glut him. Mercy on us!

MARINERS

We split, we split! Farewell, my wife and children! Farewell, brother! We split, we split, we split!

ANTONIO

Let's all sink wi'th' King.

SEBASTIAN

Let's take leave of him.

Exit [Antonio and Sebastian].

GONZALO

Now would I give a thousand furlongs of sea for an acre of barren ground — long heath, brown furze, anything. The wills above be done, but I would fain die a dry death.

Exit.

[1.2]

Enter Prospero and Miranda.

MIRANDA

4If by your art, my dearest father, you have
5Put the wild waters in this roar, allay them.
6The sky, it seems, would pour down stinking pitch
7But that the sea, mounting to th'welkin's cheek,
8Dashes the fire out. Oh! I have suffered
9With those that I saw suffer. A brave vessel
10(Who had no doubt some noble creature in her)
11Dashed all to pieces. Oh, the cry did knock
12Against my very heart. Poor souls, they perished.
13Had I been any god of power, I would
14Have sunk the sea within the earth, or ere
15It should the good ship so have swallowed and
16The fraughting souls within her.

PROSPERO

The fraughting souls within her. Be collected.
17No more amazement; tell your piteous heart
18There's no harm done.

MIRANDA

There's no harm done. Oh, woe the day!

PROSPERO

There's no harm done. Oh, woe the day! No harm!
19I have done nothing but in care of thee —
20Of thee my dear one, thee my daughter — who
21Art ignorant of what thou art, not knowing
22Of whence I am, nor that I am more better
23Than Prospero, master of a full poor cell,
24And thy no greater father.

MIRANDA

And thy no greater father. More to know
25Did never meddle with my thoughts.

PROSPERO

Did never meddle with my thoughts. 'Tis time
26I should inform thee farther. Lend thy hand
27And pluck my magic garment from me. So
28Lie there, my art. Wipe thou thine eyes; have comfort.
29The direful spectacle of the wreck, which touched
30The very virtue of compassion in thee,
31I have, with such provision in mine art,
32So safely ordered that there is no soul
33(No, not so much perdition as an hair!)
34Betide to any creature in the vessel
35Which thou heard'st cry, which thou saw'st sink. Sit down,
36For thou must now know farther.

MIRANDA

For thou must now know farther. You have often
37Begun to tell me what I am, but stopped
38And left me to a bootless inquisition,
39Concluding, "Stay — not yet."

PROSPERO

Concluding, "Stay — not yet." The hour's now come.
40The very minute bids thee ope thine ear:
41Obey and be attentive. Canst thou remember
42A time before we came unto this cell?
43I do not think thou canst, for then thou wast not
44Out three years old.

MIRANDA

Out three years old. Certainly, sir, I can.

PROSPERO

45By what? By any other house or person?
46Of anything the image tell me that
47Hath kept with thy remembrance.

MIRANDA

Hath kept with thy remembrance. 'Tis far off —
48And rather like a dream than an assurance
49That my remembrance warrants. Had I not
50Four or five women once that tended me?

PROSPERO

51Thou had'st, and more, Miranda. But how is it
52That this lives in thy mind? What seest thou else
53In the dark backward and abysm of time?
54If thou remember'st aught ere thou cam'st here,
55How thou cam'st here, thou mayst.

MIRANDA

How thou cam'st here, thou mayst. But that I do not.

PROSPERO

56Twelve years since, Miranda, twelve years since,
57Thy father was the Duke of Milan and
58A prince of power.

MIRANDA

A prince of power. Sir, are not you my father?

PROSPERO

59Thy mother was a piece of virtue, and
60She said thou wast my daughter; and thy father
61Was Duke of Milan — and his only heir
62And princess no worse issued.

MIRANDA

And princess no worse issued. Oh, the heavens!
63What foul play had we that we came from thence —
64Or blessèd was't we did?

PROSPERO

Or blessèd was't we did? Both, both, my girl.
65By foul play (as thou say'st) were we heaved thence,
66But blessedly holp hither.

MIRANDA

But blessedly holp hither. Oh, my heart bleeds
67To think o'th'teen that I have turned you to,
68Which is from my remembrance. Please you, farther.

PROSPERO

69My brother and thy uncle, called Antonio —
70I pray thee, mark me, that a brother should
71Be so perfidious! — he whom, next thyself,
72Of all the world I loved, and to him put
73The manage of my state as, at that time,
74Through all the seigniories, it was the first,
75And Prospero, the prime duke, being so reputed
76In dignity, and for the liberal arts
77Without a parallel (those being all my study),
78The government I cast upon my brother,
79And to my state grew stranger, being transported
80And rapt in secret studies. Thy false uncle —
81Dost thou attend me?

MIRANDA

Dost thou attend me? Sir, most heedfully —

PROSPERO

82Being once perfected how to grant suits
83(How to deny them, who t'advance, and who
84To trash for over-topping), new created
85The creatures that were mine, I say, or changed'em,
86Or else new formed them; having both the key
87Of officer and office, set all hearts i'th'state
88To what tune pleased his ear, that now he was
89The ivy which had hid my princely trunk
90And sucked my verdure out on't — thou attend'st not.

MIRANDA

91O good sir, I do.

PROSPERO

O good sir, I do. I pray thee, mark me!
92I (thus neglecting worldly ends, all dedicated
93To closeness and the bettering of my mind
94With that which, but by being so retired,
95O'er-prized all popular rate) in my false brother
96Awaked an evil nature, and my trust,
97Like a good parent, did beget of him
98A falsehood in its contrary as great
99As my trust was, which had indeed no limit —
100A confidence sans bound. He being thus lorded,
101Not only with what my revenue yielded
102But what my power might else exact like one
103Who, having into truth by telling of it,
104Made such a sinner of his memory
105To credit his own lie, he did believe
106He was indeed the duke out o'th'substitution
107And executing the outward face of royalty
108With all prerogative; hence, his ambition growing —
109Dost thou hear?

MIRANDA

Dost thou hear? Your tale, sir, would cure deafness.

PROSPERO

110To have no screen between this part he played
111And him he played it for — he needs will be
112Absolute Milan. Me (poor man), my library
113Was dukedom large enough. Of temporal royalties
114He thinks me now incapable. Confederates
115(So dry he was for sway) wi'th' King of Naples
116To give him annual tribute, do him homage,
117Subject his coronet to his crown, and bend
118The dukedom, yet unbowed (alas, poor Milan!),
119To most ignoble stooping.

MIRANDA

To most ignoble stooping. Oh, the heavens!

PROSPERO

120Mark his condition and th'event, then tell me
121If this might be a brother.

MIRANDA

If this might be a brother. I should sin
122To think but nobly of my grandmother:
123Good wombs have born bad sons.

PROSPERO

Good wombs have born bad sons. Now the condition:
124This King of Naples, being an enemy
125To me inveterate, hearkens my brother's suit,
126Which was that he (in lieu o'th'premises
127Of homage and I know not how much tribute)
128Should presently extirpate me and mine
129Out of the dukedom, and confer fair Milan,
130With all the honors, on my brother — whereon,
131A treacherous army levied, one midnight
132Fated to the purpose, did Antonio open
133The gates of Milan, and i'th'dead of darkness
134The ministers for the purpose hurried thence
135Me and thy crying self.

MIRANDA

Me and thy crying self. Alack, for pity!
136I, not remembering how I cried out then,
137Will cry it o'er again; it is a hint
138That wrings mine eyes to't.

PROSPERO

That wrings mine eyes to't. Hear a little further,
139And then I'll bring thee to the present business
140Which now's upon's, without the which this story
141Were most impertinent.

MIRANDA

Were most impertinent. Wherefore did they not
142That hour destroy us?

PROSPERO

That hour destroy us? Well demanded, wench.
143My tale provokes that question. Dear, they durst not,
144So dear the love my people bore me, nor set
145A mark so bloody on the business, but
146With colors fairer, painted their foul ends.
147In few, they hurried us aboard a bark,
148Bore us some leagues to sea, where they prepared
149A rotten carcass of a butt: not rigged,
150Nor tackle, sail, nor mast. The very rats
151Instinctively have quit it. There they hoist us
152To cry to th'sea that roared to us, to sigh
153To th'winds, whose pity, sighing back again,
154Did us but loving wrong.

MIRANDA

Did us but loving wrong. Alack, what trouble
155Was I then to you?

PROSPERO

Was I then to you? Oh, a cherubin
156Thou wast that did preserve me. Thou didst smile,
157Infusèd with a fortitude from heaven
158(When I have decked the sea with drops full salt
159Under my burden groaned), which raised in me
160An undergoing stomach to bear up
161Against what should ensue.

MIRANDA

162How came we ashore?

PROSPERO

How came we ashore? By providence divine.
163Some food we had and some fresh water that
164A noble Neapolitan, Gonzalo,
165Out of his charity (who being then appointed
166Master of this design) did give us, with
167Rich garments, linens, stuffs, and necessaries,
168Which since have steaded much. So of his gentleness,
169Knowing I loved my books, he furnished me
170From mine own library with volumes that
171I prize above my dukedom.

MIRANDA

I prize above my dukedom. Would I might
172But ever see that man.

PROSPERO

But ever see that man. Now I arise.
173Sit still and hear the last of our sea-sorrow.
174Here in this island we arrived, and here
175Have I, thy schoolmaster, made thee more profit
176Than other princes can that have more time
177For vainer hours, and tutors not so careful.

MIRANDA

178Heavens thank you for't! And now I pray you, sir,
179For still 'tis beating in my mind: your reason
180For raising this sea-storm?

PROSPERO

For raising this sea-storm? Know thus far forth:
181By accident most strange, bountiful Fortune
182(Now, my dear lady!) hath mine enemies
183Brought to this shore, and by my prescience
184I find my zenith doth depend upon
185A most auspicious star, whose influence
186If now I court not, but omit, my fortunes
187Will ever after droop. Here cease more questions.
188Thou art inclined to sleep; 'tis a good dullness,
189And give it way. I know thou canst not choose.
190Come away, servant, come, I am ready now.
191Approach, my Ariel, come.

Enter Ariel.

ARIEL

192All hail, great master! Grave sir, hail! I come
193To answer thy best pleasure, be't to fly,
194To swim, to dive into the fire, to ride
195On the curled clouds. To thy strong bidding, task
196Ariel and all his quality!

PROSPERO

Ariel and all his quality! Hast thou, spirit,
197Performed to point the tempest that I bade thee?

ARIEL

198To every article.
199I boarded the King's ship — now on the beak,
200Now in the waist, the deck, in every cabin,
201I flamed amazement. Sometime I'ld divide
202And burn in many places. On the topmast,
203The yards and bowsprit would I flame distinctly,
204Then meet and join. Jove's lightning, the precursors
205O'th'dreadful thunderclaps, more momentary
206And sight out-running were not. The fire and cracks
207Of sulphurous roaring, the most mighty Neptune
208Seemed to besiege and made his bold waves tremble —
209Yea, his dread trident shake!

PROSPERO

Yea, his dread trident shake! My brave spirit,
210Who was so firm, so constant, that this coil
211Would not infect his reason?

ARIEL

Would not infect his reason? Not a soul
212But felt a fever of the mad, and played
213Some tricks of desperation. All but mariners
214Plunged in the foaming brine and quit the vessel;
215Then all afire with me, the King's son, Ferdinand,
216With hair up-staring (then like reeds, not hair!)
217Was the first man that leapt, cried, "Hell is empty,
218And all the devils are here!"

PROSPERO

And all the devils are here!" Why, that's my spirit!
219But was not this nigh shore?

ARIEL

But was not this nigh shore? Close by, my master.

PROSPERO

220But are they, Ariel, safe?

ARIEL

But are they, Ariel, safe? Not a hair perished;
221On their sustaining garments, not a blemish,
222But fresher than before. And as thou bad'st me,
223In troops I have dispersed them 'bout the isle.
224The King's son have I landed by himself,
225Whom I left cooling of the air with sighs
226In an odd angle of the isle, and sitting,
227His arms in this sad knot.

PROSPERO

His arms in this sad knot. Of the King's ship,
228The mariners — say how thou hast disposed,
229And all the rest o'th'fleet.

ARIEL

And all the rest o'th'fleet. Safely in harbor
230Is the King's ship, in the deep nook, where once
231Thou called me up at midnight to fetch dew
232From the still-vexed Bermudas, there she's hid.
233The mariners all under hatches stowed,
234Who, with a charm joined to their suffered labor,
235I have left asleep; and for the rest o'th'fleet,
236Which I dispersed, they all have met again
237And are upon the Mediterranean float,
238Bound sadly home for Naples,
239Supposing that they saw the King's ship wracked
240And his great person perish.

PROSPERO

And his great person perish. Ariel, thy charge
241Exactly is performed — but there's more work.
242What is the time o'th'day?

ARIEL

What is the time o'th'day? Past the mid season.

PROSPERO

243At least two glasses — the time 'twixt six and now —
244Must by us both be spent most preciously.

ARIEL

245Is there more toil? Since thou dost give me pains,
246Let me remember thee what thou hast promised,
247Which is not yet performed me.

PROSPERO

Which is not yet performed me. How now? Moody?
248What is't thou canst demand?

ARIEL

What is't thou canst demand? My liberty.

PROSPERO

249Before the time be out? No more!

ARIEL

Before the time be out? No more! I prithee,
250Remember I have done thee worthy service,
251Told thee no lies, made thee no mistakings, served
252Without or grudge or grumblings. Thou did promise
253To bate me a full year.

PROSPERO

To bate me a full year. Dost thou forget
254From what a torment I did free thee?

ARIEL

From what a torment I did free thee? No.

PROSPERO

255Thou dost, and think'st it much to tread the ooze
256Of the salt deep,
257To run upon the sharp wind of the north,
258To do me business in the veins o'th'earth
259When it is baked with frost.

ARIEL

When it is baked with frost. I do not, sir.

PROSPERO

260Thou liest, malignant thing! Hast thou forgot
261The foul witch Sycorax, who with age and envy
262Was grown into a hoop? Hast thou forgot her?

ARIEL

263No, sir.

PROSPERO

264Thou hast. Where was she born? Speak: tell me.

ARIEL

265Sir, in Algiers.

PROSPERO

Sir, in Algiers. Oh, was she so? I must
266Once in a month recount what thou hast been,
267Which thou forget'st. This damned witch Sycorax,
268For mischiefs manifold and sorceries terrible
269To enter human hearing, from Algiers
270Thou know'st was banished. For one thing she did,
271They would not take her life. Is not this true?

ARIEL

272Ay, sir.

PROSPERO

273This blue-eyed hag was hither brought, with child,
274And here was left by th'sailors. Thou, my slave,
275As thou report'st thyself, was then her servant;
276And, for thou wast a spirit too delicate
277To act her earthy and abhorred commands,
278Refusing her grand hests, she did confine thee,
279By help of her more potent ministers
280And her most unmitigable rage,
281Into a cloven pine, within which rift
282Imprisoned, thou didst painfully remain
283A dozen years, within which space she died
284And left thee there, where thou didst vent thy groans
285As fast as millwheels strike. Then was this island
286(Save for the son that she did litter here,
287A freckled whelp, hag-born) not honored with
288A human shape.

ARIEL

A human shape. Yes — Caliban, her son.

PROSPERO

289Dull thing, I say so — he, that Caliban,
290Whom now I keep in service. Thou best know'st
291What torment I did find thee in: thy groans
292Did make wolves howl, and penetrate the breasts
293Of ever-angry bears; it was a torment
294To lay upon the damned, which Sycorax
295Could not again undo. It was mine art,
296When I arrived and heard thee, that made gape
297The pine and let thee out.

ARIEL

The pine and let thee out. I thank thee, master.

PROSPERO

298If thou more murmur'st, I will rend an oak
299And peg thee in his knotty entrails till
300Thou hast howled away twelve winters.

ARIEL

Thou hast howled away twelve winters. Pardon, master.
301I will be correspondent to command
302And do my spriting gently.

PROSPERO

And do my spriting gently. Do so, and after two days
303I will discharge thee.

ARIEL

I will discharge thee. That's my noble master!
304What shall I do? Say what. What shall I do?

PROSPERO

305Go make thyself like a nymph o'th'sea.
306Be subject to no sight but thine and mine, invisible
307To every eyeball else. Go take this shape
308And hither come in't. Go hence
309With diligence.

Exit [Ariel].

310Awake, dear heart, awake, thou hast slept well,
311Awake.

MIRANDA

Awake. The strangeness of your story put
312Heaviness in me.

PROSPERO

Heaviness in me. Shake it off. Come on,
313We'll visit Caliban, my slave, who never
314Yields us kind answer.

MIRANDA

315'Tis a villain, sir, I do not love to look on.

PROSPERO

316But as 'tis,
317We cannot miss him; he does make our fire,
318Fetch in our wood, and serves in offices
319That profit us. What ho! Slave Caliban!
320Thou earth, thou, speak!

CALIBAN

Thou earth, thou, speak! (within) There's wood enough within.

PROSPERO

321Come forth, I say, there's other business for thee.
322Come, thou tortoise, when!
Come, thou tortoise, when! Enter Ariel like a water nymph. Fine apparition, my quaint Ariel:
323Hark in thine ear.

ARIEL

Hark in thine ear. My Lord, it shall be done.

Exit [Ariel].

PROSPERO

324Thou poisonous slave, got by the devil himself
325Upon thy wicked dam, come forth!

Enter Caliban.

CALIBAN

326As wicked dew as e'er my mother brushed
327With raven's feather from unwholesome fen
328Drop on you both! A southwest blow on ye
329And blister you all over.

PROSPERO

330For this be sure: tonight thou shalt have cramps,
331Side-stitches that shall pen thy breath up; urchins
332Shall, for that vast of night that they may work,
333All exercise on thee. Thou shalt be pinched
334As thick as honeycomb, each pinch more stinging
335Than bees that made them.

CALIBAN

Than bees that made them. I must eat my dinner.
336This island's mine by Sycorax, my mother,
337Which thou tak'st from me. When thou cam'st first,
338Thou strok'st me and made much of me, wouldst give me
339Water with berries in't, and teach me how
340To name the bigger light and how the less
341That burn by day and night. And then I loved thee
342And showed thee all the qualities o'th'isle:
343The fresh springs, brine-pits, barren place and fertile.
344Cursed be I that did so! All the charms
345Of Sycorax — toads, beetles, bats light on you!
346For I am all the subjects that you have,
347Which first was mine own King; and here you sty me
348In this hard rock whiles you do keep from me
349The rest o'th'island.

PROSPERO

The rest o'th'island. Thou most lying slave,
350Whom stripes may move, not kindness — I have used thee
351(Filth as thou art) with human care, and lodged thee
352In mine own cell till thou didst seek to violate
353The honor of my child.

CALIBAN

354Oh ho! Oh ho! Would't had been done!
355Thou didst prevent me. I had peopled else
356This isle with Calibans.

MIRANDA

This isle with Calibans. Abhorrèd slave,
357Which any print of goodness wilt not take,
358Being capable of all ill! I pitied thee,
359Took pains to make thee speak, taught thee each hour
360One thing or other when thou didst not, savage,
361Know thine own meaning, but wouldst gabble like
362A thing most brutish. I endowed thy purposes
363With words that made them known, but thy wild race
364(Though thou didst learn) had that in't which good natures
365Could not abide to be with; therefore wast thou
366Deservedly confined into this rock,
367Who hadst deserved more than a prison.

CALIBAN

368You taught me language, and my profit on't
369Is — I know how to curse. The red plague rid you
370For learning me your language.

PROSPERO

For learning me your language. Hag-seed, hence!
371Fetch us in fuel, and be quick. Thou'rt best
372To answer other business. Shrug'st thou, malice?
373If thou neglect'st or dost unwillingly
374What I command, I'll rack thee with old cramps,
375Fill all thy bones with aches, make thee roar
376That beasts shall tremble at thy din.

CALIBAN

That beasts shall tremble at thy din. No, pray thee.
377I must obey; his art is of such poweraparte
378It would control my dam's god Setebosaparte
379And make a vassal of him.aparte

PROSPERO

And make a vassal of him. So, slave, hence.

Exit Caliban.

Enter Ferdinand, and Ariel invisible, playing [music] and singing.

ARIEL

380Song. Come unto these yellow sands,
381And then take hands;
382Curtsied when you have, and kissed,
383The wild waves whist;
384Foot it featly here and there,
385And, sweet sprites, bear the burden.
386Hark, hark!
387Burden, dispersedly[, within]. Bow-wow.
388The watchdogs bark!
389[Burden, dispersedly, within.] Bow-wow.
390Hark, hark! I hear
391The strain of strutting chanticleer:
392Cry [within]. Cock-a-diddle-dow!

FERDINAND

393Where should this music be? I'th'air or th'earth?
394It sounds no more, and sure it waits upon
395Some god o'th'island. Sitting on a bank,
396Weeping again the King my father's wrack,
397This music crept by me upon the waters,
