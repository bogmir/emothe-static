Act IV

[4.1]

Enter Prospero, Ferdinand, and Miranda.

PROSPERO

1012If I have too austerely punished you,
1013Your compensation makes amends, for I
1014Have given you here a third of mine own life,
1015Or that for which I live, who once again
1016I tender to thy hand. All thy vexations
1017Were but my trials of thy love, and thou
1018Hast strangely stood the test. Here, afore heaven,
1019I ratify this my rich gift. O Ferdinand,
1020Do not smile at me that I boast of her,
1021For thou shalt find she will outstrip all praise
1022And make it halt behind her.

FERDINAND

And make it halt behind her. I do believe it
1023Against an oracle.

PROSPERO

1024Then as my gift, and thine own acquisition
1025Worthily purchased, take my daughter. But
1026If thou dost break her virgin knot before
1027All sanctimonious ceremonies may
1028With full and holy rite be ministered,
1029No sweet aspersion shall the heavens let fall
1030To make this contract grow; but barren hate,
1031Sour-eyed disdain, and discord shall bestrew
1032The union of your bed with weeds so loathly
1033That you shall hate it both. Therefore take heed
1034As Hymen's lamp shall light you.

FERDINAND

As Hymen's lamp shall light you. As I hope
1035For quiet days, fair issue, and long life
1036With such love as 'tis now, the murkiest den,
1037The most opportune place, the strong'st suggestion
1038Our worser genius can, shall never melt
1039Mine honor into lust to take away
1040The edge of that day's celebration
1041When I shall think or Phoebus' steeds are foundered,
1042Or night kept chained below.

PROSPERO

Or night kept chained below. Fairly spoke.
1043Sit then and talk with her; she is thine own.
1044What, Ariel! My industrious servant Ariel!

Enter Ariel.

ARIEL

1045What would my potent master? Here I am.

PROSPERO

1046Thou and thy meaner fellows, your last service
1047Did worthily perform, and I must use you
1048In such another trick: go bring the rabble
1049(O'er whom I give thee power) here to this place.
1050Incite them to quick motion, for I must
1051Bestow upon the eyes of this young couple
1052Some vanity of mine art; it is my promise,
1053And they expect it from me.

ARIEL

And they expect it from me. Presently?

PROSPERO

1054Ay, with a twink.

ARIEL

1055Before you can say "come" and "go",
1056And breathe twice and cry "so, so",
1057Each one, tripping on his toe,
1058Will be here with mop and mow.
1059Do you love me, master, no?

PROSPERO

1060Dearly, my delicate Ariel. Do not approach
1061Till thou dost hear me call.

ARIEL

Till thou dost hear me call. Well I conceive.

Exit [Ariel].

PROSPERO

1062[To Ferdinand] Look thou be true: do not give dalliance
1063Too much the rein. The strongest oaths are straw
1064To th'fire i'th'blood. Be more abstemious,
1065Or else good night your vow.

FERDINAND

Or else good night your vow. I warrant you, sir,
1066The white-cold virgin snow upon my heart
1067Abates the ardor of my liver.

PROSPERO

Abates the ardor of my liver. Well.
1068Now come, my Ariel. Bring a corollary
1069Rather than want a spirit: appear, and pertly!

Soft music

1070No tongue — all eyes — be silent!

Enter Iris.

IRIS

1071Ceres, most bounteous lady, thy rich leas
1072Of wheat, rye, barley, vetches, oats, and peas;
1073Thy turfy mountains where live nibbling sheep,
1074And flat meads thatched with stover, them to keep;
1075Thy banks with pionèd and twillèd brims,
1076Which spongy April at thy hest betrims
1077To make cold nymphs chaste crowns; and thy broomgroves,
1078Whose shadow the dismissèd bachelor loves,
1079Being lass-lorn; thy pole-clipped vineyard
1080And thy sea-marge, sterile and rocky-hard,
1081Where thou thyself dost air: the Queen o'th'sky,
1082Whose watry arch and messenger am I,
1083Bids thee leave these, and with her sovereign grace

Juno descends [slowly in her chariot].

1084Here on this grass-plot, in this very place,
1085To come and sport. Here peacocks fly amain.
1086Approach, rich Ceres, her to entertain.

Enter [Ariel as] Ceres.

CERES

1087Hail, many-colored messenger, that ne'er
1088Dost disobey the wife of Jupiter;
1089Who, with thy saffron wings, upon my flowers
1090Diffusest honey-drops, refreshing showers,
1091And with each end of thy blue bow dost crown
1092My bosky acres and my unshrubbed down,
1093Rich scarf to my proud earth: why hath thy queen
1094Summoned me hither to this short-grassed green?

IRIS

1095A contract of true love to celebrate,
1096And some donation freely to estate
1097On the blessed lovers.

CERES

On the blessed lovers. Tell me, heavenly bow,
1098If Venus or her son, as thou dost know,
1099Do now attend the queen? Since they did plot
1100The means that dusky Dis my daughter got,
1101Her and her blind boy's scandaled company
1102I have forsworn.

IRIS

I have forsworn. Of her society
1103Be not afraid — I met her deity
1104Cutting the clouds towards Paphos, and her son
1105Dove-drawn with her. Here thought they to have done
1106Some wanton charm upon this man and maid,
1107Whose vows are that no bed-right shall be paid
1108Till Hymen's torch be lighted; but in vain,
1109Mars's hot minion is returned again;
1110Her waspish-headed son has broke his arrows,
1111Swears he will shoot no more but play with sparrows
1112[Juno alights.] And be a boy right out.

CERES

[Juno alights.] And be a boy right out. Highest Queen of state,
1113Great Juno comes; I know her by her gait.

JUNO

1114How does my bounteous sister? Go with me
1115To bless this twain, that they may prosperous be
1116And honored in their issue.

They sing.

JUNO AND CERES

1117Honor, riches, marriage-blessing,
1118Long continuance and increasing,
1119Hourly joys be still upon you!
1120Juno sings her blessings on you.
1121Earth's increase, foison plenty,
1122Barns and garners never empty,
1123Vines with clustering bunches growing,
1124Plants with goodly burthen bowing;
1125Spring come to you at the farthest
1126In the very end of harvest!
1127Scarcity and want shall shun you;
1128Ceres' blessing so is on you.

FERDINAND

1129This is a most majestic vision, and
1130Harmonious charmingly — may I be bold
1131To think these spirits?

PROSPERO

To think these spirits? Spirits, which by mine art
1132I have from their confines called to enact
1133My present fancies.

FERDINAND

My present fancies. Let me live here ever —
1134So rare a wondered father and a wise
1135Makes this place paradise.

PROSPERO

Makes this place paradise. Sweet, now silence;
1136Juno and Ceres whisper seriously.
1137There's something else to do: hush and be mute
1138Or else our spell is marred.

Juno and Ceres whisper and send Iris on employment.

IRIS

1139You nymphs called naiads of the windering brooks,
1140With your sedged crowns and ever-harmless looks:
1141Leave your crisp channels, and on this green land
1142Answer your summons, Juno does command.
1143Come, temperate nymphs, and help to celebrate
1144A contract of true love — be not too late.

Enter certain nymphs.

1145You sunburned sicklemen, of August weary:
1146Come hither from the furrow and be merry —
1147Make holiday! Your rye-straw hats put on,
1148And these fresh nymphs encounter every one
1149In country footing.

Enter certain reapers, properly habited; they join with the nymphs in a graceful dance, towards the end whereof Prospero starts suddenly and speaks, after which, to a strange, hollow, and confused noise, they heavily vanish.

PROSPERO

1150I had forgot that foul conspiracyaparte
1151Of the beast Caliban and his confederatesaparte
1152Against my life; the minute of their plotaparte
1153Is almost come.aparte

FERDINAND

1154This is strange — your father's in some passion
1155That works him strongly.

MIRANDA

That works him strongly. Never till this day
1156Saw I him touched with anger so distempered.

PROSPERO

1157You do look, my son, in a moved sort,
1158As if you were dismayed. Be cheerful, sir.
1159Our revels now are ended. These our actors,
1160As I foretold you, were all spirits and
1161Are melted into air — into thin air —
1162And, like the baseless fabric of this vision,
1163The cloud-capped towers, the gorgeous palaces,
1164The solemn temples, the great globe itself,
1165Yea, all which it inherit, shall dissolve,
1166And, like this insubstantial pageant faded
1167Leave not a rack behind. We are such stuff
1168As dreams are made on, and our little life
1169Is rounded with a sleep. Sir, I am vexed,
1170Bear with my weakness; my old brain is troubled.
1171Be not disturbed with my infirmity.
1172If you be pleased, retire into my cell
1173And there repose. A turn or two I'll walk
1174To still my beating mind.

FERDINAND AND MIRANDA

To still my beating mind. We wish your peace.

PROSPERO

1175[To Ariel] Come with a thought. [To Ferdinand and Miranda] I thank thee.
[To Ariel] Come with a thought. [To Ferdinand and Miranda] I thank thee. Exit [Ferdinand and Miranda]. Ariel: come.

Enter Ariel.

ARIEL

1176Thy thoughts I cleave to; what's thy pleasure?

PROSPERO

1177Spirit, we must prepare to meet with Caliban.

ARIEL

1178Ay, my commander. When I presented Ceres
1179I thought to have told thee of it, but I feared
1180Lest I might anger thee.

PROSPERO

1181Say again, where didst thou leave these varlets?

ARIEL

1182I told you, sir; they were red-hot with drinking,
1183So full of valor that they smote the air
1184For breathing in their faces, beat the ground
1185For kissing of their feet, yet always bending
1186Towards their project. Then I beat my tabor,
1187At which like unbacked colts they pricked their ears,
1188Advanced their eyelids, lifted up their noses
1189As they smelt music — so I charmed their ears
1190That calf-like they my lowing followed through
1191Toothèd briars, sharp furze, pricking gorse and thorns,
1192Which entered their frail shins. At last I left them
1193I'th'filthy-mantled pool beyond your cell,
1194There dancing up to th'chins that the foul lake
1195O'erstunk their feet.

PROSPERO

O'erstunk their feet. This was well done, my bird.
1196Thy shape invisible retain thou still.
1197The trumpery in my house, go bring it hither
1198For stale to catch these thieves.

ARIEL

For stale to catch these thieves. I go, I go.

Exit.

PROSPERO

1199A devil — a born devil, on whose nature
1200Nurture can never stick; on whom my pains
1201Humanely taken, all, all lost, quite lost!
1202And as with age his body uglier grows,
1203So his mind cankers. I will plague them all
1204Even to roaring. Come: hang them on this line.

Enter Ariel, loaden with glistering apparel, etc. Enter Caliban, Stephano, and Trinculo, all wet.

CALIBAN

Pray you, tread softly, that the blind mole may not hear a footfall; we now are near his cell.

STEPHANO

Monster, your fairy, which you say is a harmless fairy, has done little better than played the jack with us.

TRINCULO

Monster, I do smell all horse-piss, at which my nose is in great indignation.

STEPHANO

So is mine. Do you hear, monster? If I should take a displeasure against you, look you —

TRINCULO

Thou wert but a lost monster.

CALIBAN

1205Good my Lord, give me thy favor still.
1206Be patient, for the prize I'll bring thee to
1207Shall hoodwink this mischance; therefore speak softly —
1208All's hushed as midnight yet.

TRINCULO

Ay, but to lose our bottles in the pool!

STEPHANO

There is not only disgrace and dishonor in that, monster, but an infinite loss.

TRINCULO

That's more to me than my wetting, yet this is your harmless fairy, monster.

STEPHANO

I will fetch off my bottle, though I be o'er ears for my labor.

CALIBAN

1209Prithee, my King, be quiet. Seest thou here;
1210This is the mouth o'th'cell — no noise, and enter.
1211Do that good mischief which may make this island
1212Thine own for ever and I, thy Caliban,
1213For aye thy foot-licker.

STEPHANO

For aye thy foot-licker. Give me thy hand —

I do begin to have bloody thoughts.

TRINCULO

O King Stephano! O peer! O worthy Stephano, look what a wardrobe here is for thee!

CALIBAN

1214Let it alone, thou fool; it is but trash.

TRINCULO

Oh ho, monster! We know what belongs to a frippery. O King Stephano!

STEPHANO

Put off that gown, Trinculo! By this hand, I'll have that gown.

TRINCULO

Thy grace shall have it.

CALIBAN

1215The dropsy drown this fool. What do you mean
1216To dote thus on such luggage? Let's alone
1217And do the murder first — if he awake,
1218From toe to crown he'll fill our skins with pinches,
1219Make us strange stuff.

STEPHANO

Be you quiet, monster. Mistress Line, is not this my jerkin? Now is the jerkin under the line. Now, jerkin, you are like to lose your hair and prove a bald jerkin.

TRINCULO

Do, do! We steal by line and level, an't like your grace.

STEPHANO

I thank thee for that jest; here's a garment for't. Wit shall not go unrewarded while I am king of this country. "Steal by line and level" is an excellent pass of pate — there's another garment for't.

TRINCULO

Monster, come put some lime upon your fingers, and away with the rest.

CALIBAN

1220I will have none on't — we shall lose our time
1221And all be turned to barnacles or to apes
1222With foreheads villainous low.

STEPHANO

Monster, lay to your fingers: help to bear this away where my hogshead of wine is, or I'll turn you out of my kingdom. Go to; carry this.

TRINCULO

And this.

STEPHANO

Ay, and this.

A noise of hunters heard. Enter divers spirits in shape of dogs and hounds hunting them about, Prospero and Ariel setting them on.

PROSPERO

1223Hey, Mountain, hey!

ARIEL

Hey, Mountain, hey! Silver — there it goes — Silver!

PROSPERO

1224Fury, Fury! There, Tyrant, there! Hark, hark!
1225Go charge my goblins that they grind their joints
1226With dry convulsions, shorten up their sinews
1227With aged cramps, and more pinch-spotted make them
1228Than pard or cat o'mountain.

ARIEL

Than pard or cat o'mountain. Hark, they roar!

PROSPERO

1229Let them be hunted soundly. At this hour
1230Lies at my mercy all mine enemies.
1231Shortly shall all my labors end, and thou
1232Shalt have the air at freedom: for a little,
1233Follow, and do me service.

Exeunt.
