Ben Jonson

The Masque of Blackness

By this, one of the tritons, with the two sea-maids, began to sing to the others’ loud music, their voices being a tenor and two trebles.

Song

1Sound, sound aloud
2The welcome of the orient flood
3Into the west;
4Fair Niger, son to great Oceanus,
5Now honoured thus,
6With all his beauteous race,
7Who, though but black in face,
8Yet are they bright
9And full of life and light;
10To prove that beauty best,
11Which not the colour, but the feature
12Assures unto the creature.

OCEANUS

13Be silent, now the ceremony’s done.
14And Niger, say, how comes it, lovely son,
15That thou, the Ethiops’ river, so far east,
16Art seen to fall into th’extremest west
17Of me, the king of floods, Oceanus,
18And in mine empire’s heart salute me thus?
19My ceaseless current now amazèd stands
20To see thy labour through so many lands
21Mix thy fresh billow with my brackish stream,
22And, in thy sweetness, stretch thy diademe
23To these far distant and unequalled skies,
24This squarèd circle of celestial bodies.

NIGER

25Divine Oceanus, ’tis not strange at all
26That, since the immortal souls of creatures mortal
27Mix with their bodies, yet reserve for ever
28A power of separation, I should sever
29My fresh streams from thy brackish, like things fixed,
30Though with thy powerful saltness thus far mixed.
31Virtue, though chained to earth, will still live free;
32And hell itself must yield to industry.

OCEANUS

33But what’s the end of thy Hercúlean labours,
34Extended to these calm and blessèd shores?

NIGER

35To do a kind and careful father’s part,
36In satisfying every pensive heart
37Of these my daughters, my most lovèd birth;
38Who, though they were the first-formed dames of earth,
39And in whose sparkling and refulgent eyes
40The glorious sun did still delight to rise;
41Though he (the best judge, and most formal cause
42Of all dames’ beauties) in their firm hues draws
43Signs of his fervent’st love; and thereby shows
44That in their black the perfect’st beauty grows;
45Since the fixed colour of their curlèd hair
46(Which is the highest grace of dames most fair)
47No cares, no age can change, or there display
48The fearful tincture of abhorrèd grey;
49Since Death herself (herself being pale and blue)
50Can never alter their most faithful hue;
51All which are arguments to prove how far
52Their beauties conquer in great beauty’s war,
53And more, how near divinity they be,
54That stand from passion or decay so free.
55Yet, since the fabulous voices of some few
56Poor brain-sick men, styled poets here with you,
57Have, with such envy of their graces, sung
58The painted beauties other empires sprung,
59Letting their loose and wingèd fictions fly
60To infect all climates, yea our purity;
61As of one Phaëton, that fired the world,
62And that before his heedless flames were hurled
63About the globe the Ethiops were as fair
64As other dames, now black with black despair;
65And in respect of their complexions changed,
66Are each-where, since, for luckless creatures ranged.
67Which when my daughters heard – as women are
68Most jealous of their beauties – fear and care
69Possessed them whole; yea, and believing them,
70They wept such ceaseless tears into my stream
71That it hath thus far overflowed his shore
72To seek them patience: who have since, e’ermore
73As the sun riseth, charged his burning throne
74With volleys of revilings, ’cause he shone
75On their scorched cheeks with such intemperate fires,
76And other dames made queens of all desires.
77To frustrate which strange error, oft I sought –
78Though most in vain, against a settled thought
79As women’s are – till they confirmed at length
80By miracle what I with so much strength
81Of argument resisted; else they feigned:
82For in the lake where their first spring they gained,
83As they sat cooling their soft limbs one night,
84Appeared a face, all circumfused with light –
85And sure they saw’t, for Ethiops never dream –
86Wherein they might decipher through the stream
87These words:
88That they a land must forthwith seek,
89Whose termination (of the Greek)
90Sounds -tania; where bright Sol, that heat
91Their bloods, doth never rise or set,
92But in his journey passeth by,
93And leaves that climate of the sky
94To comfort of a greater light,
95Who forms all beauty with his sight.
96In search of this have we three princedoms passed,
97That speak out -tania in their accents last;
98Black Mauritania first, and secondly
99Swart Lusitania; next we did descry
100Rich Aquitania; and, yet, cannot find
101The place unto these longing nymphs designed.
102Instruct and aid me, great Oceanus;
103What land is this, that now appears to us?

OCEANUS

104This land, that lifts into the temperate air
105His snowy cliff, is Albion the fair;
106So called of Neptune’s son, who ruleth here;
107For whose dear guard, myself four thousand year,
108Since old Deucalion’s days, have walked the round
109About his empire, proud to see him crowned
110Above my waves.

At this, the moon was discovered in the upper part of the house, triumphant in a silver throne, made in figure of a pyramis; her garments white and silver, the dressing of her head antique, and crowned with a luminary, or sphere of light, which, striking on the clouds, and heightened with silver, reflected as natural clouds do by the splendour of the moon. The heaven about her was vaulted with blue silk, and set with stars of silver which had in them their several lights burning. The sudden sight of which made Niger to interrupt Oceanus with this present passion:

NIGER

Above my waves. — Oh, see, our silver star!
111Whose pure, auspicious light greets us thus far!
112Great Ethiopia, goddess of our shore,
113Since with particular worship we adore
114Thy general brightness, let particular grace
115Shine on my zealous daughters. Show the place
116Which long their longings urged their eyes to see.
117Beautify them, which long have deified thee.

ETHIOPIA

118Niger, be glad; resume thy native cheer.
119Thy daughters’ labours have their period here,
120And so thy errors. I was that bright face
121Reflected by the lake, in which thy race
122Read mystic lines (which skill Pythagoras
123First taught to men by a reverberate glass).
124This blessed isle doth with that -tania end,
125Which there they saw inscribed, and shall extend
126Wished satisfaction to their best desires.
127Britania, which the triple world admires,
128This isle hath now recovered for her name;
129Where reign those beauties that with so much fame
130The sacred muses’ sons have honourèd,
131And from bright Hesperus to Eos spread.
132With that great name, Britania, this bless’d isle
133Hath won her ancient dignity and style,
134A world divided from the world ; and tried
135The abstract of it in his general pride.
136For were the world, with all his wealth, a ring,
137Britania, whose new name makes all tongues sing,
138Might be a diamond worthy to enchase it,
139Ruled by a sun that to this height doth grace it;
140Whose beams shine day and night, and are of force
141To blanch an Ethiop and revive a corse.
142His light sciential is, and, past mere nature,
143Can salve the rude defects of every creature.
144Call forth thy honoured daughters, then;
145And let them, ’fore the Britain men,
146Indent the land with those pure traces
147They flow with in their native graces.
148Invite them boldly to the shore,
149Their beauties shall be scorched no more:
150This sun is temperate, and refines
151All things on which his radiance shines.

Here the tritons sounded, and they danced on shore, every couple, as they advanced, severally presenting their fans; in one of which were inscribed their mixed names, in the other a mute hieroglyphic, expressing their mixed qualities. Which manner of symbol I rather chose than imprese , as well for strangeness as relishing of antiquity, and more applying to that original doctrine of sculpture which the Egyptians are said first to have brought from the Ethiopians.

The names of the Oceaniae were:

Their own single dance ended , as they were about to make choice of their men, one from the sea was heard to call ’em with this charm, sung by a tenor voice:

Song

152Come away, come away,
153We grow jealous of your stay.
154If you do not stop your ear,
155We shall have more cause to fear
156Sirens of the land, than they
157To doubt the sirens of the sea.

Here they danced with their men several measures and corantos. All which ended, they were again accited to sea with a song of two trebles, whose cadences were iterated by a double echo from several parts of the land.

Song

FIRST TREBLE

158Daughters of the subtle flood,
159Do not let earth longer entertain you;

FIRST ECHO

160Let earth longer entertain you.

SECOND ECHO

161Longer entertain you.

SECOND TREBLE

162’Tis to them enough of good,
163That you give this little hope to gain you.

FIRST ECHO

164Give this little hope to gain you.

SECOND ECHO

165Little hope to gain you.

FIRST TREBLE

166If they love,

SECOND TREBLE

167You shall quickly see;

FIRST TREBLE

168For when to flight you move
169They’ll follow you, the more you flee.

FIRST ECHO

170Follow you, the more you flee.

SECOND ECHO

171The more you flee.

BOTH TREBLES

172If not, impute it each to other’s matter;
173They are but earth,

FIRST ECHO

174But earth,

SECOND ECHO

175Earth,
176both trebles
177And what you vowed was water.

FIRST ECHO

178And what you vowed was water.

SECOND ECHO

179You vowed was water.

ETHIOPIA

180Enough, bright nymphs, the night grows old,
181And we are grieved we cannot hold
182You longer light; but comfort take,
183Your father, only, to the lake
184Shall make return; yourselves, with feasts,
185Must here remain the Ocean’s guests.
186Nor shall this veil the sun hath cast
187Above your blood, more summers last.
188For which, you shall observe these rites:
189Thirteen times thrice, on thirteen nights,
190(So often as I fill my sphere
191With glorious light, throughout the year)
192You shall, when all things else do sleep
193Save your chaste thoughts, with reverence steep
194Your bodies in that purer brine,
195And wholesome dew, called rosmarine;
196Then with that soft and gentler foam,
197Of which the ocean yet yields some
198Whereof bright Venus, beauty’s queen,
199Is said to have begotten been,
200You shall your gentler limbs o’er-lave,
201And, for your pains, perfection have;
202So that, this night, the year gone round,
203You do again salute this ground;
204And in the beams of yond bright sun
205Your faces dry, and all is done.

At which, in a dance, they returned to the sea, where they took their shell; and, with this full song, went out.

Song

206Now Dian, with her burning face,
207Declines apace;
208By which our waters know
209To ebb, that late did flow.
210Back seas, back nymphs; but with a forward grace
211Keep still your reverence to the place;
212And shout with joy of favour you have won
213In sight of Albion, Neptune’s son.
