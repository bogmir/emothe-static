RepresentacionesPerformances

  • c. 1620 Cockpit · teatro comercial Cockpit · commercial theatre [1] vol. 5, p. 39

    The play appears on a list apparently on the repertory of Prince Charles’ Men. The list was compiled at the Revels Office. It was being considered for performance at court.

  • 1636 Blackfiars · The King’s Men · teatro comercial Blackfiars · The King’s Men · commercial theatre [1] vol. 6, p. 40

    The play was in repertory.

  • 1636 St. James’s Palac · The King’s Men · palacio St. James’s Palac · The King’s Men · palace / court [1] vol. 6, p. 40
  • 1636 The Cockpit · teatro comercial The Cockpit · commercial theatre [1] vol. 6, p. 40

    The audience included King Charles I, Queen Henrietta Maria.

  • Whitefriars · Children of the Queen's Revels · teatro comercial Whitefriars · Children of the Queen's Revels · commercial theatre [1] vol. 6, p. 39

    The cast included Richard Allen, Hugh Attwell, William Barksted, John Blaney, Gilbert Carey, Nathan Field, William Penn, John Smith. Jonson says that a poem was found on the stage after the performance commenting on the lack of applause. The play was suppressed, according to reports, after Lady Arabella Stuart took offence at an allusion to the self-proclaimed Prince of Moldavia.

TO THE TRULY NOBLE, BY ALL TITLES,

Sir Francis Stuart:
Sir,
My hope is not so nourished by example, as it will conclude this dumb piece should please you because it hath pleased others before; but by trust, that when you have read it, you will find it worthy to have displeased none. This makes that I now number you not only in the name of favour but the names of justice to what I write; and do presently call you to the exercise of that noblest and manliest virtue, as coveting rather to be freed in my fame by the authority of a judge than the credit of an undertaker. Read therefore, I pray you, and censure. There is nor a line or syllable in it changed from the simplicity of the first copy. And, when you shall consider, through the certain hatred of some, how much a man’s innocency may be endangered by an uncertain accusation, you will, I doubt not, so begin to hate the iniquity of such natures I shall love the contumely done me, whose end was so honourable as to be wiped off by your sentence.
Your unprofitable but true lover,
Ben Jonson

PROLOGUE

Truth says of old the art of making plays
Was to content the people, and their praise
Was to the Poet money, wine, and bays.
But in this age a sect of writers are,
That only for particular likings care
And will taste nothing that is popular.
With such we mingle neither brains nor breasts ;
Our wishes, like to those make public feasts,
Are not to please the cooks’ tastes but the guests’.
Yet if those cunning palates hither come,
They shall find guests’ entreaty and good room ;
And though all relish not, sure there will be some
That, when they leave their seats, shall make ’em say,
Who wrote that piece could so have wrote a play,
But that he knew this was the better way.
For to present all custard or all tart
And have no other meats to bear a part,
Or to want bread and salt, were but coarse art.
The Poet prays you then with better thought
To sit, and when his cates are all in brought,
Though there be none far fet, there will dear-bought
Be fit for ladies, some for lords, knights, squires,
Some for your waiting-wench, and city-wires,
Some for your men, and daughters of Whitefriars.
Nor is it only while you keep your seet
Here that his feast will last, but you shall eat
A week at ord’naries on his broken meat,
If his muse be true
Who commends her to you.

Another : Occasion’d by some person’s impertinent exception.

The ends of all who for the scene do write
Are of should be to profit and delight.
And still’t hath been the praise of all best times,
So persons were not touch’d, to tax the crimes.
Then in this play wich we present tonight
And make the object of your ear and sight,
On forfeit of yourselves, think nothing true,
Lest so you make the maker to judge you.
For he knows poet never credit gain’d
By writing truths, but things (like truths) well feign’d.
If any yet will, with particular sleight
Of application, wrest what he doth write
And that he meant or him or her will say,
They make a libel wich he made a play.
